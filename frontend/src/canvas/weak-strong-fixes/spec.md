# Canvas spec: A sign, a checklist or a new order book?

## Teaching claim
A reminder asks people to remember; a better design means they do not have to. A weak fix (a sign) loses to a stronger one on the tests that make a fix last, and stays behind even when cheapness matters most.

## Why a canvas
Learners often choose the cheap reminder because it is quick. Letting them raise the weight of "Cheap and quick to start" and watch the sign still fail to win makes the ranking feel earned, not told.

## Model
- Inputs: one weight per criterion.

  | Name | Unit | Min | Max | Step | Default |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | Works at the busy moment | weight level | 1 | 3 | 1 | 3 |
  | Works for a new packer | weight level | 1 | 3 | 1 | 2 |
  | Needs no one to remember | weight level | 1 | 3 | 1 | 3 |
  | Cheap and quick to start | weight level | 1 | 3 | 1 | 1 |

- Fixed data (illustrative judgement, 1 to 5): sign 1, 2, 1, 5; tick as you pack 3, 3, 2, 4; order book with swap box 5, 5, 4, 2.
- Outputs: totals per fix, the leader or a tie, the margin.
- Formula: total(option) = sum of weight(criterion) x score(option, criterion).
- Checked totals: default sign 15, tick 25, book 39; cheap at 3 and the rest at 1: sign 19, tick 20, book 20 (a tie); all at 1: sign 9, tick 12, book 16.
- Limitations: scores are judgement; a strong fix that does not fit the work can still fail.

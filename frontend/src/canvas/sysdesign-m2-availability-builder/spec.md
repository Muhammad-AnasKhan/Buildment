# Canvas spec: sysdesign-m2-availability-builder

- **Course and chapter:** system-design, 2.6 "Availability maths: nines, series and parallel". Suggested home: the beat "Find the weakest link", in place of its Mermaid diagram (new slug; replaces no canvas).
- **Renderer:** custom beat canvas on `BeatFrame`, `VerdictBanner`, `useBeatClock`, with the new reusable primitive `primitives/year-strip.tsx` (`YearStrip`: 365 day squares, marked days filled in proportion, a week cursor). Custom because no kit renderer builds a chain by tapping. `YearStrip` is data-driven and fits error-budget and SLO pictures later (roadmap 17.3).
- **Claim:** Parts in series multiply their availabilities, so every part added costs a little; copies in parallel multiply their down chances, so one copy adds a lot; the part with the most downtime is the one to fix next.
- **Input:** four columns (load balancer 99.99%, app 99.9%, cache 99.95%, database 99.9%), each with 0 to 3 copies. Tap a dashed "+ copy" (or "+ Cache") to add; tap a card to remove it and the copies below it. Every slot is a `role="button"` with Enter and Space.
- **Model:** `models/availability.ts`, unit tested. Group = 1 - (1 - a)^n; chain = product of groups; downtime = (1 - chain) x 8,760 hours. Availability shows decimals until the first non-nine and never rounds up to 100%. Downtime in seconds, minutes, hours or days. Red days: each group's share of the downtime (add-the-downtimes shortcut, scaled to the exact total), worst group first, spread over fixed days.
- **Verdict:** "<availability>: <downtime> down a year", green up to 1 hour, amber up to a day, red above; at most 38 characters for all 256 mixes (tested).
- **Default and the chapter's steps:** load balancer, app and database: "99.79%: 18 hours down a year". Two app copies: "99.89%: 9.6 hours". Two database copies as well: "99.99%: 54 minutes" (the chapter says "close to 53"; 53.6 rounds to 54). Three 99.9% parts: "99.7%: 26 hours".
- **Motion:** request dots run along the chain and take turns through copies. A cursor sweeps the year week by week; on a red day it holds and the responsible part shows "down" in red while dots stop at it. Reduced motion shows the still chain and the red days, no cursor.
- **What to notice:** taking a part out raises the total; a copy of the weakest part removes most of the downtime; a third copy changes almost nothing; the caption names the part with the most downtime.
- **Limits (in the fold):** example targets, not measurements; the copy rule assumes independent failures, which shared hosts, racks, zones and deploys often break; failover is drawn as instant; red days are placed for the picture.
- **Sources:** example targets and arithmetic from the chapter; error budgets [S95, S96] are in the chapter, not in this picture.

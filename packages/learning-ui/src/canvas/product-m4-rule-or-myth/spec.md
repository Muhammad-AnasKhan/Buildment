# Canvas spec: product-m4-rule-or-myth

- **Course and chapter:** product-sense, 4.5 "Popular rules that fail a check", beat 2 ("Sort nine popular rules").
- **Renderer:** custom, on the reusable `Sorter` primitive (`primitives/sorter.tsx`, model `models/sorter.ts`) with three buckets. This folder holds only data. Reason: the skill is to judge a claim by its evidence, which the learner practises by sorting claims and reading the rule behind a wrong bucket.
- **Claim:** Popular design rules differ in how much evidence they have. One rule separates the buckets. Supported: a standard, a study or written practice backs the rule as it is stated. Misread: a real finding is used where it was not tested. Unsupported: no test backs the rule as stated, because it failed, the evidence is mixed, or its author took it back. Judging this is part of taste.
- **Model:** nine cards. Supported (3): normal text needs 4.5 to 1 [A1]; most viewing time is near the top of a page [A53]; a form label should stay visible above its field [A25, A74]. Misread (3): five users find all the problems [A20]; a menu should hold seven items, plus or minus two [A55, A83]; lay out your page to follow the F-pattern [A68]. Unsupported (3): every page within three clicks [A54]; the golden ratio makes a design beautiful [A56]; blue makes people trust you [A57]. The golden ratio and blue are filed Unsupported because the sources call the evidence mixed or context dependent, so no test backs the rule as stated; the chapter text says the same.
- **Cue conflicts:** digits appear in supported (4.5 to 1), misread (five, seven) and unsupported (three) cards alike, and the F-pattern card, which has no digit, is Misread.
- **Inputs:** tap a bucket or drag the card onto a tray. Each wrong try shows a rule written for that card and bucket.
- **What changes:** bucket counts, the card, and the rule shown after a wrong try.
- **What the learner should notice:** a popular rule that sounds precise (three, seven) usually has an origin narrower than the rule; "supported" rules name a standard, a study or a maker's reasons; digits and absolute words do not decide the bucket.
- **Accessibility:** tray buttons are labelled with the name, gloss and count; a polite live region reads each result and rule; text at least 11.5 units; every rule fits two lines of 60 characters.
- **Limits (in the data):** evidence changes and some of it is thin or old; the labels describe the sources opened for this course, checked 2026-10-25; unsupported does not mean the opposite is true.
- **Sources:** [A1], [A20], [A25], [A53], [A54], [A55], [A56], [A57], [A68], [A74], [A83].

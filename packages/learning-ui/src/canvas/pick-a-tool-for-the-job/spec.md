# Canvas spec: Which kind of tool for this job?

## Teaching claim
The right kind of tool depends on the job: with every need weighted the same, the chat assistant leads; when "stays inside my sources" matters most, the source notebook wins; when "finds recent pages" matters most, the search tool with sources wins.

## Why a canvas
Learners tend to pick one familiar tool for every job. Letting them raise one need at a time and watch the leader change makes "jobs, not tools" something they see, not a rule they are told. The chapter's predict is the intuitive answer (the chat assistant leads at equal weights), which the canvas confirms before it shows the leader moving.

## Model
- Inputs: one weight per criterion.

  | Name | Unit | Min | Max | Step | Default |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | Stays inside my sources | weight level | 1 | 3 | 1 | 2 |
  | Explains with pictures | weight level | 1 | 3 | 1 | 2 |
  | Finds recent pages | weight level | 1 | 3 | 1 | 2 |
  | Talks it through with me | weight level | 1 | 3 | 1 | 2 |

- Weight levels: 1 "Matters a little", 2 "Matters", 3 "Matters a lot".
- Fixed data (illustrative judgement, scale 1 to 5), in criterion order: chat assistant 2, 4, 2, 5; source notebook 5, 3, 1, 3; search with sources 1, 2, 5, 2.
- Outputs: totals per kind of tool, the leader or a tie, the margin.
- Formula: total(option) = sum of weight(criterion) x score(option, criterion).
- Checked totals (each worked twice by hand):
  - Default, all weights 2: chat 2 x (2 + 4 + 2 + 5) = 26; notebook 2 x (5 + 3 + 1 + 3) = 24; search 2 x (1 + 2 + 5 + 2) = 20. Chat leads by 2.
  - Sources 3, the rest 2: chat 6 + 8 + 4 + 10 = 28; notebook 15 + 6 + 2 + 6 = 29; search 3 + 4 + 10 + 4 = 21. Notebook leads by 1.
  - Sources 3, the rest 1: chat 6 + 4 + 2 + 5 = 17; notebook 15 + 3 + 1 + 3 = 22; search 3 + 2 + 5 + 2 = 12. Notebook leads by 5.
  - Recent 3, the rest 1: chat 2 + 4 + 6 + 5 = 17; notebook 5 + 3 + 3 + 3 = 14; search 1 + 2 + 15 + 2 = 20. Search leads by 3.
  - Recent 3, the rest 2: chat 4 + 8 + 6 + 10 = 28; notebook 10 + 6 + 3 + 6 = 25; search 2 + 4 + 15 + 4 = 25. Chat still leads: recent pages must matter more than the other needs for search to win.
  - All weights 1: chat 13; notebook 12; search 10.
- Assumptions: kinds of tool, not named products; one study job at a time.
- Limitations: scores are judgement and date quickly (a chat assistant with web search would score higher on recent pages); the three-question check (opens here, my age, my device) is outside the model.

## Rendering
- Primary: the shared factor renderer (HTML weight controls, SVG bars).
- Static fallback: chapter 2.3 gives the jobs table with example tools and fallbacks, and the "what it showed" paragraph states the checked totals in prose.

## Controls
One weight control per need, at three levels. Each exists because a different job raises a different need.

## Visualisation
Three bars, one per kind of tool, labelled directly with their totals.

## Edge cases
All weights equal at any level keep the same order (chat, notebook, search). Criterion names are 24 characters or fewer; option names 19 or fewer.

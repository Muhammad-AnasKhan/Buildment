# Canvas spec: sysdesign-m15-backlog-play

- **Course and chapter:** system-design, 15.2 ("Why queues"), beat 3, "Drag the burst, watch the line". Playable; new slug.
- **Renderer:** custom beat canvas on `BeatFrame`, built from the traffic-playground parts: `flowDots` and `flowDotsPerSecond` for the job dots, `serverLoad` (the Module 2.5 knee model) for how busy the workers are and their tone, `SliderRail` for the burst rail, `SnapStopsRail` for the workers, `SvgNode` cards and `VerdictBanner`. `TrafficPlayground` itself is not used as a whole: its fixed layout (server plus inset curve) has no room for a backlog over time, so its parts are composed instead. One toggle outside the picture: queue limit (none, or 5,000).
- **Claim:** While a burst brings more jobs than the workers finish, the backlog grows by the difference every second. After the burst it shrinks only by the spare capacity, so the line outlives the burst. With capacity at or below the normal 150 a second it never shrinks. A queue limit refuses jobs instead of letting the line grow.
- **Input:** burst rate 150 to 400 jobs a second on a rail inside the picture (step 10, snaps to 150, 200 ... 400, default 300; arrows step 10, Page keys 50, Home and End). Workers on a snap rail 3, 4, 5, 6, 8 (default 5; arrows move to the next stop). Toggle "Queue limit": No limit or Limit 5,000.
- **Model** (`model.ts`, `runBacklog`): normal arrivals 150 a second; burst from second 30 to 90; each worker finishes 40 a second, capacity C = 40 x W. Each second backlog = max(0, backlog + arrivals - C); above a limit L the excess is refused and counted. Peak = (B - C) x 60 when B is above C. Oldest job waits = peak / C. Empty after the burst = peak / (C - 150).
- **Numbers to check** (tested): 5 workers: burst 250, 300, 350, 400 give peak 3,000, 6,000, 9,000, 12,000, oldest 15, 30, 45, 60 s, empty 60, 120, 180, 240 s. 6 workers: burst 300 gives 3,600, 15 s, 40 s; burst 400 gives 9,600, 40 s, 107 s. 4 workers: burst 250 gives 5,400, 34 s, 540 s; burst 300 gives 8,400, 53 s, 840 s. 3 workers: never empties. 8 workers, burst 300: no line. 5 workers with limit 5,000: burst 300 refuses 1,000 and empties 100 s after; burst 400 refuses 7,000.
- **Picture:** App, Queue and Workers cards with job dots between them (rate follows the arrivals and the busy share); one small square per worker, lit while it has work; a chart of the backlog over 0 to 420 seconds with the burst as a shaded band and a dashed line at the limit; a playhead sweeps the chart and the cards show the state at the playhead; three number cards: peak line, oldest job waits, empty after burst.
- **Verdicts:** "No line forms" (good), "Spike absorbed, empty in N s" (good, up to 120 s), "Line outlives the burst by N min" (warn), "The line never empties" (bad), "Queue full: N jobs refused" (warn; the refused count is filled in).
- **Reduced motion:** no dots, no sweep; the playhead rests at second 90 (the peak) and the whole curve is drawn.
- **Accessibility:** the burst rail is a `role="slider"` with value text "burst 300 jobs per second, 5 workers, peak 6,000, empty 120 seconds after the burst"; the workers rail is a slider too; the toggle is native buttons.
- **Sources:** Little's law and queueing [S5, S6]; the numbers are course numbers.

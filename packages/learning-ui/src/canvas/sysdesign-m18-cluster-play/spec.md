# Canvas spec: sysdesign-m18-cluster-play

- **Course and chapter:** system-design, 18.4 "Kubernetes essentials", beat 5 ("Play: kill, fail, scale"). The signature playable of Module 18.
- **Renderer:** custom beat canvas on `BeatFrame`, `SvgButton` (the pod and its probe pill), `TightRailSlider` with `SliderRail` (the load), `BeatChoice` (autoscaler off or on, outside the picture), `BeatButton` (Reset), `VerdictBanner`, `useBeatClock`. `block-map` and `traffic-playground` do not fit a cluster that changes size, so the pods are drawn here.
- **Claim:** Pods are replaceable. Readiness decides who gets traffic. CPU decides how many pods run, and a saturated CPU reading under-counts the need.
- **Model:** `model.ts`, a pure state machine (`advance(sim, dt)`), unit tested. A Deployment of 3 pods behind a Service. Every serving pod handles 100 requests a second at 100% CPU. Load 0 to 600 (default 180, steps of 20). CPU per serving pod = min(100, 100 x load / (100 x serving pods)); the rest is dropped. A killed pod flashes red for 0.5 s, then a replacement starts in its place and warms up for 3 play-seconds before it is ready. A failed probe keeps the pod running but takes it out of the Service. Autoscaler (when on): every 3 play-seconds desired = ceil(serving pods x CPU / 60), no change when the reading is within 10% of the target, kept within 2 to 8; scale-down only after 8 play-seconds of lower readings (to the most it wanted in that time); new pods take 3 play-seconds to become ready.
- **Check points (tested):** load 180, 3 ready: 60%. One killed or one probe failed: 90%. Load 300, autoscaler on, 3 ready: 100% and desired 5, then 60% once five are ready. Load 600: 100% with 300 dropped, desired 5, then ceil(5 x 100 / 60) = 9 capped at 8.
- **Inputs:** tap a pod to kill it; tap the pill under a pod to fail or fix its readiness probe; drag the load rail (arrow keys step 20, Page keys 200, Home and End); outside the picture: Autoscaler off or on, and Reset. Under reduced motion each action jumps to the settled picture (the replacement is ready, the autoscaler has finished).
- **Motion:** request dots flow from the Service bar to serving pods at a rate that follows each pod's share of the load; failed or starting pods get none. Each pod has a CPU bar (green under 75%, amber, red at 100%) and a pill (ready, starting, not ready). A killed pod turns red and a new card fades in as "starting" with a warm-up bar. Dropped requests fall as red dots at the right edge. A tick on the load rail marks "full at N", the capacity of the serving pods, and moves as pods are added.
- **Verdicts (all 38 characters or fewer; most urgent first):** "Over capacity: requests dropped"; "Scaling: new pods are not ready yet"; "Alive but not ready: no traffic"; "One pod short: the rest take its load"; otherwise "On target: 60% busy" (50 to 70%), "N% busy: near the knee" (over 70%), "N% busy: room to spare" (under 50%), "No traffic: the pods are idle".
- **What to notice:** one pod down at load 180 leaves the rest at 90%, near the knee; an unready pod costs the same capacity as a dead one; at load 600 the first scale-up is not enough because saturated pods report only 100%.
- **Limits (in the fold):** equal pods, equal sharing, sped-up clocks; no discovery delay, rolling updates, memory limits or control plane cost.
- **Sources:** Kubernetes probes [S20], Deployment behaviour [S35], HPA formula, 15 s loop, 10% tolerance and 300 s scale-down window [S34]; chapter 4.6 for the saturated-reading effect. Course measurement (challenge 17): a deleted pod was replaced in under 1 s and 3 pods were ready again after about 6 s.

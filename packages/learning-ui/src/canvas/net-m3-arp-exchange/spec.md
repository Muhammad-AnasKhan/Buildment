# Canvas spec: net-m3-arp-exchange

- **Course and chapter:** networking, 3.3 and 3.4 (ARP). Renderer spec: `agent-ip/work/networking/canvas-specs/netstep-renderer.md`.
- **Renderer:** the generic `[netstep]` beat canvas, `NetStepCanvas.tsx` in `net-step/`, on `BeatFrame`, `DotGrid` and `VerdictBanner`. Model and schema: `models/net-step.ts`. The data is plain and serialisable, so a Markdown `canvas` fence can feed it later.
- **Claim:** A device that knows an IP address but not the MAC asks the whole link with a broadcast; only the owner of the address answers, straight back, and the asker saves the answer in its ARP table.
- **Input:** **Next** plays the next step; **Start over** goes back to the first. At step 2 the learner first taps one of three options for where the ARP request goes; the step plays after the pick, and the feedback says why. No other control.
- **Data:** a laptop (192.168.1.10) and a phone (192.168.1.20) on a switch. Six steps: the empty ARP table; the broadcast to `ff:ff:ff:ff:ff:ff` (pick); the switch floods it; the phone's unicast reply to the laptop's MAC; the switch passes it on; the laptop's table gains one row (verdict: "One broadcast, one answer, one entry"). Field order and words follow the real request recorded in the lab (`recordings/cable-capture.txt`, `Request who-has 192.168.1.20 tell 192.168.1.10`). Devices, addresses and MACs (`02:...`) are made up. The table is a model view, not a recording.
- **Accessibility:** the caption, the packet fields and the changed rows are in a polite live region and in the picture's description; pick options are native buttons with `aria-pressed` and say "your pick" and "right answer" in words; changed rows carry the word "new" or "changed", not only a colour; the packet rides its link in about 550 ms and shows at once under reduced motion.
- **Limits (in the fold):** two made-up devices; the table is a simplified view of the ARP cache (`ip neigh` shows the real one); the switch's own MAC learning and the timeout of an ARP entry are not drawn.

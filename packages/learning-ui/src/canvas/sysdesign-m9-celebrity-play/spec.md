# Canvas spec: sysdesign-m9-celebrity-play

- **Course and chapter:** system-design, 9.4 ("Hot partitions and skew"), beat 2, "More shards do not help; splitting does".
- **Renderer:** custom beat canvas on `BeatFrame`. Reuses `RailSlider` and `SliderRail` (the shard rail), `SvgNode` (the hottest shard's card), `DotGrid`, `VerdictBanner`. New small primitives: `primitives/svg-radio-chip.tsx` (a 44px chip that is a radio button) and `primitives/beat-choice.tsx` (`BeatActionButton`, for Reset).
- **Claim:** Adding shards cools every shard except the one holding a hot key. Splitting the key into sub-keys (salting) brings the hottest shard under its limit, but the sub-keys land by hash, so the result depends on luck.
- **Inputs:** (1) The shard count S, 4 to 16, on a rail inside the picture; snaps to 4, 8, 12 and 16; arrow keys step 1; default 4. (2) Five salt chips, 1, 2, 4, 8 and 16 (1 means no salt), default 1. (3) "Reset" under the picture.
- **Model** (`model.ts`, hash from `models/hash32.ts`): background load 1,200 writes a second, 1,200 / S on each shard. Noor's key `seller:noor-crafts` takes 1,800 a second. With salt k, sub-keys `seller:noor-crafts#0` to `#(k-1)` take 1,800 / k each (k = 1 is the plain key). A key goes to shard `hash32(key) mod S`. The limit is 1,000 a second on a shard. The read cost is k reads for Noor's total.
- **Numbers to check** (busiest shard, writes a second; salt 1, 2, 4, 8, 16): 4 shards: 2,100, 1,200, 1,200, 975, 1,088. 6: 2,000, 2,000, 1,100, 650, 763. 8: 1,950, 1,050, 600, 600, 600. 12: 1,900, 1,000, 550, 550, 438. 16: 1,875, 975, 525, 300, 413. At 4 shards and salt 4 the sub-keys land on shards 2, 4, 4 and 1 (loads 750, 750, 300, 1,200). The cells that are not monotone are the lesson and are not smoothed.
- **Picture:** one bar per shard on one fixed scale (the same from 4 to 16 shards, so the bars really do fall): grey for the background load, a stack of blue blocks for the sub-keys that landed there, with a dashed line at the 1,000 limit and a dotted line at the average (3,000 / S) that shows how misleading the average is. The hottest shard's bar has a ring (red above 1,000, amber from 800 to 1,000, green below) and a value pill; its card (name, pill, ring) sits top left with a key to the lines and the read cost "Reading Noor's total: k reads". Pressing a salt chip drops one blue dot per sub-key onto its shard, so collisions show where two land together; the over-the-limit ring pulses.
- **Deviation from the spec:** one row of up to 16 bars instead of rows of up to 8. Two rows of 8 made every bar too short to read at 16 shards; one row keeps the scale and the bar positions the same as the learner drags.
- **Verdicts:** over the limit: "Hottest shard: 2,100 of 1,000 limit" (bad). From 800 to 1,000: "Hottest shard: 975 of 1,000 limit" (warn). Under 800: "Hottest shard: 600 of 1,000 limit" (good).
- **Reduced motion:** the blocks appear in place; no pulse.
- **Accessibility:** the rail is a `role="slider"` ("8 shards, hottest shard 1,950 writes a second"); the chips are `role="radio"` in a `radiogroup`; Reset is a real button. The SVG description says the hottest shard, its load, the average and the read cost; the verdict repeats the hottest number in words.
- **Sources:** per-partition limit [S40]; hot partitions as a common early problem [S44]. The figures are the course's own simulation with Suq's made-up load.

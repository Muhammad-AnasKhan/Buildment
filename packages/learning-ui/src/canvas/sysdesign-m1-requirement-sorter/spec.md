# Canvas spec: sysdesign-m1-requirement-sorter

- **Course and chapter:** system-design, 1.2 "Requirements first", beat 4 ("Sort Hana's list"), right after the three beats that name the kinds.
- **Renderer:** custom, on the reusable `Sorter` primitive (`primitives/sorter.tsx`, model `models/sorter.ts`). This folder holds only data. Reason for a primitive rather than a scene: the learner must make a choice and get a consequence, which a scene cannot do; any course can sort its own cards into its own buckets with the same component.
- **Claim:** Every line on a requirements list is one of three kinds: functional (an action users take), non-functional (how well, with a number) or a constraint (a fact the team must accept). Telling them apart is a skill you practise, not a definition you read.
- **Model:** one card on top at a time, dealt in data order (seed 0) and in a seeded shuffle after "Sort again". A right bucket moves the card into that bucket and deals the next one. A wrong bucket keeps the card, shows the reason written for that card and that bucket, and marks the card as missed. The round ends when the deck is empty; the verdict counts the cards that took a second try.
- **Cards (8, all from the chapter):** upload a PDF (functional); search p99 under 1 second (non-functional); 2 engineers (constraint); find every note for CS101 (functional); a saved note is never lost (non-functional, tricky: sounds like a feature); ready in 6 weeks (constraint); a new note in search within 1 minute (non-functional, tricky); sign in with university accounts (constraint, tricky: sign-in sounds functional, the chapter lists it under existing systems). A budget card is left out on purpose: the chapter lists cost both as a non-functional target and as a money constraint.
- **Inputs:** tap a bucket (a real button over each bucket, so Tab and Enter work), or drag the card onto a bucket (pointer capture, `touch-none` on the card only, so the page still scrolls).
- **What moves:** a right card flies into its bucket and the bucket's count ticks up when it lands; a wrong card shakes, its border turns amber and the reason appears under it in red; progress dots on the card fill as cards are sorted. Under reduced motion there is no flight or shake; the counts, the banner and the reason change at once.
- **Numbers on screen:** the three bucket counts (and one count in the end verdict).
- **What the learner should notice:** a number about speed, uptime or freshness makes a line non-functional even when it mentions a feature; a line that limits the team (people, time, existing systems) is a constraint even when it sounds like a feature.
- **Accessibility:** bucket buttons are labelled with name, gloss and count; a polite live region reads each result and reason; the SVG has a label with the current card; `FOCUS_RING` on every button; text at least 11.5 units on a 420 viewBox.
- **Limits (fold):** real lists mix kinds in one sentence, and cost can be read either way. Daftar is fictional.
- **Sources:** the chapter's own text and its sources (Google SRE book on SLOs [S95]; Fowler, "MonolithFirst" [S36]).

# Canvas spec: sysdesign-m2-latency-ladder

- **Course and chapter:** system-design, 2.2 "Latency numbers you should feel". Suggested home: the beat "The ladder on one page", in place of its Mermaid ladder (new slug; replaces no canvas).
- **Renderer:** custom beat canvas on `BeatFrame`, `SvgNode`, `VerdictBanner`, `useBeatClock`, and the new reusable primitive `primitives/log-ruler.tsx` (`LogRuler`). Custom because no kit renderer has a draggable log ruler; the ruler is data-driven (rungs and bands are props) so another course can lay out its own ladder (sizes, prices, costs).
- **Claim:** The rungs of the latency ladder differ by factors of thousands to millions; in human time (1 ns = 1 s) they go from seconds inside the CPU to days across a network and years across a continent.
- **Input:** two rungs. Tap a rung on the ruler: it becomes the current rung and the old one becomes the rung to compare with. Drag along the ruler to move the current rung. Keyboard: the ruler is one `role="slider"`; arrows move, Home and End jump, Enter pins the current rung as the one to compare with.
- **Model:** `models/latency-ladder.ts`, unit tested. Human seconds = nanoseconds. `compareRungs(a, b)`: ratio = slow / fast; verdict `"<slow> = <ratio> <fast plural>"`, at most 38 characters for every pair (tested). `raceFrame(seconds)`: the slow round trip takes 3.2 s on screen and holds 1.6 s; the count is progress x ratio; the human clock is progress x human time.
- **Picture:** "Your code" (pill "1 ns = 1 s") sends a packet to the slow rung's place (Singapore, Redis, SSD ...) and back while a green packet bounces to the fast rung's place at the true ratio (above a ratio of 12 it becomes a stream). Chips: the count ("x 1.1 million reads") on the fast track and the human clock ("3.4 years") on the slow track. The ruler below names the human-time bands: seconds, minutes, hours, days, months, years.
- **Numbers (from the chapter):** L1 hit 1 ns, memory 100 ns (Colin Scott's model [S2]); context switch 10 us, SSD random 8 KiB read 100 us, round trip in a region 250 us, Redis or MySQL query 500 us, disk seek 10 ms, EU West to NA East 80 ms, EU West to Singapore 160 ms (napkin-math 2026 [S3]). Source ids refer to `agent-ip/work/system-design/01-research-dossier.md`.
- **Default:** memory read vs Singapore: "Asia trip = 1.6 million memory reads", clock ends at 5.1 years.
- **What to notice:** each step to the right is a jump in unit, not a small step; the count for a network trip against a memory read runs into the millions; neighbours in the same band (SSD read, region trip, Redis query) differ by only 2 to 5 times.
- **Motion and reduced motion:** the race replays from the start on every new pair; pressing the picture freezes it. Reduced motion shows the finished race: the full count and the full human time.
- **Accessibility:** the race SVG has a sentence label with both real and human times and the verdict; the ruler is a labelled slider with a full `aria-valuetext`. Coordinates are rounded so server and browser render the same numbers.
- **Limits (in the fold):** rounded estimates that can be 2 to 3 times off; the slow trip always takes about 3 s on screen; a context switch and a disk seek are drawn as trips though they are waits.

# Canvas spec: Yousef's two offers

## Teaching claim

The winner of a weighted decision matrix depends on the weights, so a small change in what matters (power hours) can turn a clear win into a tie, while a much bigger score on one criterion (pay) does not win on its own.

## Why a canvas

The chapter's "Test the result" section asks the learner to imagine one weight change and recompute. Doing that once in prose is fine; trying several changes and watching the totals and the leader move is what makes "the result depends on the weights" feel true. A static table can only show one set of weights.

## Model

- Inputs: one weight per criterion.

  | Name | Unit | Min | Max | Step | Default |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | Pay weight | weight level | 1 | 3 | 1 | 3 |
  | Power hours weight | weight level | 1 | 3 | 1 | 3 |
  | Learning weight | weight level | 1 | 3 | 1 | 2 |
  | Future work weight | weight level | 1 | 3 | 1 | 2 |

- Fixed data: Yousef's scores from the chapter (Offer A 5, 2, 2, 3; Offer B 3, 4, 4, 3), on a 1 to 5 scale.
- Outputs: total points per offer (integer), the leader or a tie, the margin in points.
- Formula: total(option) = sum of weight(criterion) x score(option, criterion).
- Assumptions: scores and weights are exact; must-haves already passed.
- Limitations: no uncertainty in scores, no missing criteria, small gaps are treated like big ones.

## Rendering

- Primary: HTML radio groups for the weights, inline SVG for the total bars (two fixed sizes, one per breakpoint), HTML for the summary and the "What changed" sentence.
- Static fallback: the chapter's worked example table and the "Test the result" paragraph teach the same claim without the canvas.

## Copy

- What this shows: in `data.ts` (`whatThisShows`).
- Try this: power hours to 1 gives a tie at 27; pay 3 with the rest at 1 gives Offer A by 2; all weights 1 gives Offer B 14 to 12.
- What changed: generated by `describeChange` in `renderers/factor/model.ts`.
- Limits of the model: in `data.ts` (`limits`).

## Placement

In "Test the result", after the worked example and a one-sentence prediction prompt, before "Where a matrix can mislead you". The chapter's two quizzes after it are the transfer check: the first applies the formula to a new decision (internet plans), the second is about bending weights.

# Canvas spec: sysdesign-m19-route-users

- **Course and chapter:** system-design, 19.5 "Global traffic routing", beat 2 ("Route users, then fail a region").
- **Renderer:** custom beat canvas on `BeatFrame`, `SvgRadioChip` (the four policies, inside the picture), `SvgButton` (the regions), `LinkLine`, `BeatChoice` (the TTL), the new `primitives/svg-step-bar.tsx` (`StepBar`, here in seconds), `VerdictBanner`, `useBeatClock`. Custom drawing rather than `traffic-playground`, which is a one-server load picture and has no groups or regions.
- **Claim:** For DNS-based policies the time users are dark is the health check time plus the DNS TTL; anycast removes the TTL. The survivor receives the failed region's users.
- **Model:** `model.ts`, unit tested. Groups Europe, NA East, Singapore; regions EU West, NA East. Homes while healthy: latency, location (by country, a course assignment) and anycast: Europe and Singapore to EU West, NA East to NA East; failover (EU West first): all to EU West. Dark time = 90 s (health check 30 s x 3, made up) + TTL (60 s or 300 s); anycast 90 s (a course assumption: the same detection time). Groups not on the failed region are unaffected. Round trips for the status pills: 80 ms between Europe and NA East, 160 ms from Singapore to EU West [S3]; Singapore to NA East is not in the table and shows "farther". Tested: latency with EU West down and TTL 60: Europe and Singapore dark 150 s then on NA East; TTL 300: 390 s; anycast 90 s; failover with NA East down: nobody moves.
- **Inputs:** four policy chips; tap a region to fail it and tap again to bring it back (both can fail); outside the picture: "TTL 60 s / TTL 300 s" and Reset. Any change after a failure re-runs the timeline.
- **Motion:** request dots flow from each group to its region; on the tap the failed region turns red, a timeline counts the seconds of the story (60 story seconds for each second on screen), the affected groups go grey ("dark", red line) and then their line jumps to the other region; the survivor's pill reads "serves 2 of 3" in amber. Reduced motion shows the settled state.
- **Verdicts:** "Pick a policy, then fail a region", "Europe reaches NA East after 150 s" (and 390 s), "Moved after 90 s; no cache to wait", "Nobody was on it; no one moves", "No region left to route to", plus "Everyone reaches NA East after 150 s" when all three groups move and "NA East reaches EU West after 150 s" for the NA East group (all within 38 characters, tested for every policy, TTL and failure set).
- **What to notice:** the dark time is the same for the three DNS policies and is mostly the cache when the TTL is 300 s; anycast moves at the detection time; failover leaves the secondary alone when it fails.
- **Deviation from the spec text:** the survivor's pill says "serves 2 of 3" (the longer "serves 2 of 3 groups" does not fit the card at the minimum text size); the policy chips for latency, location and anycast give the same picture while both regions are healthy because the spec gives them the same homes.
- **Limits (in the fold):** two regions, three user groups, a course health check of 30 s x 3 and a TTL you can switch; DNS routing also depends on where the resolver is, not the phone (chapter 3.3). Failing back is not drawn.
- **Sources:** [S111] Route 53 policies; [S112] Global Accelerator anycast; [S107] Global Accelerator avoids DNS caching issues; [S3] round trips; Route 53 health check intervals (not yet in the dossier); chapter 3.3 for the TTL floor.

# Canvas spec: sysdesign-m19-write-latency

- **Course and chapter:** system-design, 19.6 "Data residency and global consistency", beat 3 ("Write latency across regions for each choice").
- **Renderer:** custom beat canvas on `BeatFrame`, `SnapStopsRail` (the customer), `SvgRadioChip` (the three designs, inside the picture), `SvgButton` (the regions), `InfoChip` (new, `primitives/svg-info-chip.tsx`), `VerdictBanner`, `useBeatClock`.
- **Claim:** The write delay depends on how far the data's leader and its confirming copy are from the user. A single far leader makes far users pay; a global majority makes every user pay the trip to the nearest other region; a home region makes the write local and also meets a residency rule.
- **Model:** `model.ts`, unit tested. Regions NA West, NA East, EU West. Round trips [S3]: NA East to NA West 60 ms, EU West to NA East 80 ms; the EU West to NA West figure is not in the table and no design needs it. Write time = trip from the customer to the leader + 0.55 ms (0.3 ms disk plus 0.25 ms to a follower in its own region, 8.3) + (global database only) the round trip from the leader to the nearest other region. One leader in NA East: 0.55 / 60.55 / 80.55 (NA East, NA West, EU West); home region: 0.55 everywhere; global with the leader in the customer's region: 60.55 / 60.55 / 80.55. Shown as 0.6 / 61 / 81 ms, matching the chapter's table. EU rows stay in the EU only in the home region design. In the global design the leader is in the customer's region, so only the confirmation trip travels.
- **Inputs:** the customer knob on a three stop rail (NA West, NA East, EU West, in map order; default EU West; arrow keys, Home, End), or tap a region card (a real button); three design chips ("One leader", "Home region", "Global DB"); outside the picture: Reset. The spec lists the rail stops as NA East, NA West, EU West; the map order is used so the rail and the picture agree.
- **Motion:** a packet crosses the link between regions (write, copy, confirmation, reply); a crown marks the leader and its ring fills while the leader works and waits; the customer marker slides between regions. A result chip shows "Write: 81 ms" and a second chip "EU rows stay in the EU: no" (red) or "yes" (green). Reduced motion shows the finished write with the ring full and no packet.
- **Verdicts (tested up to 38 characters for all nine cases):** one leader: "0.6 ms: leader is next door", "61 ms: trip to the NA East leader", "81 ms: trip to the NA East leader"; home region: "0.6 ms: data lives where you are"; global: "61 ms: nearest other copy is NA West" (customer in NA East), "... is NA East" (NA West and EU West; 61 and 81 ms).
- **What to notice:** one leader and the global database cost the same for Europe (81 ms), while the global database slows NA East from 0.6 to 61 ms; the home region design is the only one that is fast everywhere and keeps EU rows in the EU.
- **Limits (in the fold):** steady conditions, one write at a time, round trips from the course latency table; queueing, busy leaders and loss add to every figure; copies kept inside the EU are not drawn because the delay between EU regions is not in the table.
- **Sources:** [S3] round trips and disk write; [S57] PACELC; chapter 8.3 for the wait for a copy; 12.3 for majorities; GDPR Chapter V, Articles 44 to 46 and CockroachDB documentation on super regions (not yet in the dossier).

# Canvas spec: product-m1-problem-or-feature

- **Course and chapter:** product-sense, 1.2 "Start from the problem, not the feature", beat 1, the playable sorter.
- **Renderer:** custom, on the reusable `Sorter` primitive (`primitives/sorter.tsx`, model `models/sorter.ts`). This folder holds only data. Reason: telling a problem from a feature is a skill the learner practises with a consequence for each wrong tap; a diagram cannot give that.
- **Claim:** A request usually names a feature, which is one fix someone chose. Underneath is a problem: something that already happened to a person at a time. A line about what people "would" do is a guess until someone checks it.
- **Model:** nine cards, three per bucket, dealt in data order (seed 0) and shuffled after "Sort again". Buckets: Problem (it happened to someone), Feature (a thing to build), Guess (what people would do). A right bucket moves the card in; a wrong bucket keeps the card and shows the reason written for that card and bucket. Every reason states the rule that separates the buckets, not only the cue.
- **Cards (9):** Problem: "3 of 6 classmates Maryam asked found no group before an exam." (a checked observation with a number; it answers a different question from the first check of 2 of 5 in 1.5), "Adel could not tell how to join an online group.", "Rawan tried to add her course and found no group." (contains "add"). Feature: "Add voice rooms.", "Add dark mode.", "Halaqa has no streaks yet." (sounds like a fault, names a missing thing). Guess: "Students would pay for AI summaries of notes.", "Everyone wants to see a score for each group.", "Adel said he would use polls every day." (a named person and a past verb, but it is about next time). Adel and Rawan are the two friends who use Halaqa with Maryam.
- **Inputs:** tap a bucket (a real button over each bucket), or drag the card onto a bucket.
- **What moves:** a right card flies into its bucket and the count ticks up; a wrong card shakes and shows its reason. Under reduced motion there is no flight or shake.
- **What the learner should notice:** the first word does not decide the bucket. "Add" can start a problem (Rawan), a line with no verb can be a feature (no streaks), and a named person can give a guess (Adel said he would). The test is the same each time: did it already happen to a person, does it name a thing to build, or does it say what people would do.
- **Accessibility:** bucket buttons are labelled with name, gloss and count; a polite live region reads each result and reason; text at least 11.5 units.
- **Limits (fold):** real requests mix the three. Halaqa, Adel and Rawan are fictional.
- **Sources:** Shape Up, chapter 3, on narrowing down the problem before choosing a solution [A27]; the habit of asking about the last time it happened is the course's own.

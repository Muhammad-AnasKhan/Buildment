# Canvas spec: How many areas fit in your plan?

## Teaching claim
AI makes building an area faster, but it does not make learning it much faster, so a strong project needs enough total weeks and a large share of them spent learning. Squeezing the plan into one month leaves most areas unlearned; spending almost nothing on learning does the same; spending everything on learning leaves too little time to build.

## Why a canvas
Fellows hear "AI builds it in a weekend" and plan a month. The arithmetic of hours a week times weeks, split between learning and building, is easy to state and easy to ignore. Moving the months slider from 6 to 1 and watching the ready areas fall from 12 to 2 makes the six-month rule feel earned, and the learning-share slider shows that the answer is a large share, not all of it.

## Model
- Inputs:

  | Name | Unit | Min | Max | Step | Default |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | Hours a week | hours | 2 | 20 | 1 | 8 |
  | Months | months | 1 | 12 | 1 | 6 |
  | Share spent learning | % | 20 | 80 | 10 | 60 |

- Fixed data (Yara's planning numbers, labelled as hers in the chapter): 12 areas; 8 learning hours and 4 building hours per area; 4 weeks per month.
- Formulas: total = hours a week x months x 4; learning = total x share / 100; building = total minus learning; areas learned = floor(learning / 8); areas built = floor(building / 4); areas ready = the smallest of areas learned, areas built and 12. The limit is learning, building, both (equal and under 12) or none (12 ready).
- Outputs: stat cards for total hours, areas ready out of 12 and what limits you; four bars on one hours scale: learning hours you have, learning needed (96), building hours you have, building needed (48). The two "you have" bars are good when they reach the need and warn when they fall short.
- Checked values:
  - Default (8 hours, 6 months, 60%): 192 hours, 115 learning, 77 building, 14 learned, 19 built, 12 of 12 ready, limit none.
  - 1 month: 32 hours, 19 learning, 13 building, 2 learned, 3 built, 2 ready, limit learning.
  - 6 months at 20%: 38 learning, 154 building, 4 ready, limit learning.
  - 6 months at 80%: 154 learning, 38 building, 9 built, 9 ready, limit building.
  - 3 months at 60%: 96 hours, 58 learning, 38 building, 7 ready, limit learning.
  - Minimums (2 hours, 1 month, 20%): 8 hours, 0 ready, limit learning. Maximums (20 hours, 12 months, 80%): 960 hours, 12 ready.
- Non-finite input falls back to the default; negative input counts as 0.
- Limitations: the hours per area are a plan's estimate, every area is the same size, real weeks are uneven, and review, rest and writing time are left out.

## Rendering
- Primary: the shared slider renderer (HTML range inputs, stat cards, bars).
- Static fallback: chapter 5.2 works the same arithmetic in prose for one month and six months, so a learner who cannot use the canvas still meets the claim.

## Controls
Three range inputs with visible values and units, keyboard operable (arrow keys move one step), each at least 44px tall on a phone.

## Visualisation
Three stat cards above four horizontal bars on one shared hours scale. Colour appears only on the two "you have" bars, as good or warn, always next to the value text.

## Copy
- What this shows: the `intro` in `data.ts` sets the scene and asks for the prediction.
- Try this: the first line matches the chapter's predict (one month); the other two lines are the 20% and 80% checks above.
- What changed: generated by `describeProjectHoursChange` in `data.ts`, naming the input that moved, the new total or the hours moved, and how many areas are now ready.
- Limits of the model: in `data.ts`.

## Edge cases
At 2 hours, 1 month and 20%, the total is 8 hours and nothing is ready; the cards still read cleanly. At the maximums, both bars pass their need and the limit reads "Nothing".

## Accessibility
Every result is also in the screen-reader summary sentence. Bars carry their value as text. Colour is never the only signal: the "What limits you" card names the limit in words.

## Acceptance criteria
- Every checked value above is covered by a unit test.
- The first `tryThis` line matches the chapter's predict and the chapter's numbers.
- No NaN or Infinity in any output.
- Readable at 375px in light and dark mode, with no horizontal scroll.

## Placement
In chapter 5.2, after the predict about squeezing the project into one month and before the worked plan. A quiz item later in the chapter transfers the claim to a different fellow's hours.

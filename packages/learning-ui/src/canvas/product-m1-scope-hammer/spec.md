# Canvas spec: product-m1-scope-hammer

- **Course and chapter:** product-sense, 1.4 "Fit the scope to the time, say no to the rest", beat 3, the playable sorter.
- **Renderer:** custom, on the reusable `Sorter` primitive (`primitives/sorter.tsx`, model `models/sorter.ts`). This folder holds only data. Reason: scope hammering is a set of decisions with reasons; the learner makes each decision and sees the rule when it is wrong.
- **Claim:** Fix the time first, then shape the scope to fit it. Ask of each feature: does the job fail without it, who does it affect, and what do people do today? One rule separates the three buckets. Must have: the job fails without it. Nice to have (marked with a tilde): the job works without it, but it makes this same job easier and it is small. Not now: it does not make this job easier, whatever its size, because it serves a different job or students already do it in another app.
- **Model:** nine of Maryam's fourteen features, sorted for a 12-hour appetite and the job "find two or three classmates to revise with tonight or tomorrow". Must have: group search, create group. Nice to have: notifications, calendar sync. Not now: leaderboard, profile themes, voice rooms, streaks, chat. The five features not on the cards (file sharing, polls, badges, dark mode, an AI summary of notes) all go to Not now; the chapter's fold says so. Cards are dealt in data order (seed 0) and shuffled after "Sort again". Every reason names the rule of the bucket.
- **Cards where the cue and the answer disagree:** "Create group" sounds like an extra but the job fails without it (Must have). "Profile themes" is small and cheap but eases no part of the job (Not now), so small does not mean nice. "Chat" sounds central to a group app but students already message elsewhere and the job fails at finding a group (Not now). Cards that a reasonable expert could file differently (polls, file sharing, an AI summary) stay in the fold's table and are not on the cards.
- **Inputs:** tap a bucket (a real button over each bucket), or drag the card onto a bucket.
- **What moves:** a right card flies into its bucket and the count ticks up; a wrong card shakes and shows its reason. Under reduced motion there is no flight or shake.
- **What the learner should notice:** the same question decides every card, "does the job fail without it, and if not, does it ease this job?". Voice rooms and chat are not cut because they are bad; they are cut because the job fails at finding a group, and students already have a way to talk.
- **Accessibility:** bucket buttons are labelled with name, gloss and count; a polite live region reads each result and reason; text at least 11.5 units.
- **Limits (fold):** another job or time budget changes the answers. Halaqa is fictional.
- **Sources:** Shape Up, chapter 14, on scope hammering, nice-to-haves marked with a tilde and comparing to what the person has now [A76]; chapter 3 on appetite [A27]; the 12-hour appetite and the one rule for the three buckets are the course case's own.

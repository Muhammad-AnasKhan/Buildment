# Canvas spec: sysdesign-m9-ring-play

- **Course and chapter:** system-design, 9.3 ("Consistent hashing and virtual nodes"), beat 4, "Virtual nodes: many small points per node". The signature picture of module 9.
- **Renderer:** custom beat canvas on `BeatFrame`. No existing primitive draws a ring, so this one is new; it is written for this slug but its model (`model.ts`: ring, arcs, owner lookup, move counts) is pure and reusable. Reuses `RailSlider` and `SliderRail` for the dial, `DotGrid` and `VerdictBanner`. Uses `primitives/beat-choice.tsx` (`BeatActionButton`, a disabled-able 44px pill).
- **Claim:** Giving each node many points evens out the load; adding a node moves only about 1 key in N+1 and only to the new node; removing a node shares its keys over the survivors.
- **Inputs:** (1) The points per node dial on a snap rail: 1, 2, 5, 10, 20, 50, 100, 200 (default 1, the lumpy ring), by dragging the rail or with the arrow keys. (2) A key you drag round the ring: press anywhere on the ring and the key follows the pointer; arrow keys move it 1 degree, Page keys 15. (3) Two buttons under the picture: "Add node" (disabled at 8 nodes) and "Remove node" (removes the most recently added node; disabled at 2 nodes). Start with 4 nodes, node-a to node-d; added nodes are node-e, node-f and so on.
- **Model** (`model.ts`, hash from `models/hash32.ts`): 10,000 keys `order:0` to `order:9999`, hashes cached once. Node `name` with `v` points has points at `hash32(name + "#" + i)`. A key belongs to the first point at or after its hash, wrapping to the first point; ties go to the smaller node name. Outputs: keys per node, busiest over fair share, and after an add or remove the share of keys that changed owner, and how many of those moved between nodes that were on the ring before and after (always 0).
- **Numbers to check** (10,000 keys, node-a to node-d, adding node-e), as busiest over fair at 4 nodes, moved when adding, busiest over fair at 5 nodes, moved when removing node-b from the 4: 1 point: 1.68, 1.3%, 2.10, 25.9%. 5: 1.56, 16.0%, 1.45, 16.8%. 10: 1.26, 20.9%, 1.17, 31.4%. 50: 1.26, 17.7%, 1.32, 19.3%. 100: 1.15, 19.7%, 1.17, 23.7%. 200: 1.08, 18.9%, 1.07, 22.9%. Hash mod 4 to mod 5 moves 79.9% of the 10,000 keys. (The tests also remove node-b from the four; the buttons remove the newest node.)
- **Picture:** a big ring (about 60% of the picture width): each node's arcs in its own colour, cut at the points (dots on the ring while there are at most 40; at 200 points the colours blend into thin bands, which is the point). Round the outside, 179 of the 10,000 keys as dots in the colour of their owner; small dots rain onto the ring so that big arcs catch more. Inside the ring, one row per node: colour swatch, name and a bar against a dashed "fair share" line. The key you drag is a handle on the ring with an accent arc to the point that owns it, and the owner's row is outlined. Under the ring: a "moved" pill, a meter with a dashed "ideal" tick (1 in N+1 on an add, 1 in N on a remove) and a grey "hash mod N" tick, and the count of keys that changed owner. After an add or remove the new node's arcs glow (or the removed node's arcs fade), the moved sample keys fly from their old node's bar to their new node's bar and flash on landing, and the bars change over about 1.3 seconds.
- **Numbers visible at once:** the verdict, the moved pill, the ideal tick and the mod N tick, the dial's value.
- **Verdicts:** after the dial or at the start: "Busiest node: 1.68 times its share" (bad above 1.5, warn above 1.15, otherwise good). After an add: "Added node-e: 19.7% of keys moved" (good when at most 1.3 times ideal, else warn). After a remove: "Removed node-e: 18.9% of keys moved".
- **Reduced motion:** no rain, no flash, no flight; the final bars and recoloured keys show at once, and the changed keys are listed as a count under the meter.
- **Accessibility:** the dial is a `role="slider"` ("100 points per node, busiest node 1.15 times its share"); the key is a `role="slider"` ("key at 108 degrees, it belongs to node-b"); the buttons are real buttons; the colours are paired with names on the rows. The SVG description says the node count, the points, the busiest ratio, the move and the key.
- **Sources:** Karger et al. 1997 [S18]; Dynamo virtual nodes [S45]; Envoy ring hash [S16]. The figures are the course's own simulation.

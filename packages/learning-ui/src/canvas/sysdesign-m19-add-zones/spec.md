# Canvas spec: sysdesign-m19-add-zones

- **Course and chapter:** system-design, 19.2 "Regions, availability zones and failure domains", beat 2 ("Add zones and watch the gain stop").
- **Renderer:** custom beat canvas on `BeatFrame`, `SnapStopsRail`, `SvgButton`, `LinkLine`, `VerdictBanner`, `useBeatClock`. The zone cards are a compact version of the `SvgNode` look (92 wide instead of 112) so four fit in a row on a phone.
- **Claim:** More zones raise uptime only until the shared parts (load balancer rules, deploy tool) dominate the downtime, while the capacity needed to survive a zone loss is n / (n - 1) times the peak.
- **Model:** `model.ts`, unit tested. Zone availability 99.9% and shared layer 99.95% (made up), independent zone faults, 525,600 minutes a year. Availability = shared x (1 - (1 - zone)^n). Capacity = n / (n - 1) x peak (1.00 for one zone). Load on a live zone after f failures = (n - 1) / (n - f); the app is down when no zone is live or the load passes 100%. Worked values (tested): n = 1: 99.850%, 788 min (13.1 h), 1.00; n = 2: 99.950%, 263 min (4.4 h), 2.00; n = 3: 263 min, 1.50; n = 4: 263 min, 1.33. Bad release: every zone fails at once; the downtime readout shows the shared layer's 263 min.
- **Inputs:** a rail with four snap points (1 to 4, default 3; arrow keys, Home, End); tap a zone card to fail or restore it (real buttons); outside the picture: a "Bad release to all zones" toggle and Reset.
- **Motion:** request dots flow from the users to each live zone at a rate that follows its load; a failed zone gets no arrows and the others get more dots; load bars turn amber at 100% and red above it; zone cards slide to make room when the count changes. Reduced motion draws still dots.
- **Verdicts (38 characters or fewer, tested for every n, failure count and release state):** the spec's seven, plus three added for states the spec did not cover: "Zones down: the rest are overloaded" (three or four zones with two or more failed), "All zones down: the app is down" (every zone failed, two or more zones).
- **What to notice:** the downtime readout stops falling after two zones; the capacity bar grows from 1.00 to 2.00 and then shrinks; one failed zone puts 100% on each of the others; a bad release takes every zone down at any n.
- **Limits (in the fold):** made-up availabilities, independent zone faults; real zones have correlated faults and cross-zone data transfer costs money.
- **Sources:** AWS "Fault isolation boundaries" whitepaper (not yet in the dossier); the availability arithmetic is derived and labelled as a model in the chapter.

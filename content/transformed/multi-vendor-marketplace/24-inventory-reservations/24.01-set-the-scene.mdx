# 24.01 — Set the scene

> **[Open]** · Chapter 24 — Inventory reservations · *Why deploy is not the finish line*

You shipped FreshMarket on HTTPS in Chapter 23. Customers can browse, pay, and vendors get async notifications. That is a real portfolio milestone — and it is **not** the same as a production grocery marketplace.

Perishable inventory is unforgiving. If two customers checkout the last three units of bananas at the same time, someone arrives to an empty shelf or your vendor eats a refund. Real platforms solve this with **reservations**: stock is held while payment completes, then confirmed or released.

## Objectives

By the end of Chapter 24 you will:

- Explain the difference between **available**, **reserved**, and **sold** stock on a product.
- Implement a `StockReservation` document with TTL-style expiry and idempotent confirm/release.
- Hook reservations into **checkout start**, **payment success**, and **payment abandon**.
- Run a worker job that releases expired holds without racing paid orders.
- Prove concurrency with a deliberate two-tab checkout test.

## Production scope (this module)

**In scope:** per-product holds keyed to cart or checkout session; 15-minute default TTL; atomic confirm on `payment_intent.succeeded`; release on expiry or explicit cart abandon; vendor-visible **available** count = `stockQty - sum(active reservations)`.

**Out of scope (name them so interviews go well):** multi-warehouse allocation, negative stock backorders, ERP sync, batch lot tracking (use-by dates). Those belong in a warehouse module — not this course pass.

## What you will touch in the repo

- `server/models/stockReservation.model.js` (new)
- `server/modules/inventory/` — reserve, confirm, release helpers
- Checkout route — reserve before creating unpaid orders / PaymentIntent
- Stripe webhook — confirm reservations when payment succeeds
- `worker.js` — periodic or delayed job `reservation.expired`
- Product read APIs — expose `availableQty` for catalogue and vendor dashboard

This is not a tutorial you copy-paste from start to finish. It's a **guided build**: each chapter teaches an idea in depth, compares the approaches a professional would weigh, and then walks you through implementing it on **your own project** — one folder, one file, one verification step at a time. You write the logic. The course tells you what it must do, what to avoid, and how to know it's working.

Read this section carefully. Everything else in the course assumes you understand how progress works here.

## If you're newer to full-stack — how to read this course

**Don't worry if this feels new.** You do not need to already know MongoDB, JWT, Redis, deployment, or every buzzword on a job posting. This course is built for people who are learning as they go.

We structured it as a **guided path**, not a pile of random tutorials. Every chapter is in a deliberate order. Later chapters only make sense because earlier ones set things up — you are never expected to jump in cold.

### How each chapter is organised

The same rhythm repeats throughout the course. You always know what kind of step you are on:

1. **Understand the problem** — what we are solving and why it matters for the marketplace
2. **See what you will build** — the outcome for this chapter, in plain language
3. **Learn the ideas** — concepts, trade-offs, and "why professionals choose this" before you touch code
4. **Rethink and check** — short questions and prompts so the idea sticks, not just the syntax
5. **Build it** — one small step at a time on your own repo, with a way to verify it works
6. **Gate** — a checklist plus answer questions before the next chapter unlocks

You read and think first. You build at the end. That order is intentional — it is how you actually understand what you are shipping, not just copy files.

### What that means for you

- **Go in order.** The sidebar is your path. Skipping ahead usually means hitting a wall you could have avoided.
- **It is okay not to know things yet.** The **Learn it** sections and **In plain English** boxes exist for exactly that.
- **One chapter, one focus.** You are not building the whole app at once. Each chapter adds one real piece.
- **Verify as you go.** If a step says "check the health route" or "log in as vendor," do that before moving on — that is how you know you are on track.

### When something feels hard

| If you feel… | Try this |
|---|---|
| "I don't understand the words" | Re-read **In plain English** in that lesson — then come back to the main text |
| "The build step failed" | Read **Troubleshooting** and compare your repo to **Where the project is now** |
| "This chapter jumped too fast" | Read **Bridge from last chapter** at the top — it links what you just finished to what is new |
| "I'm not sure I'm doing it right" | Run the verify step for that lesson — if it passes, you are on track; if not, fix that before moving on |

You are not expected to figure everything out alone on the first pass. Read, build a little, get stuck, re-read — that loop is normal. The structure is here so you always know **what to do next** and **why you are doing it**.

## How each lesson is labeled

Every lesson in the sidebar has a **type badge** — Read, Learn, Build, Check, or Gate. Glance at it and you know what you are about to do: read and think, write code, answer a quiz, or finish the chapter.

| Type | What you do | Code in your repo? | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Read** | Set the scene — where you are in the project and what this chapter adds | No | *"Your repo has a server and client folder; this chapter connects them"* |
| **Learn** | Understand the idea before you code — concepts, trade-offs, why we chose this approach | No | *"Sessions vs JWT — which fits a marketplace, and why"* |
| **Build** | Hands-on — create files, wire routes, run commands, and verify it works | **Yes** | *"Add the health route, start the server, see a success response in the browser"* |
| **Check** | Quick quiz in the platform — multiple choice or short answer to see if the idea landed | No | *"What should never be stored in a JWT payload?"* |
| **Gate** | Finish the chapter — tick the checklist and answer the questions here | Check your work | *"Demo vendor login; answer: why did we hash passwords?"* |

**Simple rule:** finish **Read** and **Learn** before **Build** in the same chapter. On **Gate**, complete every checklist item and answer at the bottom of the page — the next chapter stays locked until you do.

Work through the **sidebar** top to bottom within each module. The badge tells you whether to open your editor or just read and think.

## The gate is mandatory — do not skip it

Every chapter ends with a **Gate** lesson. This is **required**, not optional — it is how we check that you are ready for the next chapter.

We evaluate you in **two ways** at every gate:

### 1. Checklist

A list of things you must be able to **show, demo, or explain**. Each box is concrete — not "I read the chapter," but "I can log in as a vendor and see only my orders."

Tick every box honestly. If a box is not true yet, that is your work before moving on.

### 2. Answer questions

Short written answers **in the platform** — a few sentences in your own words. These check that you understand **why** you built something, not just that it runs.

You are not writing essays. Two or three clear sentences per question is enough.

**Both parts are mandatory.** The next chapter stays **locked** until the checklist is complete and every question is answered. Later chapters assume earlier work is actually finished — skipping the gate means building on gaps you will have to fix later.

At the **end of this page** you will see real examples of both: a sample gate checklist and sample answer questions. Use them to see exactly what every chapter gate looks like.

## Along the way — mandatory reads and more

Between build steps, we point you to **mandatory reads**, **recommended articles**, **case studies**, and **hints**. They are not filler — they sharpen how you think, not just what you type.

| Type | What it is | Do you have to do it? |
|---|---|---|
| **Mandatory read** | An external article or doc you read **before** the next build step | **Yes** — tick "I have read" on the card |
| **Recommended article / case study** | Extra depth when you want to go further | Optional, but worth it |
| **Hint** | A nudge when you are stuck | Use when needed — never full solution code |

Mandatory reads often appear as a **card with a link** — open the article in a new tab, read it, then tick the box. That tick is your confirmation; treat it like part of the lesson.

## When you're stuck — in order

1. Read the error message — full stack trace
2. Read the official docs for the tool throwing the error
3. Explain expected vs actual out loud
4. Try a smaller test — one route, one query
5. Take a short break
6. Ask for help with: *"I expected X, I got Y, I tried Z"*

## Key ideas from this page

- Use the **sidebar** — lessons in order, one at a time
- Every gate has **two parts**: checklist + answer questions — both mandatory
- **Read** and **Learn** before **Build** in the same chapter
- **Mandatory reads** appear as cards — open the link, read, tick the box
- Functional = **what** the product does; non-functional = **how well** it must do it

Store order lines as `{ productId, quantity }` only. Six months later the vendor changes basmati rice from ₹120/kg to ₹140/kg. Your order history page reloads and shows **₹140** for a purchase that happened at **₹120**. Accounting, customer trust, and your viva all fail.

This sub-chapter explains the trap **before** you implement the fix in 18.11.

## In plain English

| Term | Simple meaning |
|---|---|
| **Live join** | Loading order line, then fetching **current** product price from catalogue |
| **Snapshot** | Copying name, price, unit onto the order line **at checkout** — frozen forever |
| **Immutable order** | After creation, the receipt does not change when vendor edits the product |
| **Historical truth** | "What did the customer pay that day?" — must come from order, not product |

**The trap in one sentence:** Joining live product data into old orders **rewrites history**.

## The bug in one sentence

**Joining live product data into historical orders rewrites the past.**

## How teams accidentally build this

| Step | What goes wrong |
|---|---|
| Order stores only `productId` | Seems normalized — DRY |
| Order detail API populates Product | Shows current `name`, `price`, `unit` |
| Vendor edits price | Every old order display changes |
| Customer disputes charge | You have no frozen receipt |

MongoDB does not prevent this. SQL foreign keys do not prevent this. **Design** prevents this.

## Grocery makes it worse

From Chapter 3 — units matter:

- ₹120 **per kg** vs ₹120 **per piece**
- Weight-based quantity (2.5 kg)
- Perishable products renamed seasonally

Snapshot must capture at minimum:

| Field | Purpose |
|---|---|
| `nameAtPurchase` | Product title at checkout time |
| `priceAtPurchase` | Unit price at checkout time |
| `unitAtPurchase` | `kg`, `litre`, `piece`, etc. |
| `quantity` | How many units bought |
| `productId` | Optional link for "buy again" — not for pricing display |

Line total = `priceAtPurchase × quantity` — computed from snapshot fields only on read.

## What snapshots are not

Three misconceptions that make developers reach for the wrong fix — and the truth that keeps you on snapshots:

| Misconception | Truth |
|---|---|
| "Denormalization is always bad" | Orders are **events** — events are immutable |
| "I'll version products instead" | Still need point-in-time copy on the order |
| "Snapshots duplicate data" | Yes — intentionally — receipts always duplicate |
| "Cart already has price" | Cart is mutable; order is committed |

## UI consequence (Chapter 20)

Order history UI reads **only embedded order line fields** — no `populate('productId')` for price display. Populate for product image link is optional; price display is not.

Chapter 20 sub-chapter 20.2 expands this for frontend.

## Test you will run (18.16)

1. Checkout product at price P1.
2. Vendor PATCHes product to price P2.
3. `GET /orders/my` still shows P1 on that line.

If it shows P2, snapshot implementation is wrong.

## ✅ Do / ❌ Don't

**✅ Do:** Copy name, price, unit from product document **inside checkout transaction** at commit time.

**✅ Do:** Display order totals from embedded lines only.

**❌ Don't:** Use `populate` on product for historical price in order detail.

**❌ Don't:** Recompute order total from live catalogue on read.

## Mentor interview angle

*"How do you handle price changes after an order is placed?"*

Strong answer: snapshot at purchase; order lines self-contained; product link for reference only.

Weak answer: "We use the product table" — describes the trap.

## Common beginner questions

<FaqGroup>

<FaqItem question="Isn't copying the price into the order \"bad normalization\"?">

Not on an order — because an order isn't a table of facts about products; it's a record of an **event**. On the Product collection you keep one price field, so editing it once updates the catalogue everywhere, which is what normalization is for. But the moment someone buys something, that event has to stand on its own: what was bought, at what price, that day. The copies on an order line aren't redundant data — they're the receipt. Receipts have always duplicated the shelf price tag; that's exactly the point of a receipt.

</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Why can't I just store productId and look up the price when I show the order?">

Because by the time you look it up, the catalogue may have moved on. The vendor edits the price, and every old order suddenly displays the *current* price as if it were historical truth — that's the "live join" trap. The fix is to copy `nameAtPurchase`, `priceAtPurchase`, `unitAtPurchase`, and `quantity` onto the line at checkout, and compute totals only from those embedded snapshot fields. Keep `productId` if you like, but treat it as a link for "buy again" — never for pricing display. Historical truth must come from the order, not the product.

</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Can I populate the product for the image on order history?">

Yes — for a product image or link, that's an optional extra and it's fine. The rule is specific: never use `populate` to display **price or unit** on a fulfilled order, because those must come from the frozen snapshot. If the vendor renames an item or changes it from ₹120 to ₹140, your old orders must keep showing exactly what was bought and paid. An image going slightly stale is cosmetic; a price going wrong on a receipt is a customer dispute waiting to happen.

</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="What if the product is deleted later?">

Your order still tells the full story, because the snapshot fields live inside the order line itself — `nameAtPurchase`, `priceAtPurchase`, `unitAtPurchase`, and `quantity` are all right there. The `productId` link might 404 once the product is gone, but that's fine: the receipt is self-contained and doesn't need the product document to render correctly. This is exactly why snapshots exist — your order history survives catalogue cleanups, renames, and price changes without breaking a single historical receipt.

</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Should cart lines snapshot the price too?">

Not for this MVP. A cart is mutable intent — you add, remove, and change quantities, so a cart price is just a preview to help you decide. Checkout is the commit point, and that's the only moment you need a frozen copy. In sub-chapter 18.11 you'll copy the price from the product onto the order line **inside the checkout transaction**, so the snapshot and the stock decrement happen together. Snapshotting the cart too would just mean updating stale copies on every cart change — extra work with no benefit.

</FaqItem>

</FaqGroup>

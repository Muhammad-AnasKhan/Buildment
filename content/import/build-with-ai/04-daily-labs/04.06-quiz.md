```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Karim does his labs at a café with internet. Four facts about tonight. 1: He can pay for one session of 40 minutes. 2: His lab question is 'does my app keep working when its cache stops?' 3: Last week he ran this app with its cache and database, and it worked. 4: His plan is study 10, build 45 (start the app, add a load balancer, add a second database), break 5, write 5. What should he change?"
options:
  - "Drop the break and write parts, so that the 45-minute build fits"
  - "Spend tonight setting up the app and its cache again from nothing"
  - "Cut the build to starting the app he has, and stop only the cache"
  - "Keep the full 65-minute plan, and buy a second session if needed"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "His question is answered by the break step: stopping the cache. Dropping it means the lab cannot answer its own question."
  - "Fact 3 says the app already runs with its cache and database. Building it again uses the evening on something he has already done."
  - "Right. The load balancer and the second database do not help answer tonight's question. With them gone, a plan such as study 10, build 15, break 10 and write 5 fits his 40 minutes."
  - "Fact 1 says he can pay for one 40-minute session. A plan should fit the time he really has, not the time he wishes for."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Raghad, a university student, asks a chat assistant: 'My compose.yaml has restart: unless-stopped on every part. What happens after docker compose stop db?' It answers: 'Because of unless-stopped, Docker will start the database again within a few seconds, so your app will hardly notice. You do not need to start it yourself.' What is wrong with this answer?"
options:
  - "A part she stops herself stays stopped until she starts it"
  - "Nothing; unless-stopped means it restarts unless she deletes it"
  - "It is right, but only for a cache, and never for a database"
  - "It is right, but only in a codespace, and not on a laptop"
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. With unless-stopped, Docker restarts a part that crashes, but a part you stop yourself stays stopped until you start it. The next check is Docker's own documentation on restart policies, not the same chat."
  - "The policy's name means the opposite: Docker restarts the part unless you stopped it. Stopping is enough to keep it down."
  - "The policy works the same way for every part in the file. What changes between a cache and a database is what the app loses, not the restart rule."
  - "The restart rule belongs to Docker, wherever it runs. A codespace and a laptop follow the same policy."
```

Which break step fits tonight's lab?"

  - "Stop the app container again, and refresh the quiz page"
  - "Stop only the database, submit one answer, then start it"
  - "Rebuild every container from nothing, and time the rebuild"
  - "Stop the database, and wait for Docker to bring it back"

explanations:
  - "Fact 3 says she did this yesterday, and it does not test her question about the database."
  - "Right. It changes one part, it answers her question, and it fits in 8 minutes. Starting the database again leaves tomorrow's lab clean."
  - "Fact 2 gives her 8 minutes. A full rebuild is likely to take most of that, and it does not answer her question."
  - "Fact 4 means a database she stops stays stopped. She would wait for nothing, and she must start it again herself."

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Hala works at a pharmacy and does her labs in its back office after closing. Four facts about tonight: 1) She has 40 minutes before she must leave. 2) Her lab question is 'does my order app keep working when its cache stops?' 3) Last week she ran this app with its cache and database, and it worked. 4) Her plan is study 10, build 45 (start the app, add a load balancer, add a second database), break 5, write 5. What should she change?"
options:
  - "Drop the break and write parts, so that the 45-minute build fits"
  - "Set up the app and its cache again, since rebuilding is good practice"
  - "Cut the build to starting her app, because the cache stop answers it"
  - "Keep the 65-minute plan, as she can stay late if the lab runs over"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "Her question is answered by the break step: stopping the cache. Dropping it means the lab cannot answer its own question."
  - "Rebuilding has some value, but fact 3 says the app already runs with its cache and database. The evening would go on something already done."
  - "Right. The load balancer and the second database do not help answer tonight's question. Without them, a plan such as study 10, build 15, break 10 and write 5 fits her 40 minutes."
  - "Fact 1 says she has 40 minutes. A plan should fit the time she really has, not the time she hopes for."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Raghad, a university student, asks a chat assistant: 'My compose.yaml has restart: unless-stopped on every part. What happens after docker compose stop db?' It answers: 'Because of unless-stopped, Docker will start the database again within a few seconds, so your app will hardly notice. You do not need to start it yourself.' What is your verdict on this answer?"
options:
  - "It is wrong, since a part she stops waits for her to start it"
  - "It is right, because unless-stopped restarts a part unless it is deleted"
  - "It is right for a cache, since only a database must stay stopped"
  - "It is right in a codespace, since a laptop follows other restart rules"
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. With unless-stopped, Docker restarts a part that crashes, but a part you stop yourself stays stopped until you start it. The next check is Docker's own documentation on restart policies, not the same chat."
  - "The policy does restart parts, but its name means the opposite of this answer: Docker restarts the part unless you stopped it."
  - "The policy works the same way for every part in the file. What changes between a cache and a database is what the app loses, not the restart rule."
  - "Codespaces and laptops can differ in other ways, but the restart rule belongs to Docker, wherever it runs."
```

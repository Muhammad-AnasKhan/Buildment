```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Amira volunteers at an NGO office that runs a winter clothes day for 300 families. She told a chat assistant: '300 families, 4 volunteers, one day.' It wrote: 'Open the hall at 9:00. Serve families in order of arrival. Each of the 4 volunteers serves 75 families. Post the time on the NGO's page.' Amira knows the hall is booked by a school until 13:00. What should she do first?"
options:
  - "Give the AI the hall's real hours, then read the new plan herself"
  - "Ask the AI for a longer plan, with a time for every single step"
  - "Ask the AI to share the 300 families among the volunteers again"
  - "Post the plan as it is, and fix the opening time on the day itself"
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. The plan's mistake comes from a fact only Amira knew: the hall is busy until 13:00. She adds that context, and she still reads and decides on the new plan herself."
  - "A longer plan built on the wrong opening time is still wrong. More detail cannot replace a fact the AI was never given."
  - "The sum is fine: 300 families shared by 4 volunteers is 75 each. The problem is the opening time, which the AI could not know."
  - "Families would arrive at 9:00 at a hall that is busy until 13:00. Checking the plan is Amira's call, and it comes before posting, not on the day."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Rami is a freelance web developer on a freelance marketplace. A client's booking site crashes, and he wants help from a chat assistant. Four facts: the error log holds 200 customers' emails; the settings file holds the database password; the contract says no client code goes into outside tools without the client's written permission; the crash is in a 30-line function with no personal data. What is his best first step?"
options:
  - "Paste the full error log, since it shows the exact error message"
  - "Paste the settings file, since the crash may come from a setting"
  - "Get the client's written yes, then paste only the 30-line function"
  - "Paste the whole project folder, so the AI can see all of it at once"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "The log holds 200 customers' emails, which is other people's personal data. It does not go into an AI tool as it is."
  - "The settings file holds the database password. A password or key never goes into an AI tool."
  - "Right. The function holds no personal data and no secrets, and the contract asks for written permission first. Asking takes one short message."
  - "The folder would carry the password with it, and the contract forbids sending client code to outside tools without permission."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Firas is studying lecture 7 of his university databases course, on indexes. He made an audio overview of his lecture notes and listened on his walk home. The audio said: 'An index makes every query faster, so add one to every column.' His lecture notes say an index speeds up reading data but slows down saving it, and takes extra space. What should he do next?"
options:
  - "Trust the audio, since the tool made it only from his own notes"
  - "Open the notes at that part, and fix the claim on his notes page"
  - "Listen to the same part of the audio again, to be sure he heard it"
  - "Make a longer audio overview, so the tool can explain more detail"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "A tool that works from your sources can still get them wrong, and Google says its audio overviews may contain mistakes. Here his own notes say something different."
  - "Right. The audio is for meeting a topic, and the source is where you learn it and check it. His notes show the claim is wrong, so his notes page should say what the source says."
  - "Hearing it again only confirms what the audio said, not whether it is true. The check has to be against the source."
  - "A longer overview from the same sources can repeat the same mistake at greater length. The lecture notes already hold the answer."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Ola has 40 minutes for a lab at a café with internet after work. She asked a chat assistant for a lab plan on caching. It gave: study caching basics, 15 minutes; build a cache in front of a small app, 45 minutes; write notes, 10 minutes. What should she change first?"
options:
  - "Keep the plan, and run it over two evenings instead of one"
  - "Leave out the notes, since writing can wait for the weekend"
  - "Ask the AI to add more study time before the build begins"
  - "Shrink the build, and add a step where she stops one part"
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "Two evenings would fit the time, but the plan would still have no part where she breaks something on purpose, and that is where a lab shows what each part is for."
  - "Writing is one of the four parts of a lab. Notes written days later lose what broke and why."
  - "The plan already takes 70 minutes for a 40-minute slot. More study makes it longer still."
  - "Right. The plan adds up to 70 minutes, not 40, and it has no break step. A smaller build leaves time to stop one part on purpose and write what happened."
```

```quiz

question: "Tamer plans a booking app for a school's computer room. His plan: 6 hours a week for 2 months (8 weeks), 10 areas, and 10% of the time for learning. His own estimate is about 6 hours to learn an area and 3 hours to build it with AI help. What does the arithmetic tell him?"
options:
  - "The plan works, as long as AI writes the code for all 10 areas"
  - "Cut learning to zero, and learn each area after the app is done"
  - "48 hours cannot cover the 90 he needs: add months and learning"
  - "Add two more areas, so the finished app impresses reviewers more"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "Even if AI did all the building, his own estimate puts learning at 60 hours. At 10% of 48 hours, he has under 5 hours for learning."
  - "He would have an app he cannot explain. A reviewer's first 'why?' would find the gap, as it did for Khaled in module 5."
  - "Right. 6 hours a week for 8 weeks is 48 hours, and 10 areas need about 90: 60 for learning and 30 for building. A longer plan with a larger learning share fits."
  - "More areas need even more hours, and the plan is already too short. A project you understand beats a bigger one."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Lama is building a book search page for her school's library. She has 6 hours a week for 6 months, and her project has 9 areas. She asks a chat assistant for a schedule. It answers: 'With AI tools, each area takes about 2 hours, so all 9 areas fit in your first 3 weeks.' What is the main mistake in this answer?"
options:
  - "It gets the sum wrong for 9 areas in 3 weeks"
  - "It counts building time and leaves out the learning"
  - "It plans more hours a week than she has to give"
  - "It plans more areas than a library page will need"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "Nine areas at 2 hours is 18 hours, and 3 weeks at 6 hours is also 18 hours. The arithmetic is fine; what it counts is the problem."
  - "Right. Two hours an area is a building estimate with AI help. Learning an area takes much longer, and the answer has no learning hours at all. Her 6 months give 6 times 4 times 6, which is 144 hours, room for both."
  - "The answer used her 6 hours a week correctly. The hours a week are not where it went wrong."
  - "Nothing in the facts says 9 areas is too many. The number of areas is Lama's decision, and the answer accepted it."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Duaa, from the coding bootcamp, has 5 hours a week for her tailor's order page. Her plan for week 5: 1) New areas: file uploads and payments. 2) Resources: two videos and one article, all opened and checked. 3) Last day: a recall test with her notes closed. 4) Hours: 5, as in her plan. Which change does this course's way of planning call for?"
options:
  - "Open the two videos and the article again to check they exist"
  - "Replace the recall test with a rewatch of both of the videos"
  - "Raise the week to 10 hours so that both of the areas can fit"
  - "Move payments to a later week as its own new area"
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "She already opened and checked all three, so the 'I checked it exists' column is done."
  - "The recall test is the right way to end the week. Rewatching feels like learning, but closing the notes and recalling shows the gaps."
  - "Her plan says 5 hours, and planning with hours she does not have makes the plan fall behind in the first month."
  - "Right. Two new areas in one week break the rule of one new area at a time, and each area gets half her attention. Payments can be the new area of a later week."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Review from module 4: Malak has 40 minutes for a lab on her school timetable app, which runs in two containers: an app and a database. Her plan: 1) Study for 10 minutes. 2) Build for 15 minutes. 3) Stop the app and the database at the same moment to see what breaks. 4) Write for 10 minutes. What should she change first?"
options:
  - "Stop only one part, after writing down what she predicts"
  - "Cut the writing part so the break step gets more of the time"
  - "Leave out the study part to save ten minutes"
  - "Stop both parts, then run the whole lab a second time"
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. If two parts stop at once and the app fails, she cannot tell which one caused it. One part, with a written prediction first, shows what that part is for."
  - "Writing is where the lab turns into something she can use later. More minutes on a break that stops two parts at once would not make it clearer."
  - "The study part is short and tells her what to look for. Dropping it does not fix the real problem, which is stopping two parts together."
  - "Repeating the same lab repeats the same confusion. With both parts stopped, she still cannot tell which one caused the failure."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Nizar, a freelance developer, is building a weekly feedback page for a coding bootcamp. The facts: 1) 25 students answer once a week. 2) The staff already edit a shared spreadsheet every day. 3) The budget is 10 hours of work. A chat assistant suggests a new database with its own admin page. The other option is to save answers into the staff's spreadsheet. Which option do the facts support?"
options:
  - "Build a new database, as the bootcamp may grow to thousands of students"
  - "Give staff an admin page on a new database, as they need their own tool"
  - "The staff's spreadsheet, as it fits 25 weekly answers and their daily use"
  - "Either one, as both can store 25 answers a week without trouble"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "Nothing in the facts says the bootcamp will grow to thousands. With 25 weekly answers, growth should carry little weight."
  - "The staff already edit the spreadsheet every day. A new admin page means new things for them to learn, which is a cost, not a gain."
  - "Right. Twenty-five weekly answers is a small load, the staff already work in the spreadsheet, and 10 hours favours the simpler build. Nizar should write down what it gives up and what would make him change."
  - "Both can store the answers, but they are not equal on the other facts. The staff's daily habit and the 10-hour budget both point to the spreadsheet."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Adam, still building the online shop for the sewing workshop, finds a product-photo function in a project under the Apache 2.0 licence. He wants to reuse it and change 10 of its 30 lines. What does the licence ask of him?"
options:
  - "Nothing beyond using the code as he likes"
  - "Keep the notices and state his changes"
  - "Share his whole shop's source under Apache 2.0"
  - "Ask the author for permission before he uses it"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "Apache 2.0 is permissive, but permissive means few conditions, not none. It still asks him to keep the notices and say what he changed."
  - "Right. Apache 2.0 lets him reuse and change the code if he keeps its notices and states his changes. A credit line in his README is the course's extra habit."
  - "Sharing the whole source under the same licence is what GPL asks when you distribute. Apache 2.0 does not ask that."
  - "Asking first is for code with no licence. This project has a licence that already gives permission, with conditions."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Wael studies SQL at a coding bootcamp. Week 1: on Sunday he reread his notes 3 times and felt 'very sure'. Week 2: on Sunday and again on Wednesday, he answered 10 questions with his notes closed, then checked them, and felt 'unsure'. Both Thursday tests had 10 questions of the same kind. He scored 4, then 8. What do these facts support?"
options:
  - "Feeling sure was a good guide to how ready he was"
  - "Week 2's questions were easier, so the method did not matter"
  - "Closed-book recall on two days helped more than rereading"
  - "Recall on Sunday alone would have given him the same 8"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "In week 1 he felt very sure and scored 4. In week 2 he felt unsure and scored 8. His feeling pointed the wrong way both times."
  - "Both tests had 10 questions of the same kind, so the facts do not show easier questions in week 2."
  - "Right. Same kind of test, two methods, 4 against 8. One person over two weeks is a hint, not proof, but it matches what larger studies found about testing yourself and spacing study out."
  - "He recalled on both Sunday and Wednesday. The facts cannot separate the two days, so they do not support this."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Layan builds websites for 2 small shops. She asks a chat assistant to quiz her on 6 HTTP status codes. It replies: 'Great! Question 1: What does 404 mean? Answer: the server could not find the page. Question 2: What does 500 mean? Answer: something went wrong on the server. Ready for more?' What is the main problem with this reply for her learning?"
options:
  - "Its answer for status code 404 is wrong"
  - "It should explain all 6 codes in full before asking anything"
  - "Two questions are too few for a useful practice"
  - "It shows each answer before she can try to recall it"
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "404 does mean the server could not find what was asked for. The facts in the reply are fine; the format is the problem."
  - "Explaining first would make the AI do even more of the recall. She asked to be quizzed, which was the right request."
  - "The number of questions is not the issue. Six questions with the answers attached would have the same problem."
  - "Right. With the answer under each question, her eyes reach it before she has tried. She should ask for one question at a time, with the AI waiting for her answer."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Noor volunteers at an NGO office and is preparing a talk on safe passwords for 20 volunteers. She has 4 possible sources: (a) her NGO's IT guide, updated this year; (b) a forum post from 2012 with no author; (c) a national cyber security agency's public guide; (d) a chat assistant's summary that names no sources. Which two should go into her source notebook?"
options:
  - "Her NGO's IT guide and the 2012 forum post"
  - "Her NGO's guide and the agency's guide"
  - "The agency's guide and the chat summary"
  - "The forum post and the chat summary"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "The forum post is old and has no author, so nobody can say who checked it or whether it is still true. The notebook would repeat it in the same calm voice as the good sources."
  - "Right. Both are current and come from people responsible for the advice. A source notebook only answers from what you give it, so the choice of sources decides what it can say."
  - "The chat summary names no sources, so it is not a source. It is another AI answer that would need checking itself."
  - "Both have a problem: the forum post is old with no author, and the summary names no sources. Neither is something she could check a claim against."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
```
```quiz
question: "Maram is learning CSS for her small online shop. Her test in 10 days asks her to choose between flexbox and grid for 12 page layouts. A chat assistant suggests: 'Day 1: 15 flexbox exercises. Day 2: 15 grid exercises. Days 3 to 9: reread your notes once a day. Day 10: test.' Which change fits this module's habits best?"
options:
  - "Keep the blocks, since doing one tool at a time is clearer"
  - "Add 15 more flexbox exercises on day 3, then reread as planned"
  - "Mix both tools in each practice, and recall on days 4 and 8"
  - "Replace the exercises with AI explanations of both, read twice"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "Blocks feel clearer, and that is the trap: her test asks her to choose between the two tools, and blocked practice leaves out that choice."
  - "More exercises of one kind add practice, but the six days that follow are still rereading, not recall."
  - "Right. Her test asks her to tell two similar tools apart, which is where mixing helps most. Recall on days 4 and 8 spreads the practice out; with only 10 days, the gaps are shorter than a week, because the best gap depends on how far away the test is."
  - "Reading explanations lets the AI do the recall. Exercises she solves herself are worth more."
```

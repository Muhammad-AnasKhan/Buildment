```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Wael studies SQL at a coding bootcamp. 1) Week 1: on Sunday he reread his notes 3 times and felt 'very sure'. 2) Week 2: on Sunday and again on Wednesday, he answered 10 questions with his notes closed, then checked them; he felt 'unsure'. 3) Both Thursday tests had 10 questions of the same kind. 4) He scored 4, then 8. What do these facts support?"
options:
  - "Feeling sure was a good guide, since he felt sure before one test"
  - "Week 2 was easier, since a method alone cannot double a score"
  - "In his case, recall on two days beat rereading, since the tests matched"
  - "Sunday's recall alone made the 8, since it came before Wednesday"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "He did feel sure before one test, and it was the test he scored 4 on. In week 2 he felt unsure and scored 8. His feeling pointed the wrong way both times."
  - "Both tests had 10 questions of the same kind, so the facts do not show an easier week 2."
  - "Right. Same kind of test, two methods, 4 against 8. One person over two weeks is a hint, not proof, but it matches what larger studies found about testing yourself and spacing study out."
  - "He recalled on both Sunday and Wednesday. The facts cannot separate the two days, so they do not support this."
```

  - "Its 404 answer is wrong, since 404 means the server crashed"
  - "It should explain all 6 codes first, since she is still learning them"
  - "Two questions are too few, since she asked to practise 6 codes"
  - "It shows each answer too early, since she cannot recall it first"
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "404 does mean the server could not find what was asked for. The facts in the reply are fine; the format is the problem."
  - "She is still learning, but explaining first would make the AI do even more of the recall. She asked to be quizzed, which was the right request."
  - "She did ask about 6 codes, but more questions with the answers attached would have the same problem."
  - "Right. With the answer under each question, her eyes reach it before she has tried. She should ask for one question at a time, with the AI waiting for her answer."

question: "Maram is learning CSS for her small online shop. Her test in 10 days asks her to choose between flexbox and grid for 12 page layouts. A chat assistant suggests: 'Day 1: 15 flexbox exercises. Day 2: 15 grid exercises. Days 3 to 9: reread your notes once a day. Day 10: test.' Which change fits this module's habits best?"
options:
  - "Keep the blocks, since one tool at a time is clearer"
  - "Add 15 flexbox exercises on day 3, since practice builds skill"
  - "Mix the two tools and recall on days 4 and 8, since the test mixes them"
  - "Swap exercises for AI explanations, since they are quicker to read"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "Blocks feel clearer, and that is the trap: her test asks her to choose between the two tools, and blocked practice leaves out that choice."
  - "Practice does build skill, but more exercises of one kind still leave six days of rereading, not recall."
  - "Right. Her test asks her to tell two similar tools apart, which is where mixing helps most. Recall on days 4 and 8 spreads the practice out. With only 10 days, the gaps are shorter than a week, because the best gap depends on how far away the test is."
  - "They are quicker to read, but reading explanations lets the AI do the recall. Exercises she solves herself are worth more."

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Tamer, the water delivery driver, plays a phone game in his cab at lunch and is often 20 minutes late back on his round. He asks an AI chat tool for help. It answers: 'The key is discipline. Keep the game on your home screen and practise saying no every lunch break. Each time you resist, your willpower grows stronger, like a muscle.' What is the main problem with this answer?"
options:
  - "Nothing, since practising saying no is how discipline is built"
  - "It keeps the cue close to fight it, instead of moving it away"
  - "It should tell him to skip his lunch break to save the time"
  - "It should say that all phone use at work is harmful to him"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "Research found that people who seem disciplined mostly face fewer temptations, and the large tests did not support the simple 'willpower muscle' story. Practising fights is not the plan this module teaches."
  - "Right. The answer sounds reasonable, but it plans a fight every lunch break with the game one tap away. A stronger plan moves the game off his home screen and gives his lunch break a different routine."
  - "His lunch break is not the problem, and he needs it. The problem is what starts the game during it."
  - "The facts are about one game at lunch, not all phone use. A plan should target what starts this habit."
```

question: "Sara closes the tailor's shop at 18:00, then sits on the step and scrolls before walking home. Her notes for 5 evenings: Sunday, busy day, 'wanted quiet', 40 minutes. Monday, quiet day, 5 minutes. Tuesday, busy day, 'wanted quiet', 50 minutes. Wednesday, busy day, 'feet hurt', 45 minutes. Thursday, quiet day, no scrolling. Which replacement do her notes support?"
options:
  - "Sit on the step for 10 minutes with tea from the shop kettle"
  - "Walk home straight away after closing on every single day"
  - "Scroll only on quiet days, when there is less to recover from"
  - "Delete the video apps from her phone on her busiest days"
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. She scrolls long only after busy days, when she wants quiet and her feet hurt. Ten quiet minutes sitting on the same step meets that need at the same cue, closing time, without the 45 minutes of scrolling."
  - "Walking home at once takes away the rest and the quiet she needs after a busy day, so the urge stays and the old routine is likely to return."
  - "She hardly scrolls on quiet days already. The long scrolling is on busy days, so this changes nothing that matters."
  - "Deleting the apps leaves the need for quiet rest unmet at the same moment, and the step and the phone are still there."

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Anas, the youth coach, wants to stop scrolling in bed. Four facts: his phone charges beside his bed; its alarm wakes him for the dawn prayer; a family group sends messages until about midnight; his scrolling usually starts with one of those messages. Which plan removes the cue without causing a new problem?"
options:
  - "Phone in the hall, and trust himself to wake for the dawn prayer"
  - "Keep the phone beside the bed, but with the screen face down"
  - "Turn the phone off at 23:00 and keep it under his pillow"
  - "Phone in the hall, and a battery clock set for the dawn prayer"
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "This removes the cue, but the phone was also his alarm. Without another alarm, he may miss the dawn prayer, and the phone is likely to come back to the bedroom."
  - "Face down, the phone still buzzes with the family group's messages, and those messages are what start his scrolling."
  - "With the phone off, his dawn alarm cannot ring. The phone also stays under his pillow, so the cue is still in his bed."
  - "Right. The phone leaves the bedroom, so the late messages cannot start the habit there. The clock takes over the alarm's job first, so nothing he needs goes with the phone."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Lama, the nurse, decided to leave her phone in her locker during her breaks, so she would rest instead of scrolling. It worked on 9 of 10 shifts. On shift 10 she scrolled through her whole break. Her note says: 'a colleague showed me a video, then I went and got my phone'. What should she conclude?"
options:
  - "The plan failed, which shows she has very little willpower"
  - "She should wait until next month and then start from zero"
  - "The plan works; add a line for a colleague's video"
  - "She should stop taking breaks so the phone cannot tempt her"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "Nine of ten shifts went to plan. One slip, with a clear cause, does not show weak willpower. It shows where the plan is thin."
  - "Waiting a month throws away a plan that works on most shifts, and a long gap makes restarting harder. Her next break is the next cue."
  - "Right. The note shows exactly what started the slip. A line such as 'If a colleague shows me a video, I watch that one and then go back to my tea' covers it, and the rest of the plan stays."
  - "Breaks are not the problem; she needs them to rest. The plan already works on most shifts."
```

Which plan fits these facts best?"

  - "Promise 30 minutes again, with an alarm set on his phone"

  - "Stop playing backgammon for good, starting from tomorrow"
  - "Stop at the stall each day, but only watch his friends play"

explanations:
  - "A promise to stop after 30 minutes already failed on 4 of 5 days. An alarm asks for the same fight when he is in the middle of a game."
  - "Right. The other-campus days show that taking the other road avoids the cue completely. Playing on a planned day keeps the game and the company he loves, so the need is still met."
  - "He loves the game and the company. Giving them up completely leaves that need unmet, and the stall is still on his road home."
  - "Watching keeps him at the stall, with the game and his friends in front of him. He meets the temptation every day, for longer."

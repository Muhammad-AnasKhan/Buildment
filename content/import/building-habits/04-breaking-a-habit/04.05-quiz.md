```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Tamer, the water delivery driver, plays a phone game in his cab at his lunch stop and is often late for his afternoon round. An AI chat tool tells him: 'The key is discipline. Keep the game on your home screen and practise saying no each lunch. Each time you resist, your willpower grows, like a muscle.' What is the main problem?"
options:
  - "It is fine, since practising saying no builds discipline"
  - "It keeps the game one tap away and plans to resist it daily"
  - "It should tell him to delete the game from his phone"
  - "It should give him a small reward each time he resists the game"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "Practising saying no is still a plan to resist every lunch break. People who seem disciplined mostly face fewer temptations; they rely on habits and surroundings, not on daily practice at resisting."
  - "That is the problem. The advice sounds reasonable, but it keeps the cue one tap away and plans a struggle every day. A stronger plan moves the game off his home screen and gives his lunch break a different routine."
  - "Deleting removes one route to the game, but it leaves the lunch break empty and the need unmet. Maha's deleted app came back within a day for the same reason."
  - "A reward for resisting still keeps the game one tap away and plans a struggle at every lunch break. It changes the prize, not the temptation."
```

type: mcq
question: "Sara closes the tailor's shop at 18:00, then sits on the step and scrolls before walking home. Her notes: Sunday, busy day, 'wanted quiet', 40 minutes. Monday, quiet day, 5 minutes. Tuesday, busy, 'wanted quiet', 50 minutes. Wednesday, busy, 'feet hurt', 45 minutes. Thursday, quiet, none. Which replacement do her notes support?"
options:
  - "Sit on the step for 10 minutes with tea from the shop kettle"
  - "Walk home straight after closing, whatever the day was like"
  - "Scroll only on quiet days, when there is less to recover from"
  - "Delete the video apps from her phone on her busiest days"
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. She scrolls long only after busy days, when she wants quiet and her feet hurt. Ten quiet minutes sitting on the same step meets that need at the same cue, closing time, without the 45 minutes of scrolling."
  - "Walking home at once takes away the rest and the quiet she needs after a busy day, so the urge stays and the old routine is likely to return."
  - "She hardly scrolls on quiet days already. The long scrolling is on busy days, so this changes nothing that matters."
  - "Deleting the apps leaves the need for quiet rest unmet at the same moment, and the step and the phone are still there."

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Anas, the youth coach, wants to stop scrolling in bed. Facts: his phone charges beside his bed; its alarm wakes him for the dawn prayer; a family group sends messages until about midnight; his scrolling usually starts with one of those messages. Which plan removes the cue without causing a new problem?"
options:
  - "Phone in the hall, and trust himself to wake for the dawn prayer"
  - "Phone beside the bed, but with the screen turned face down"
  - "Phone switched off at 23:00, and kept under his pillow"
  - "Phone in the hall, and a battery clock set for the dawn prayer"
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "This removes the cue, but the phone was also his alarm. Without another alarm, he may miss the dawn prayer, and the phone is likely to come back to the bedroom."
  - "Face down, the phone still buzzes with the family group's messages, and those messages are what start his scrolling."
  - "With the phone off, his dawn alarm cannot ring. The phone also stays under his pillow, so the cue is still in his bed."
  - "Correct. The phone leaves the bedroom, so the late messages cannot start the habit there. The clock takes over the alarm's job first, so nothing he needs goes with the phone."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Lama, the nurse, leaves her phone in her locker during her breaks, so she rests instead of scrolling. It worked on 9 of 10 shifts. On shift 10 she scrolled through her whole break. Her note says: 'a colleague showed me a video, then I went and got my phone'. What should she conclude?"
options:
  - "The plan failed, which shows she has very little willpower"
  - "She should wait until next month and then start from zero"
  - "The plan works, and she needs a line for a colleague's video"
  - "She should stop taking breaks so the phone cannot tempt her"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "Nine of ten shifts went to plan. One slip, with a clear cause, does not show weak willpower. It shows where the plan is thin."
  - "Waiting a month throws away a plan that works on most shifts, and a longer gap makes the next start harder. Her next break is the next cue."
  - "That fits the note. It shows exactly what started the slip. A line such as 'If a colleague shows me a video, I watch that one and then go back to my tea' covers it, and the rest of the plan stays."
  - "Breaks are not the problem; she needs them to rest. The plan already works on most shifts."
```

question: "Iyad, the computer science student, often stops at a roadside stall on his way home to play backgammon with friends for about 90 minutes. When his last lecture is at the other campus, he goes home another way and never stops. He loves the game and the company. Promising 'only 30 minutes' failed on 4 of 5 days. Which plan fits best?"
options:
  - "Promise 30 minutes again, with an alarm set on his phone"
  - "Take the other road home, and play at the stall on Fridays"
  - "Stop playing backgammon, starting from tomorrow"
  - "Stop at the stall each day, but only watch his friends play"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "A promise to stop after 30 minutes already failed on 4 of 5 days. An alarm asks for the same struggle in the middle of a game."
  - "Right. The other-campus days show that taking the other road avoids the cue completely. Playing on a planned day keeps the game and the company he loves, so the need is still met."
  - "He loves the game and the company. Giving them up completely leaves that need unmet, and the stall is still on his road home."
  - "Watching keeps him at the stall, with the game and his friends in front of him. He meets the temptation every day, for longer."

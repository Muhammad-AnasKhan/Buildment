## Learning log

1. **Your cues:** Think of one habit you already have, or use Khaled's evening from chapter 3.1. Which kind of cue starts it most strongly: a place, a time, other people, or the action just before?
2. **Your chain:** In one of your daily routines, or in Khaled's evening, what is the first link of the chain, and what is the last? Which one is easier to change?
3. **Your hard days:** Which kind of day is hardest for your habit: no power, weak internet, no time, or a busy house? If you prefer, answer for Khaled's study habit instead.
4. **Your next change:** Which tool from this module do you expect to help you most: moving a cue, changing the steps, or writing versions? Say why in one or two sentences.

## Redesign one place or routine

This task brings the module together on the habit you started in module 2. You will change one place or one routine so the habit's cue and its tools meet. You will count the steps before and after. Then you will write the versions that keep the habit going on hard days.

The point is not a perfect room. The point is to show your reasoning. Which cue starts the habit? What stood between you and the first step? What did you change? What happens on the days when the power or the internet is off?

Keep it to one place or one routine. A redesign of the whole house is hard to test and hard to keep. One shelf, one table corner, or one charger moved to another room is enough. If your course habit already needs no power or internet, your backup and your smallest version may be the same thing. Say so, and say which hard day it covers instead, such as a morning with no time.

Here is what a strong answer looks like, told about a fictional person. Ruba is a pharmacist. She is studying for a short certificate course, and her habit is 15 minutes of a recorded lecture after the evening meal.

Before her redesign, her tablet lived in a drawer in the bedroom, and its charger was in the kitchen. Starting took 4 steps: find the tablet, find the charger, find her earphones, find a seat away from the television. She chose one place: the small desk in the hall, where her phone already charges at night. The tablet, its charger and the earphones now stay on that desk. Starting takes 1 step.

Her full version is one 15-minute lecture. Her backup version, for evenings with no power or weak internet, is reading the printed notes for the same lecture by a battery lamp, 10 minutes. Her smallest version, for evenings with no time, is reading the three key points she wrote at the end of the last lecture.

She wrote one if-then line: "If the lecture will not play, then I read the printed notes." She added a preparation line: "When the power comes on, I download the next two lectures and charge the lamp." And a return line: "On the first evening with power and a lecture ready, I go back to the full version."

Look at the shape of Ruba's answer. The cue stays the same in every version: after the evening meal. She counted steps before and after. Each version removes what a hard evening takes away. The preparation is tied to a moment that really happens, the power coming on. The return line stops the small version from becoming the only one.

Compare it with a weak answer: "I will put my books somewhere better and try to study even when the power is off." It names no place, no cue and no steps. "Try to study when the power is off" is a hope, not a version. It does not say what to do, for how long, or with what light. It would change nothing on the first dark evening.

If you prefer not to use your own habit, use Nada's, which you met in module 2. Nada is 22 and studies accounting. Her habit is: "After I wash my breakfast cup, I open my English vocabulary notebook and write one sentence with a new word." It takes about 3 minutes. She tracks it with a tick or a cross and a few words on a paper card kept inside the notebook.

So far she has done it every morning, but it is harder than it needs to be. The notebook lives in her bag in the bedroom she shares with her sister. After washing her cup, she walks to the bedroom, finds the notebook in the bag and looks for a pencil: about 4 steps. Twice she nearly gave up, because her sister was still asleep. Next to the kitchen sink there is a drying rack with a small shelf beside it. On some mornings the power is off and the kitchen is dim, and on lecture mornings she has only a minute or two.

For Nada, redesign her place, and write her full version and a version for those hard mornings. Her habit needs no power or internet, so her backup and her smallest version can be the same: say so in your answer.

## How your mentor will read it

A mentor will read your answer with the table below. There is no single right redesign. If your plan is different from the one your mentor would choose, that is fine, as long as it fits the facts you give.

| Part | Met | Partly | Not yet |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cue and place | The tools sit within one step of the cue, with steps counted before and after | A new place, but not at the cue, or no step counts | No change of place or routine |
| Easier and harder | At least one named step removed, and any rival habit given at least one named extra step | Only "make it easier", with no step named | Nothing made easier or harder |
| Versions | A backup that needs no internet or no power, and a smallest of 5 minutes or less, both at the same cue | One version only, or a backup that needs what the full one needs | No backup or smallest version |
| Hard-day plan | An if-then line for a hard day, plus a preparation or a return line | Only one of these | No plan for a hard day |

**Pass rule:** your answer passes when no part is "Not yet" and at least three parts are "Met".

Write your answer in plain English. Short sentences are fine. You may add a voice note link as an extra.

The mentor then replies with one or two short questions. For example: "Which step did you remove first, and why that one?" or "What will tell you it is time to go back to the full version?" Answer them in writing; a voice note can be added as an extra. That reply is part of the gate, so look out for it after you submit.

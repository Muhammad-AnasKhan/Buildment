```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Ibrahim keeps the accounts for a hardware shop. He wants to stop opening the news on the shop laptop. For three days he noted each time: 9:40, after sending an invoice; 11:15, after finishing a stock list; 14:05, after an invoice; 16:30, after the till report. What does his log show is the cue?"
options:
  - "Boredom, because work in a shop office is often dull"
  - "The time of day, since it happens at the same hours each day"
  - "Finishing a task, since every entry comes right after one"
  - "The laptop itself, since the news is one click away"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "Boredom may be part of it, but it is a feeling with no clear moment. His log shows a moment he can point to: each time comes right after he finishes a task."
  - "The times are all different: 9:40, 11:15, 14:05 and 16:30. What they share is not the hour but what he had just done."
  - "That fits the log. Each entry comes right after a finished task. Module 1 showed that the action you just finished is a common cue. Once he knows it, he can plan what to do at that moment instead."
  - "The laptop is in front of him all day, but he opens the news only at four moments. Something else marks those moments: a finished task."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Majd repairs bicycles. His plan: 'If I have a free evening, then I will revise my electronics notes.' In two weeks he revised on 2 evenings. His notes on the others say 'no free evening' or 'forgot'. He eats with his family at 19:00 every evening. What should he change first?"
options:
  - "Tie it to the evening meal, with opening his notes as the first step"
  - "Set a phone alarm for 21:00 that says it is time to revise"
  - "Revise for a full hour every Friday to make up for all the lost evenings"
  - "Keep the plan, and track it on a card to find out why it fails"
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Good choice. 'A free evening' does not come on most days, so the plan rarely starts. The meal happens every evening at 19:00, and 'open my notes' is a first step he can see. Module 2 called this naming a moment you will surely meet."
  - "An alarm can help him remember, but a reminder is not a cue, and many of his misses were 'no free evening', not forgetting. The plan would still wait for a free evening."
  - "One big session a week is the big-plan mistake from module 2. It also leaves the weekday plan as weak as before."
  - "He already has two weeks of notes, and they show the problem: the 'if' part rarely happens. More tracking would show the same thing again."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Rawan sells fabric. After she locks the shop at 18:00, she practises typing for 10 minutes. Her card for 12 days: done on days 1 to 4, 7, 8, 11 and 12. Misses: day 5 'late customer', day 6 'late customer', day 9 'forgot', day 10 'late customer'. It is day 13. What should she do?"
options:
  - "Start over on the first of next month, with a fresh card and plan"
  - "Double up on the next two evenings to catch up on the four misses"
  - "Track for one more full month first, before she changes anything"
  - "Restart at the next lock-up, with a 2-minute late-night version"
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "Waiting for next month turns four missed days into weeks of gap. A longer gap makes the next start harder, and her card already shows what to fix."
  - "Doubling up makes the habit bigger on evenings that may run late again. It does not touch the cause, and it is the catch-up mistake from module 5."
  - "She already has 12 days of evidence: three of the four misses name a late customer. Another month of tracking would show the same thing and delay the fix."
  - "This is the one. The next lock-up is the next cue, so she restarts there. Her notes point to late customers, so she gives late nights a version that still fits, and 8 days done out of 12 is a good base."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Nabil designs posters at a desk in his family's flat. Every client message brings a sound and a pop-up on his laptop, about 40 a day, and each one breaks his work. Most messages can wait two hours. Last week he tried hard to ignore them and still opened almost all. What should he try first?"
options:
  - "Try harder for one more week, since ignoring pop-ups takes practice"
  - "Turn off pop-ups while he designs and read messages at set times"
  - "Count each message he opens for a month before he changes anything"
  - "Move his phone to another room while he designs at the laptop"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "He already tried hard for a week. Module 4 showed that people who seem disciplined mostly face fewer temptations; they do not win by resisting more."
  - "That removes the cue. The pop-up starts the habit, so taking it away in advance works better than resisting 40 urges a day. Most messages can wait two hours, so nothing important is lost."
  - "He already knows the size of the problem: about 40 a day. Another month of counting delays a change he could make today."
  - "Moving the phone is often a good idea, but these pop-ups come from the laptop. The cue that breaks his work would stay on the screen."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Hadeel answers phones at a taxi office. On the 1st she started three habits. After 10 days: English after breakfast, 7 done; a walk at 17:00, 2 done; bed by 23:00, 3 done. She feels she failed at all three and plans to restart them all next month. What should she do first?"
options:
  - "Shrink all three habits to their smallest versions and keep all three"
  - "Restart all three on the 1st of next month, with a fresh plan"
  - "Add phone alarms for the walk and for bedtime, and keep all three"
  - "Keep English and pause the other two until it starts by itself"
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "Smaller versions help, but she would still be running three new habits at once, the pattern module 6 warned about."
  - "Waiting makes the gap longer, and restarting all three repeats the same plan. Her English habit is already working on 7 of 10 days."
  - "Alarms help her remember, but a reminder is not a cue, and three new habits would still compete for her attention."
  - "That fits module 6. One habit is already running on 7 of 10 days. Keep it, let it start by itself, and add the next habit after that, one at a time."
```

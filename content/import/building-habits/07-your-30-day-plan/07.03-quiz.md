```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Majd, 31, drives a water delivery round. Each day, after he parks the van at 15:00, he stretches for 5 minutes. He has done it on 19 of the last 21 days. It feels easier than in week 1, but he still has to remember it. A friend says: 'After 21 days it should be automatic, so this one will never stick.' Which idea should Majd use first?"
options:
  - "Restart at day 1, because he missed 2 of the 21 days"
  - "Add a loud phone alarm at 15:00, so the stretching starts by itself"
  - "Expect months, not weeks: habits grow fast at first, then slowly"
  - "Stop, since a habit that is not automatic in 21 days will not form"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "Module 1 showed that one missed day did not undo progress in the study of habit forming. 19 of 21 days is a strong start, and starting again from zero would throw that away."
  - "An alarm helps him remember, but a reminder is not a cue. The habit grows from the situation, parking the van, not from the alarm."
  - "Right. For the typical person in one well-known study, a habit took about two months to feel automatic, often longer, and people were very different. Feeling easier already is the fast early growth. He should keep going."
  - "The '21 days' idea comes from stories about patients getting used to a new face after surgery, not from studies of habits. A habit that feels easier after three weeks is growing."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Mustafa, 27, works in the store room of a pharmacy. After lunch at work, he listens to a 15-minute English lesson downloaded on his phone. He eats lunch at the pharmacy every working day. He did the lesson on 11 of the last 14 days. All 3 misses say: 'no power at the pharmacy all morning, phone battery empty'. The power hours change every week. What should he plan first?"
options:
  - "A backup for no-power days: a printed word list kept in his bag"
  - "A new anchor, since lunch at work is a weak moment for this habit"
  - "A phone alarm at lunch, so he never forgets to start the lesson"
  - "A fresh start from day 1, because the 3 misses broke his habit"
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. Every miss has the same cause: no power, so the phone is empty. A backup version that needs no power, such as reading the lesson's word list on paper, keeps the habit going on those days. Printing or downloading while the power is on is a module 3 tool."
  - "Lunch at work happens every day, and he did the habit on 11 of 14 days. The anchor works; only the no-power days fail."
  - "On the missed days his phone battery was empty, so a phone alarm would not ring either. The problem is not forgetting."
  - "Three misses do not erase 11 days of practice. Module 5 taught you to restart at the next cue, not from day 1."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Shahd, 26, designs posters for clients at a desk in her family's flat. While she works, a sound and a pop-up come with every client message, about 40 a day. Each time, she stops to read it and loses her place. Most messages can wait two hours. Last week she tried hard to ignore the pop-ups, and still opened almost every one. What should she try first?"
options:
  - "Try harder for one more week, since ignoring pop-ups takes practice"
  - "Count each message she opens for 30 days before changing anything"
  - "Wait for the start of next month, then make a fresh start"
  - "Turn off the pop-ups while she designs; read messages at set times"
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "She already tried hard for a week and opened almost every message. Module 4 showed that people who seem disciplined mostly face fewer temptations; they do not win by resisting more."
  - "She already knows the size of the problem: about 40 a day. Counting for another month delays a change she could make today."
  - "A new month can be a helpful push, but there is no reason to wait here. Her next work session is a good time to change."
  - "Right. The pop-up is the cue that starts the habit. Removing the cue in advance, and reading messages at set times, works better than fighting 40 urges a day. Most messages can wait two hours, so nothing important is lost."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Nabil, 30, coaches children's football at a sports club. After each evening session he writes three lines in a notebook: what worked, and what to try next time. He did it after 12 sessions in a row. Then a weekend tournament came, and he missed 3 days. Now he says: 'The streak is broken. I will start a new 30-day challenge on the 1st, in 9 days.' Tonight there is a session at 18:00. The next tournament is in 3 months. What should he do first?"
options:
  - "Wait the 9 days, since a new month gives him a fresh start"
  - "Write one line in the notebook after tonight's session"
  - "Choose a different habit, because this one broke after a tournament"
  - "Write an if-then plan for the next tournament before anything else"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "Waiting 9 days turns a 3-day gap into a 12-day gap, and a longer gap makes restarting harder. A new month can be an extra push, but the best time to restart is the next cue: tonight's session."
  - "Right. Restart at the next cue, even with a smaller version. Tonight's session is the cue, and one line is easy after a long weekend. Twelve sessions in a row are not erased by 3 missed days."
  - "The habit worked for 12 sessions in a row, and the misses came from one unusual weekend. A slip is not a reason to drop a habit that works."
  - "A plan for tournaments is useful, but the next one is 3 months away. Restarting tonight comes first; the tournament plan can come later."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Aya, 23, studies accounting and helps at her father's farm stall. In winter the family ate the evening meal at about 17:30, so she set her English listening for 18:00 every day, right after the meal. Now it is summer. The meal has moved to about 19:30, and at 18:00 she is still at the stall. In the last 14 days she listened on 5. Her evenings after the meal are free. What should she change first?"
options:
  - "Shrink the lesson to 2 minutes, so it fits into her busy day"
  - "Set a louder alarm for 18:00, so she does not forget the lesson"
  - "Tie the lesson to the meal itself, not to a time on the clock"
  - "Add two more habits after the meal, to use her free evenings well"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "Her evenings after the meal are free, so time is not the problem. The habit fails because 18:00 no longer comes right after the meal."
  - "At 18:00 she is working at the stall, so an alarm would ring at the wrong moment. Remembering is not the problem; the clock time no longer fits her day."
  - "Right. The habit worked while 18:00 came right after the meal. In summer the meal moved, but the clock time did not. A habit tied to the event, 'after the evening meal', moves with the day through the seasons."
  - "Module 6 showed why several new habits at once tend to fail. Fix the one habit she already has before adding more."
```

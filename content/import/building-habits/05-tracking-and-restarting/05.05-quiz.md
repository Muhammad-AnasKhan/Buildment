```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Wael, a logo designer, spends 10 minutes on his portfolio after his first coffee. He did it 40 days in a row, then missed two days during a deadline. An AI chat tool told him: 'You missed two days in a row, so the habit is gone. Rebuild it from zero with a new 40-day streak, starting tomorrow after your coffee.' What is the mistake?"
options:
  - "It keeps the same coffee cue, which failed him twice"
  - "It treats two missed days as if the habit had been erased"
  - "It tells him to restart tomorrow instead of next week"
  - "It keeps the same 10 minutes instead of a longer session"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "The misses came from a deadline, not from the cue. His coffee cue worked for 40 days, so keeping it is good advice."
  - "Yes. Research tested one missed day and found it did not undo progress. Two days is a longer gap, and a longer gap makes the next start harder, but his 40 days of work are not wasted. The way to protect them is to restart at the next cue, which the answer does say."
  - "Restarting tomorrow, at the next cue, is the good part of the answer. Waiting a week would make the gap longer."
  - "A longer session would be harder to start after a break. Keeping the normal 10 minutes is fine."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Rana reads one short English story in the staff room after the school bell at 13:30. Over two school weeks she read on 6 of 10 days. All four crosses say 'end-of-term marking meeting at 13:30'. Those meetings finished last Thursday. Every day without a meeting is a tick. What should her plan for next week be?"
options:
  - "Keep the plan, restarting at Sunday's bell, with no change"
  - "Move the habit to the evening, away from school meetings"
  - "Add a second anchor for meeting days, after the meeting ends"
  - "Read two stories a day next week to make up the four misses"
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "That fits. The cause was a one-time run of meetings that has ended, and the bell works on every day without one. Nothing needs fixing, so she restarts at the next cue."
  - "Her bell works on every day without a meeting. Moving the habit throws away a working cue for a cause that has already ended."
  - "A second anchor is a good tool when a cause comes back. These meetings have finished, so there are no meeting days left to plan for."
  - "Doubling up makes the habit harder to start and does nothing about the cause. A normal story at the next bell is enough."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Manar, the nursing student, missed 4 of her last 7 evenings of flashcards during exam week. Since then her tracker card has stayed in a drawer for 5 days, because she does not want to see the crosses. She still does her cards on some evenings. What should she do first?"
options:
  - "Wait for a better week before she looks at the card again"
  - "Throw the card away and start a clean one from tomorrow"
  - "Read the card tonight, then group the notes on the crosses"
  - "Keep the card in the drawer and mark only the good days"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "Avoiding the card after bad news is the ostrich problem. A hard week is the week the card has the most to tell her."
  - "A clean card throws away the notes that explain the misses. The record of a hard week is useful, not shameful."
  - "That matches the facts. Looking at the card after a hard week is how she learns what got in the way. Grouping the notes on the crosses turns a bad feeling into a cause she can plan for."
  - "Marking only the good days hides the misses and their causes, so the same week could happen again."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Jamal does a 15-minute coding lesson after he parks his delivery van at 14:00. On Monday and Tuesday the van broke down and he was out until evening. On Tuesday night he writes a note on his card. Which note follows this module's advice?"
options:
  - "'Van broken Mon, Tue. Next: restart when things calm down.'"
  - "'Van broken Mon, Tue. Wed after parking: 30 minutes, to catch up both days.'"
  - "'Missed two days. Next: try much harder from Wednesday on.'"
  - "'Van broken Mon, Tue. Wed after parking: 5 minutes of the lesson.'"
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "The note is kind and states the fact, but 'when things calm down' is not a cue. The gap grows while he waits."
  - "It names the next cue, but a 30-minute session is harder to start than his normal one, on the day starting is hardest."
  - "'Try harder' is a promise, not a plan. It names no cue and no version."
  - "This is the module's advice in one line: the cause as a fact, the next cue named, and a small version that will surely happen."
```

  - "A monthly score check instead of the weekly one he does now"

  - "Four columns a day is a lot to keep up on a tired day. One mark and a few words is enough to show the pattern."
  - "Checking scores less often is fine, but it adds nothing about the practice itself."

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Wael designs logos for clients abroad from a desk in his family's shop. His habit: 'After my first coffee, I spend 10 minutes on my portfolio.' He did it 40 days in a row, then missed two days during a big client deadline. He asked an AI chat tool what to do. It answered: 'Well done on 40 days. Because you missed two days in a row, the habit is gone and you will have to rebuild it from zero. Start a fresh 40-day streak tomorrow, after your first coffee as before.' What is the mistake in this answer?"
options:
  - "It keeps the same cue, which has already failed him twice"
  - "It says two misses wiped out 40 days of progress"
  - "It praises the 40 days, which hides the real problem"
  - "It tells him to restart tomorrow instead of next week"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "The cue did not fail: the misses came from a deadline, and his coffee cue worked for 40 days. Keeping it is good advice."
  - "Right. One missed day did not undo progress in the research, and two days in a row is a short gap. The habit from 40 days is still there. He only needs to restart at the next cue."
  - "Noticing 40 days of work is fair and true. It is not where the answer goes wrong."
  - "Restarting tomorrow, at the next cue, is the good part of the answer. Waiting a week would make the gap longer."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Rana teaches at a primary school. Her habit: 'After the school bell at 13:30, I read one short English story in the staff room.' Over two school weeks she did it on 6 of 10 days. Three crosses say 'canteen duty until 14:30', all on Tuesdays and Thursdays. One cross says 'staff meeting'. Every Sunday, Monday and Wednesday is a tick. Which change do her notes support?"
options:
  - "Move the habit to the evening every day, away from school"
  - "Read two stories on the other days to make up the misses"
  - "Stop tracking on duty days, since duties are not her fault"
  - "On duty days, read after canteen duty ends at 14:30"
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "Her 13:30 bell works on every day without duty. Moving the habit on all days would throw away a cue that works."
  - "Extra stories on good days do nothing about Tuesdays and Thursdays, so the same misses would come back."
  - "Not tracking would hide the pattern instead of fixing it. A cross is a fact, not a judgement of her."
  - "Right. Three of her four misses come from canteen duty on Tuesdays and Thursdays. A different cue for those days, the end of duty, aims at the cause her notes show."
```

  - "Days 17 and 18 were both missed, so the longest gap is 2 days. A streak of 12 is his longest run, days 5 to 16, not his streak on day 30."
  - "His streak on day 30 is 1, not 0, and 26 of 30 is a strong month. The streak is not the measure of a month."
  - "10 is the run from day 19 to day 28. He missed day 29, so on day 30 the streak is only 1."

type: mcq
question: "Jamal drives a water delivery van in the mornings and does a 15-minute coding lesson after he parks at 14:00. On Monday and Tuesday the van broke down and the repair kept him out until evening. On Tuesday night he writes one of these four notes on his card. Which note follows this module's advice?"
options:
  - "'Lazy again. No more excuses from now on.'"
  - "'Two misses do not matter. I will start again when I feel like it.'"
  - "'Van broken Mon, Tue. Wed after parking: 5 minutes of the lesson.'"
  - "'I keep failing, so I will put the card away until next month.'"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "This turns a breakdown into a verdict about him. Harsh words make giving up more likely, and they give him no plan."
  - "Misses do matter as information, and waiting for a feeling lets the gap grow. He needs a time and a version."
  - "Right. It states the cause as a fact, names the next cue, and chooses a small version that will surely happen. That is kind, then a plan."
  - "Waiting for next month makes the gap much longer, and the card is how he would see what went wrong."

  - "A quiz score is a result. It moves slowly and would not show him what gets in the way of practising."
  - "Memory is not a careful witness. It joins facts into stories, and a few written words keep them apart."

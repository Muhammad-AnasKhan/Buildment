1. **Your record:** What does your tracker record now: the action, a result, or both? If you have no tracker yet, think about Yousef's first calendar, which had only ticks. What would a few words on each cross have told him?
2. **Your first thought:** When you miss a day, what is the first sentence in your head? Is it a fact or a verdict? If you prefer, answer for Yousef on day 29.

4. **Your results:** What result do you hope your habit will bring, and how often will you check it? What sign would tell you the habit is forming even while the result is slow?

## Your tracker review and restart plan

This task uses everything in the module on one tracker. You will count, find what caused the misses, notice what went well, and write a plan for the next cue. The point is not to show a perfect tracker. A tracker with many crosses and clear notes can earn a strong review. A tracker with no crosses and no notes has less to teach you. What your mentor wants to see is how you read the evidence and what you decide because of it.

Use your own course habit tracker from module 2 if you have one, even if it covers fewer days than Nada's or has a hard week in it. If you prefer not to use your own, or you have not been tracking, use Nada's tracker below. Nobody needs to explain why they chose the provided case.

Here is what a strong review looks like, told about a fictional person. Duaa studies pharmacy and shares a flat with two cousins. Her habit is "After I make my morning tea, I read 2 pages of my notes." After 20 days, her card shows 15 days done and 5 misses: days 3, 7, 9, 11 and 17. Her longest gap is 1 day, because no two misses came together. She reads her notes and groups them. Four misses say "kitchen busy, no tea": those were the mornings her cousins made breakfast before her. One says "slept at my aunt's". So one cause explains 4 of her 5 misses: on busy mornings her anchor, making tea, does not happen.

Duaa also writes what went well: on three mornings she noted "very tired", and she still read on all three. Her restart plan names the next cue and adds one change for the main cause. "Tomorrow, after I make my tea: 2 pages. On mornings when the kitchen is busy, after I fill my water glass in my room: 2 pages. If I am short of time, 1 paragraph counts." She ends with a check: "For the next two weeks I will count the busy mornings and how many of them have a tick."

Look at the shape of Duaa's review. Every number comes from the card. The causes come from her notes, grouped and counted, not from her memory. She names a strength as well as a problem. Her plan says when, what, and the one thing she is changing, and she says how she will know whether it helped. Nothing in it is a verdict about her character.

Compare it with a weak review of the same card: "I missed 5 days, which is bad. I need to be more disciplined. From tomorrow, no more misses." That review counts only the misses and never reads the notes, so it misses the busy kitchen completely. It turns five facts into a verdict about Duaa, which makes the next miss feel worse. Its plan is a promise, not a cue and a version. On the next busy morning, nothing would be different, and the promise would break.

If you prefer not to use your own tracker, use this provided case. Nada is 22, studies accounting and lives with her family. Her habit: "After I wash my breakfast cup, I open my English vocabulary notebook and write one sentence with a new word." It takes about 3 minutes. ## How your mentor will read it

A mentor will read your review with the table below. Two careful people may group the notes a little differently, and that is fine, as long as every number and cause comes from the tracker.

| Part | Met | Partly | Not yet |
|---|---|---|---|
| Counts | Days done, misses and the longest gap, all correct | One or two of the three, or one small counting error | No counts, or only the streak |
| Causes from the notes | Misses grouped by cause, with the main cause and how many misses it explains | Causes listed, but not grouped or counted | No causes, or a verdict such as "lazy" |
| Restart plan | Next cue named exactly, a version that will surely happen, and one change for the main cause | Next cue and version, but no change for a cause that will come back | No plan, "start over", or "try harder" |
| Facts, not verdicts | Misses written as facts, and one thing that went well | Mostly facts, with one harsh word | Mostly verdicts, or misses left out |

**Pass rule:** your review passes when no part is "Not yet" and at least three parts are "Met". A tracker with many misses can pass with a strong review; the mentor grades the reading and the plan, not the number of ticks.

The mentor then replies with one or two short questions. For example: "Which of your causes will come back next week, and what does your plan do about it?" or "What is the smallest version you would accept on your hardest morning?" You answer them in writing or in a voice note. That reply is part of the gate, so look out for it after you submit.

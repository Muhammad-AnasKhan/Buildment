2. **Your first thought:** When you miss a day, what is the first sentence in your head? Is it a fact, or a judgement about you? If you prefer, answer for Yousef on day 29.

## Your tracker review and restart plan

This task uses everything in the module on one tracker. You will count, find what caused the misses, notice what went well, and write a plan for the next cue. The point is not to show a perfect tracker. A tracker with many crosses and clear notes can earn a strong review. A tracker with no crosses and no notes has less to teach you. What your mentor wants to see is how you read the evidence and what you decide because of it.

Use your own course habit tracker from module 2 if you have one, even if it covers only a week or two, or has a hard week in it. If you prefer not to use your own, or you have not been tracking, use Nada's tracker below. Nobody needs to explain why they chose the provided case.

Here is what a strong review looks like, told about a fictional person. Osama runs a farm stall with his father. His habit is "After I close the stall at 18:00, I walk for 15 minutes." After 20 days, his card shows 15 days done and 5 misses: days 4, 5, 11, 16 and 17. His longest gap is 2 days, and it happened twice: days 4 and 5, and days 16 and 17.

Osama groups his notes. Four misses say "harvest, stall open until dark". One says "rain all evening". So one cause explains 4 of his 5 misses: on harvest days, the stall closes late and his walk has no time and no light. The rain was a single evening, so it needs no change.

He also writes what went well. On three evenings he noted "tired from lifting crates", and he still walked on all three. His restart plan names the next cue and adds one change for the main cause. "Tomorrow, after I close at 18:00: 15 minutes. On harvest days, after lunch at the stall: 10 minutes along the field edge. If time is short, 5 minutes counts." This is the second anchor you met in 2.4. He ends with a check: "For the next two weeks I will count the harvest days and how many of them have a tick."

Look at the shape of Osama's review. Every number comes from the card. The causes come from his notes, grouped and counted, not from his memory. He names a strength as well as a problem. He changes something only for the cause that will come back. His plan says when, what, and the one thing he is changing, and he says how he will know whether it helped. Nothing in it is a judgement about his character.

Compare it with a weak review of the same card: "I missed 5 days, which is bad. I need to be more disciplined. From tomorrow, no more misses." That review counts only the misses and never reads the notes, so it misses the harvest days completely. It turns five facts into a judgement about Osama, which makes the next miss feel worse. Its plan is a promise, not a cue and a version. On the next harvest day, nothing would be different, and the promise would break.

## How your mentor will read it

A mentor will read your review with the table below. Two careful people may group the notes a little differently, and that is fine, as long as every number and cause comes from the tracker.

| Part | Met | Partly | Not yet |
|---|---|---|---|
| Counts | Days done, misses and the longest gap, all three correct | One or two of the three correct | No counts, or only the streak |
| Causes from the notes | Misses grouped by cause, with the main cause and the number of misses it explains | Causes listed, but not grouped or not counted | No causes, or a judgement such as "lazy" |
| Restart plan | A next cue you could point to, a version that will surely happen, and one change for the main cause | A next cue and a version, but no change for a cause that will come back | No plan, "start over", or "try harder" |
| Facts, not judgements | Every miss written as a fact, and one thing that went well | One harsh word about yourself, or nothing that went well | Two or more harsh words, or misses left out |

**Pass rule:** your review passes when no part is "Not yet" and at least three parts are "Met". A tracker with many misses can pass with a strong review; the mentor grades the reading and the plan, not the number of ticks.

Write your answer in plain English. Short sentences are fine. You may add a voice note link as an extra.

The mentor then replies with one or two short questions. For example: "Which of your causes will come back next week, and what does your plan do about it?" or "What is the smallest version you would accept on your hardest morning?" Answer them in writing; again, a voice note link can be added as an extra. That reply is part of the gate, so look out for it after you submit.

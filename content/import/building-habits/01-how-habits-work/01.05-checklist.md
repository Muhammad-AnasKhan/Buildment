## Your habit, taken apart

This task asks you to look closely at one habit you already have and describe its three parts: the cue that starts it, the routine itself, and the reward you get from it. Then say how strong the habit is, with one fact that shows it. You do not have to change the habit. The point is to practise seeing a habit clearly, because every tool in the rest of this course works on one of these parts.

Choose any habit that runs most days without a decision. It can be good, like making tea after a meal, or one you would like to weaken, like checking your phone. Small habits are fine and often easier to describe. If you prefer not to use your own life, use the provided case below. Using it costs you nothing in the grade.

Here is what a strong answer looks like, told about a fictional person. Nadia runs the library corner in a community centre and opens it at 9:00. Her habit is sorting the returns box. The cue is putting her bag under the desk after she unlocks the door. It happens in the same place, at the same time, right after the same action. The routine is emptying the returns box and sorting the books onto the trolley, which takes about ten minutes. The reward is a clear desk before the first visitor, and the small pleasure of seeing which books people have been reading. Nadia judges the habit as strong. Her evidence: one morning the box was empty because the centre had been closed the day before, and she opened it and looked inside anyway.

Look at the shape of Nadia's answer. Her cue is a situation you could point to, not a time of day or a mood. Her routine is an action you could film, with a length. Her reward comes right away and is described honestly, including a part that is only a small pleasure. And her judgement of strength rests on a fact: the routine started even when the reward was missing, which you learned in chapter 1.3 is a sign of a strong habit.

Compare it with a weak answer: "My habit is my phone. The cue is being bored. The routine is using my phone. The reward is fun." This answer has the right three words, but each one is vague. "Being bored" happens at many moments, so it does not say which situation starts the habit. "Using my phone" does not say what the hand actually does, or for how long. "Fun" could describe almost anything. And there is no judgement of strength and no fact. A mentor reading it would not learn anything about this person's habit that they could not have guessed.

If you prefer not to use your own life, use this provided case. Adam is 27 and drives his family's water delivery round, which starts at 7:30. His phone is his alarm, and it lies on the floor next to his mattress. When the alarm rings at 6:30, he switches it off and, still lying down, opens his messages. He reads the family group, two groups of friends, and any delivery requests that customers sent overnight. This takes 15 to 20 minutes, so he often rushes his breakfast. Most mornings there are two or three customer requests he really needs to see. On some mornings there is nothing new, and he reads old messages anyway. On Fridays there is no round and no alarm, and he still reaches for the phone the moment he wakes.

## How your mentor will read it

A mentor will read your answer with the table below. There is no single right answer: two people can describe the same habit in slightly different words, and both can be correct. What matters is that each part is specific and that your judgement rests on a fact.

Your mentor is not judging the habit itself. A habit you would like to weaken is as good a choice as one you are proud of, and nobody will think less of you for an honest answer about your phone. The mentor is checking one skill: whether you can see the parts of a habit clearly enough to work on them. If you use Adam's case, the mentor reads it in exactly the same way.

| Part | Met | Partly | Not yet |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cue | A situation you could point to: a place, a time, people or the action just before | Only a time of day or a feeling, with no situation | No cue, or "habit" or "laziness" as the cue |
| Routine | The exact action, with about how long it lasts | The action is named but vague, such as "my phone" | No routine described |
| Reward | What the person gets right away, in honest words | Only a far future goal, or one vague word such as "fun" | No reward named |
| Strength | How strong the habit is, with one fact that shows it | A judgement with no fact | No judgement of strength |

**Pass rule:** your answer passes when no part is "Not yet" and at least three parts are "Met".

The mentor then replies with one or two short questions. For example: "What happens on a morning when the reward is missing?" or "Which of the three parts would you change first if you wanted to weaken this habit?" You answer in writing or in a voice note. That reply is part of the gate, so look out for it after you submit.

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Rana, the primary school teacher, wants to start one new habit. Lesson planning matters now, because a school inspection is in 3 weeks; it can shrink to 3 lines, and it can follow the evening meal every day. Swimming matters for a holiday in 4 months, but the pool is 40 minutes away and open only on Fridays. Reading English news has no fixed moment in her day. Which habit should she start first?"
options:
  - "Lesson planning, after the evening meal each day"
  - "Swimming, since the holiday makes it matter most"
  - "English news, since it fits into any spare moment"
  - "All three at once, each kept as small as possible"
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. It matters now, it can be very small, and it has a daily anchor. Swimming and the news can wait their turn."
  - "The holiday is 4 months away, and the pool is open only on Fridays and is 40 minutes away. A weekly, distant habit is a weak first habit."
  - "Fitting 'any spare moment' means it has no anchor. A habit with no fixed moment depends on finding free time."
  - "Three new habits mean three decisions a day. Start one, and add the next when it starts on most days without a push."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Manar, the nursing student, studies for 30 minutes after the evening meal, using online lectures. In 5 of her last 6 blocks the evening internet was too weak to load the lecture, and she stopped after 10 minutes. The internet is usually good in the mornings. Her phone already stays in another room, her two sisters are quiet during the block, and she writes the next step each night. Which change fits these facts?"
options:
  - "Move her phone to another room during the block"
  - "Ask her sisters to stay out of the room for the whole block"
  - "Write tomorrow's first step at the end of each block"
  - "Download each lecture in the morning, when it loads well"
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "Her phone already stays in another room, so this changes nothing."
  - "Her sisters are already quiet during the block. They are not what stops her."
  - "She already writes the next step each night. The block fails later, when the lecture will not load."
  - "Right. The weak evening internet stopped 5 of her 6 blocks. Downloading in the morning, while the internet is good, means the evening block no longer needs a connection."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Jamal, who drives the water delivery van, sleeps from about 1:00 to 6:00 on work days and from 3:00 to 11:00 on Fridays. He often codes on his phone in bed until he falls asleep. Which plan fits the basic sleep guidance in this module?"
options:
  - "Keep his work-day hours and sleep much longer on Fridays to catch up"
  - "Bed at 23:00 and up at 6:00, every day, phone in the kitchen"
  - "Sleep whenever he feels tired, so his body can decide"
  - "Have strong coffee at 21:00, so he can code later at night"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "Five hours on work days is less than the seven most adults need, and very different times on Fridays are the opposite of a regular schedule."
  - "Right. 23:00 to 6:00 is seven hours, at the same times every day, and the phone in the kitchen keeps the screen away before bed."
  - "Health agencies advise a regular time for going to bed and getting up. 'Whenever I am tired' is not regular."
  - "The guidance is to avoid caffeine in the afternoon and evening. Coffee at 21:00 works against sleep."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Wael spends 10 minutes on his portfolio after his first coffee each morning. His card for the last 4 weeks shows 5, 6, 7 and 7 days done. In week 1 he wrote 'had to push myself' 4 times; in week 4 he wrote 'started without thinking' 5 times. He is thinking about adding a daily English habit. What does his card support?"
options:
  - "Wait two more months before adding anything at all"
  - "Never add a second habit, to keep the first one safe"
  - "Add the English habit now, at a different anchor"
  - "Add English and two more habits, since he is doing well"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "There is no fixed number of days to wait, and people differ widely. His own card is the test, and it shows the first habit already starting easily."
  - "One at a time does not mean one forever. When the first habit starts on most days without a push, the next can begin."
  - "Right. Crosses are rare and his notes say the start is easy, so his portfolio habit is running by itself. A different anchor keeps the two habits from competing."
  - "Three new habits at once is the same mistake Dina made. Add one, and wait for it to start easily before the next."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Karim, the bakery worker, plans to practise spreadsheets after his afternoon nap. He says he waits until he feels focused before he starts, and on 4 of the last 7 days that feeling never came. Which change matches this module?"
options:
  - "Wait for days when he has more energy, and study longer then"
  - "Keep his phone on the desk, so music can help him focus"
  - "Study only on Fridays, when he does not work at the bakery"
  - "A start signal after his nap: same chair, yesterday's note"
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "Waiting for the right feeling is what failed on 4 of 7 days. Focus usually comes a few minutes after starting, not before."
  - "A phone on the desk is within reach, and one message can take the whole block."
  - "Once a week is far fewer repetitions, and it still leaves the start to a feeling."
  - "Right. A start signal makes starting the habit, so he does not need to feel focused first. Yesterday's note means no deciding what to do."
```

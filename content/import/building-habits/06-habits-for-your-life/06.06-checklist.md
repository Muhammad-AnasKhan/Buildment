## Your weekly rhythm plan

This task asks you to put your habits where they can live: on the fixed points of a real week. A daily habit is easy to plan for an imaginary day with no guests, no lectures and no changes. Real weeks have lecture days and home days, visits, family meals and prayer times that move with the seasons. The point of the plan is to give each habit a home in that real week, so that it does not depend on finding free time, and to decide in advance what happens on the days that do not look normal.

Use your own course habit if you have been tracking one, or use Nada's week below. Either way, the plan should show the same thinking: fixed points first, habits second, one new habit at a time, and a plan for the days that change. The prompt at the end of this page is shorter than in earlier gates, on purpose: by now you know the parts of a good plan.

Here is what a strong plan looks like, told about a fictional person. Sana is a tailor's apprentice. She works at a tailor's shop from Saturday to Thursday, 9:00 to 17:00, and eats lunch at the shop at 13:00. Her habit, reading 5 pages of a sewing book after lunch, has run on most days for six weeks. She starts her plan with the fixed points of her week. She wakes at 7:00. The shop opens at 9:00, lunch is at 13:00, and she closes the shop at 17:00. At home, the family eats the evening meal after the sunset prayer, and she goes to bed at about 23:00. She marks the sunset prayer and the meal as the points that move through the year: about three hours later in summer than in winter, in this example. Everything at the shop stays at the same clock time.

Then she places her habits. The reading stays after lunch on work days. On Fridays, when she is at home, it follows the family's Friday lunch. Her one new habit is sketching one dress idea for 10 minutes at the cutting table, right after she closes the shop at 17:00. That point does not move with the seasons. She will start it next week, because her reading card has had only two crosses in the last three weeks and her notes say "started without thinking". Her smallest versions are one page of reading and one quick line in her sketchbook. For the busy wedding season, when lunch at the shop is short, she writes: "One page only, every day." And she protects one thing that is not work: "Friday afternoons with my sisters, phone in the drawer."

Look at the shape of Sana's plan. Every habit is tied to something that already happens on the days it is planned, and the days are named. She knows which of her points move with the seasons, and her new habit sits on one that does not. She adds one habit, with a reason and a start date that follow from her tracker. She has a smaller version ready for hard days and a plan for at least one changed season. And her plan makes room for family, not only for work.

Compare it with a weak plan: "Every day I will study English for 30 minutes, walk, sleep early, review my lectures and read, in my free time." That plan has no fixed points, so every habit depends on free time, and Dina's month showed what happens to free time. It starts five habits at once. It has no smaller versions and nothing for visitors or lecture days. It would feel ambitious on the first day and fall apart in the second week.

If you prefer not to use your own week, use this provided case. Nada is 22, studies accounting and lives with her family. Her habit since the end of module 2: "After I wash my breakfast cup, I open my English vocabulary notebook and write one sentence with a new word." Since the end of module 5, on mornings with no breakfast at home, her anchor is "after I fill my water bottle", at the tap next to the shelf where her notebook lives. Her smallest version is to read yesterday's sentence aloud. She has ticks on days 19 to 24. Here is her normal week.

| Day | Morning | Afternoon and evening |
|---|---|---|
| Sunday, Tuesday, Thursday | Breakfast at home, leaves at 7:45; lectures 8:30 to 12:30 | Home by about 13:15; family meal after the sunset prayer |
| Monday, Wednesday | Breakfast at home; studies at home | Helps her mother cook lunch; family meal after the sunset prayer |
| Friday | Breakfast at home | Family lunch at her grandmother's; relatives often visit |
| Saturday | Breakfast at home; washing day | Cousins often visit; family meal after the sunset prayer |

Nada prays, and the afternoon and sunset prayers are fixed points in her day that move through the year. She wants two more habits: 15 minutes reviewing each lecture on lecture days, before her exams in two months, and a regular bedtime, because she goes to bed anywhere between 23:00 and 1:00.

## How your mentor will read it

A mentor will read your plan with the table below. There is more than one good plan for any week, so your mentor checks the thinking, not whether your plan matches theirs.

| Part | Met | Partly | Not yet |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fixed points | Five or more fixed points of the week, with the ones that move through the year marked | Fixed points listed, but fewer than five, or none marked as moving | No fixed points; habits placed on free time |
| Habits on anchors | Every habit follows a fixed point that happens on the days it is planned, and the days are named | All habits but one have such an anchor; one depends on free time or lands badly in some season | Two or more habits with no anchor |
| One at a time | At most one new habit, with its anchor and what the tracker must show before it starts | One new habit with no sign for when to start it, or two at once with a reason | Two or more new habits at once with no reason |
| Changed days | A smallest version for each habit, and a plan for one changed day such as visitors or exam week | A smallest version, but no plan for a changed day | Nothing for days that change |

**Pass rule:** your plan passes when no part is "Not yet" and at least three parts are "Met".

Write your answer in plain English. Short sentences are fine. You may add a voice note link as an extra.

The mentor then replies with one or two short questions. For example: "Which of your anchors will move when summer comes, and does the habit still land in a good part of the day?" or "What does your tracker need to show before you add the next habit?" Answer them in writing; a voice note link can be added as an extra. That reply is part of the gate, so look out for it after you submit.

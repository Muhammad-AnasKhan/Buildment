## Your course habit

From this module on, you may run one small habit through the rest of the course: your course habit. You design it now, track it from today, and look back at it in the later gates and in the capstone. This is optional. If you prefer not to use your own life, or your month is likely to be too disrupted, you can follow Nada, a provided person, through the same steps instead, with no penalty. Her plan is described below.

The task is to design the habit and start tracking it. Write it as an if-then plan or an anchor plan, with one small first step. Make sure the first step is a smallest version that fits your hardest day. Say why your anchor is a good one, using the four questions from chapter 2.4. And say how you will track it: where you will mark each day, what a mark looks like, and what you will write when you miss a day. Keep the tracking simple. A tick or a cross and a few words is enough.

Here is what a strong answer looks like, told about a fictional person. Fadi works at a bakery and finishes his shift at about 13:00, sometimes later. His habit: "After I hang up my apron at the end of my shift, I open my English word list and read five words aloud." It takes about three minutes. His hardest day is a long Friday shift that ends at 15:00 with a flat phone, so his word list is printed and lives in his bag. His anchor happens at the end of every shift, early or late, in the same place, and it ends at a clear moment: the apron on its hook. He has a few minutes before he walks home. He tracks the habit on a small card in his bag, with a tick for done, a cross for missed, and a few words on any missed day, such as "shift ran to 16:00, forgot".

Look at what makes Fadi's answer strong. The moment is one he will surely meet, and it moves with his real shift, not with the clock. The first step is one action he could film, and it needs no power or internet. He checked his anchor against the four questions. And his tracking says where, how and what he writes on a miss, so his later reviews will have real notes to read.

Compare it with a weak answer: "I will study English every day when I can, and I will remember how it went." There is no moment, because "when I can" is never a single point in the day. There is no first step, because "study English" cannot be filmed. Nothing in it fits a hard day. And "I will remember" is not tracking: by next week, the misses and their reasons will be gone.

If you prefer not to use your own life, use Nada's plan. Nada is 22, studies accounting, and lives with her family. She wants to build her English vocabulary for her studies. Her habit: "After I wash my breakfast cup, I open my English vocabulary notebook and write one sentence with a new word." It takes about 3 minutes. She tracks it with a tick or a cross and a few words on a paper card that she keeps inside the notebook. Write about her plan as if you were her mentor: check the anchor against the four questions, check the first step against a hard day, check the tracking, and name one thing she should watch for in the first weeks.

## How your mentor will read it

A mentor will read your plan with the table below. There is no single right habit. A plan for drinking water, reading, studying code or calling a relative can pass as easily as one for English. What matters is that the plan has a real moment, a small first step, a reason for the anchor, and a way to track.

| Part | Met | Partly | Not yet |
|---|---|---|---|
| The moment | A moment that happens every day, at a clear point, in a named place | A moment that is real but vague, moves around, or is not daily | No moment, or only "when I have time" |
| The first step | One small action you could film, that fits the hardest day | One action, but too big for a hard day | A wish such as "study more", or several actions |
| The anchor's reasons | Checked against at least three of the four questions | Checked against one or two questions | No reasons given |
| Tracking | Where, how, and what is written on a missed day | How, but not where or what happens on a miss | No tracking, or "I will remember" |

**Pass rule:** your answer passes when no part is "Not yet" and at least three parts are "Met".

The mentor then replies with one or two short questions. For example: "What happens on a morning when your anchor does not happen?" or "Where will the card be when you need it?" You answer in writing or in a voice note. That reply is part of the gate, so look out for it after you submit. Then keep tracking: module 3 builds on the habit you design here.

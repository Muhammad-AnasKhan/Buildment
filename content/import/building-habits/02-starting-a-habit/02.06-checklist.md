## Module 2 checklist

- [ ] I can explain why motivation alone is not a reliable way to start a habit
- [ ] I can shrink a habit until it fits my hardest day, and I count that smallest version as done
- [ ] I can write an if-then plan with a moment I will surely meet and one first step I could film
- [ ] I can choose an anchor that happens every day, in the right place, ends at a clear moment and leaves a few free minutes, and add a second anchor for days when it does not happen
- [ ] I answered the questions in chapters 2.2 to 2.4, and finished the practice check in 2.5
- [ ] I submitted my course habit plan, or Nada's, below

## Learning log

1. **Plans that faded:** Think of a plan that faded within a week or two, or use Lina's Saturday plan. Was it built for a good day or for a hard day?
2. **Your hardest day:** Describe an ordinary hard day in one line: what is missing on that day (time, power, internet, quiet)? Or use Lina's Tuesday with no power.
3. **A day without the anchor:** Is there a kind of day when your anchor does not happen, such as a visit, a trip or a different shift? What could be your second anchor for it? Or answer for Nada's plan.
4. **Growing later:** How will you know when your habit is ready to grow a little, and what would the next small size be? Or answer for Nada's plan.

## Your course habit

From this module on, you may run one small habit through the rest of the course: your course habit. You design it now, track it from today, and look back at it in the later gates and in the capstone. This is optional. If you prefer not to use your own life, or your month is likely to be too disrupted, you can follow Nada, a provided person, through the same steps instead, with no penalty. Her plan is described below.

A plain word about time. At the course's pace, you will reach the capstone in module 7 with about two to three weeks of tracking, not a full 30 days. That is fine and expected. The capstone asks you to design a 30-day plan and run as much of it as you can while you take the course. Then you review the real days you have, or you review a provided month instead. You can keep the plan running after the course and, if you like, send the full 30-day review to your mentor later.

The task is to design the habit and start tracking it. Write it as an if-then plan or an anchor plan, with one small first step. Make sure the first step is a smallest version that fits your hardest day. Say why your anchor is a good one, using the four questions from chapter 2.4. And say how you will track it: where you will mark each day, what a mark looks like, and what you will write when you miss a day. Keep the tracking simple. A tick or a cross and a few words is enough.

Here is what a strong answer looks like, told about a fictional student of graphic design who lives with her grandparents. Her habit: "After I put the bread on the table for my grandparents' breakfast, I open my sketchbook and draw one simple shape." It takes about two minutes. Her hardest day is a lecture day that starts at 8:00, with no power in the morning and little sleep. A sketchbook and a pencil need no power, and two minutes fit even that morning. Breakfast with her grandparents happens every day, in the kitchen, and it starts at a clear moment: the bread on the table. She keeps the sketchbook on the kitchen shelf, and she has a few free minutes while her grandmother makes the tea. She tracks the habit on a card inside the sketchbook: a tick, a cross, and a few words on any missed day, such as "slept at my cousin's, no sketchbook". Because she stays at her cousin's about once a week, she adds a second anchor for those mornings: "after I fold the blanket".

Look at what makes her answer strong. The moment is one she will surely meet, and she has planned for the one kind of day when it does not happen. The first step is one action she could film, and it needs no power or internet. She checked her anchor against all four questions. And her tracking says where, how and what she writes on a miss, so her later reviews will have real notes to read.

Compare it with a weak answer: "I will study English every day when I can, and I will remember how it went." There is no moment, because "when I can" is never a single point in the day. There is no first step, because "study English" cannot be filmed. Nothing in it fits a hard day. And "I will remember" is not tracking: by next week, the misses and their reasons will be gone.

If you prefer not to use your own life, use Nada's plan. Nada is 22, studies accounting, and lives with her family. She wants to build her English vocabulary for her studies. Her habit: "After I wash my breakfast cup, I open my English vocabulary notebook and write one sentence with a new word." It takes about 3 minutes. She tracks it with a tick or a cross and a few words on a paper card that she keeps inside the notebook. Write about her plan as if you were her mentor. Check the anchor against the four questions, the first step against a hard day, and the tracking. Then name one thing she should watch for in the first weeks.

## How your mentor will read it

A mentor will read your plan with the table below. There is no single right habit. A plan for drinking water, reading, studying code or calling a relative can pass as easily as one for English. What matters is that the plan has a real moment, a small first step, a reason for the anchor, and a way to track.

| Part | Met | Partly | Not yet |
|---|---|---|---|
| The moment | A moment that happens every day, at a clear point, in a named place | A moment that is real but vague, moves around, or is not daily | No moment, or only "when I have time" |
| The first step | One small action you could film, that fits the hardest day (described in one line) | One action, but too big for a hard day | A wish such as "study more", or several actions |
| The anchor's reasons | Checked against at least three of the four questions | Checked against one or two questions | No reasons given |
| Tracking | All three: where, how, and what is written on a missed day | One or two of the three | No tracking, or "I will remember" |

**Pass rule:** your answer passes when no part is "Not yet" and at least three of the four parts are "Met".

The mentor then replies with one or two short questions. For example: "What happens on a morning when your anchor does not happen?" or "Where will the card be when you need it?" Write your answer in plain English. Short sentences are fine. You may add a voice note link as an extra. Your reply to the mentor is part of the gate, so look out for it after you submit. Then keep tracking: module 3 builds on the habit you design here.

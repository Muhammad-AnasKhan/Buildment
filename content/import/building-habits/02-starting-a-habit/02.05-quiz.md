```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Tareq runs a small tailor's shop and wants to learn pattern drawing. He tried a different plan each week, over 6 working days. Week 1, 'study 45 minutes when the shop is quiet': done on 1 day. Week 2, a phone alarm at 20:00 for one video lesson: done on 3 days, and all 3 misses were evenings with weak internet. Week 3, 'after I lock the shop, I draw one practice line from my printed sheet': done on 6 days. What do the facts support?"
options:
  - "Keep week 3's plan, and grow it only once it feels easy"
  - "Go back to 45 minutes, now he has shown he can keep a plan"
  - "Keep week 2's plan, since the alarm helped him remember to study"
  - "Run all three plans together, to practise as much as he can"
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. Week 3's plan has a daily moment (locking the shop), one small first step, and a printed sheet that needs no internet. It happened on all 6 days. Grow it slowly, once it is easy."
  - "Week 1's plan happened on 1 day out of 6. 'When the shop is quiet' is not a moment, and 45 minutes is too big for his busy days."
  - "Week 2's misses all came on evenings with weak internet. The alarm reminded him, but a video lesson cannot load without a connection."
  - "Two of the three plans already failed on their own. Running them together adds more weight, and too much at once is how big plans fade."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Reem coaches a girls' team at a sports club and has a coaching certificate test in 4 months. She asks an AI chat tool how to build a study habit. It replies: 'Start with a step so small it fits your busiest day, such as reading one paragraph of your coaching book. Tie it to a moment you already have, like locking the equipment room after each session. Do it for 21 days and it will be automatic, so you can stop tracking after that.' Which part should Reem not trust?"
options:
  - "Starting with one paragraph, which is too small to make any difference"
  - "Locking the equipment room, which is a weak moment to choose"
  - "The promise that the habit will be automatic after 21 days"
  - "Tying the step to a moment, instead of waiting until she feels ready"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "A step small enough for her busiest day is exactly what helps the habit repeat. One paragraph a day can grow later."
  - "Locking the room happens at the end of every session, in the same place, at a clear moment. That is a strong anchor for a session-day habit."
  - "Right. Habits often take about two months or longer, and people differ a lot. The 21-day idea came from stories about surgery patients, not habit studies, so stopping her tracking at day 21 would be too early."
  - "Waiting to feel ready puts the habit behind a decision every day. A clear moment is what lets the situation start it."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Bilal runs a vegetable stall. He shrank his spreadsheet practice to 'one exercise on the laptop after I close the stall'. In 2 weeks he did it on 9 of 12 working days, and on 7 of those 9 he did more than one exercise. His notes on the 3 misses: 'closed late, family meal waiting' twice, and 'no power, laptop flat' once. He opens the stall at 6:30 and customers come at once. What change do the facts support?"
options:
  - "Grow it to three exercises a day, since he often does more"
  - "Keep it, and add a smaller version for the hard evenings"
  - "Drop it, since 3 misses in 2 weeks show the plan has failed"
  - "Move it to the morning, when he is fresh and not yet tired"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "He still misses on late and no-power evenings. Grow a habit after it is easy every day, not while some days still defeat it."
  - "Right. 9 of 12 is a strong start, and every miss came from a hard evening: late, or no power. A smaller version that needs no laptop, such as one printed exercise, would fit those evenings too."
  - "9 done days out of 12 is a habit that is working. The misses show where it needs help, not that it has failed."
  - "His mornings have no free minutes: customers come as soon as he opens at 6:30. The evening anchor is fine on most days."
```

```quiz
question: "Adam drives his family's water delivery round from Saturday to Thursday and lifts about 60 heavy water bottles a day. He wrote this plan: 'If my back hurts after work, then I will stretch and go for a walk.' Which rewrite fixes both of the plan's weak points?"
options:
  - "If my back hurts after work, then I will stretch for longer"
  - "After I park the truck, I will stretch and then take a walk"
  - "If my back hurts, then I will do one stretch before walking"
  - "After I park the truck, I will do two stretches beside it"
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "This drops the walk, but it keeps a feeling as the moment. Back pain comes at different times, so it is not a moment he will surely meet."
  - "Parking the truck is a good moment, but the plan still joins two actions. On a tired evening the walk makes the whole plan heavy."
  - "This keeps both weak points: a feeling as the moment, and two actions."
  - "Right. Parking the truck is a moment he meets at the end of every round, and two stretches beside it is one small first step he could film."
```

For four weeks she has read two pages of an English novel right after sorting the returns box, on 23 of 24 working days. It now starts without any thought, and she often reads past two pages. She wants to grow the habit. What fits best?"
```quiz
options:
  - "Grow a little: three pages, after the same returns box"
  - "Jump to a full chapter a day, plus a list of new words"
  - "Move it to a new anchor, so the habit stays fresh and fun"
  - "Count only days when she reads past two pages as done"
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. The habit is easy and starts by itself, so it is time to grow it, but by a little, and with the anchor that already works."
  - "A big jump turns a working habit back into a big plan. Grow by a small step, and only grow again once the new size is easy."
  - "The returns box is a strong anchor: every working day, same place, clear end. Changing it would throw away the cue she has built."
  - "This makes the target bigger without saying so. Two pages must still count as done, so a busy day is a success, not a failure."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Tareq, a tailor, tried three plans for pattern drawing, one per 6-day week. Plan 1, 'study 45 minutes when the shop is quiet': done on 1 day. Plan 2, a 20:00 alarm for a video lesson: 3 days, every miss on a weak-internet evening. Plan 3, 'after I lock the shop, I draw one line from a printed sheet': 6 days. What do the facts support?"
options:
  - "Keep plan 3, and grow it slowly once it feels easy"
  - "Go back to 45 minutes, now that he has kept a plan for a week"
  - "Keep plan 2, since the alarm helped him remember"
  - "Run all three plans together, to practise as much as he can"
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "This is the one. Plan 3 has a daily moment (locking the shop), one small first step, and a printed sheet that needs no internet. It happened on all 6 days."
  - "Plan 1 happened on 1 day out of 6. 'When the shop is quiet' is not a moment, and 45 minutes is too big for his busy days."
  - "Plan 2's misses all came on evenings with weak internet. The alarm reminded him, but a video lesson cannot load without a connection."
  - "Two of the three plans already failed on their own. Running them together adds more weight, and too much at once is how big plans fade."
```

type: mcq
question: "Reem, a sports club coach, asks an AI chat tool how to start studying for her coaching certificate. It replies: 'Read one paragraph of your coaching book after you lock the equipment room each session, and tick a card. But wait until next month to start, when you will feel fresh and motivated.' Which part should she not follow?"
options:
  - "Reading only one paragraph, which is too little to help her test"
  - "Locking the equipment room as the moment that starts reading"
  - "Ticking a card, which turns a small habit into extra work"
  - "Waiting until next month, when she expects to feel more motivated"
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "One paragraph is a smallest version: small enough for her busiest day, so it can repeat. It can grow later."
  - "Locking the room happens at the end of every session, in the same place, at a clear moment. That is a strong anchor for a session-day habit."
  - "A tick takes a second, and it is a small reward right after the step. It also gives her a record to look back on."
  - "That is the mistake. Motivation rises and falls, so waiting for it puts the habit behind a feeling, and the wait itself is a long gap before the first step. The rest of the answer fits what this module taught."

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Bilal runs a vegetable stall. After closing, he does one spreadsheet exercise on the laptop. In 2 weeks he did it on 9 of 12 days, often more than one exercise. Misses: 'closed late, family meal waiting' twice, 'no power, laptop flat' once. Customers come as soon as he opens at 6:30. What change fits?"
options:
  - "Grow it to three exercises a day, since he often does more"
  - "Keep it, and add a smaller version for the hard evenings"
  - "Drop it, since 3 misses in 2 weeks show the plan has failed"
  - "Move it to the morning, when he is fresh and not yet tired"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "He still misses on late and no-power evenings. Grow a habit after it is easy every day, not while some days still defeat it."
  - "This is the one. 9 of 12 is a strong start, and every miss came from a hard evening: late, or no power. A smaller version that needs no laptop, such as one printed exercise, would fit those evenings too."
  - "9 done days out of 12 is a habit that is working. The misses show where it needs help."
  - "His mornings have no free minutes: customers come as soon as he opens at 6:30. The evening anchor works on most days."
```

  - "Correct. One page is her smallest version for Thursdays, so each of those days counts as done. Marking them as crosses hides a habit that ran on all 24 days."
  - "Her card shows a habit that ran every working day. Throwing it away would hide real progress, and a fresh start is not needed."

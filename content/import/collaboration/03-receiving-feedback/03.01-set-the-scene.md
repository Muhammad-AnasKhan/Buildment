## A message at 11 at night

Adam is a junior developer on a remote team. His team lead, Daniel, works from Berlin. Adam spent two days on a new feature. The power was off for most of the second day, so he finished the last part late at night, using mobile internet from his phone.

At 11 at night, a review comment arrives:

> This function is too long and hard to follow. Please split it before we merge.

Adam feels his face get hot. He thinks: "He has no idea how hard it was to finish this today." He starts typing a reply.

## Two ways the conversation can go

**Version A.** Adam sends his reply straight away:

> It works and all the tests pass. I didn't have time to make it pretty. Not everyone has stable power.

Daniel reads it the next morning. He did not know about the power cut. Now he feels attacked for a normal review comment, and his answer is short and cold. The conversation is now about the relationship, not the code. Next time, Daniel may soften his comments or stop giving them. Adam loses a good teacher.

**Version B.** Adam notices how upset he is. He saves his draft and goes to sleep. In the morning he writes:

> Thanks, Daniel. Do you mean split it by step (validate, save, notify), or something else? I'll update it today. Also, the power was off most of yesterday, so I rushed the last part.

Daniel answers in five minutes with a clear example. The fix takes an hour. Daniel now knows about the power situation, and he offers to plan a backup for the next deadline.

Same comment. Same feelings. A different first reaction, and a very different result.

## Your feelings are normal

If criticism hurts, nothing is wrong with you. Most people feel heat, shame or anger when someone criticizes their work, especially work they cared about or finished in hard conditions.

The goal of this module is not to stop feeling. It is to notice the feeling, give it a moment, and then choose what you say. The first reaction is often automatic. The first reply is your choice.

## Key words

| Word | Plain English |
|---|---|
| **Feedback** | Information about how your work or behavior affects others |
| **Criticism** | Feedback about what is wrong or weak |
| **Defensive** | Protecting yourself instead of listening |
| **Clarifying question** | A question that helps you understand exactly what someone means |
| **Follow up** | Going back later to show what you did |

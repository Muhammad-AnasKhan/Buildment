## One question, one lost day

Nadia is a developer in Gaza. Her team lead, Ethan, is in Toronto, which is usually seven hours behind Gaza.

At 11:00 Gaza time, Nadia has a question about the payment page. It is 04:00 in Toronto. She writes: "Hi Ethan, are you there?" and waits.

Ethan starts work at 09:00 Toronto time, which is 16:00 in Gaza. He replies: "Hi! Yes, what's up?" But the power in Nadia's area went off at 15:00. When it comes back at 20:00, she sends her real question. Ethan is in meetings all afternoon. His answer arrives at midnight Gaza time, and Nadia reads it the next morning.

One simple question cost a full working day. Nobody was lazy or rude. The message was written for a live chat, but the team works async.

## Habit 1: write messages that stand alone

In an async team, every message should make sense without a conversation around it. Put the question in the first message. Add the context, what you already tried, and what you will do while you wait. That last part is a **default**: the plan you follow if nobody answers in time.

> Hi Ethan, a question on the payment page. The client's design shows prices with tax, but the API sends prices without tax. Should I add tax in the frontend, or will the API change? I tried the API docs and found nothing. Until I hear from you, I will build it with prices without tax, and change it later if needed.

Ethan can answer this with one line. And Nadia keeps working while she waits.

## Habit 2: use your overlap hours well

**Overlap hours** are the hours when both people are working at the same time. For Nadia and Ethan they look like this:

| Gaza time | Toronto time | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 10:00 | 03:00 | Nadia starts, Ethan is asleep |
| 16:00 | 09:00 | Ethan starts: overlap begins |
| 19:00 | 12:00 | Nadia logs off: overlap ends |
| 01:00 | 18:00 | Ethan logs off |

Three hours of overlap is precious. Use it for things that are hard in writing: a decision with many people, a problem that is blocking you, or a difficult conversation. Use async messages for status updates and simple questions.

If your overlap hours change because of study, power or a family need, tell the team early. Put your usual hours in the team agreement from the last chapter.

## Habit 3: hand off cleanly

A **handoff** is when you pass your work, or its current state, to someone else. It happens at the end of your day, before you go offline, or when someone continues your task. A good handoff answers four questions:

1. **Done:** what is finished?
2. **Where:** where is the work saved?
3. **Next:** what is the next step?
4. **Blocked:** what is stuck, and what do you need?

```text
End of day, Wed:
Done: booking form saves to the database.
Where: pushed to branch booking-form.
Next: send the confirmation email.
Blocked: email fails on staging. I think the mail
settings are missing. Ethan, can you check them?
Back online: tomorrow 10:00 Gaza time.
```

<Callout type="warning" title="When power or internet may go">
If you know a power cut is coming, or your connection is weak, save and push your work often, and post a short handoff before you lose it. Say when you expect to be back. This is not an excuse. It is what a dependable teammate does, and remote teams value it highly.
</Callout>

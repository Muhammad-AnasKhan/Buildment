## The task everyone thought someone else had

Amal, David and Salma build websites for small businesses. Amal works on the frontend from Gaza, David works on the backend from Lisbon, and Salma is the designer. On a call, a client asks for a "forgot password" feature. Everyone says, "Sounds good, let's do it."

A week later the client asks where the feature is. Nobody has started.

- Amal thought David would build it, because it needs backend work.
- David was waiting for Salma's design.
- Salma thought the feature was planned for next month.

Each person acted sensibly from their own point of view. The problem was not effort or skill. The team never agreed on a few simple things.

## Three questions every task needs

Before a team starts any piece of work, it should be able to answer three questions.

**1. Who owns it?** Give every task exactly one owner. The owner does not have to do all the work alone. The owner makes sure the work gets done and is the person others ask about it. When two people share a task, each one can believe the other is doing it.

**2. What does done mean, and by when?** "Done" can mean "code written", "tested", or "the client has seen it and agreed". Say which one. Give a date and a time, and name the time zone, because 18:00 on Thursday in Gaza is usually 11:00 on Thursday in Toronto.

**3. How will we communicate?** Agree where updates go, how fast people should reply, and what to do when someone is stuck. Without this, one person sends an urgent message by email while another only reads the team chat.

| Vague | Clear |
|---|---|
| "Someone should look at the bug" | "Amal will fix the login bug by Wednesday 18:00 Gaza time" |
| "Let's finish it soon" | "Done means tested on staging and seen by the client" |
| "Ping me if you need anything" | "Questions go in the project channel. We reply within one working day" |

**Staging** means a test copy of the app, where the team checks work before real users see it. **Ping** means to send someone a short message.

## Write it down: a team agreement

A **team agreement** (also called a working agreement) is a short, shared note that answers the three questions for the whole team. It does not need to be formal. It needs to be written, so new people can read it and nobody has to remember a call.

Here is what Amal's team wrote after the missed week:

```text
Team agreement: client websites
- Every task in the tracker has one owner and a due date
- Due dates use Gaza time, and we write the zone
- "Done" = tested on staging and seen by the client
- Everyone posts a short update in #websites before logging off
- Normal questions: reply within one working day
- Urgent: mention the person by name in the channel
- Stuck for more than half a day? Say so in the channel
```

<Callout type="tip" title="Repeat it back">
When someone gives you a task in a call, send one line afterwards: "To confirm: I will build the reset email by Thursday 17:00 Gaza time, and David will review it." It takes a minute, and it catches misunderstandings while they are still easy to fix.
</Callout>

## Expectations go both ways

Roles are not only about tasks. Your teammates also have expectations about you: when you are online, how quickly you answer, and whether you warn them about problems. If you study in the mornings, or your power usually comes back in the evening, say so in the agreement. A clear limit that you keep builds more trust than a promise to always be available that you cannot keep.

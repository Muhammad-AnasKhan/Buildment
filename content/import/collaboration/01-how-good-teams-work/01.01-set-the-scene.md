## Two teams, one project

Two small teams are building the same kind of app: a booking system for a clinic run by an NGO (a non-profit organization).

In the first team, Faris, Maria and Dina are all skilled. But nobody is sure who is building the login page, so two people build it and nobody builds the payment page. Faris finds a bug in his own code but stays quiet, because last week someone laughed at his question. When the client asks for an update, three people give three different answers.

In the second team, Hala, Kenji and Rami have less experience. Every task has one name next to it. Every evening each person posts a short update. When Rami loses his internet connection for two days, he posts a note before he goes offline, and Hala picks up his task. When Kenji makes a mistake, he says so in the team chat, and the team fixes it in an hour.

Which team is more likely to deliver first? Almost always, the second one.

## Talent is not enough

Google studied many of its own teams in a research project called Project Aristotle. The researchers found that **how** people worked together mattered more than **who** was on the team. The strongest factor was psychological safety. Other important factors were **dependability** (people do what they say, on time) and **structure and clarity** (everyone knows their role, the plan and the goals).

This is good news. You cannot quickly change how talented your teammates are. But you can change how your team works, and you can start with your own habits.

## Why this matters for your career

When you work remotely, your teammates rarely see you work. They see your messages, your updates, and how you react when something goes wrong. A developer who is easy to work with gets invited back, gets more responsibility, and gets recommended to other clients. A developer who is skilled but hard to work with often does not.

Collaboration (working together toward a shared goal) is a skill, like writing code. You can practice it, notice your mistakes, and get better.

## Key words

| Word | Plain English |
|---|---|
| **Team** | A group of people working toward the same goal |
| **Role** | The part of the work one person is responsible for |
| **Expectation** | What people believe will happen, often without saying it |
| **Feedback** | Telling someone how their work or behavior affects you or the goal |
| **Conflict** | A disagreement that has started to hurt the work or the relationship |
| **Negotiation** | Talking together to reach an agreement both sides can accept |

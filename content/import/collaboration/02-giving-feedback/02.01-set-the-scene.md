## Two messages that did not help

Bassel is a junior developer on a remote team. The team is spread across Gaza, Berlin and Toronto. After his first big pull request, his team lead writes one line in the chat:

> Good job, but the code could be cleaner.

Bassel reads it five times. Which part was good? What does "cleaner" mean? Is his lead unhappy with him? He spends the evening rewriting code that was probably fine, and he still does not know what to fix.

In the same week, Hana volunteers for an NGO. She is building a small website with two other volunteers. One of them, Karim, keeps sharing design files a day after the agreed date. Hana is annoyed, but she says nothing. She does not want to sound rude. Two weeks later she is doing Karim's part herself, and she is quietly angry with him. Karim has no idea anything is wrong.

## Vague feedback leaves people guessing

"Good job" feels kind, and "this needs work" feels honest. But both are **vague**: they do not say what happened or what to do next. The person on the other side fills the gap with guesses, and the guesses are often wrong or worse than the truth.

Vague praise has a cost too. If you only say "great work", the person does not know which part to repeat.

## Silence is also feedback

Hana thought she was giving no feedback. She was giving some. When you say nothing, most people take it to mean "everything is fine". Karim kept doing the same thing because nobody told him it caused a problem.

Silence also hurts the relationship. Small problems that nobody names grow into frustration. By the time someone finally speaks, they are often angry, and the feedback comes out harsh.

## Key words

| Word | Plain English |
|---|---|
| **Feedback** | Telling someone how their work or behavior affected others |
| **Vague** | Not clear or exact, so the reader has to guess |
| **Specific** | Clear and exact: it names what happened |
| **Behavior** | What a person did or said, which others could see or hear |
| **Peer review** | Checking the work of someone at your own level, such as a teammate |

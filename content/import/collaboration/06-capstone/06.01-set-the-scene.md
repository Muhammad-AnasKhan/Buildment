## Reading is not enough

In five modules you read about feedback, criticism, disagreement and negotiation. You chose good answers in quizzes. But a real conversation does not give you four options. The other person interrupts, gets upset or says something you did not expect. You have a few seconds to reply.

That is why this course ends with practice out loud. In the capstone you take part in a short role-play with two other people, or with a mentor. Then you write a reflection about what you did and what you would change. A mentor reads it and replies.

## A practice session

Huda, Tamer and Ana are fellows in the same cohort. A cohort is a group of learners who go through a program together. Huda lives in Gaza City and Tamer in Khan Younis. Ana joins from Lisbon, where she volunteers with an NGO. They agree a one-hour video call on a Saturday morning, when Huda and Tamer usually have power.

In the first round, Huda plays a developer who must give feedback to a teammate. Tamer plays the teammate. Ana is the observer: she watches, takes notes and stays silent until the end. After ten minutes the timer rings, and Ana shares what she saw. Then they swap roles and run the next script.

Afterward, Huda wrote: "I knew the situation, behavior, impact pattern. But when Tamer said 'Nobody told me that', I forgot it for a moment. Practicing it once with friends was much easier than doing it for the first time with a real client."

## Your toolbox

You already have the tools. Here is where each one came from.

| Module | Tools you can use in the role-play |
|---|---|
| 1 | Clear roles, written updates, handoffs across time zones |
| 2 | Situation, behavior, impact; must-change and optional points in a review |
| 3 | Noticing defensiveness, pausing, a clarifying question, following up |
| 4 | Disagreeing with the idea, not the person; empathy; steps to resolve a conflict |
| 5 | Interests behind positions; trading scope, time and cost |

You will not use all of them in ten minutes. Pick the two or three that fit your role.

## How the session works

The full scripts are in chapter 6.2. Here is the shape:

- **Three people, three roles.** Two people talk. The third person is the observer.
- **Two scripts.** Script A is a feedback conversation. Script B is a conflict about scope and a deadline.
- **Ten minutes per conversation.** A timer keeps each round short and fair.
- **A debrief after each round.** A debrief is a short talk after an activity about what happened and what you learned. The observer speaks first.
- **Swap roles.** In three rounds, everyone plays each role once.

Cannot find two other fellows? A mentor can play the other role and give you the observer's feedback at the end. Ask your mentor early, because their time is limited too.

<Callout type="info" title="Plan around power and internet">
Agree the time in writing, in everyone's time zone. Share the scripts before the call, so nobody needs to load them during it. If video is weak, use audio only. If someone drops, pause the timer and continue when they are back.
</Callout>

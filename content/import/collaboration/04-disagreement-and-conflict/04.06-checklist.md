## Module 4 checklist

- [ ] I can name two signs that a disagreement has become a conflict between people
- [ ] I can rewrite a sentence aimed at a person so it is aimed at the idea
- [ ] I can list the three tools for disagreeing: a question, evidence and a proposal
- [ ] I can list the five steps for moving a conflict toward an agreement
- [ ] I can name two situations where I should involve a team lead or mentor
- [ ] I completed the quiz in chapter 4.5 and submitted the plan at the bottom of this page

## Learning log

1. **Your disagreement:** Go back to the disagreement you described in chapter 4.1. Looking at it now, at what moment did it become personal, or what kept it from becoming personal?
2. **Your habit:** When you disagree, do you usually stay quiet, push hard, or ask questions? What is one thing you want to do differently?
3. **The other side:** Think of someone you often disagree with. What pressure or experience might explain their view?
4. **Asking for help:** Who could you go to for help with a conflict at work or in your studies: a team lead, a mentor, a teacher? What would you want from them?

## Your disagreement plan

Choose a real disagreement of your own, from work, study, a project or volunteering. If you do not have one you want to use, use this practice scenario:

> You and Duaa are building a mobile app for an NGO that supports small farmers. The launch for field workers is in two weeks. Duaa wants to add a new reporting feature the NGO director mentioned in a call. You think the launch date matters more, because field workers have been waiting for months. The discussion in your team chat has become tense, and Duaa wrote: "You never want to do anything extra." The NGO's project manager, Hassan, has not said which matters more.

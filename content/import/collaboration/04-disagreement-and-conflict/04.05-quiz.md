Six short work scenarios. Each one has one most constructive answer. Read the explanation after every question, even when you get it right: the reasons matter more than the score.

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Which of these is a sign that a disagreement has turned into a conflict between people?"
options:
  - "Two teammates argue for twenty minutes in a call about which database to use"
  - "After the decision, one teammate tells others privately that 'she never listens'"
  - "One teammate asks the other for test results before agreeing to the new plan"
  - "A reviewer comments that a function is too long and suggests how to split it"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "A long debate about the work is still a disagreement about ideas. It can be healthy if it ends in a decision."
  - "Right. It continues after the decision, in private, and it labels the person. That is conflict between people."
  - "Asking for evidence is a constructive way to disagree. It keeps the focus on the plan."
  - "This is feedback on the work, with a proposal. It is aimed at the code, not the person."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "In a team call, Elena, a senior architect, proposes a new caching approach. Tamer, a junior developer, tested a similar approach last week and it was slower on phones. What is the most constructive thing for Tamer to do?"
options:
  - "Stay quiet, because Elena has many more years of experience than he has"
  - "Say 'That will not work' and then explain the test he ran last week"
  - "Agree in the call, then tell the other juniors afterwards that the plan is wrong"
  - "Ask if he can share a quick test he ran, and suggest comparing both for a week"
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "His test is useful evidence. Staying quiet means the team may make a choice he knows is risky."
  - "The evidence is good, but opening with a verdict makes Elena defend her idea. There is also no way forward."
  - "This moves the disagreement out of the meeting and into private talk, where it can turn into conflict."
  - "Right. A question, evidence and a proposal. It invites Elena to check the result instead of admitting she was wrong."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "A client says the website you designed for their small shop is 'too plain'. You think the clean design works well for their customers. What is the best first response?"
options:
  - "Ask what 'too plain' means to them, and which sites they like"
  - "Explain why clean designs load faster and are easier to use on phones"
  - "Add more colors and images right away, because the client is paying"
  - "Tell them that plain designs are the modern style that designers prefer"
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. You do not yet know what they need. 'Too plain' might mean missing photos, or a brand color, or something else."
  - "These may be good reasons, but you are defending before you understand. The client may feel unheard."
  - "This gives up your judgment without knowing what they want. You may change the wrong things."
  - "This tells the client their view is out of date. It does not find out what is bothering them."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "You proposed switching to a different payment provider. The team listened to your reasons, discussed it, and the team lead decided to keep the current one. What should you do now?"
options:
  - "Keep raising your proposal in every meeting until the team changes its mind"
  - "Build the integration slowly, so the team sees that your idea was better"
  - "Support the decision fully, and note the risk you saw in case it appears later"
  - "Quietly build a test version with your provider, so you can show it works"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "You were heard and a decision was made. Reopening it every meeting wastes time and damages trust."
  - "Working slowly on purpose hurts the team and the client. It is a way of secretly working against the decision."
  - "Right. This is disagree and commit: you shared your view, and now you help the decision succeed."
  - "Working around a team decision in secret breaks trust, even if your idea was good."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "At 10:00 you disagreed with Fatima's plan in a team thread. It is now evening and she has not replied, although she is usually quick. Her area has had long power cuts this week. What is the best next step?"
options:
  - "Post again in the thread and ask why she is ignoring your point"
  - "Ask the team lead to decide, since Fatima is not willing to discuss it"
  - "Decide that she is upset, and send a long apology for disagreeing"
  - "Assume a reasonable reason, and check in tomorrow with a short message"
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "'Ignoring' is a guess, and a public one. It turns a quiet thread into a conflict."
  - "One evening of silence is not a refusal to discuss. Escalating now is far too early."
  - "This is also a guess. You did nothing wrong by disagreeing respectfully, so there is nothing to apologize for."
  - "Right. A power cut is a likely reason. A short, friendly check-in keeps the discussion open without blame."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "You and Ali disagree about which feature to build first. You have discussed it twice, you both have good reasons, and only the team lead sets priorities. The work starts tomorrow. What is the best move?"
options:
  - "Each of you starts on your own choice, and the results will show who was right"
  - "Agree with Ali to bring both options to the team lead and ask for a decision"
  - "Pick the feature you prefer, because you have spent more time on this project"
  - "Message the team lead privately to say that Ali is being difficult about this"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "This splits the team's time and turns the disagreement into a contest. The client gets half of two features."
  - "Right. The decision belongs to the lead, and going together keeps it fair and keeps the relationship safe."
  - "Priorities are not yours to set here, and time spent on a project does not decide what comes first."
  - "This asks the lead to judge Ali, not the options. It is a label, and Ali will see it as going behind his back."
```

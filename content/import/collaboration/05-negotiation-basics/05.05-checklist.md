This is the gate for module 5. Here you prepare for a negotiation of your own. Tick each box only when it is true. Your answers save as you type.

## Module 5 checklist

- [ ] I can explain the difference between a position and an interest, with one example from my own work or study
- [ ] I can name the three parts that move together in a trade: scope, time and cost
- [ ] I chose a real or practice negotiation and wrote my preparation plan below
- [ ] My plan has at least two options that could serve both sides, and my walk-away option or limit
- [ ] I answered the questions in chapters 5.2 to 5.4

## Learning log

1. **Your negotiation:** Go back to the negotiation you described in chapter 5.1. What were the positions? What interests might have been behind them, on both sides?
2. **A better option:** Looking back, was there an option that could have served both of you better? What was it?
3. **Giving and getting:** Do you more often give something without asking for anything back, or hold on to your position too long? Why do you think that is?
4. **Your hard part:** Asking about interests, talking about money, or saying no to a deal: which is hardest for you, and what will you try next time?

## Your preparation plan

Choose a negotiation you will really have soon, such as asking for more time, agreeing on scope with a client, or setting a rate for a new project. If nothing is coming up, choose a practice one from this module.

```openquestion
prompt: "Write your preparation plan. Include: 1. the situation and who you will talk to; 2. what you will ask for; 3. your interests; 4. their likely interests, and one open question to check them; 5. two or three options that could serve both sides; 6. your walk-away option or limit; 7. the first two or three sentences you will say or write."
required: true
minWords: 120
allowSpeechInput: true
modelAnswer: "1. Situation: a café owner in Germany wants a menu and ordering website. We have a call on Sunday to agree the price and the date. 2. I will ask for 700 US dollars and four weeks. 3. My interests: a fair price for about 35 hours of work, time to test properly, and a good review for my profile. I can work about six hours a day because of power cuts. 4. His likely interests: a site ready before the summer season, a cost that fits his budget, and staff who can change the menu on their own. Question: 'What would make this site a success for you this summer?' 5. Options: a) full site in four weeks for 700 dollars; b) menu pages first in two weeks, online ordering two weeks later, same price; c) menu pages only for 400 dollars, ordering added later. 6. Walk-away: I will not go below 20 dollars an hour. If there is no deal, I will use the time to finish my portfolio project. 7. Opening: 'Thanks for making time today. Before we talk about price, can you tell me what you most need the site to do by the summer? Then I will share some options.'"
```

If you can, read your opening sentences aloud to a friend, a teammate or your mentor. Ask them to reply as the other person would, and notice where you feel unsure.

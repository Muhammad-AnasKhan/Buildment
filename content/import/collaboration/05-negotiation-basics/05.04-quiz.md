Six short work scenarios about negotiation. Each one has one best answer. Read the explanation for every option after you answer, even when you get it right: the reasons matter more than the score.

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "A school principal asks you to build a website. In the first call, she says four things. Which one is an interest, not a position?"
options:
  - "The whole site has to be live before the first of March."
  - "We want it built with WordPress and no other system."
  - "Our teachers need to post news without asking a developer."
  - "The total price for everything must stay under 500 US dollars."
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "This is a position: a fixed date. The interest behind it might be the start of a new term, which you could ask about."
  - "This is a position: a fixed tool. It may come from the interest in the correct option, but other tools could serve that need too."
  - "Right. This is the need behind the request. Any option that lets teachers post news on their own serves it."
  - "This is a position: a fixed number. The interest behind it might be a school budget set for the year."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Rasha wants to release a new feature on Friday. Tom, the tester, refuses until every test passes. They have argued for two days. As their teammate, what is the most useful next step?"
options:
  - "Ask each one what they need from the release, then list options together"
  - "Suggest they meet in the middle and release half of the feature on Friday"
  - "Ask the team lead to decide, since the two of them clearly cannot agree"
  - "Support Rasha, because a missed Friday release looks bad to clients"
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. They are stuck on positions. Their interests, such as a promise to a client and fear of bugs, may fit together in an option nobody has seen yet."
  - "Meeting in the middle without knowing the interests may serve neither of them. Half a feature may still be untested, or still useless to the client."
  - "Asking for help is right if they still cannot agree. But nobody has looked at the interests yet, so this hands over a problem that may be easy to solve."
  - "This picks a side on a position. Tom's worry about bugs is also about clients, and it is ignored here."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "A client tells Manar, a freelance developer: 'Another freelancer offered to build this for half your price.' Manar knows her price is fair for the work. What is the best reply?"
options:
  - "Offer to match the other price, so that she does not lose the client"
  - "Warn the client that cheap freelancers usually deliver poor quality work"
  - "Lower her price by a quarter, so that they meet somewhere in the middle"
  - "Explain what her price includes, and ask what matters most to them"
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "This gives away half her price with nothing in return. She would do the same work for an amount she knows is not fair."
  - "This attacks a person she does not know. It sounds defensive, and it says nothing about the value of her own work."
  - "This bargains over a number, not interests. There is no reason a quarter less is fair, and it still gives without a trade."
  - "Right. A clear breakdown is a fair standard the client can check, and the question finds the interest. If budget is the problem, she can offer less scope."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Ibrahim is building a data dashboard for an NGO. Halfway through, the manager asks for three extra charts with the same deadline and budget. Ibrahim has no spare time. What should he do?"
options:
  - "Add the charts and save time by testing the whole dashboard less"
  - "Offer options, such as a later date, more budget, or fewer other charts"
  - "Add the charts quietly, then charge for the extra hours on the final invoice"
  - "Refuse, because the scope was agreed at the start and cannot change now"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "This lets quality pay for the trade without telling anyone. Bugs in a data dashboard can lead the NGO to wrong decisions."
  - "Right. More scope means time, cost or other scope must change. Real options let the manager choose what matters most."
  - "A surprise on the invoice damages trust. Any change in cost must be agreed before the work, not after."
  - "Scope can change. The manager may have good reasons for the charts. The point is to agree what else changes with it."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Your team lead asks you to deliver a feature on Thursday instead of next Tuesday. You estimate the full feature needs until Tuesday. What is the best first reply?"
options:
  - "Agree, and plan to work late every night this week to finish by Thursday"
  - "Agree, and quietly leave out the tests so that you finish on time"
  - "Ask what must work by Thursday, and offer to ship that part first"
  - "Explain that your estimate is Tuesday and that the date cannot move"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "This hides the trade. Your team lead does not learn the real cost, and one power cut or tired night can still make it late."
  - "This trades away quality in secret. Bugs found later cost more time than the tests would have."
  - "Right. The question finds the interest behind Thursday, and shipping part of the scope first may serve it."
  - "This defends a position without asking why Thursday matters. There may be an option that works for both of you."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Amir has a call tomorrow with a new client about the price of a three-month project. Which preparation will help him most in the call?"
options:
  - "Know his lowest rate, and what he will do if they cannot agree"
  - "Write a script and say it word for word, whatever the client says"
  - "Plan to open with a high price and lower it until the client agrees"
  - "Decide to accept any offer, because a three-month project is rare"
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. His lowest rate and his walk-away option tell him when to say yes and when to say no calmly, without deciding under pressure."
  - "A script can help him start, but a negotiation needs listening. He must answer what the client actually says."
  - "This is bargaining over a number, not interests. Without knowing his lowest rate, Amir does not know where to stop, and he could agree to a price that does not cover his costs."
  - "Fear of losing the project can lead to a deal he cannot keep for three months. Knowing his limits protects both sides."
```

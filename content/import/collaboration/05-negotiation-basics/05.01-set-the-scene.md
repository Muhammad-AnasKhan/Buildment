## Yousef's Monday

Yousef is a mobile developer. He works part time for a startup in Amsterdam and takes freelance projects in the evenings. On Monday morning he has four messages:

- His team lead, Anke, asks if the new login screen can ship on Thursday instead of next Tuesday.
- A freelance client, Martin, writes: "Your quote is a bit high for us. Can you do better?"
- His teammate Nisreen asks to swap code review duty this week, because she has an exam.
- An NGO he volunteers for wants "a few more pages" on the website he finished last month.

None of these messages says "negotiation". But each one is a negotiation. Two people want different things, and they need to agree on something both can accept.

## Why this matters for your career

Many people think negotiation means hard bargaining: a seller and a buyer pushing each other until one of them gives up. At work it is usually smaller and quieter. It sounds like "Could we move the date?" or "What if we do the main part first?"

If you avoid these conversations, you still get an outcome. You just do not get a say in it. You may agree to deadlines you cannot meet, or work for a price that does not cover your time. Then the work suffers, and so does trust.

In module 4 you learned to disagree and resolve conflict without damage. Negotiation uses the same habits: stay calm, listen, and focus on the problem, not the person. The difference is that negotiation ends with a clear deal: who does what, by when, and for how much.

<Callout type="info" title="Fair, not tough">
A good negotiation is not about winning. It ends with an agreement both sides can keep, and a relationship that is still good the next day.
</Callout>

## Key words

| Word | Plain English |
|---|---|
| **Position** | What someone says they want, such as a date or a price |
| **Interest** | The real need or worry behind that position |
| **Option** | One possible agreement |
| **Scope** | What a piece of work includes: features, pages or tasks |
| **Rate** | The price you charge for your work, per hour, per day or per project |
| **Trade** | Giving something in return for getting something |
| **Deal** | The agreement you reach at the end |

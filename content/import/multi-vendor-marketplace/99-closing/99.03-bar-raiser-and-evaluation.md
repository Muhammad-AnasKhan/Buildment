# 99.3 — Bar-raiser and evaluation

> **[Wrap]** · Chapter 99 — Closing · *Bar-raiser, viva prep, portfolio framing*

You shipped code and docs. This sub-chapter is the **evaluation contract** — one-line bar-raiser, viva questions, portfolio framing, and final learning log gate.

Read this when you think you are "done." Honest self-assessment here saves awkward vivas.

## The whole journey — recap for beginners

Four weeks, one product, many skills:

```
Week 1   Skeleton + MongoDB + models + seed
         ↓
Week 2   Auth + vendor shop + products + photos
         ↓
Week 3   Customer browse + cart + Stripe checkout + order UI
         ↓
Week 4   Cache + search + async jobs + HTTPS deploy
         ↓
99       Ship · document · defend in viva
```

You did not learn "a little React" and "a little Node." You learned **how a multi-vendor marketplace is structured** — who owns what data, what must be synchronous, what can wait, where secrets live, and how to prove it with a live URL.

## Bar-raiser (one sentence)

**Pass:** *A mentor can open your HTTPS URL, log in as customer and vendor, complete a two-store checkout with Stripe test payment, see split orders on both sides, and you can explain snapshots, IDOR prevention, payment verification, cache invalidation, and async job flow (API vs worker) without notes.*

If any clause fails, you are not at bar — fix before claiming course complete.

## Viva question bank

Prepare concise answers — 30–60 seconds each. Practice out loud, not only in your head.

### Product & domain

1. Why multi-vendor grocery instead of single store?
2. What do `unit` and `stockQty` mean on products?
3. Walk through one checkout with two stores — how many orders? One payment or two? Who sees what?

### Security

4. How do you prevent vendor A editing vendor B's products?
5. Where do JWT secrets live? Why not in React?
6. What is IDOR and where did you test it?

### Data

7. Why MongoDB for this project? When would SQL win?
8. Why embed order lines with snapshots?
9. What happens to order display if vendor changes price after purchase?

### Performance & ops

10. How does catalogue caching work? When do you invalidate?
11. Why enqueue checkout notifications instead of sending email in the handler?
12. How does Stripe webhook verification work? Why raw body?
13. What runs in the worker process? What happens if worker is down during checkout?
14. What happens if Redis is down?
15. How did you deploy API, worker, and client? Where are production secrets?

### Beginner-friendly framing tips

- Start answers with **what the user experiences**, then **what the server does**.
- Use FreshMarket examples — "two stalls, two receipts" — not abstract jargon.
- "I don't know" + where you'd look (logs, Redis, worker terminal) beats confident wrong.

## Common beginner questions

**Q: I finished all chapters but failed the bar-raiser on async explanation. Am I done?**  
A: Not yet. Finishing the build chapters is different from meeting the bar-raiser, because evaluation grades understanding, not just completion. For async, that means re-running the Chapter 22 end-to-end demo (22.15), watching the worker terminal during checkout, and re-reading the 22.1 bridge until you can explain why 201 returns before the `[NOTIFY]` logs. The fix is usually one targeted review away — find the weak clause and attack it.

**Q: Is the viva scary?**  
A: It feels intimidating, but it's a conversation about your own project — you already know that codebase better than the person asking. Your cheat sheet is CHALLENGES.md and your learning logs, if you wrote them honestly. Practice the question bank out loud, in 30–60 seconds, starting with what the user experiences and then what the server does. "I don't know, but here's where I'd look" is acceptable; bluffing is not.

**Q: What now?**  
A: Ship fixes until the bar-raiser honestly passes, then point your portfolio at the live URL and demo video. The repo is evidence; the viva proves the work is yours. Re-read the clause breakdown and self-audit the competency map — if one box is weak, revisit that chapter before claiming "course complete." After that, the same skeleton — skeleton → paired API/UI → polish → ship — is your template for the next project.

**Q: Why must I explain things without notes?**  
A: Because notes let you read facts, and the bar-raiser is testing whether the concepts are genuinely yours. If you can explain price snapshots or the API/worker split from memory, you understood the structure, not just the steps. That's the difference between building a marketplace and following a tutorial. You don't need perfect phrasing — you need to be able to reason about why each decision exists.

**Q: What if I can't answer a viva question?**  
A: Stay calm, say what you know, then name where you'd look for the rest — your logs, Redis, the worker terminal, or your own code. "I don't know, and here's how I'd find out" is far stronger than a confident wrong answer, because it shows real engineering instincts. The question bank in this sub-chapter is your rehearsal script; if you can answer those questions with the user-experience-first framing, you'll handle surprises fine.

# 25.13 — Recap and what's next

> **[Wrap]** · Chapter 25 — Promotions & pricing

FreshMarket now handles two production problems that separate a convincing demo from a dependable checkout: Chapter 24 made availability honest, and Chapter 25 made pricing explainable. A browser can request a coupon and display a preview, but it cannot choose what Stripe charges.

## What you built

- A `Coupon` model for mutable campaign policy and an append-only `CouponRedemption` event for successful checkout history.
- A reusable validation pipeline with time, scope, subtotal, global, and per-user rules.
- `computeCartPricing`, the single authority for cart preview and checkout.
- Deterministic line allocation whose discount cents add up exactly across vendor orders.
- Immutable order snapshots containing the coupon, funding scope, subtotal, discount, total, and pricing version.
- Idempotent webhook recording and rate limits around the code-guessing surface.

## Follow one cent through the system

The most useful mental model is traceability. A discount cent starts in a server pricing decision, is assigned to one eligible line, rolls up into one vendor order, contributes to the PaymentIntent amount, and remains in the paid order snapshot. The redemption event links the overall checkout to all vendor orders without counting the coupon once per vendor.

If those values disagree, do not patch the UI. Find the first boundary where the cent changed.

## What remains intentionally simple

This module allows one active coupon, treats merchandise lines as the eligible base, and checks redemption counts during validation. It does not build stackable campaigns, taxes, shipping discounts, refunds, or an atomic claim system for the last globally available redemption. Naming those boundaries is part of production reasoning; a count query alone is not a strict concurrency guarantee.

## Interview story

> “FreshMarket treats the coupon code as untrusted intent. One server pricing service recomputes current product prices and coupon eligibility at checkout, allocates integer-cent discounts before vendor grouping, and snapshots the exact result used for Stripe. The success webhook records one idempotent redemption per checkout.”

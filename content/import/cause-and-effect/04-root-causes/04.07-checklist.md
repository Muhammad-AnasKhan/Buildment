## Your root-cause investigation

This task brings the whole module together on one problem. You will dig down with the five whys, look wide with a fishbone, and choose a fix. The point is not to find the one perfect answer. Two careful people can build different chains for the same problem, and both can be right. The point is to show your reasoning: what you checked, what you found, and why your fix should stop the problem coming back.

Choose a problem that keeps happening and that sits mostly inside your own control or your team's: a routine, a process at work, a study habit, a small business task. Avoid problems whose causes lie far outside anyone's reach. A good problem can be stated exactly, with a number and a time, such as "6 of my last 10 client files went out after the deadline".

Here is what a good answer looks like, told about a fictional person. Wisam is a freelance data assistant who sends a weekly report to a client every Thursday by 17:00. In the last two months, 5 of 8 reports were late. His first thought was "I am bad at time". Instead, he asked why and checked each answer in his own messages and files. The reports were late because he finished the charts on Thursday evening. The charts were late because the client's numbers arrived on Thursday afternoon. The numbers arrived late because the client's staff send them when they remember. They send them when they remember because Wisam and the client never agreed a day and time for the numbers. That is a missing step, and he can change it.

Wisam's fishbone had groups for steps, people, tools, information and surroundings. Under tools, he wrote "the chart file breaks when columns move". Under surroundings, "a noisy room on busy afternoons". He checked two causes. His messages showed the numbers arrived on Thursday in all five late weeks, and the chart file broke only once. So the missing agreement was the main root cause, and the chart file was a small contributing one. His strong fix was a change to the process: numbers due every Tuesday at noon, with a short template the client fills in, so the columns never move. A weaker fix, a reminder message to the client each week, was added as support.

Look at the shape of Wisam's answer. Every why has evidence next to it. The chain ends at a step, not at a person. The fishbone shows other possible causes, and he says which two he checked and what he found. The fix changes the design, and the weak fix only supports it.

Compare it with a weak answer to the same problem: "My reports are late because I am disorganised. I will try harder and set an alarm." That answer stops at a person after one why. It has no evidence, and it never looks at other causes. Its fix is a reminder. It would feel honest, and it would change nothing, because the client's numbers would still arrive on Thursday afternoon.

If you prefer not to use your own situation, use this provided case. Raed keeps the fee records for a youth football club with 60 members. Over the last three months, 9 members got a message asking for a fee they had already paid. Some parents were upset. Here is what Raed knows. Members pay cash at training to whichever coach is there. The coach writes the payment on a paper slip and hands the slips to Raed on Sunday. Raed types the slips into the club's spreadsheet on Sunday evening. The reminder messages go out every Saturday, using the names in the spreadsheet. In 7 of the 9 cases, the member had paid during that week, and the slip had not yet reached Raed. In the other 2 cases, the slip had the wrong month written on it. Use these facts for your five whys, your fishbone and your fix.

## How your mentor will read it

A mentor will read your answer with the table below. Two careful people can draw different chains for the same problem. If your chain is different from the one your mentor would have drawn, that is fine, as long as each step is backed by evidence.

| Part | Met | Partly | Not yet |
|---|---|---|---|
| Problem stated | A number and a time period | A number or a time period, not both | No number and no time |
| Whys with evidence | Evidence next to every why | Evidence for some whys only | No evidence at all |
| Fishbone | Four or more groups, and two causes checked with results | Groups listed, but no causes checked | No fishbone |
| Root cause and fix | A process cause, and a strong or middle fix with a reason | A process cause, but only a weak fix | The root cause is a person |

**Pass rule:** your answer passes when no part is "Not yet" and at least three parts are "Met".

The mentor then replies with one or two short questions. For example: "What evidence ruled out the second cause you checked?" or "What would make your fix one step stronger?" You answer them in writing or in a voice note. That reply is part of the gate, so look out for it after you submit.

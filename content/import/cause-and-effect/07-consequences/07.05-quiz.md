```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Bilal's café near a university has 20 tables. He turns off the free Wi-Fi so that lunch customers leave sooner. A second-order effect is an effect of an earlier effect, not of the decision itself. Which of these is one?"
options:
  - "Customers can no longer go online while they sit in the café"
  - "Bilal no longer pays the monthly bill for the internet line"
  - "Students who worked there all day now leave after one coffee"
  - "With fewer students staying, the café's afternoon sales fall"
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "This comes straight from the decision. It is the first link in the chain, so it is a first effect."
  - "This also comes straight from turning the Wi-Fi off. Nothing else has to happen first, so it is a first effect."
  - "This is the students' direct reaction to the decision. It is the first link on that branch of the chain, so it is a first effect."
  - "Right. Sales fall because students leave sooner, and students leave sooner because of the decision. It is the effect of an effect: the second link in the chain."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Firas pilots a 'washed in 15 minutes' promise at his car wash, which has 5 workers. Stop rule, written first: over 5 in 100 cars sent back. Before: 40 cars a day, 1 in 100 sent back, 3 rainy days. Pilot: 52 cars a day, 9 in 100 sent back, 3 rainy days. What now?"
options:
  - "Keep the promise, because cars per day rose by 12, which was his goal"
  - "Change the plan, because the cars sent back crossed his stop rule"
  - "Ignore the cars sent back, because rain probably made cars dirtier"
  - "Move the stop rule to 10 in 100, because 9 is close to the old line"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "The goal measure did rise. But the watch measure was chosen for exactly this case, and it shows a side effect that cars per day cannot show."
  - "Right. 9 in 100 is above the line of 5 that Firas set before he started. The decision was made in advance: change the plan, then try again small."
  - "Both periods had 3 rainy days, so rain does not explain why more cars were sent back."
  - "A line moved after you see the results is no longer a stop rule. It was set in advance so that the results could not change it."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Salwa runs a post office counter with 3 clerks. An AI chat tool tells her: 'Set a target: every customer served within 5 minutes, with a monthly team bonus. Waiting is what people complain about, so when the number falls, the problem is solved.' What is the main problem?"
options:
  - "Five minutes is too long, because customers expect to be served faster"
  - "A monthly bonus is too slow, and a weekly bonus would work better"
  - "It treats the number as the goal; clerks may rush and make mistakes"
  - "It should first ask customers whether waiting is a real problem"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "The right number of minutes is a matter of judgment. It is not the causal mistake in the answer."
  - "How often the bonus is paid is a detail. Any reward attached to speed alone invites the same problem."
  - "Right. Once the waiting time is the target, the easiest path is to serve faster by checking forms and parcels less carefully. The number can fall while the service gets worse. Salwa needs a second measure that rushing would make worse, such as parcels returned for wrong addresses."
  - "The answer already says customers complain about waiting, so this is not the main gap. The gap is what the target will do to the work."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Kamal runs a shoe shop in a market, with 2 staff. He tries a new loyalty card. In the first three days, sales rise by about a third. Kamal wants to give the card to every customer and keep it for good. What is the best next step?"
options:
  - "Give the card to every customer now, since the early rise is so large"
  - "Stop the trial now, since a rise that large must be a counting mistake"
  - "Double the card's discount now, to see if sales rise even more"
  - "Run the trial for whole weeks; early results can mislead either way"
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "Three days is a very short test. Some people react to something new just because it is new, so an early jump may not last."
  - "A large early rise is not proof of an error. It is a reason to keep watching, not to stop."
  - "This changes a second thing before Kamal knows what the first change did. Then he cannot tell which change caused what."
  - "Right. In a short test, some people react to the newness and others hold back at first, so the result can be too high or too low. Whole weeks cover busy and quiet days."
```

question: "Wedad's youth centre library has 400 members. She cut loans from three weeks to one. Two months later, late returns fell from 60 to 20 a month, and borrowing from 500 to 320. New members stayed at about 30 a month. Borrowers who stopped say a week is too short. What do the facts support?"
options:
  - "Borrowers who cannot finish a book in a week have stopped borrowing"
  - "The rule worked, because late returns fell by two thirds in two months"
  - "Fewer people want the library now, so borrowing would have fallen anyway"
  - "Members simply lost interest in reading at this time of the year"
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. Borrowing fell sharply right after the rule, new members kept joining, and the people who stopped said why. The rule's side effect is fewer books read."
  - "Late returns did fall, but borrowing fell by 180 books a month. The goal measure improved while the real goal, members reading, got worse."
  - "New members kept joining at about 30 a month, so interest in the library did not drop."
  - "The borrowers who stopped gave a different reason: a week is too short. Their own answers point to the rule, not to the season."

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Abeer manages a gym with 120 members. Members who buy the new protein shakes lost more weight this term than the others. Shake buyers also come about four times a week; the others come about twice. She wants to tell members the shakes help. Which habit first, and why?"
options:
  - "Five whys, to find the root cause of slow weight loss among members"
  - "Split in half, to find the step where members stop losing weight"
  - "Check for a third factor: shake buyers also train twice as often"
  - "Ask 'and then what?', to see how members will react to her message"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "The five whys digs into a problem that keeps coming back. Here there is no repeated problem, only a claim about a cause."
  - "Splitting in half finds a fault in a chain of steps. Weight loss is not a chain where one step has broken."
  - "Right. The shake and the weight loss move together, but the buyers also come twice as often. More training could explain both, so check that third factor before making any claim."
  - "Thinking about reactions comes later. First Abeer needs to know whether the claim is even true."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Baha's hardware shop has 600 regular customers. In March he sent a discount text to 200 of them. March sales rose by about 10 percent. Last March they also rose about 10 percent, with no texts. The 400 customers with no text also spent about 10 percent more. What do these facts support?"
options:
  - "The rise is probably the usual March rise, not the texts"
  - "The texts worked, because sales rose by 10 percent soon after them"
  - "The texts worked, but only for the 200 customers who received them"
  - "Sales were too low before March, so he should dig for a root cause"
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. The customers who got no text, a comparison group, rose by the same amount, and last March rose the same way. The texts show no effect beyond what would have happened anyway."
  - "This is 'after, so because'. Last year's March rose the same amount without any texts."
  - "The 400 customers without a text also spent about 10 percent more, so the rise was not limited to those who got the text."
  - "Nothing in the facts says sales were a problem before March. The question is whether the texts caused the rise."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Dania runs a fresh juice stall at a market, with 2 helpers. For the third month in a row, a batch of juice has gone bad before noon. Each time, the helper who made that batch was told to be more careful. Which habit should Dania use first, and why?"
options:
  - "Run an A/B test of two juice recipes for a month, then keep the winner"
  - "Look for a third factor that links the juice and the hot weather"
  - "Ask 'and then what?' about replacing the helper who made the batch"
  - "Find the root cause in the steps; the same fault keeps coming back"
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "A recipe test changes the drink, but nobody knows yet why the juice goes bad. Find the cause before testing a fix."
  - "A third factor is about two things that move together. Here there is one problem that keeps coming back, which calls for a root cause."
  - "This starts from blaming a person. Module 4 taught to look for the cause in steps, tools and information first."
  - "Right. A problem that returns after the same weak fix (a reminder) is a sign that the root cause is in the process. Ask why with evidence, for example how long the fruit waits and where it is kept."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Emad's taxi office has 6 drivers. Since this morning, website bookings do not reach the drivers' phones. The chain: website form, office email, office laptop, forwarding app, phones. The email shows all this morning's bookings. The laptop is on and online. Drivers can still phone each other. What should Emad check next?"
options:
  - "The website form, because a fault often starts at the very beginning"
  - "The forwarding app, by sending one test booking to a driver's phone"
  - "The laptop, by restarting it, because computers often cause faults"
  - "The drivers' phones, by buying a new phone for one of the drivers"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "The email already holds this morning's bookings, so everything up to the email works. The fault is later in the chain."
  - "Right. The email holds the bookings, and the laptop is on and online, so the fault is most likely after the email: in the laptop's forwarding, the app or the phones. A test booking through the app checks the middle of what is left, cheaply."
  - "The laptop is on and online, so it is less likely to be the fault. A test booking through the app also shows whether the laptop passes bookings on, so it is the better first check."
  - "The phones can still make calls, and a new phone costs money. A cheap test of the app comes first."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Asma runs a plant nursery with 4 workers. Each order goes through four steps. Per hour, the team can pot 40 plants, water 60, label 20 and pack 50. Orders are waiting, so Asma plans to hire a second worker for potting. Which habit should she use first, and why?"
options:
  - "Ask 'and then what?' about a new worker, since it is a big change"
  - "Run the five whys on the late orders, since customers are waiting"
  - "Test a second potter for a month against a month without one"
  - "Find the slowest step: labelling, at 20 an hour, sets the pace"
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "Thinking about effects is useful, but first Asma needs to know whether more potting can help at all."
  - "The five whys can help, but the numbers already show the cause directly: one step is much slower than the rest."
  - "A month-long test of a change that cannot help wastes a month. The step numbers already show where the limit is."
  - "Right. The whole nursery can only finish 20 plants an hour, the speed of labelling. A second potter would only build a bigger pile of plants waiting for labels."
```

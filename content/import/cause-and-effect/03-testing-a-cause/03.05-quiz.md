```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Anas runs a barber shop and tests a new window display. Weeks 1 to 4, old display: 22, 19, 21 and 20 haircuts. Weeks 5 to 8, new display: 27, 29, 26 and 28. In week 5 he also started opening on Fridays. Wedding season began in week 6. What do the facts support?"
options:
  - "The new display alone brought about 7 more haircuts a week"
  - "The rise came from the wedding season, not the display"
  - "Nothing changed, since a few more haircuts is only luck"
  - "Haircuts rose, but the display and Fridays are tangled"
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "The average did rise by about 7 a week, from 82 to 110 in four weeks. But Friday opening started in the same week, so the display cannot take all of that rise."
  - "The season began in week 6, but haircuts were already up to 27 in week 5. The season may add a little later, but it does not explain the start of the rise."
  - "Every new-display week (26 to 29) is above every old-display week (19 to 22). That steady gap is unlikely to be luck alone."
  - "Right. The rise is clear, but two changes started together in week 5. To separate them, Anas would have to change one thing at a time."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Fatima runs a fitness class at a community gym with 80 members. She wants to know if a weekly text reminder makes members come to more classes. Which plan gives the fairest answer?"
options:
  - "Flip a coin per member: heads get reminders, tails do not, same weeks"
  - "Send reminders to the 40 members who missed the most classes last term"
  - "Send reminders to all 80 and compare attendance with last term's numbers"
  - "Let members choose whether they want reminders, then compare both groups"
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. Chance splits the members, so the two groups are alike, and both live through the same weeks. The gap in attendance points at the reminders. With 40 in each group, read it as a hint and repeat it."
  - "Members who missed many classes last term are different from the others before the test starts. Their attendance often drifts back toward normal anyway."
  - "Last term had different weeks and weather. Any change in attendance would be tangled with time."
  - "Members who choose reminders may be the more organised ones, who would come more anyway. Their choice lets a hidden third factor in."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Ibrahim runs a school canteen. Last month he sold about 30 fruit cups a day. This month a coin chose the fruit's place each morning. Front of the counter: 10 days, 52 cups a day. Usual place: 10 days, 41 a day. This month was also hotter. What do the facts support?"
options:
  - "Moving the fruit to the front added about 22 cups a day"
  - "The front spot may add about 11 cups a day; test it again"
  - "Moving the fruit made no difference, since sales rose every day"
  - "The hot weather explains all of this month's rise in sales"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "That is the rise since last month (52 minus 30). But sales also rose on the usual-place days, to 41, so part of the rise would have happened anyway."
  - "Right. The usual-place days show what happened anyway, so the front spot's likely effect is the gap: 52 minus 41, about 11 cups. Ten days of each is a small test, so it is a hint: run it again."
  - "Sales rose on both kinds of day, but more on the front days: 52 against 41. That gap points at the front spot."
  - "The hot weather reached both kinds of day, because the coin mixed them through the same month. It can explain the rise on all days, but not the gap of 11 between them."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Diyaa tests two timetable signs at a bus office to fill the 7:00 bus. His plan: count 7:00 tickets for 4 whole weeks, with a coin picking each day's sign. After 9 days: B days 61 tickets, A days 48. A days had fewer counter questions. Two B days fell in a holiday. What should he do?"
options:
  - "Switch to sign B now, since it is ahead on his chosen measure"
  - "Switch to sign A, since it cut the questions at the counter"
  - "Restart the test, since holiday days make these numbers useless"
  - "Keep going to the end of week 4, then compare 7:00 tickets"
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "B is ahead after 9 days, but early leads often shrink. His plan says 4 whole weeks, and stopping on a lead is how lucky starts become false wins."
  - "Counter questions were not the measure he wrote down. Choosing a new number after seeing the results lets a lucky number decide."
  - "Over 4 weeks, the coin will put holiday and normal days under both signs. Restarting throws away fair data and gives him a reason to keep restarting."
  - "Right. He chose the measure and the length before he started. Keeping to both protects the result from early luck and from numbers picked afterwards."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Arwa sells spices at a farm stall. One morning a coin picks which of two labels each of 20 customers sees. An AI chat tool tells her: 'The coin made it fair. B got 7 sales and A got 4, nearly twice as many. Use B from now on.' What is the main problem?"
options:
  - "Nothing; the coin flip made the test fair, so B is better"
  - "She should compare B with last month's sales, not with A"
  - "With 20 customers, B's win is only a hint to test again"
  - "She should also count how long people looked at each label"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "The coin did make the groups alike, and the AI is right about that. But a fair test can still be a small one. With 10 customers a label, 7 against 4 can easily come from luck."
  - "Last month is a weaker comparison, because the season and the market crowd change. Comparing A and B on the same morning is the better design."
  - "Right. The design is fair, but the test is small, so the win is a hint. Arwa should run it again on more market days before she changes every label."
  - "Adding a new measure after the test lets her pick whichever number looks good. Sales was the measure that answers her question."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Tala runs a free study group at the community library, with room for about 25 people. The more members it has, the noisier it gets and the harder it is to find a time for everyone, so some members stop coming. What kind of loop is this?"
options:
  - "A reinforcing loop, because more members bring more members"
  - "A balancing loop that pulls the group back toward a limit"
  - "No loop at all, since members leave for their own reasons"
  - "A reinforcing loop running down, which will empty the group"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "That would be a loop where members attract more members. Here, more members lead to fewer members, which pulls the other way."
  - "Right. A bigger group brings noise and harder times, which makes some members leave. The loop pushes the group back toward a size it can handle."
  - "Each member decides for themselves, but the reasons come from the group's size. Size affects leaving, and leaving affects size, so it is a loop."
  - "A reinforcing loop running down would keep shrinking the group. Here, people leave only while the group is large, so it settles at a limit."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Muath's sports club hall holds 40 people per class. (1) New members, January to April: 6, 9, 13, 18. (2) Most came with a friend. (3) In April every class was full, and about 5 people a week were turned away. (4) In May, new members fell to 10; classes were still full. Which conclusion fits?"
options:
  - "The club got worse in May, so fewer people now want to join it"
  - "The growth was luck, and the numbers have no clear pattern"
  - "The friends loop has turned round and is now running downwards fast"
  - "Friends bring friends in a loop, now held back by the full hall"
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "Facts 3 and 4 go against this: people were still being turned away, and classes were still full. Demand is there; space is not."
  - "Fact 1 shows a pattern: the monthly jumps grow, 3, then 4, then 5. Fact 2 gives the reason: friends bring friends."
  - "Fact 4 goes against this: classes are still full every evening. A loop running down would leave empty places."
  - "Right. Growing jumps and friends bringing friends (facts 1 and 2) show a reinforcing loop. Full classes and people turned away (facts 3 and 4) show the hall is now the limit."
```

question: "Leila's online bookshop can pack about 30 orders a week. An advert brings orders about two weeks after it runs. Week 1: 12 orders, one advert. Week 2: 12 orders, three more adverts. Week 3: 18 orders. Week 4: 45 orders, more than she can pack. Which conclusion fits?"
options:
  - "She added adverts before the first one had time to show its effect"
  - "Adverts do not really work, and the week 4 jump came from pure luck"
  - "Her customers changed their minds in week 4 for no clear reason"
  - "Her slow packing step is what caused the big jump in orders"
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. The week 1 advert showed in week 3, as the rise to 18. The three adverts from week 2 all showed together in week 4. She acted again on old information and overshot."
  - "The timing goes against luck: orders rose two weeks after each advert, just as expected."
  - "The jump came exactly two weeks after the three adverts. A clear cause fits the timing, so there is no need for a sudden change of mind."
  - "Packing limits how many orders she can send out. It cannot bring in new orders, so it cannot explain the jump."

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Adnan's workshop makes wooden chairs. Per day: cutting 10, sanding 4, painting 8. An AI chat tool tells him: 'Your cutting machine is your oldest tool, and every chair starts there. A faster one would speed up every chair and raise your output.' What is the main mistake?"
options:
  - "None: the first step sets the pace, so a faster cutter helps"
  - "It should tell him to paint faster, since painting comes last"
  - "Sanding is the slowest step, so faster cutting only adds to a pile"
  - "It should tell him to speed up sanding and painting too, to be safe"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "The pace is set by the slowest step, not the first one. Cutting already does 10 a day, more than sanding can use."
  - "The last step is not always the slowest. Painting can do 8 a day, while sanding can do only 4."
  - "Right. Sanding, at 4 a day, is the bottleneck. Faster cutting adds to the pile of chairs waiting to be sanded and does not raise output."
  - "Speeding up every step spends money on steps that are not the limit. Only the slowest step, sanding, sets the pace."
```

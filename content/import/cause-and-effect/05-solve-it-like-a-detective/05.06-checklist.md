## Your problem-solving log

This task asks you to show the whole method on one problem, in the order you would really use it. A problem-solving log is a short written record. It has four parts. First, an exact description. Second, a list of possible causes. Third, your tests in order, with what each one ruled out. Fourth, the result, with the test that confirmed the fix. The log shows that you found the cause by testing, not by luck.

You can use a real problem from your own study, work or home. It can be small: a phone that will not connect to headphones, a shared file that someone cannot open, a form that loses some entries. You do not need to have solved it at the time. If you did not test it then, write the tests you would run now, in the order you would run them. Say what each result would rule out.

If you prefer not to use your own life, use the provided case below. It has every fact you need, and it is just as good for this gate.

**The provided case: Thaer's speaker.** Thaer runs a morning exercise class for about 20 older men at the community centre. He plays music from his phone through the centre's speaker, using an audio cable. Since Saturday the speaker gives no sound. The speaker's power light is on. When Thaer tests his phone with the earbuds at home, music plays through them normally. On Saturday morning, before the class, he set up a new pair of wireless earbuds with his phone. The earbuds stay in his bag, switched on, during the class.

Here is what Thaer would find if he ran each test. When the centre manager plugs her own phone into the same cable, music plays through the speaker. When Thaer looks at the sound settings on his phone, the sound is set to go to the wireless earbuds. When he switches the earbuds off, the music plays through the cable and the speaker. Your job is not only to report these results. Choose an order for the tests, say why you chose that order, and say what each test rules out. Then say what the fix is and how Thaer would confirm it.

Here is what a strong log looks like, using a different case. Lubna keeps the office of a small farm cooperative with 35 farmers. Every week she saves a price sheet in a shared folder for the farmers. Since Wednesday, the farmers say they cannot see the new sheets. Her description notes the "is not" side. The same sheets appear in the folder when she opens it on her own phone. She lists the possible causes: her laptop not saving the files, the folder's sharing settings, and the farmers' own phones.

Her first test is free and takes one minute. She opens the folder on her phone and sees the new sheets. That rules out her laptop and the saving step, which is half of the chain. Her second test is to look at who the folder is shared with. The farmers' group is missing. She remembers that she renamed the folder on Wednesday, the day the problem started. She shares it with the group again and saves a small test file. Then she asks two farmers to confirm that they can see it. They can. She writes all of this down in six lines.

Look at what makes Lubna's log strong. Every cause on her list fits the description. Her tests are in a sensible order, cheap and likely first. Each test has a clear result, and she says what it ruled out. She asked what changed just before the problem started, and that question found the cause. And she did not stop when she made a change. She confirmed the fix with a test that two farmers could see.

Look also at what is missing from it. There is no "I tried everything." There is no "it started working again, so it is fixed." There is no guess about the cause written into the description. Those are three habits this module asks you to leave behind.

## How your mentor will read it

Your mentor reads your log with four criteria. Each criterion has three levels, and the table says what each level looks like.

| Criterion | Met | Partly | Not yet |
|---|---|---|---|
| Description | What, where, when, how much, and one real "is not" fact | Some parts, or "is not" is missing | A feeling, or a guess named as the problem |
| Causes | Three or more causes, each fits the description | Causes listed, one does not fit | One cause only |
| Tests in order | One change per test, order explained, each result says what it ruled out | Tests listed, order or results unclear | Several changes at once, or no tests |
| Fix and safety | Fix confirmed by a test you can see; nothing unsafe | Fix named but not confirmed | No fix, or a step that opens a device |

**Pass rule:** your log passes when Description and Tests in order are Met, and no criterion is Not yet. A short, clear log can pass. Length does not matter; the four criteria do.

**What happens next:** your mentor replies with one or two short questions. For example: "What would you have done if the manager's phone had also been silent?" You answer them in writing or in a voice note. Your answers count as part of the gate, so a log at "Partly" can still pass once your answers fill the gap.

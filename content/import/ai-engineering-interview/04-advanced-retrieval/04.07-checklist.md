## Your interview answer

The interviewer says: "Your basic RAG system finds the right chunk in the top 5 for about 70% of questions. What would you change, in what order, and what would each change cost?" This is the classic senior follow-up after you describe a pipeline. Answer it for your capstone scenario: policy documents for an NGO or a microfinance lender, support policies for a small online shop, or runbooks for a remote software team.

A strong answer does not list every technique in the module. It starts from the failures you expect, picks two or three upgrades that match them, and orders them by cost. For each step it gives what you expect to improve, how much latency it adds, and what it costs per question or at indexing, as numbers you will measure, not promises. Then it says how you will know it worked: which test questions, which metric, and what result would make you remove the step again.

Numbers do not need to be measured yet, but they must be honest. "Reranking 30 chunks will add about 100 to 150 ms; I will confirm on my laptop" is a good number. "Reranking makes it 30% better" with no test behind it is not. Say what you would leave out on purpose, such as Graph RAG or fine-tuning, and why; interviewers trust a plan that names what it does not need.

Order matters as much as the steps. Put cheap, low-risk changes first, such as adding BM25 or a synonym list, because they cost almost nothing per question and are easy to remove. Put changes that add a model call per question, such as rewriting, HyDE or reranking, after them, and keep each only if your test questions show it earned its latency. Changes with a large indexing cost, such as Graph RAG or fine-tuning, come last and need the strongest reason.

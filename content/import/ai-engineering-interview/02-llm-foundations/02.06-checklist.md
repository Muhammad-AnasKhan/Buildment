## Your interview answer

This question comes up in system design and in the managerial round, often in this exact form: a teammate proposes something that sounds safe, and the interviewer wants to see whether you can turn it into numbers. A strong answer does four things. It estimates the change in tokens per request with clear assumptions. It turns that into cost and latency, using example prices you say are examples. It explains what could happen to quality, without claiming more than you know. And it proposes a better option with a way to measure it.

You may use Sara's numbers from chapter 2.2 as a model, but build your answer around a system of your own or a new scenario. Write your assumptions as numbers, not adjectives. "About 400 tokens per chunk" is an assumption an interviewer can discuss; "chunks are quite big" is not. If you are not sure of a number, give a range and say how you would measure the real value in the first week.

Be careful with the quality part. Do not say that more context always hurts, and do not say it always helps. Say what you would expect, why, and which test would tell you. A small golden set, with the same questions run before and after the change, is the answer most interviewers are listening for. You will build one in module 5; for now it is enough to describe it.

Aim for about 120 to 200 words: roughly what you would say in one minute. Longer is not better here. In a real interview you would give this answer aloud and then wait for the follow-up, so practise stopping once you have made your proposal.

Keep the tone of a teammate, not a judge. The teammate in the question wants better answers, which is a good goal. Your job is to show the cost of their route and offer a cheaper route to the same goal, with a test that settles it. Interviewers listen for this, because it is how you will disagree with real colleagues on a remote team.

A mentor reads your answer and replies with one to three follow-up questions, the kind an interviewer would ask next. Answer them by editing and resubmitting. If you want to practise speaking, record yourself giving the answer and add the link; audio is enough. The written answer is still required.

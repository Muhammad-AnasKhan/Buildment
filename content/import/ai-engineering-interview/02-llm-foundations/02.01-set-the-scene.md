## The bill that nearly tripled

Sara is an engineer at Zaytoon Mobile (a fictional mobile phone network). Her team runs a support assistant that answers customer questions about bundles, bills and coverage. It searches about 300 help articles, puts the closest pieces into a prompt, and asks a model to write the answer.

One month, a teammate tried to improve quality. He raised the number of article pieces in each prompt from 3 to 10. He also started sending the whole conversation history with every question, not only the last two messages. Nobody measured anything before or after. The change looked harmless: same model, same questions, more information.

At the end of the month, the scenario's numbers looked like this. The model bill went from about $1,000 to about $2,700. Typical answers took about 6 seconds instead of 4. And quality did not improve. One customer was told the wrong rule for cancelling a bundle. The right article piece was in the prompt, but it sat in the middle, between nine others, and the model used a different one.

That was not the only problem. When a customer typed a bundle code such as `ZM-NET-15`, the search often returned a general article about bundles instead of the article for that exact bundle. And a small feature that asked the model to sort support tickets into categories as JSON crashed twice a day, because the model sometimes wrapped the JSON in a friendly sentence.

Sara's manager asked her a simple question: "Why did the bill nearly triple, and what will you change?" To answer, Sara needed to explain four things she had never had to explain before.

## Why interviewers ask about this

These basics come up in every round. In the deep dive: "Why did you send five chunks and not ten?" In system design: "Estimate what this costs per month for 20,000 users." In the managerial round: "Tell me about a cost surprise and what you did." An engineer who can turn "more context" into tokens, dollars and seconds gives a clear answer. One who cannot says "it should be fine", and the interviewer writes that down.

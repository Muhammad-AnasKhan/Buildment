This is the gate for module 10. Tick each box only when it is true, answer the learning log for yourself, and then write a deployment and monitoring plan for your capstone. A mentor will read the plan. Your answers save as you type, so a power cut or a lost connection does not lose your work.

This module had no build chapter, but it is not only theory. The capstone in module 12 asks for structured logs or traces and a design note, and the managerial round asks how you would run your system after launch. The plan you write here becomes part of that design note, so write it for your real capstone scenario, not a general one. If you have not chosen a scenario yet, pick the one you are most likely to build: a document assistant for an NGO or a lender, a support agent for a small online shop, or an internal knowledge bot for a remote team.

## Learning log

1. **What you would track:** For your capstone, which five parameters would you log with every evaluation run, and why each one?
2. **The trace you need:** Think of the most likely wrong answer your capstone could give. Which span and which attribute would show you the cause?
3. **Cold start or warm copy:** Would your capstone users accept a cold start of a few seconds? Who are they, and when do they use it?
4. **Your first alert:** If you could have only one alert for your capstone, which signal would it watch, and what threshold would you start with?
5. **Your incident notes:** Think of any failure you have seen, in a project, a job or a class. Write the five lines: what happened, impact, detection, fix, prevention.

## Your interview answer

In the managerial round, a common question is: "Your system is ready. How would you deploy it, and how would you know it keeps working?" Weak answers name a platform and stop. Strong answers tie the platform to the workload, name the signals and thresholds, and say who acts when an alert fires.

Build your plan in four parts. First, the workload: who uses the system, how many questions a day you expect, when they come, and how long answers take, with your own estimates. Second, the deployment target and two reasons for it, including what you give up, plus one or two lines on what goes in the container image and what stays out. Third, tracking and tracing: what you log with each evaluation run, and which spans and attributes each request records, with one rule for personal data. Fourth, monitoring: your sample size for online evaluation, two or three alerts with thresholds, who receives them, and how a failure turns into new golden set questions.

Numbers can be estimates, but say where they come from. "About 300 questions a day, because the NGO has 60 field staff who each ask about five" is much stronger than "low traffic". A mentor will read your plan and may send you one to three follow-up questions; answer them by editing and resubmitting. You may also record yourself giving the answer aloud and add the link. The written plan is still required, because it is what your mentor comments on.

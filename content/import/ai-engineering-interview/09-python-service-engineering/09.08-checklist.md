## Learning log

1. **Your slowest step:** In your own project or your `rag-agent-service`, which step would break the time budget first if the provider slowed down by ten times? How do you know?
2. **Retry or not:** Name one call in a project you know that is not safe to repeat. What would make it safe: an idempotency key, a status check, or something else?
3. **The limit you chose:** Your service uses a concurrency limit of 4 by default. What number would you choose for a real provider, and what information would you need to choose it well?
4. **What the user sees:** When your service returns a 504, what should the screen say? Write the exact sentence a Bustan Books customer would read.
5. **One surprise:** What behaved differently from what you expected while you built chapter 9.6, and what did you learn from it?

## Your interview answer

In system design and managerial rounds, a common question is some form of "your model provider gets slow, what happens?". Weak answers stay general: "we have retries". Strong answers walk through the minutes of the incident with numbers, and say what the user sees at each point. This is the answer you will write now, about your own service.

Think of it as a short story in four parts. First, the situation: the normal latency, the normal traffic and the slowdown, with numbers. You can use the numbers from your own service: a 2 second time budget, 3 attempts and a limit of 4 calls at once, or values you would choose for production. Second, what the service does during the slowdown: which calls time out, what the retry loop does, what the semaphore does to the extra requests, and which status code and error body come back. Third, what the user sees: the message, how long they wait, and whether other features keep working. Fourth, recovery and one honest gap: how the service returns to normal when the provider recovers, and one thing your service still does not handle, such as a circuit breaker or a retry budget.

Use the words from this module where they fit, but explain them as you would to an interviewer who is listening, not reading. A good test: could a teammate who did not take this course follow your answer and draw the request path from it? If not, add the missing step.

Write at least 120 words. A mentor will read your answer and may send you one to three follow-up questions; answer them by editing and resubmitting.

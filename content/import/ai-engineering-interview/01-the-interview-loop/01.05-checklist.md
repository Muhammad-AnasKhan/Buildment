## Learning log

1. **Your weakest round:** Which of the three rounds worries you most today? Name one question from that round you could not answer well right now.
2. **Your numbers:** Look at the project you described in chapter 1.1. Which of the four kinds of number (scale, quality, speed, cost) do you have, and which are missing?
3. **Your part:** If your project was built with other people, write one sentence that starts "My part was..." and is exact about the line between your work and theirs.
4. **Your failure:** What is one real thing that went wrong in that project? What was the cause, and what did you change?
5. **Your plan:** Which module of this course do you expect to help your weakest round most, and how many hours a week can you give it?

## Your interview answer

This is your baseline. Write your project story the way you would tell it in round 1, using the seven parts from chapter 1.3. Use the project from chapter 1.1, or a better one if you have thought of it since. It does not need to use AI. Aim for about 150 to 250 words, which is a little under two minutes when spoken.

Be honest about what you do not have yet. If you have no measured numbers, give the numbers you do know (users, documents, requests a day) and write one sentence on how you would measure quality. A story with one honest number is a better baseline than a story with three invented ones, and your mentor can only help with the real one.

If you have no finished project at all, use the one you are building now, or a course project, and say what stage it is at. An interviewer would rather hear "this is half built, and here is what I measured so far" than a polished story about something you did not do. You can also tell the story of the `rag-agent-service` plan: what it will do, and which numbers you expect to measure. That is a weaker baseline, but it is honest, and it gives you something real to compare at the end.

Before you submit, read your story once as an interviewer would. Can you find the problem in the first two sentences? Is there a sentence that starts with "I" and names your exact part? Does each quality number have a "from" and a "to", or at least a test set it was measured on? Is the failure a real bug or a wrong decision, with a cause? Is there one option you rejected, and what it would have given you? Each "no" is a follow-up question your mentor will probably ask, so you can fix it now or prepare an answer.

A mentor reads your story and replies with one to three follow-up questions, the kind an interviewer would ask next. Answer them by editing and resubmitting. Keep this first version: in the capstone you tell the story of the service you build in this course, and in the final gate you compare the two. Most fellows are surprised by how much the second one improves.

If you want to practise speaking too, record yourself telling the story and add the link. Audio is enough. The written answer is still required, because your mentor reviews it line by line.

## Your interview answer

"How do you know your RAG system works?" is one of the most common questions in an AI engineering interview. It comes up in the project deep dive about your own work, in the system design round about a new system, and in the managerial round about life after launch. A weak answer says "I tested it and it looked good." A strong answer has numbers, a method, and an honest limit.

A good shape for the answer has five parts, and each one takes one or two sentences. First, say what good means for this system: the right sources are found, and the answer only says what those sources say. Second, name your golden set: how many questions, where they came from, and who labelled them. Third, give your retrieval numbers, with the k you really use in production. Fourth, say how you check answers: rule checks, a faithfulness check, or a judge that you calibrated against people. Fifth, say how the eval runs: as a test on every pull request, with a floor that fails the build.

End with one honest limit, because the interviewer will probe for it anyway. Perhaps your golden set is small, or your questions were written by you and not by real users, or your judge has not been calibrated on tables. Naming the limit yourself, with what you would do next, shows the judgment the interviewer is looking for.

Watch for three common mistakes. The first is giving a number without its k or its golden set size: "recall is 0.9" means little until the listener knows it is recall@5 on 60 questions. The second is mixing up retrieval and answer quality: high recall does not prove the answers are faithful, and a faithful answer can still come from the wrong chunk. The third is describing a judge without saying how you checked it. If you used a model judge, say how many answers people labelled and how often the judge agreed with them.

Use your capstone project if you have picked one. If not, use the Bustan Books repository you built in chapter 5.5. You may write your answer, record it, or both. The written answer is required; the recording is optional.

## Learning log

1. **The three parts:** For your capstone agent, does it read untrusted content, read private data, and have a way to send data out? Which of the three could you remove?
2. **Your riskiest tool:** Which tool in your capstone agent could do the most damage if the model were fully fooled? What is the worst single call it could make?
3. **A protocol decision:** Would your capstone use MCP servers or plain function tools? Give the reason in one sentence.
4. **A gap you accept:** Name one attack your design would still not stop, and why you accept that risk for now.
5. **Reading a trace:** Think of the Sahel Relief trace in 8.3. If you were reviewing logs after that incident, which two log lines would have shown you the attack first, and what alert would you add so the next one is caught within an hour?

## What a strong answer sounds like

A weak threat model is a list of fears: "Someone could inject a prompt, so we will filter inputs and tell the model to be careful." It names no asset, no entry point and no tool, and its only defences depend on the model behaving well.

A strong threat model starts from what is worth stealing or abusing, such as money, private records or the power to act in someone's name. Then it lists every place where text from outside enters the agent: uploads, web pages, emails, tool results, other agents' output. Next it picks the single most dangerous tool call and describes how an attacker would reach it, as a short chain like the trace in 8.3. Only then does it list defences, one per layer, and for each one the honest gap. Interviewers trust a candidate who says "this layer does not stop X, so the next layer does Y" far more than one who claims a layer stops everything.

Finish with a priority. You cannot build every layer in the first week, so say which one you build first and why. The deterministic layers, such as least privilege, allow-lists, approval and output validation, are usually the right start, because an attacker cannot talk them out of their job.

## Your interview answer

A common question in the system design round is: "Here is your agent. How would someone attack it, and how do you defend it?" A strong answer is a short threat model.

It names what is valuable, such as money, private data or the ability to act. It shows where untrusted text enters and which tool calls an attacker would want. Then it gives one defence for each layer, with the gap that layer leaves. Write it for the agent you will build in the capstone, or for the Bustan Books agent from 7.6 with a model added for routing.

Use your real tool names and at least one number, such as a refund limit or an alert threshold. A mentor reads your answer and may send one to three follow-up questions, which you answer by editing and resubmitting.

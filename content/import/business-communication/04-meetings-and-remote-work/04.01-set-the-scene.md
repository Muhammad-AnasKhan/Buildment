## One call, three problems

Rania is a junior frontend developer. She works remotely for a small startup. Her team lives in three places: Gaza, Berlin and Toronto. Every Monday they have a one-hour video call.

Last Monday went like this:

- There was no list of topics, so the first 20 minutes were about a bug that only two people cared about.
- Rania joined from a shared workspace with weak internet. Her video froze, and she missed the part about the new sign-up page.
- The call ended with "Great, let's take this offline." This phrase means "let's discuss it later, outside this meeting." Nobody said who would do that, and nobody wrote anything down.

On Wednesday, Rania and Tom, the designer in Toronto, found out they had both built the same sign-up form. Nobody had built the password reset screen. Two days of work were lost, and nobody was sure what had been decided.

## Why this matters for your career

Six people spent one hour on that call. That is six hours of work, and it produced confusion. Remote teams often meet less than office teams, so each meeting matters more. When a meeting fails, people across three time zones lose a day before they can fix it.

Good remote teams do two things well:

1. **They run short meetings with a clear goal.** Everyone knows why they are there, and everyone leaves with written decisions and tasks.
2. **They do a lot of work in writing.** They do not wait for a call to share updates or make simple choices. People read and reply when they are online. This is called **async** work, and you will learn it in chapter 4.3.

A person who sends a clear agenda, writes useful notes and keeps work moving in writing becomes very valuable to a remote team. These are small habits, and you can start them this week.

## Key words

| Word | Plain English |
|---|---|
| **Agenda** | A short list of topics for a meeting, shared before it starts |
| **Facilitator** | The person who guides the meeting and keeps it on time |
| **Action item** | A task agreed in a meeting |
| **Owner** | The one person responsible for an action item |
| **Sync (synchronous)** | Working together at the same time, like a call. "A quick sync" (module 1) is a short call of this kind |
| **Async (asynchronous)** | Working together at different times, like comments on a document |

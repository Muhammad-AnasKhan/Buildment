## Sync or async?

Meetings are **synchronous (sync)**: everyone is there at the same time. Messages, documents and comments are **asynchronous (async)**: people take part when they are online.

Async is often better for remote teams. People can think before they reply. Nobody has to join at midnight. And the answer is already written down for anyone who was offline. But some conversations really do need a live call.

| Usually better in writing | Usually better in a call |
|---|---|
| Status updates and progress reports | A strong disagreement that messages have not settled |
| A choice between clear options | A complex problem with many open questions |
| A question with one short answer | Bad news or sensitive feedback |
| Reviewing a design, a document or code | A first meeting with a new client or teammate |
| Sharing information for later reading | Early brainstorming, where ideas build fast |

A simple rule many teams use: start in writing. If a thread goes back and forth three times and is still not settled, move to a short call. Then post the result back in the thread, so the decision lives in writing.

## Writing a request people can answer

Async only works if your message can be answered without a meeting. A vague message like "Thoughts on the settings page?" gets silence, because nobody knows what you need or by when.

A good async request has five parts:

1. **Context:** one or two sentences on what this is about.
2. **The question:** the one thing you need people to decide or check.
3. **The options:** with links or screenshots.
4. **Your recommendation:** and the main reason for it.
5. **A reply-by time:** and what happens if nobody replies.

Here is James's message:

> **Decision needed: settings page layout**
>
> We need to pick a layout before Anas starts building on Monday. Screenshots of both are below.
>
> - **A:** one long page with sections
> - **B:** separate tabs for account, notifications and privacy
>
> I recommend A: it is faster to build and works better on small phones.
>
> Please reply in this thread with A or B and one reason by **Thu 12 Nov, 12:00 UTC**. If there are no objections by then, we go with A.

The last sentence matters most. It tells people that silence means agreement, so the work does not stop if someone is busy or offline. Use it for decisions that are easy to change later. For bigger decisions, ask the key people to reply directly.

<Callout type="warning" title="Video messages and weak internet">
A short screen recording can explain a design well. But a video is heavy to download on a weak connection. If you send one, add a short written summary, so teammates with poor internet can still follow and reply.
</Callout>

## Habits that make async work

- **Answer in the thread,** not in a new message, so the whole conversation stays in one place.
- **Agree on reply times.** Many teams agree to reply within one working day. If you need more time, send a quick note: "Seen. I will reply by tomorrow noon."
- **Write down call results.** If a decision is made in a call, post it in writing for everyone who was not there.
- **Show when you are offline.** Use your status or the team calendar for working hours, study time or a known power schedule.

You learned about threads and time zones in chapter 2.4. Here the point is different: in async work, writing is how decisions get made, not only how news is shared.

## Common questions

**Q: Is it rude to reply to a message the next day?**
A: Not if your team has agreed on reply times. If the message is urgent or you need longer, a quick "Seen, I will reply by tomorrow" is polite and takes ten seconds.

**Q: Should we record meetings for people who could not join?**
A: Only if everyone in the meeting agrees to be recorded. Even then, share written notes too. A recording takes a long time to watch and is heavy to download.

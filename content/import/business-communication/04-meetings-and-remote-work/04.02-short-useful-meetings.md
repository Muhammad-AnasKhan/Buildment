## A call that keeps growing

Hadeel leads a small team that builds a volunteer app for an NGO. Every Tuesday the team has a call with Sofia, the NGO's project manager. The call is booked for 30 minutes, but it often takes 70. People share long updates, new topics appear, and at the end Sofia asks, "So what did we decide?"

Hadeel wants next Tuesday to be different.

## Three parts of a useful meeting

A useful meeting has three parts. If one is missing, the meeting usually runs long or ends in confusion.

| Part | When | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Goal** | Before | Says what must be true when the call ends |
| **Agenda** | Before | Lists the topics, in order, with time for each |
| **Notes** | After | Record decisions and action items with owners and dates |

Write the goal as an outcome, not a topic. "Discuss the release" is a topic. "Agree which screens go into the first release" is a goal. With a clear goal, you know when the meeting is done, and you can end it early.

If you cannot write a goal, you may not need a meeting at all. Chapter 4.3 helps you decide.

## A sample agenda

Send the agenda in the meeting invite or the team channel, ideally one working day before. Put status updates in writing before the call, so the meeting time goes to decisions.

```text
Agenda: volunteer app check-in

Goal: Agree what goes into the first release on Fri 20 Nov.
When: Tue 10 Nov, 14:00 to 14:30 UTC (16:00 Gaza, 15:00 Berlin)
Who: Hadeel (facilitator), Mahmoud, Ines, Sofia (NGO)
Notes: Ines

Before the call: post your written update in #volunteer-app
by Mon 9 Nov, 18:00 UTC, and read the release list (link).

1. Release scope: which 3 screens are ready?  (15 min)
2. Android login bug: fix before or after release?  (10 min)
3. Action items and next steps  (5 min)
```

Notice the small details. The time is in UTC and in local times, so nobody has to guess. Each topic is a question that needs an answer. One person is named to take notes.

## During the meeting

The **facilitator** guides the meeting. This does not need to be the most senior person. Their job is simple:

- Start on time and say the goal in one sentence.
- Keep to the agenda. When a new topic comes up, add it to a **parking lot**: a list of topics to handle later, so they are not forgotten.
- Before moving on, say the decision out loud: "So we agree the login bug is fixed before the release. Is that right?"
- In the last five minutes, read the action items aloud. Each one needs one owner and one date.

## Sample meeting notes

Notes do not need full sentences or a record of who said what. They need three things: decisions, action items and open questions. Post them in the shared channel the same day.

```text
Notes: volunteer app check-in, Tue 10 Nov
Present: Hadeel, Mahmoud (audio only), Ines, Sofia

Decisions
- First release on Fri 20 Nov has 3 screens:
  sign up, shift list, profile.
- The Android login bug is fixed before the release.

Action items
- Mahmoud: fix the Android login bug, by Thu 12 Nov
- Ines: send Sofia the test link, by Mon 16 Nov
- Sofia: ask 5 volunteers to test the app, by Wed 18 Nov
- Ines: post these notes in #volunteer-app, today

Open questions
- Do volunteers need Arabic and English at launch?
  Sofia asks her director and replies by Fri 13 Nov.
```

<Callout type="tip" title="Write dates, not 'next week'">
"Next Tuesday" or "end of week" means different days to different people, especially across time zones. Write the day and the date, like "Thu 12 Nov". Add a time and "UTC" when the hour matters.
</Callout>

## When your internet is weak

Mahmoud joined this call with a weak connection. He still took part fully, because he planned for it. You can do the same:

- **Tell the facilitator early.** Send a short message before the call starts.
- **Post your update in writing first.** Then the team has it even if your audio cuts out.
- **Join with audio only.** Turn your camera off. Video uses much more data than audio.
- **Check the invite for a phone number.** Some meeting tools let you dial in by phone, which can be more stable than mobile data.
- **Use the meeting chat.** If your audio breaks, type your question or your answer.
- **If you drop, say so when you return.** "I dropped at 14:12 and I am back. Did we decide on the login bug?"
- **Ask for the notes,** and reply in writing to confirm your action items.

Here is the message Mahmoud sent before the call:

> Hi Hadeel, my connection is weak today, so I will join with audio only. My update is already in the channel. If I drop, I will rejoin and follow the chat. Could Ines share the notes after the call? Thank you.

If you miss a call completely, read the notes first. Then ask a specific question, such as "I saw the release has 3 screens. Is the profile screen still mine?" A specific question is faster to answer than "What did I miss?"

## Check your understanding

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Which line from meeting notes is the most useful action item?"
options:
  - "Android login bug: discussed at length, and everyone agreed it is a high priority to fix soon"
  - "Everyone: watch the Android login bug and report anything new in the channel"

  - "Android login bug: Mahmoud and Ines will both look into it within the next two weeks"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "This records a discussion, not a task. It has no owner and no date, so nobody is sure who acts."

  - "Two owners often means each one waits for the other. 'The next two weeks' is also vague."
```

## Common questions

**Q: Who should write the notes?**
A: Name one person in the agenda. It can rotate each week. The facilitator should not also take notes in a busy meeting, because guiding and writing at the same time is hard.

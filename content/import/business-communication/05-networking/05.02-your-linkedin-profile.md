## A profile is an introduction you are not there to give

When you send a message, comment on a post or apply for a project, the other person often clicks your name first. They want to know one thing: who is this, and what do they do?

You are not there to explain. Your profile has to do it for you, often on a small phone screen, and often in a few seconds. That is why the top of your profile matters most: your headline and the first lines of your About section.

## The headline: what you do and for whom

A weak headline describes your feelings or your hopes. A strong headline describes your work. A simple pattern:

**Role + what you build or do + for whom.** Add one tool or one piece of proof if there is room.

Here are some before and after examples:

| Before | After |
|---|---|
| Computer Science student, looking for opportunities | CS student and junior Python developer, building data-cleaning scripts for NGOs |
| Hardworking, passionate developer | Backend developer (Node.js, PostgreSQL), building APIs for early-stage startups |
| Freelancer | Freelance WordPress developer, Arabic and English websites for clinics and shops |
| Aspiring UI/UX designer | UI designer for mobile apps, with a focus on clear forms and onboarding |

Notice what changed. The "after" versions name a role, a skill and a type of client. "Looking for opportunities" tells the reader what you want. A good headline tells them what you offer.

<Callout type="warning" title="Only write what you can defend">
Do not call yourself "senior" or "expert" if you are not. A client or interviewer will ask about it. "Junior" is not a weak word: it is honest, and it sets the right expectations.
</Callout>

## The About section: open with your work

Your About section is a few short paragraphs in your own words. On many screens, readers only see the first lines before a "see more" link. So put the most useful sentences first.

A simple structure:

1. **What you do and for whom.** One or two sentences.
2. **Proof.** Two or three real projects, each with one concrete detail.
3. **How you work.** For example, how you communicate across time zones.
4. **What you are looking for.** And how to contact you.

Here is Lina's About section before she rewrote it:

> I am a highly motivated and passionate person who loves technology and learning new things. I believe in hard work and I am always ready for new challenges. I have skills in many programming languages and I am a fast learner. I am looking for any opportunity to grow.

Every sentence is about her feelings. A reader learns nothing they can check.

Here it is after:

> I build mobile apps with Flutter for small organizations that need simple, reliable tools.
>
> Recent work: a volunteer sign-up app for a local NGO, used to organize weekly food distribution. An offline-first notes app that saves data on the phone and syncs when the internet returns. A booking screen redesign for a dental clinic.
>
> I work well with remote teams. I write a short update at the end of each day, so people in other time zones always know where things stand.
>
> I am looking for junior Flutter roles or freelance projects. Message me here.

The second version is not longer because of big words. It is longer because of facts. Each project shows a skill, and the update habit shows how she works.

## Small details that help

- **Write in the language of the people you want to reach.** For international clients, that usually means English.
- **Use the Featured section** to link your best project, so readers can see it in one click.
- **Read it on your phone.** If the first two lines do not make sense alone, rewrite them.

## Common questions

**Q: Should I mention where I live?**
A: Your location helps people plan around time zones. How much you share about your situation is your choice. Keep the focus on the work you do.

**Q: I have no paid experience yet. What do I write?**
A: Write about real projects: course projects, volunteer work, open source contributions or tools you built for yourself. Describe what you built and what problem it solved.

Six short interview scenarios. Each one has one best answer. Read the explanation after every question, even when you get it right: the reasons will help you in a real interview.

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Huda answers 'Tell me about yourself' by starting with her school years. After four minutes she has not yet reached her current job. What is the best change for next time?"
options:
  - "Keep the school years, but speak faster so the whole story fits in two minutes"
  - "Start with what she does now, and keep the answer to one or two minutes"
  - "Answer in one sentence with only her name and job title, so it stays short"
  - "Read her CV aloud in order, so that she does not forget any role or date"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "Speaking faster makes her harder to follow, and the school years are still the least useful part for the interviewer."
  - "Right. The present matters most to the interviewer, so it comes first, and one or two minutes keeps the answer focused."
  - "One sentence is too short. It gives the interviewer nothing about her skills or why she wants the role."
  - "The interviewer has already read her CV. Reading it aloud adds nothing and sounds unprepared."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Hani is asked about a hard problem. He says: 'We had a slow dashboard. We looked into it, we changed the queries, and we made it three times faster.' What is the main weakness of this answer?"
options:
  - "It does not show what Hani did himself, because every step says 'we'"
  - "It gives a number in the result, which can sound like showing off"
  - "It is about a technical problem, but these questions are about people"
  - "It needs more background about the dashboard and who was using it"
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. The interviewer is hiring Hani, not the team. He should say what he did: 'I found the slow query and rewrote it.'"
  - "A number makes the result clear and believable. It is a strength of the answer, not a weakness."
  - "Behavioral questions can be about any real situation, including technical problems."
  - "The situation should stay short. The real gap is the action: we do not know Hani's part."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Maryam is asked: 'Tell me about a time you led a team.' She has never been a team lead. What is the best way to answer?"
options:
  - "Invent a short story about leading a team, because the interviewer cannot check it"
  - "Say that she has no experience of leading, and then wait for the next question"
  - "Tell her old team lead's story as if it were hers, since she was on that team"
  - "Say she has not been a lead, then tell a true story where she led one task"
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "Follow-up questions quickly show when a story is invented, and then the interviewer cannot trust her other answers either."
  - "Being honest is right, but stopping there wastes the chance to show the skill the question is really about."
  - "This is taking credit for someone else's work. It is dishonest, and follow-up questions will expose it."
  - "Right. She is honest, and she still gives real evidence of leadership, such as organizing her group's work on a project."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Near the end of his interview, Ahmad asks: 'What is the hardest part of this team's work right now?' The interviewer says: 'New developers take about two months before they are really productive.' What is the best thing for Ahmad to say next?"
options:
  - "Thank you. My next question is about the company's plans for next year."
  - "That is normal. In most companies new developers need a few months."
  - "What makes the first months hard? In my last team I wrote our setup guide."
  - "I see. How many days of paid holiday do new team members usually get?"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "This ignores what the interviewer just shared. Moving straight to the next question on a list misses a real conversation."
  - "This closes the topic instead of exploring it, and it does not show how Ahmad could help."
  - "Right. He listens, asks a follow-up, and links the answer to something he has done that could help."
  - "Holidays are a fair topic, but this ignores the answer and suggests he is not interested in the team's problem."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Ten minutes into a video interview, Rana's home internet drops. Her phone still has mobile data. What should she do first?"
options:
  - "Wait for the home internet to come back, then rejoin and explain the delay"
  - "Send the interviewer a short email that she is rejoining, then join by phone"
  - "Email the recruiter to cancel and ask for a new interview next week instead"
  - "Rejoin from her phone, then apologize several times so they see she cares"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "She does not know how long the outage will last. The interviewer is left waiting with no information."
  - "Right. A short message tells the interviewer what is happening, and the phone gets her back quickly."
  - "Cancelling is too much for a problem she can solve in two minutes, and it may cost her the chance."
  - "Rejoining is good, but many apologies take time and make the problem bigger than it is. One short sorry is enough."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Which sentence is the strongest Result for a STAR answer about improving a client's website?"
options:
  - "Page load time fell from six seconds to two, and the client renewed for a year."
  - "In the end the client was very happy with it, and everything went really well."
  - "I worked very hard on it, often late at night, for almost three whole weeks."
  - "We used React, Node.js and Redis, which are very modern and popular tools."
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. It shows what changed with a number, and how the client reacted. The interviewer can believe and remember it."
  - "This is a feeling, not a result. It gives the interviewer no facts about what changed."
  - "This describes effort, not the result. Hard work matters, but the interviewer wants to know what it achieved."
  - "This lists tools, not results. It also says 'we', so it does not show what the speaker did."
```

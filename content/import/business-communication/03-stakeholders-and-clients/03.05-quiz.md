Six short work scenarios about clients, teammates and users. Each one has one best answer. Read the explanation after every question, even when you get it right: the reasons matter more than the score.

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Farah works on a remote team. A database change will make the app's admin panel unavailable for two hours on Saturday. The client's support team uses the admin panel every day. What should Farah's message to the support team focus on?"
options:
  - "When the admin panel will be unavailable, and what to do with customer requests during that time"
  - "The full technical plan for the database change, so they can answer any question from customers"
  - "Nothing, because the client's manager will pass on whatever the support team needs to know"
  - "A short apology for the trouble, and a promise that the change will make the app faster later"
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. The support team cares about their working day. They need the time window and a plan for customer requests."
  - "The support team does not need the technical plan. A long technical message hides the two facts they need."
  - "The manager may forget, or pass on only part of it. The support team is directly affected, so they should hear it clearly."
  - "An apology and a promise do not tell them when the panel is down or what to do. They still cannot plan their day."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Mei is two weeks into a fixed-price website project for a bakery. The owner asks her to 'quickly add' online ordering. It was not in the agreed scope and would take about two weeks. What is the best reply?"
options:
  - "Agree to add it, because refusing a request this early could damage the new relationship"
  - "Refuse, because the agreed scope does not include online ordering and the price is fixed"
  - "Thank her, say it is outside the agreed scope, and offer options with their time and cost"
  - "Add a simple version over the weekend for free, then mention the extra work at the end"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "Two weeks of extra work with no change in date or price is scope creep. Mei will be late or work for free, and the owner will be surprised either way."
  - "The scope is on Mei's side, but a flat refusal closes the door. The owner may be happy to pay more or wait longer."
  - "Right. Mei treats it as a choice, not a yes or a no. The owner sees the cost and decides."
  - "This hides the real cost. A rushed free version may be poor, and raising the extra work later feels like a surprise bill."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Wael's NGO client expects a reporting dashboard on the 20th. On the 8th, he learns that the NGO's data export will not be ready until the 18th, which leaves him too little time. When should he tell the client?"
options:
  - "On the 18th, when the data arrives and he can see exactly how much work is left"
  - "Now, explaining what the late data means for the 20th and what the options are"
  - "On the 20th, together with the parts of the dashboard he has finished by then"
  - "After asking the data team to hurry, in case they deliver sooner than planned"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "On the 18th the client has only two days left to adjust. He already knows the date is at risk, so he should share it."
  - "Right. Twelve days before the deadline, the client still has real choices, such as moving the date or getting the data out sooner."
  - "This turns a risk the client could have managed into a surprise on the deadline itself."
  - "He can ask the data team to hurry, but that is no reason to wait. The client should hear about the risk now, at the same time."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Malik writes to his client: 'The login bug is fixed on our test server, but the fix broke password reset. Users cannot reset passwords until Tuesday. Option A: release the login fix now, without password reset. Option B: release both on Tuesday.' What is the most important thing missing?"
options:
  - "An apology at the start of the message, before any of the facts"
  - "More technical detail on why the fix broke the password reset"
  - "The names of the teammates who worked on fixing the login bug"
  - "His recommendation, and when he needs the client's decision"
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "A short apology can help if the problem is his fault, but the message works without one. A missing decision path is a bigger gap."
  - "The client needs to decide, not debug. More technical detail makes the message longer without helping the choice."
  - "Names do not help the client choose between the options, and they can sound like blame."
  - "Right. He has the facts, the impact and the options. Without a recommendation and a deadline, the client is left to decide alone, with no time frame."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Sophie's release for a client is one week late, because a teammate delivered his part late. Which sentence is best in her message to the client?"
options:
  - "One part of our work took a week longer than planned. The new release date is 3 June."
  - "My teammate delivered his part a week late, so this delay is not caused by my work."
  - "Some unexpected issues came up in the team, so the date may move a little bit later."
  - "I am very sorry for the delay, it is completely my fault and it will not happen again."
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. It says honestly what happened and gives the new date, without naming or blaming anyone. The client gets what they need to plan."
  - "This puts blame on a teammate in front of the client. The client sees one team, so blame makes the whole team look weak."
  - "This hides the news. 'May move a little bit' gives no date, so the client cannot plan."
  - "Taking all the blame is not honest here, and a long apology does not tell the client the new date."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Basma maintains a small online store for a client. During an update, she deleted 40 product photos by mistake. She restored most of them from a backup, but 6 new photos are lost. What is the best way to tell the client?"
options:
  - "Send a short chat message tonight, and give the full details at next week's update"
  - "Call the client today, then send a written summary of the facts, impact and next steps"
  - "Upload the restored photos quietly, and tell the client only if they notice the 6 missing"
  - "Write a long email explaining exactly how the deletion happened, step by step, before she calls"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "Lost data is a big problem for a store. A short message and a week's wait leave the client without the full picture for too long."
  - "Right. Lost data deserves a call, so the client can ask questions. The written summary makes sure nothing is forgotten."
  - "Hiding the loss breaks trust. If the client finds out later, they will wonder what else was hidden."
  - "The client needs the impact and next steps, not every step of the mistake. A long story delays the news that matters."
```

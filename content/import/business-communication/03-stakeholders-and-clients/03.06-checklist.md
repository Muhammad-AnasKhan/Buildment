## Module 3 checklist

- [ ] I can name the four groups of stakeholders: decide, build, use and affected
- [ ] I can list the three things to agree at the start of a project: scope, timing and communication
- [ ] I can answer a new request with options and their costs, instead of a quick yes or no
- [ ] I can name the four parts of a bad-news message and the next step that ends it
- [ ] I answered the questions in chapters 3.1 to 3.4, and finished the quiz in 3.5
- [ ] I submitted my bad-news message at the bottom of this page

## Learning log

1. **Your stakeholders:** Go back to the project you described in chapter 3.1. Which person did you think about least while you worked on it? What did they need from you?
2. **Your agreement:** At the start of that project, what was agreed in writing, and what was only in people's heads? What would you write down next time?
3. **Your habit with bad news:** When something goes wrong, do you usually tell people early, or wait until you have fixed it? Why?
4. **The hardest part:** Of facts, impact, options and recommendation, which part is hardest for you to write? What will help you?

## Your bad-news message

Write a message to a client that shares a delay or a problem. Use a real situation from your work, studies or volunteering if you have one. Change the names if you like. If you do not have one, use this:

> You are building a website for Nadia, who runs a small travel agency in Berlin. The site should launch on the 15th. Today is the 9th. The online form that sends booking requests to Nadia's email stopped working after the hosting company changed its email settings. A full fix, tested, takes about four working days. You could also launch with a simple "email us" link instead of the form, and add the form later.

A mentor will review your message.

```openquestion
prompt: "Write the actual message you would send to the client, with a subject line. Include: (1) the facts, in plain words; (2) the impact on the client; (3) two or three options, each with its cost; (4) your recommendation and why; (5) a next step: what you need from the client, by when, and when you will update them. Then add one sentence on why you chose email, a chat message or a call for this news."
required: true
minWords: 120
allowSpeechInput: true
modelAnswer: "Subject: Website launch: booking form problem and two options. Hi Nadia, the booking form on your new website stopped sending requests to your email. This started after the hosting company changed its email settings this week. No customer requests were lost, because the site is not live yet. Impact: a full fix, tested, takes about four working days, so it may not be ready before the 15th. That leaves no time for you to check it before launch. Options: 1. Move the launch to the 18th, with the form fully working and tested. 2. Launch on the 15th with an 'Email us' button instead of the form, and add the form on the 18th. Customers can still contact you, but their requests will be less organized for three days. My recommendation: option 2. You keep your launch date, and customers can reach you from day one. Could you tell me by tomorrow at 14:00 your time which option you prefer? I am working on the fix today either way, and I will update you on Friday. Best regards, Huda. I chose email because the problem is not urgent today and Nadia needs time to think, but I would offer a short call if she has questions."
```

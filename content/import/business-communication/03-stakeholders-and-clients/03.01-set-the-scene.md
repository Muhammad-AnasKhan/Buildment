## One project, four people

Rami is a freelance web developer in Gaza. A language school in Lyon, France, has hired him to build an online booking page, so students can book classes without calling the school. The project is six weeks long.

Four people care about this project:

- **Claire** owns the school. She pays Rami and approves the work.
- **Dina** is a freelance designer in Amman. She designs the screens, and Rami builds them.
- **Samir** is the school's receptionist. He will use the booking system every day to manage classes.
- **The students** will book classes on their phones. Rami never talks to them directly.

In week three, three things happen. Dina's designs arrive four days late, because Claire changed the colors twice. On a call, Claire asks for "one small extra": a waiting list for full classes. And Samir sends a message to Claire, not to Rami: "I tried the test version. I cannot see which students cancelled. This will make my job harder."

Rami had only talked to Claire. Now everyone is a little unhappy, and he does not know who to answer first.

## Why this matters for your career

When you work remotely, people cannot see you working. They see your messages. If the right people hear the right news at the right time, they trust you, even when things go wrong. If they hear it late, or from someone else, they start to worry, even when your work is good.

In module 2 you practiced writing clear emails and updates. This module is about **who** reads them, **what** each person needs to know, and **how** to share news that nobody wants to hear.

<Callout type="info" title="This is not about pleasing everyone">
You cannot give every stakeholder everything they want. The goal is that nobody is surprised: each person knows what is happening, what it means for them, and what comes next.
</Callout>

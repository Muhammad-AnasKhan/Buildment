## Two updates, one morning

Tariq and Salma are junior developers at a startup in Berlin. Every morning before 10:00 Berlin time, each person posts a written standup in the team channel. Their team lead, Jonas, reads them all with his first coffee.

Tariq wrote:

```text
Worked on the login stuff. Will continue today. No blockers.
```

Salma wrote:

```text
Done: password reset email works on the test server (PR #142).
Next: add the "remember me" option, ready for review by
Wednesday.
Blockers: none.
```

Both are short. But only Salma's update tells Jonas something. From Tariq's, Jonas cannot tell whether the login page is 10% done or 90% done. So he has to message Tariq and ask, which costs both of them time.

## Done, next, blockers

Most standups use three parts. Many teams write them as three short lines or bullets.

| Part | What to write | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Done** | What you finished since the last update | Fixed the date bug in the booking form (PR #88) |
| **Next** | What you will work on until the next update | Write tests for the booking form |
| **Blockers** | What stops you, and who can help | Need the staging password from Felix |

Four habits make these lines useful.

**Finished results, not effort.** "Worked on the login page" describes effort. "Login page works with email and password" describes a result. If the work is not finished, say how far it is: "Login page: form done, error messages still to do."

**Things people can check.** Add a link or a number when you have one: a pull request (PR, a request to add your code to the project), a ticket, a page. Your lead can click and see.

**A date for bigger tasks.** "Ready for review by Wednesday" helps people plan around you.

**"None" when there are no blockers.** It tells the reader that you checked, not that you forgot.

## Blockers need a name and a request

A **blocker** stops your work until someone or something changes it. Many people hide blockers because they feel it looks weak. In good teams it is the opposite: an early blocker is a gift, because there is still time to fix it.

```predict
prompt: "A week later, Salma is stuck on the payment feature. Which blocker line is more likely to get her unblocked today?"
options:
  - id: specific
    label: "Blocked: I need the payment test key. Felix, could you share it today?"
  - id: general
    label: "Blocked: still waiting on the payment API, will try again tomorrow."
correctOptionId: specific
explanation: "The first line names the person who can help, the exact thing needed and a time. The second describes a problem but asks nobody for anything, so it can stay stuck for days."
```

A good blocker line says three things: what you need, who can give it, and by when. Mention the person by name (with an @mention in chat) so they see it.

## When something outside your control slows you

Some days you lose hours to a power cut or a weak connection. You do not need to explain or apologize at length. Say the fact once and give the effect on your plan:

```text
Done: search results page, first version (PR #151).
Next: filters for the search page. Lost about three hours
yesterday to a power cut, so filters will be ready
Thursday, not Wednesday.
Blockers: none.
```

This is professional. Your lead now has the real date and can plan with it. Hiding the delay until the deadline is much worse for trust.

## The weekly status update

A standup is for your team, every day. A **weekly status update** is for a manager or a client, once a week. It is a little longer and starts with one line on the overall state of the work, so a busy reader gets the main point first.

```text
Subject: Booking system: weekly status, week 4

Status: on track for launch on 30 June.

Done this week:
- Payments work in test mode
- Staff can block dates in the calendar

Next week:
- Email reminders to guests
- Mobile layout fixes

Risks: the guesthouse's new logo is not ready yet. The site
can launch with the old logo if needed.

Need from you: confirm the reminder text by Tuesday.
```

Common overall states are **on track** (going as planned), **at risk** (might be late, and you have a plan) and **off track** (will be late). In module 3 you learn how to share bad news like an "off track" week well.

## Check your understanding

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Reem is a junior QA tester at an NGO. Which standup update is the most useful for her team?"
options:
  - "Done: tested a lot of things on the app. Next: more testing. Blockers: none for now."
  - "Done: tested sign-up on Android, found 2 bugs (tickets 31, 32). Next: test sign-up on iPhone. Blockers: none."
  - "Busy day yesterday with many meetings and lots of testing. Today I hope to finish more if the internet stays stable."
  - "Done: everything planned. Next: whatever the team needs. Blockers: none."
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "This describes effort with no detail. Nobody can tell what was tested or what is left."
  - "Right. Each part is specific and can be checked, and the bugs have ticket numbers."
  - "It talks about being busy, not about results. It also has no clear next step or blockers line."
  - "This sounds complete but says nothing. The team still has to ask what was done."
```

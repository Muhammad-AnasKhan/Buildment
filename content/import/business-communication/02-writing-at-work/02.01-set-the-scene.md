## A day in writing

Nour is a junior frontend developer in Gaza. She works for a small startup with a team lead in Amsterdam and a designer in Toronto. She also has a freelance client, a small NGO in Jordan.

Here is what her day looked like on Monday:

- At 08:30 she read six chat messages that arrived overnight from Toronto.
- At 09:30 she posted her daily update in the team channel.
- At 11:00 she emailed the NGO to ask for the final text for their website.
- At 14:00 she asked the designer a question in chat. He answered at 16:30, when his day started.
- At 17:00 she replied to a code review comment.

Nour did not speak to anyone on Monday. All of her work with other people happened in writing. Her team lead has never watched her code. He knows her through her messages: what she finished, what she needs, and how quickly people understand her.

## Why writing matters so much

In an office, you can fix a confusing sentence with a quick conversation. In a remote team, a confusing message can cost a whole day. If Nour's question at 14:00 is unclear, the designer in Toronto may answer the wrong question at 16:30. Then she has to ask again, and the right answer may not come until the next day.

Clear writing does three things for you:

1. **It saves time.** One clear message replaces three rounds of questions.
2. **It builds trust.** People trust colleagues whose updates are easy to read and match what really happened.
3. **It survives problems.** If the power or the internet goes out, a clear message you already sent keeps the work moving.

<Callout type="info" title="Good news">
Clear work writing is not about fancy English. It is about a few simple habits: say what you need, say it early, and make the next step obvious. You can learn these habits even if English is your second language.
</Callout>

## Key words

| Word | Plain English |
|---|---|
| **Subject line** | The short title of an email that people see in their inbox |
| **Request** | What you are asking the other person to do |
| **Status update** | A short report on how your work is going |
| **Standup** | A short daily update: what you did, what you will do, what is stopping you |
| **Blocker** | Something that stops your work until someone or something changes it |
| **Thread** | A chain of replies under one chat message, so one topic stays in one place |
| **Time zone** | The local time in a place. Your 15:00 may be someone else's 05:00 |

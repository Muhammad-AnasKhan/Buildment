## One short message

Nour is a freelance web developer. She has a new client, Anna, who runs a small online shop in Germany. Nour needs access to the shop's hosting account before she can start. She writes quickly, between two power cuts:

> Send me the hosting login today.

Nour means it as a normal request. The message is short because she is busy, not because she is upset. But Anna reads it at her desk and frowns. To her, it sounds like an order from a boss. She replies two days later, and her reply is shorter and colder than her first messages.

Nothing big went wrong. But Nour lost two days of work, and a little bit of trust, because of one sentence.

## Why this matters for your career

When you work remotely, clients and teammates rarely see you work. They see your messages. They decide how reliable you are from your emails, your chat messages and your calls. Clear and polite English tells them: this person is easy to work with.

The good news: you do not need perfect grammar or a big vocabulary. Most international teams use simple English, and many of your teammates also speak English as a second language. What helps most is a small set of habits: short sentences, clear requests, polite forms, and knowing the common words people use at work.

<Callout type="info" title="Your language is not the problem">
Every language has its own way of being polite. A message that sounds warm in Arabic can sound cold when you translate it word for word into English, and the same happens in the other direction. This course does not ask you to change how you think. It gives you the English tools that international readers expect.
</Callout>

## Key words

| Word | Plain English |
|---|---|
| **Tone** | How a message feels to the reader, for example friendly, neutral or cold |
| **Client** | A person or company that pays you for your work |
| **Stakeholder** | Anyone affected by your work or interested in it |
| **Status update** | A short message about what is done and what comes next |
| **Remote work** | Working from a different place than your team or client |
| **Time zone** | A region of the world that uses the same clock time |

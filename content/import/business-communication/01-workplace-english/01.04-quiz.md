```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Karim's client sends him a long list of feedback on a website. Karim wants to say that he received it and will start tomorrow. Which reply has the best tone?"
options:
  - "Thanks for the feedback. I will start on it tomorrow morning."
  - "OK, received. I will look at all of it tomorrow when I can."
  - "That is a lot of changes!! I will try to do them all tomorrow."
  - "Received your feedback. Noted. Will start tomorrow."
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. It thanks the client, says clearly what happens next, and gives a time. It sounds friendly and reliable."
  - "'OK, received' can feel cold, and 'when I can' makes the plan sound uncertain."
  - "'!!' and 'a lot of changes' sound like a complaint. 'I will try' also makes the client worry."
  - "Short notes like 'Noted' with no greeting or thanks can read as cold or annoyed, even when you do not mean it."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "In a team call, Leen thinks her teammate's estimate of two days for a feature is too short. Which sentence says this clearly and politely?"
options:
  - "Your estimate is wrong. This will take at least a week, not two days."
  - "Maybe it is fine, I don't know, it is just a feeling I have, sorry."
  - "Two days is not realistic. You always estimate too low."
  - "I think it may take longer. The API part alone could take two days."
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "This is clear, but 'your estimate is wrong' sounds like an attack on the person, not a point about the work."
  - "This is so soft that the team cannot tell what Leen thinks, and it gives no reason they can check."
  - "'You always' turns a point about one estimate into a criticism of the person."
  - "Right. 'I think' softens the point, and the reason about the API part gives the team something specific to check."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Mazen works from Gaza for a startup in Singapore, which is several hours ahead of him. In the morning, his team lead writes: 'Please send the test results by EOD.' What should Mazen do?"
options:
  - "Plan to send them by the end of his own working day in Gaza"
  - "Reply 'Sure!' and send them whenever the testing work is finished"
  - "Confirm the time first, for example: 'Is 5 pm your time OK?'"
  - "Send them tomorrow morning, since EOD is only a rough guess"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "Singapore is ahead of Gaza, so the end of Mazen's day may come after his team lead's day has ended. He could be late without knowing it."
  - "'Sure!' is friendly, but 'whenever' ignores the deadline. The team lead asked for a specific day."
  - "Right. EOD depends on whose day it is. One short question gives Mazen an exact time in his team lead's time zone."
  - "EOD is a deadline, not a guess. Tomorrow morning is after the end of today in both time zones."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Hana is building a five-page website for a client, Daniel. Their agreement does not include a blog. Daniel writes: 'Can you also add a blog?' Which reply is the most professional?"
options:
  - "Sure, no problem. I will add the blog this week as well."
  - "That is outside our current scope. Can I send you a price for it?"
  - "Sorry, I cannot do that. It was not in our agreement."
  - "That is a lot of extra work, and I am not sure I will have the time."
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "This is friendly, but Hana takes on unpaid work and may miss the agreed deadline for the five pages."
  - "Right. 'Outside our current scope' says politely that this is new work, and the question offers a clear next step."
  - "This is clear, but it ends the conversation and sounds cold. Daniel may want to pay for the blog."
  - "This is vague. Daniel cannot tell if the answer is yes or no, or what would make it possible."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Aya's project manager writes: 'What is your ETA on the search feature?' What is she asking for?"
options:
  - "How many hours Aya has spent on the feature so far"
  - "Whether anything is stopping Aya's work on the feature"
  - "How much the feature will cost the client in total"
  - "When Aya expects the search feature to be finished"
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "That is time already spent. ETA looks forward, not back."
  - "That would be a question about blockers, not an ETA."
  - "ETA is about time, not money. A cost question would mention a price or a budget."
  - "Right. ETA means 'estimated time of arrival': when she expects the work to be done."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Nour asked her client, Anna, whether her shop will accept cards only or digital wallets too. Anna has not replied for three days, and Nour cannot continue without the answer. Which follow-up message is best?"
options:
  - "Hi Anna, following up on Monday: cards only, or wallets too? I need this to continue."
  - "Hi Anna, why have you not answered my question from Monday? I am still waiting for you."
  - "Hi Anna, I am sorry to disturb you again, I hope it is not a problem, just checking in."
  - "Hi Anna, please reply to my last email ASAP. This is now very urgent for the project."
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. It repeats the question so Anna can answer in one line, and it gives the reason it matters, without blame."
  - "'Why have you not answered' sounds like blame. Anna may feel accused instead of helped."
  - "This is polite, but it does not repeat the question or say why it matters. Anna still has to search for the first email."
  - "'ASAP' and 'very urgent' add pressure but no detail. Anna still has to find the question and does not know why it blocks the work."
```

Souq is a small online shop (a made-up app). A shopper who cannot add to the cart goes to another shop. Look at the two pictures below and ask yourself: what would you try first, and what do you think it will cost?

## The cart that said try again

<InteractiveCanvas slug="sysdesign-m13-cart-refuses" />

Hana taps Add milk. Souq's cart has one leader, the database that takes every change. The leader is down, so nobody may take the add. The other copy can only be read. Hana sees an error and may leave. Nothing was lost, except a sale.

<GoDeeper title="Go deeper: why one leader refuses, and why a cart cannot wait">

In chapters 8.2 to 8.5 you built stores with one leader. They are easy to reason about, because the leader puts every write in one order. The price is that writes stop while the leader is down. A follower can be promoted, but detecting the failure and promoting takes time, and during that time every add is refused.

For many data, that is fine. A bank transfer should wait rather than guess. A shopping cart is different. An add is a small, friendly act, and a refused add costs a sale and a bit of trust. The Dynamo paper from Amazon was written around this need: the cart should accept an add even during failures, and sort out any difference later [S45].

Souq is a fictional composite. The failure in the picture is a made-up minute, not a measured one.

</GoDeeper>

## The cart that always said yes

<InteractiveCanvas slug="sysdesign-m13-cart-accepts" />

Now the same failure, with no leader. Node B is down, and nobody waits for it. Hana's add goes to node A and Dina's goes to node C, at the same time. Both shoppers are told OK. Then the nodes compare notes and find two different carts.

<GoDeeper title="Go deeper: what this module builds, and in what order">

Saying yes all the time is a real design, not a trick. This module builds it in the order a learner meets the problems.

Chapter 13.2 draws the whole Dynamo store: how a key finds its nodes, and how many nodes a write or a read must reach (N, W and R). Chapter 13.3 covers what happens when nodes are down or slow: stand-ins, hints, and the background repair that finds the rest. Chapter 13.4 sets this beside DynamoDB, the managed service, which is a different design.

Then the price. Chapter 13.5 shows the simplest way to settle two versions, last write wins, and how it drops a write silently. Chapter 13.6 keeps both versions and merges them, and shows a deleted item coming back. Chapter 13.7 shows data types, CRDTs, that merge to the right answer in any order. You write them in 13.8 and run them on a simulated network in challenge 12.

The question to hold on to is the one the second picture ends on: when two shoppers change the same cart at the same time, what should the cart look like afterwards? Every chapter after this one is a different answer, with a different cost.

</GoDeeper>

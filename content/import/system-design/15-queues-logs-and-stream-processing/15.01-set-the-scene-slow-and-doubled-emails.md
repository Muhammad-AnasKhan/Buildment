Zaytoun Market is a made-up neighbourhood shop with an app. When a customer creates an account, the app sends a welcome email. Two small things go wrong, and both come from the same choice.

## Layla waits for an email

<InteractiveCanvas slug="sysdesign-m15-signup-waits" />

Layla taps Create account. The app saves her account in 40 ms, then calls the email service and waits. Today the email service is slow, so she waits 3 seconds before the app answers. She does not care about the email. She only wants to be inside the app.

<GoDeeper title="Go deeper: what the wait costs the shop">

Use Little's law from chapter 2.5: the number of requests inside the system equals the arrival rate times the time each one stays. Say 50 signups arrive each second on a busy evening (a course number). If each signup takes 40 ms, about 2 requests are inside the API at any moment. If each one also waits 3 seconds for the email service, each request stays 3.04 seconds, and about 152 requests are inside at any moment.

That is 76 times more. Each waiting request holds a connection, some memory and a worker. A pool of 20 workers, which was plenty at 2, is now far too small. The email service did not get busier. Our own app became slow and then full, because it did work that did not need to finish before it answered.

There is a second cost. The signup now fails whenever the email service fails. A customer who could have created an account is told "something went wrong" because a welcome message could not be sent.

</GoDeeper>

## The retry sends it twice

<InteractiveCanvas slug="sysdesign-m15-email-twice" />

The app gives up after 2 seconds and tries again. The first request was only slow, not lost, so the email service sends both. Layla finds two welcome emails. The app could not tell "slow" from "lost", and the retry did the work twice.

What would you try first, to make the signup fast and send only one email?

<GoDeeper title="Go deeper: why the app cannot tell">

From where the app stands, silence has two causes. The email service may never have received the request, or it may have received it and be slow. Both look the same: no answer in 2 seconds. Chapter 12.2 showed the same problem for a monitor that cannot tell a slow node from a dead one.

If the app does not retry, a lost request means no email. If it retries, a slow request means two emails. Neither choice is free. Later in this module you will see that most systems choose the retry, because a repeat can be made harmless and a loss cannot be repaired. The repair for the repeat is the idea from chapter 14.5: make the receiving side safe to run twice.

</GoDeeper>

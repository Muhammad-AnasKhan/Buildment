## Your gate answer: Halqa

This is the case study again on a different product. Halqa (a made-up service) is an app for study circles. A member joins circles, and her feed shows the posts from the circles she belongs to. Here are the assumptions. There are 2 million members, and 1 million of them are active each day. An active member opens the feed 6 times a day. There are 20,000 circles. A member belongs to about 10 circles, so there are about 20 million memberships and a circle has 1,000 members on average. The biggest circle has 300,000 members. All circles together get 100,000 posts a day. The busiest second is 3 times the average, and a feed should open in under 300 ms at p99. In an interview this is "design a news feed" with a twist, and it is the same loop you just ran.

A weak answer copies Hikaya: a timeline store, a fan-out fleet, a star threshold and a ranker, without checking the numbers. A strong answer starts from the numbers and lets them decide which parts to draw. Compute the feed opens a second and the posts a second at the peak. Then price both ways of building a feed. For push, count the timeline writes a day and the cost of one post in the biggest circle. For pull, count the lookups for one feed open and the lookups a second. Chapter 21.1 showed that Hikaya's reads were 50 times its posts and that decided almost everything. Does Halqa have that problem? If both ways fit easily, you may defend the simpler one, and say when you would change your mind.

Then say what the read path looks like, how a reader scrolls to the next page without repeats, and whether you rank at all. Say what happens when your most important block fails. A reader of your answer should be able to redraw your design from the words.

Be explicit about one trade-off. Write it in the same shape each time: the choice, why now, what it buys, what it costs, and when you would switch. A good cost line has a number or a name. For example: "10 lookups on every feed open, so about 1 open in 10 meets a slow lookup," or "a timeline store of 6 GB to keep in step with every join and leave." If your cost line says "some complexity", it is not finished.

A good order for 250 words is the order of the loop. Numbers come first, in two or three lines. Then the build and the read path, which are the sketch and the deep dive. Then the trade-off, and last the failure. If you run short of words, cut the sketch, not the numbers or the trade-off.

Watch for three common mistakes. The first is choosing a design before computing a single number, so parts appear that nothing in the estimate asks for. The second is forgetting the reader's own post: whatever you cache, a member who posts must see the post at once. The third is a cursor that is a page number, which repeats posts whenever a new one arrives.

A mentor reads for method, not for one right design. Two students can choose differently and both pass, as long as each one starts from the numbers, ties the choice to them, names the failure the choice answers, and says when they would change their mind. A mentor may ask one or two follow-up questions, such as what happens when a circle grows to 3 million members, or how you would keep a member from seeing a circle she has left. Answer them by editing and resubmitting. You may write your answer, record it, or both. The written answer is required, about 250 words. A recording is optional and should show only your work, not your surroundings.

Push would cost 100,000 posts x 1,000 members, 100 million timeline writes a day, about 3,500 a second at the peak, and a post in the biggest circle is 300,000 writes. Both fit, so I choose the simpler one: pull. Each circle keeps its latest 100 posts in a cache. A feed open reads the member's 10 circle lists, merges them by time and returns 20 posts with a cursor made of the last post's time and id, so the next page never repeats. A member's own post is added to the cache entry at once. Trade-off: I leave out timelines and a fan-out fleet. That buys three fewer parts, instant joins and leaves, and nothing to repair. It costs 10 lookups on every open, so about 1 open in 10 meets a slow lookup (1 minus 0.99 to the power 10), and a merge on every read. I would add timelines if members join 50 circles, if opens grow 10 times, or if the slow-open share passes 20%. Failure: if the circle cache restarts empty, reads fall to the post store at about 2,100 a second, which it can serve, and the cache refills."

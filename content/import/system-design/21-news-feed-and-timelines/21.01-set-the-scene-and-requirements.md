Hikaya is a made-up app (a fictional product). People post short updates and follow each other. When you open it, you see your feed: the newest posts from the accounts you follow. This module runs the design loop from chapter 1.4 on Hikaya, from clarify to failure. This first chapter does the first two steps: say what Hikaya must do, then turn that into numbers.

## Each person gets a different list

<InteractiveCanvas slug="sysdesign-m21-two-feeds" />

Salma and Omar open Hikaya in the same second. They see different lists, because they follow different people. There is no single list to hand out. Hikaya must make one for every reader, and at the busiest moment about 17,000 people open the feed every second.

<GoDeeper title="Go deeper: clarify first, the way chapter 1.4 does">

Before any numbers, agree what Hikaya does and what it does not do. All of this is a course choice, because Hikaya is made up.

| Bucket | What Hikaya promises |
|---|---|
| Does | Publish a post, follow and unfollow, a scrolling home feed, and a profile page |
| Does not (for now) | Exact counts, search, notifications, ads, direct messages, moderation |
| Fast | Opening the feed takes under 200 ms at the service (p99) |
| Fresh | A new post reaches most followers within about 10 seconds |
| Reliable | The feed opens 99.95% of the time, and an accepted post is never lost |
| Honest about | Counts may lag, and feed order is ranked, not strictly by time |
| Limits | A team of six, a cloud account and a budget |

A post is text of up to 280 characters with an optional photo, and the profile page shows one account's own posts. The list of things left out is long on purpose. Each of them is a fair feature, and some have their own modules (22 and 23), but each would add work to every later step. Saying "not now" out loud is part of the design.

For speed, the 200 ms is a p99 (chapter 2.4) and does not count the reader's own network. For freshness, the author sees her own post at once. For reliability, when something is slow Hikaya shows a slightly older or less ranked feed instead of an error. The "Honest about" row admits that two readers may see a new post a few seconds apart.

The "Fresh" and "Honest about" rows decide a trade-off later. Because a few seconds of delay is allowed, Hikaya may prepare feeds ahead of time instead of building each one when it is asked for.

## Drag the volume, find the driver

<InteractiveCanvas slug="sysdesign-m21-estimate-play" />

Drag the daily users, the opens a day and the follows. Reading is the load: about 17,000 feed opens a second at the busiest moment, against about 350 new posts. If every open built its list from scratch, that would be 2.6 million lookups a second. The ringed card is the number that drives the design.

<GoDeeper title="Go deeper: the working, and what is an assumption">

Nobody gave Hikaya these numbers, so each one is written down as an assumption: 100 million accounts, 50 million of them active each day, 10 feed opens per active user per day, 0.2 posts per active user per day, 150 follows per account on average, and a busiest second that is 3 times the average. The method is the one from chapter 2.3: start from people, divide by seconds, and keep the unit next to every number.

| Quantity | Working | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Feed opens a day | 50,000,000 x 10 | 500,000,000 |
| Feed opens a second, average | 500,000,000 / 86,400 | about 5,800 |
| Feed opens a second, peak | 5,787 x 3 | about 17,400 |
| Posts a day | 50,000,000 x 0.2 | 10,000,000 |
| Posts a second, average | 10,000,000 / 86,400 | about 116 |
| Posts a second, peak | 116 x 3 | about 350 |
| Reads for each post | 500,000,000 / 10,000,000 | 50 to 1 |
| Lookups a second if each open builds its own list | 17,400 x 150 follows | about 2.6 million |
| Follow edges | 100,000,000 x 150 | 15 billion |
| Storage for follows | 15 billion x 16 bytes, kept in both directions | about 480 GB |
| Storage for posts | 10,000,000 x 365 x 1 KB | about 3.7 TB a year |

Now ask what drives the design. Posts are few: 350 a second is nothing for one ordinary database. Reads are not few. Every feed open needs news from about 150 accounts, and 17,400 opens a second means a very large number of small lookups. Chapter 2.2 gave a simple query to a database about 0.5 ms, so one connection serves about 2,000 a second and one node a few thousand. Reading is the problem, and reading 50 times more than writing is the hint: do work when a post is published so the feed is cheap to open.

One number in this table is a trap. Multiply 350 posts a second by 150 followers and you get 52,000 list updates a second, but only if every account has 150 followers. Chapter 21.3 shows what real follower counts do to that number.

These are guesses. Say them out loud and invite correction. If people open the feed only twice a day, the ratio drops from 50 to 1 to 10 to 1, and the answer may change.

</GoDeeper>

<GoDeeper title="In an interview">

**Say:** "Before I draw, let me fix scope and numbers. I will support publish, follow and unfollow, a home feed with scrolling, and a profile page. I will leave out search, ads, messages and exact counts. I will assume 50 million daily users who open the feed 10 times a day and post 0.2 times a day. That is about 17,000 feed opens a second and 350 posts a second at peak, so reads outnumber writes 50 to 1. The feed should open in under 200 ms at p99, and a new post should reach most followers within about 10 seconds. So I will design the read path first."

**Expect:** "Why 10 opens a day?" **Answer:** "It is an assumption. I would check it against real traffic, because the ratio of reads to posts decides how much work I should do ahead of time."

</GoDeeper>

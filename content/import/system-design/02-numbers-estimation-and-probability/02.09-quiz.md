```quiz
type: mcq
question: "A team's app servers run in EU West. Each page makes 6 calls, one after another, to a recommendation service that runs only in Singapore, about 160 ms away per round trip. Pages take about a second. Which change cuts the most time?"
options:
  - "Move the app servers to bigger machines"
  - "Cache the user's profile in app memory"
  - "Run the recommendation service in EU West"
  - "Compress every response sent from Singapore"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "Bigger machines do not shorten the distance. The 6 round trips of 160 ms, about 960 ms, are spent travelling, not computing."
  - "The profile is not the slow part. The page still makes 6 trips to Singapore."
  - "Right. Six round trips of 160 ms cost about 960 ms. In the same region, each round trip is well under 1 ms, so most of the second disappears."
  - "Smaller responses save a little transfer time, but each call still waits a full 160 ms round trip."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "A ride app saves each online driver's location every 4 seconds. At the busiest hour, 50,000 drivers are online. About how many location writes per second must the store take at that hour?"
options:
  - "About 12,500"
  - "About 200,000"
  - "About 750,000"
  - "About 50,000"
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. Each driver writes once every 4 seconds, which is 0.25 writes per second. 50,000 times 0.25 is 12,500 per second."
  - "This multiplies by 4 instead of dividing. A write every 4 seconds is a quarter of a write per second, not four."
  - "This is the number of writes per minute: 50,000 drivers times 15 writes each. Divide by 60 for per second."
  - "This assumes every driver writes once a second. They write once every 4 seconds, so divide by 4."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "A checkout page calls the payment, stock and shipping services in parallel and waits for all three. Each service has a p50 of 20 ms and a p99 of 300 ms. The team's report says 'checkout p99 is 300 ms'. What is wrong with that?"
options:
  - "Nothing, since parallel calls take the time of just one call"
  - "Page p99 is likely higher: each call can be the slow one"
  - "Page p99 is 900 ms, since the three p99 values add up"
  - "It should be the average of the three services' p99s"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "Parallel calls take the time of the slowest call, not of a typical one. With three calls, a page meets a slow call more often than one service does."
  - "Right. A page is slow if any of its three calls is slow, so more than 1 page in 100 waits about 300 ms or more. Measure the page's own p99."
  - "The calls run at the same time, so their times do not add. The page waits for the slowest one."
  - "Percentiles cannot be averaged. Merge the raw page times, or measure the page directly."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "A worker can finish 100 jobs per second. Jobs arrive at 80 per second, and each spends about 50 ms in the system, waiting plus work, like an M/M/1 queue. A campaign will add 15 jobs per second. What should the team expect?"
options:
  - "Wait rises to about 60 ms"
  - "Wait stays at about 50 ms"
  - "The queue grows without limit"
  - "Wait jumps to about 200 ms"
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "This grows the wait in step with traffic. In a queue, the wait depends on spare capacity, which falls from 20 to 5 jobs per second."
  - "The wait changes long before arrivals reach 100 per second. Less spare capacity means longer queues from every burst."
  - "95 per second is still below the 100 the worker can finish, so the queue stays bounded, though long."
  - "Right. Time in system is 1 / (100 - 95) seconds, which is 200 ms: four times as long for about a fifth more traffic."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "A page needs a load balancer (99.99%), then one of two app copies in different zones (99.9% each), then one database (99.9%). The team can afford one change this quarter. Which raises the page's availability most?"
options:
  - "Add a third app copy"
  - "Upgrade the load balancer to five nines"
  - "Add a database replica in another zone"
  - "Put both app copies in one zone"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "The two app copies together are already down only about 30 seconds a year. A third copy barely changes the total."
  - "The load balancer gives under an hour of the downtime. Improving it saves less than fixing the part that gives most."
  - "Right. The single database gives about 8.8 of the page's 9.6 hours down a year. A second copy that can take over removes most of that."
  - "Copies in one zone share a cause of failure, so they can fail together. This lowers availability instead of raising it."
```

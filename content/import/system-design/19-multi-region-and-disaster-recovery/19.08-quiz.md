```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Tawsil's payments must lose at most 1 minute of writes (RPO 1 minute) and be back within 30 minutes (RTO 30 minutes). Today it has a nightly backup copied to another region, and a restore of the 1 TB database takes 67 minutes. Which change meets both limits?"
options:
  - "Take the backup every hour instead of nightly"
  - "Run a live copy in the other region"
  - "Save the change log every 5 minutes as well"
  - "Buy faster disks so the restore takes 40 minutes"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "An hourly backup loses up to an hour of writes, which breaks the 1 minute RPO. The restore would still take 67 minutes, which breaks the RTO."
  - "Right. A live copy is about a second behind, so the RPO is met. Promoting it and moving users takes about 10 minutes, so the RTO is met too."
  - "A log saved every 5 minutes loses up to 5 minutes, above the 1 minute limit. The restore plus replay would also take longer than 30 minutes."
  - "Faster disks shorten the restore but not the data lost. The newest day of writes is still gone, and 40 minutes is still above the 30 minute RTO."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "At 10:00 a release writes a wrong price into 20,000 menu items. The team notices at 10:40. Tawsil has replicas in two other zones and an asynchronous copy in another region. What can bring back the correct prices?"
options:
  - "Promote the copy in the other region"
  - "Fail over to a replica in another zone"
  - "Restore to a point in time just before 10:00"
  - "Nothing, because every copy has the wrong prices"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "That copy received the wrong prices from the primary within about a second. Promoting it changes the machine, not the data."
  - "Replicas copy every change, including the wrong prices. A failover to one of them keeps the damage."
  - "Right. A backup plus the saved change log can be replayed to a moment before the bad release. It costs restore time, and the good writes made after 10:00 need a plan."
  - "The live copies are all wrong, but the backup and the log still hold the earlier state. Point-in-time recovery exists for this case."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Region A fails at second 0. The DNS health check runs every 30 seconds and needs 2 failures in a row. The DNS answer has a TTL of 120 seconds. What is the longest a user can stay on the failed region?"
options:
  - "About 60 seconds"
  - "About 120 seconds"
  - "About 150 seconds"
  - "About 180 seconds"
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "That is only the detection time, 30 s x 2. Users with the old answer cached keep using it after the answer changes."
  - "That is only the cache time. The answer does not change until the check has failed twice."
  - "That adds one check interval to the TTL. Detection needs two failed checks, so it takes 60 seconds, not 30."
  - "Right. Detection takes 30 s x 2 = 60 s. A phone that cached the answer just before then keeps it for up to 120 s more, so about 180 s in total."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Tawsil runs 20 equal cells. A release that breaks every request goes to one cell first. Separately, a typo in a config file is pushed to all 20 cells at once, and every cell reads it. Which can take down more customers?"
options:
  - "The shared config typo: every cell reads it"
  - "The release: it breaks about 5% of customers"
  - "Both reach the same share of customers"
  - "Neither, since cells isolate every fault"
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. Cells only isolate what they do not share. A config every cell reads is one failure domain across all 20, so the typo can reach every customer at once."
  - "The release is contained in one cell, so it reaches about 5% of customers. That is the smaller one."
  - "They differ. The release stays in the first cell, and the shared config reaches all of them."
  - "Cells contain faults inside a cell. They do not help with something that every cell shares."
```

  - "Right. The rows and their copies stay in the EU, and the write is local, about 0.6 ms, because it never leaves the region."
  - "The leader is local and fast, but the backup is a copy of EU data in North America, which breaks the rule."

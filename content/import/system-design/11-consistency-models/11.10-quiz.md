```quiz
type: mcq
question: "One register starts at 0. Ada writes 3 from time 0 to 10. Ben reads from 5 to 15 and gets 3. Cam reads from 20 to 30 and gets 0. Dan reads from 35 to 45 and gets 3. Which statement is true?"
options:
  - "Linearizable, because Ben and Dan both saw 3"
  - "Not linearizable, but sequentially consistent"
  - "Not sequentially consistent, since Cam and Dan disagree"
  - "Linearizable, because Cam's and Dan's reads overlap"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "Two reads that saw 3 do not outweigh a third that saw 0. Cam started at 20, after the write ended at 10, so Cam must read 3."
  - "Right. Cam's read started after the write finished and returned 0, which breaks the real-time rule. Sequential consistency has no such rule: put Cam's read first, then Ada's write, then Ben's and Dan's reads, and every client keeps its own order."
  - "Cam and Dan are different clients, so nothing forces their reads into one fixed order. A single order exists: Cam, then Ada, then Ben, then Dan."
  - "Cam's read ends at 30 and Dan's starts at 35, so they do not overlap. Even if they did, Cam's read would still start after the write ended."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "A service has users in two regions. Two people try to register the same username at the same moment, one in each region. Which design makes sure only one of them gets it?"
options:
  - "Check locally, and settle clashes by last writer wins"
  - "Check both copies and accept if either says it is free"
  - "Add a third replica to each region to share the reads"
  - "Send every claim to one leader that orders them"
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "Both people were already told yes by their own region. Last writer wins can only take the name away from one of them later, which is the bug."
  - "If either copy saying free is enough, both claims succeed. This makes the duplicate more likely."
  - "More replicas help with read load. They do not decide who is first, and each can still say free."
  - "Right. One place puts the two claims in one order. The first sees the name free and takes it, and the second sees it taken. This is a compare-and-set through a linearizable store."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Samir's session token says he has seen position 120 of a shard. Replica A is at 118, replica B is at 125, and the leader is at 130. The router uses monotonic reads and raises the token after each read. Where does his next read go, and what is his token afterwards?"
options:
  - "Replica B, and the token becomes 125"
  - "Replica A, and the token becomes 118"
  - "The leader, and the token becomes 130"
  - "Replica B, and the token stays at 120"
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. Replica B is past his 120, so it cannot show him an older state. After the read the token rises to 125, so no later read can land on a replica behind that."
  - "Replica A is at 118, below the 120 he has already seen. Reading it could show him an older state, and a token never goes down."
  - "The leader would be correct but is not needed: a replica is already far enough ahead, and the leader should keep its time for reads that need it."
  - "Replica B is the right choice, but the token must rise to 125. If it stays at 120, a later read could go to a replica at 121 and show him less than he saw."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "A bank's transfer service loses the link between its two regions. The cut-off region keeps accepting transfers, so customers are not blocked. Which statement describes this choice?"
options:
  - "It chose consistency, so no conflicts can happen"
  - "It escaped the trade-off by tolerating the cut"
  - "It chose availability and must settle conflicts"
  - "It stayed linearizable because it kept answering"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "Accepting writes on both sides of a cut is the opposite of choosing consistency. A consistent choice would have the cut-off side refuse."
  - "Tolerating a partition means surviving it, not avoiding the choice. During the cut the service still had to pick between refusing and answering."
  - "Right. Answering on both sides is availability. The two regions may now hold conflicting balances, and something must settle them afterwards."
  - "Linearizable needs every read to see every finished write. Two regions that cannot talk cannot promise that, however quickly they answer."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "A write is made in EU West. A copy in North America East is 80 ms away round trip, and a local disk write takes 0.3 ms. Option 1: the write waits for the far copy. Option 2: it does not. What is the write time under option 1, and for how long can readers near the far copy be stale under option 2?"
options:
  - "About 80.3 ms; about 80 ms"
  - "About 80.3 ms; about 40 ms"
  - "About 40.3 ms; about 40 ms"
  - "About 160.3 ms; about 80 ms"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "The wait is right, but the stale window is one way, not the round trip. The copy takes half of 80 ms, about 40 ms, to arrive."
  - "Right. Waiting for the far copy costs the 0.3 ms disk write plus the 80 ms round trip. Not waiting leaves the far copy behind for one one-way trip, about 40 ms."
  - "A one-way trip is 40 ms, but the write waits for the answer to come back, so its wait is the full round trip, not half."
  - "The round trip already includes the way there and the way back. Counting it twice doubles the cost."
```

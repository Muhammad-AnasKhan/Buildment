An interviewer says one sentence: "Design a link shortener like Wasla." You have 45 minutes and a blank board. Two candidates, both made-up, get exactly this prompt. You have studied modules 1 to 27. This module is about how you show what you know.

## Candidate A fills the board with boxes

<InteractiveCanvas slug="sysdesign-m28-silent-boxes" />

Candidate A starts drawing in the first minute. A load balancer, a cache, a queue, a database. The boxes are sensible, and the board looks busy. But the interviewer has heard no numbers and no reasons. At minute 30 they still cannot say why any box is there. What do you notice about the notes they can write?

<GoDeeper title="Go deeper: why a busy board scores badly">

An interviewer cannot see inside your head. They can only score what you say and what you draw. A box with no number behind it is a claim with no evidence. "I would add a cache" tells them you know the word. "Opens are 100,000 a second, so one database is 40 times over its plan, so I add a cache" tells them you can reason.

Candidate A may know everything in this course. The round still ends with a thin set of notes: a diagram, no scope, no numbers, no cost, no failure. That is what a silent round looks like from the other side of the table. Candidate A is a made-up composite, built for this chapter. No real person is described.

A silent drawing also hides mistakes. If A has misread the scope (for example, built a system for private pastes when the interviewer meant public links), nobody finds out until minute 30. Asking in minute 1 costs 30 seconds.

</GoDeeper>

## Candidate B talks first, then draws

<InteractiveCanvas slug="sysdesign-m28-talk-then-draw" />

Candidate B asks two questions, says three numbers, and only then draws. By minute 10 the interviewer knows the scope and the number that drives the design. Every box that follows answers a number. There is time left for one deep dive, one cost and one failure. B's drawing is no better than A's. The evidence is much better.

<GoDeeper title="Go deeper: what the interviewer writes down">

Interviewers differ, and each company has its own form. Most look for the same five things, and these are also the five rows of this course's capstone rubric (chapter 29.1):

1. **Requirements and numbers.** Did you find out what to build, and did you size it with units?
2. **Design choices.** Does each box answer a number, and did you pick between real options?
3. **Correctness under failure.** Did you say what breaks and what the user sees?
4. **Evidence.** Did you show the sum or the reason, not only the conclusion?
5. **Communication.** Could the interviewer follow you, and did you answer the question they asked?

Notice that none of these is "drew the most boxes". Candidate B leaves a mark in all five rows. Candidate A leaves a mark in one. Also notice that every row is something you say, so the skill in this module is saying it, in a good order, on a clock.

</GoDeeper>

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "A metrics graph shows checkout p99 doubling at 14:00. One customer sends you order id r-9120 and says it took 12 seconds. You want to know which service made that one order slow. What do you do?"
options:
  - "Compare the average latency of each service"
  - "Search every log file for the word slow"
  - "Open the trace of order r-9120"
  - "Compare CPU graphs of all servers at 14:00"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "An average mixes every request, so one slow order disappears inside it. It cannot say what happened to r-9120."
  - "A slow request often writes no line that says slow. Searching for a word is a guess, and the pile is large."
  - "Right. A trace of that one order lists its calls with start and end times, so the slow call stands out. Its trace id then leads to the log lines."
  - "CPU is a resource view for all traffic at once. A slow call to an outside provider can leave the CPU idle."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "In one minute an API logs 10,000 requests: 9,700 fast 200s, 50 slow 200s over the 800 ms limit, 150 404s and 100 503s. Good means status below 500 and under 800 ms, and 4xx requests are not valid. What is the availability SLI?"
options:
  - "98.5%"
  - "97.0%"
  - "99.0%"
  - "99.5%"
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. Valid requests are 10,000 minus 150 404s, which is 9,850. Good ones are the 9,700 fast 200s. 9,700 divided by 9,850 is 98.5%."
  - "This keeps the 404s as valid requests: 9,700 divided by 10,000. A caller's mistake is not our failure and is left out."
  - "This ignores the 50 slow requests: 9,750 divided by 9,850. A response over the limit is bad for the user, so it is not good."
  - "This counts only the 50 slow requests as bad: 9,800 divided by 9,850. The 100 server errors are bad too."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "The SLO is 99.9% over 30 days. At 10:00 a bad config makes 2% of requests fail, and nothing failed before. The fast rule pages when both a 1 hour and a 5 minute window burn at 14.4 times the allowed rate. When does it page, and how much of the 30-day budget is gone by then?"
options:
  - "At once, with almost none of the budget gone"
  - "After about 5 minutes, with 0.2% gone"
  - "After about 22 minutes, with 1% gone"
  - "After about 43 minutes, with 2% gone"
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "The long window averages the whole hour, and it starts clean. It cannot read 14.4 times the allowed rate on the first minute."
  - "The short window is ready after 5 minutes, but the long window is still near 0.17%, far below the 1.44% it needs."
  - "At 22 minutes the long window averages about 0.73%, half of the 1.44% line. It needs twice as long to reach it."
  - "Right. 2% is a burn of 20. The long window reaches 1.44% when 2% times x over 60 minutes equals 1.44%, at about 43 minutes. By then 0.02 times 43 is about 0.86 bad minutes of 43.2, which is 2%."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "At 4 am an alert fires: the disk of the logging server is 85% full and will be full in about 3 days at the current rate. Users are not affected. What should this alert do?"
options:
  - "Page the on-call person to fix it now"
  - "Open a ticket for working hours"
  - "Page the whole team in case it speeds up"
  - "Delete the alert, since disks never matter"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "No user is hurt, and three days is plenty of time. A page at 4 am buys nothing that a ticket at 9 am would not."
  - "Right. It needs a human, but not now. A ticket keeps the work visible without waking anyone."
  - "Waking more people multiplies the cost and the alert fatigue, and the risk does not change."
  - "A full disk is a real risk and will become a symptom. The fix is a better channel for the alert, not removing it."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "After an outage caused by a wrong value in a config file, the postmortem draft says: 'Root cause: Karim typed the wrong value.' What is the best rewrite?"
options:
  - "Keep it: the record must name who erred"
  - "Delete the cause so nobody feels blamed"
  - "Name the check that was missing"
  - "Call it bad luck and add no actions"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "Naming the person teaches everyone to hide mistakes. Anyone could have typed the value, and the system let it through."
  - "Hiding the cause avoids blame but also avoids learning. A postmortem must say what happened and why it was possible."
  - "Right. The system had no check, so a wrong value went live. The action is to add validation, with an owner and a date."
  - "Luck is not a cause, and without actions the same outage can come back with a different person."
```

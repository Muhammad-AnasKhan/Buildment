```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Rafiq (a made-up courier app) has 300,000 couriers online at the busiest time, each reporting its place every 6 seconds. 40,000 customers have the map open and each refreshes every 4 seconds. Which is bigger each second, and about how big?"
options:
  - "Searches, about 10,000 a second"
  - "Location reports, about 50,000 a second"
  - "They are equal, about 50,000 each"
  - "Location reports, about 1,800,000 a second"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "40,000 / 4 s is 10,000 searches a second, but that is the smaller number. The reports are 300,000 / 6 s, which is five times more."
  - "Right. 300,000 couriers / 6 s is 50,000 reports a second. 40,000 customers / 4 s is 10,000 searches a second. Reports are the larger load."
  - "The searches are 10,000 a second, not 50,000. Only the reports reach 50,000."
  - "That multiplies 300,000 by 6. A report every 6 seconds is a division: the rate is the count divided by the gap."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "A ride service uses 7-letter geohash boxes, each about 153 m wide and 153 m tall. A rider wants every driver within 500 m. How many boxes must the search read to be sure of finding them all?"
options:
  - "9 boxes (1 ring)"
  - "25 boxes (2 rings)"
  - "49 boxes (3 rings)"
  - "81 boxes (4 rings)"
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "One ring reaches only 153 m from the edge of the rider's box. A driver 400 m away would be missed."
  - "Two rings reach about 306 m. That is short of 500 m, so drivers between 306 m and 500 m can be missed."
  - "Three rings reach about 459 m. That is just short of 500 m, so a driver at 480 m north of a rider at the box edge can still be missed."
  - "Right. Four rings reach about 612 m from the edge of the rider's box, which covers the 500 m radius in every direction. The search then measures the true distance."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "A concert ends, and one box gets ten times more map searches than its shard can answer. Matching must keep using live driver places. Which change cuts the load without hurting matching?"
options:
  - "Show the map from a copy up to 2 seconds old"
  - "Choose drivers from a copy up to 2 seconds old"
  - "Ask drivers in that box to report less often"
  - "Drop the drivers who have waited longest"
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. Thousands of map screens share one copy that is recomputed every 2 seconds. A car moves about 17 m in that time, and matching still reads live places."
  - "A copy is fine for a map screen but not for matching. A driver in an old copy may already have taken another ride, so riders would be offered busy drivers."
  - "Fewer reports lower the update load, not the search load. The problem here is the searches, and older places would hurt matching."
  - "Dropping waiting drivers removes the cars riders need, and the searches still arrive in the same numbers."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "A dispatcher claims Driver D for a rider, then crashes before sending the offer. Claims last 10 seconds. What happens to D?"
options:
  - "D stays held until someone clears it by hand"
  - "D is free at once, since no offer reached them"
  - "D is free again when the 10 second hold runs out"
  - "D is removed from the map until the next restart"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "That is the danger a hold with no expiry would cause. An expiry exists so that a crash never locks a driver."
  - "Nothing tells the store the dispatcher died, so it cannot free D at once. The claim stays until it expires."
  - "Right. The hold carries its own end time, like a lease. After 10 seconds another search can claim D."
  - "The claim is on D's status record, not on the location map. A crash does not touch the map."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "A map node restarts empty. Drivers report every 4 seconds, and each entry expires after 12 seconds. About how long until a typical driver is back on that node's map?"
options:
  - "About 12 seconds, the expiry time"
  - "Within about 4 seconds, the next report"
  - "Until the nightly backup is restored"
  - "Until the driver restarts the app"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "The expiry removes drivers who stop reporting. It does not delay a driver who is reporting, because the next report adds them straight away."
  - "Right. The map holds only the latest place, and every driver sends a new one within 4 seconds, so the node fills itself without any backup."
  - "There is no nightly backup of the live map, and none is needed. The data is replaced every 4 seconds."
  - "The app keeps reporting while it runs. Nothing on the driver's phone needs to restart."
```

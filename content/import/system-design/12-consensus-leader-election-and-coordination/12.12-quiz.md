```quiz
type: mcq
question: "A team runs a coordination cluster on 4 servers: 2 in each of two data centres. The link between the data centres fails. What happens?"
options:
  - "Both sides keep working on their own data"
  - "The side that had the leader keeps working"
  - "Neither side has 3 servers, so both stop"
  - "The side with the lower server numbers wins"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "Two sides that both accept writes would hold two different truths, and nothing could merge them safely. The majority rule exists to stop exactly this."
  - "A leader on a side of 2 can store entries, but it cannot commit them, because a majority of 4 is 3. Having the leader is not enough."
  - "Right. A majority of 4 is 3. Each side has only 2, so neither can commit, and both wait. This is why an even size adds cost and no extra safety over the odd size below it."
  - "No rule favours server numbers. The only thing that counts is whether a side holds a majority."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Jobs sometimes run twice after a long process pause. A colleague proposes to raise the lock lease from 10 seconds to 60 seconds. Is that enough?"
options:
  - "No: a longer pause can still happen; add a token check"
  - "Yes: no real process pauses for as long as 60 seconds"
  - "Yes: a longer lease means the lock never moves by mistake"
  - "No: the lease must be renewed twice as often instead"
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. Any lease can be outlived by a longer pause. Only the storage can reject the old holder, by comparing the token of each write with the newest one it has seen."
  - "Pauses of tens of seconds happen: stalled virtual machines, long garbage collections, swapped memory. And no number is safe, because the pause has no upper bound."
  - "A longer lease also makes a real crash take longer to recover from, and it still cannot stop a paused holder that wakes up after the lease ended."
  - "Renewing more often does not help a frozen process. It cannot renew while it is frozen, so its lease still ends."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "A follower was cut off for a minute and comes back at term 40. The cluster's healthy leader is at term 7. What happens first?"
options:
  - "F becomes the leader at once, since 40 is the largest term"
  - "The leader ignores F, because F's log is behind the leader's"
  - "F adopts term 7 and rejoins without any change at all"
  - "The leader steps down at term 40; a new election runs"
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "A larger term does not make a leader. F must win a vote, and servers refuse a candidate whose log is behind theirs."
  - "The leader cannot ignore a larger term. A server that sees a larger term must adopt it and become a follower, whatever the sender's log looks like."
  - "Terms never go down. The server with the smaller term is the one that adopts the larger one, so it is the leader that changes."
  - "Right. The leader sees term 40, adopts it and steps down. An election follows, and F loses it if its log is behind. The cluster pays one short election for F's absence."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "A leader in a cluster of five stored a command on itself and one follower. The other three are unreachable and the client has no reply. Then the leader crashes. What can you say about the command?"
options:
  - "It is safe: one follower holds a copy of it"
  - "It may be kept or erased; it was never committed"
  - "It is lost for sure, because the leader crashed"
  - "It is kept, because the leader wrote it to its log"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "Two copies of five are not a majority. A later leader that never saw the entry can be elected, and it will overwrite the follower's copy."
  - "Right. If the follower with the copy wins the next election, the entry survives. If another server wins, it is erased. Either way, the client was never told it was saved."
  - "It is not lost for sure. The follower that holds the copy can still become the leader, since its log is the most up to date."
  - "A write to the leader's own log is not a promise. Only an entry on a majority is promised, and the client has no reply for that reason."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "A cluster of 2,000 servers must know which servers are alive, and must run one billing job at a time. Which design fits?"
options:
  - "Every node pings all others, and the lowest id leads"
  - "Gossip for both; the leader is whoever gossip names"
  - "Gossip for who is alive, a consensus group for the leader"
  - "One central server decides liveness and leadership by itself"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "Every node pinging every other node sends about 4 million pings per round, and the lowest id is a guess, not a decision. Two nodes can disagree about who is lowest and alive."
  - "Gossip gives eventual agreement. For a while, different nodes can name different leaders, and a billing job needs exactly one."
  - "Right. Gossip keeps the cost per node flat and spreads membership news in about log N rounds. A small consensus group, such as an etcd cluster, makes the one decision that must be exactly right."
  - "A single central server is a single point of failure. If it fails or is cut off, nobody knows who leads, and if it comes back it may disagree with a newer choice."
```

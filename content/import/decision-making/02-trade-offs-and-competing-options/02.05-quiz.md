```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Amal manages a small NGO project. With the same budget, she can hire one senior developer for three months, or two junior developers for three months. Which sentence names the trade-off most clearly?"
options:
  - "Seniors are always better than juniors, so hiring one senior is the easy choice"
  - "Two juniors are better, because two people can always get more work done than one person"
  - "A senior needs less guidance. Two juniors give more hours but need more of Amal's time"
  - "Both options cost the same amount of money, so there is no real trade-off here"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "This is a general opinion, and it is not always true. It does not say what Amal gets and gives up with each option."
  - "More people can mean more hours, but this hides the cost: juniors usually need more guidance and review."
  - "Right. It names what each option gives and what it costs, so Amal can weigh them against her project."
  - "The same price does not mean the same value. The trade-off is in experience, hours and Amal's time, not money."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "In a team call, a teammate says: 'Moving to the new framework has no downsides. We should just do it.' What is the most useful reply?"
options:
  - "What would we give up by moving, such as time, stability or delayed work?"
  - "If there are no downsides, let's start moving the main app this week"
  - "Let's not move. New tools always have hidden problems that cost us later"
  - "Let's take a quick vote now so we do not waste more of the call"
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. 'No downsides' usually means the cost has not been found yet. This question helps the team see it before deciding."
  - "This accepts the claim without checking it. A big move almost always costs time and adds risk."
  - "This rejects the idea without looking at it. The new framework might be worth its cost."
  - "A vote before the cost is named means people vote without seeing the trade-off."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Sofia compares two co-working spaces. Her weights are: reliable power 3, price 2, distance 1. Space X scores 5 on reliable power, 2 on price and 3 on distance. What is Space X's weighted total?"
options:
  - "10"
  - "18"
  - "30"
  - "22"
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "This adds the scores without the weights: 5 + 2 + 3 = 10."
  - "This puts the weights in the wrong order: 5 x 1 + 2 x 2 + 3 x 3 = 18. Reliable power has weight 3, not 1."
  - "This multiplies every score by 3. Each criterion has its own weight."
  - "Right. Power: 5 x 3 = 15. Price: 2 x 2 = 4. Distance: 3 x 1 = 3. Total: 15 + 4 + 3 = 22."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Malik works remotely and is buying a laptop. The power in his area is on for only a few hours a day. All three laptops he is looking at fit his budget and are fast enough for his work. Which criterion should get the highest weight?"
options:
  - "Processor speed"
  - "Battery life"
  - "Price"
  - "Screen size"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "All three laptops are already fast enough for his work, so more speed changes little for him."
  - "Right. With power for only a few hours a day, battery life decides how long he can work. It matters most for his situation."
  - "Price always matters, but all three fit his budget. Small price differences matter less than the hours he can work."
  - "A bigger screen is more comfortable, but it does not decide whether he can work during a power cut."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "An NGO needs a data collection app for field workers who are often without internet for days. The team's decision matrix gives App A the highest total, but App A only works online. What should the team do?"
options:
  - "Choose App A, because the matrix gives it the highest total score"
  - "Raise the weight of offline use until another app gets the top score"
  - "Remove App A because it fails a must-have, then compare the other apps"
  - "Choose App A, and ask field workers to upload their data from a town each day"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "A high total cannot make up for a failed must-have. Without internet, App A does not work at all for these field workers."
  - "This bends the weights to get an answer. A must-have is not a weight: an option that fails it is out."
  - "Right. Must-haves come first. App A is out, and the team compares only the apps that can do the job."
  - "This puts the cost on the field workers and ignores their real situation. The tool must fit the work, not the other way round."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Nabil's decision matrix gives Option A 41 points and Option B 40 points. What is the best way to read this result?"
options:
  - "Option A is clearly the better choice, because it has more points in total"
  - "It is a close call. Check if a small change in one weight flips the result"
  - "The matrix was built wrong, because a good matrix always shows a clear winner"
  - "Choose Option B, because the matrix probably favored Option A by mistake"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "The numbers come from judgment. A one point gap is too small to call one option clearly better."
  - "Right. The scores and weights are judgments, so a one point gap is smaller than the guesswork inside them. Both options are reasonable, so other factors, such as how easy each is to undo, should help decide."
  - "Close results are normal when two options are both good. A matrix does not have to show a clear winner."
  - "There is no reason to think the matrix favored A by mistake. Switching to B without a reason is just guessing."
```

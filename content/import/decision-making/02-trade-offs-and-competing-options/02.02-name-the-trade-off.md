## A trade-off nobody chose

Reem is a junior developer at a startup with five people. On Monday the founder asks: "Can we launch the payment page this week?" Reem says yes. To finish in time, she skips the automated tests.

The page launches on Friday. Two weeks later a customer finds a bug in refunds. Fixing it takes Reem most of the next two weeks, because nobody is sure which other parts the fix might break.

Was it wrong to launch fast? Not always. A fast launch can be the right call. The real problem is that **nobody said the trade-off out loud**. The founder did not know that "this week" meant "without tests". So nobody chose that cost on purpose.

## The more-and-less sentence

A simple way to name any trade-off is one sentence:

> If we choose **this option**, we get more **X** and less **Y**.

The sentence forces you to say both sides. If you can only fill in the "more" part, you have not found the cost yet.

| Choice | More of | Less of |
|---|---|---|
| Launch this week without tests | Speed | Confidence that the code works |
| Take a second freelance client | Income | Time for study and rest |
| Use a free hosting plan | Money saved | Uptime and support |
| Hire one senior instead of two juniors | Experience | Total hours of work |

Some trade-offs appear again and again in tech work. Learn to spot them:

- **Speed or quality:** faster now, more fixing later.
- **Scope or deadline:** more features, or an earlier date. Rarely both.
- **Income now or skills later:** a well-paid task you know, or a lower-paid one where you learn.
- **Control or convenience:** build it yourself, or use a ready-made tool.

## Find the hidden cost

Money is easy to see. Other costs hide. Before you decide, ask: **"What can I no longer do if I choose this?"** Then check these four:

1. **Time:** what else will this time not go to?
2. **Energy:** will this leave you too tired for other work, study or family?
3. **Relationships:** will someone be let down, such as a client, a teammate or a friend?
4. **Future options:** does this close a door? This links to the one-way doors from module 1.

This is your **opportunity cost**. When Yousef from chapter 2.1 chooses the shop project, the cost is not only the daily calls. It is also the charting skills and the possible future NGO work he gives up.

<Callout type="warning" title="Watch out for 'no downside'">
If an option seems to have no cost at all, you probably have not found the cost yet. Ask one more person: "What would we give up with this?"
</Callout>

## Say the trade-off to others

Naming the trade-off also helps you talk to clients and team leads. Instead of saying yes or no, show the choice:

> "We can launch on Friday with card payments only, or on the 20th with card payments and bank transfers. Which matters more to you?"

This is honest, and it lets the person who owns the business goal make the business choice. It also protects you: nobody is surprised later by a cost they did not know about.

## Common questions

**Q: Is a trade-off a bad thing?**
A: No. A trade-off is just the price of a choice. Good decisions still have costs. The goal is to pay a cost you chose, not one that surprises you.

**Q: What if every option has a big cost?**
A: Name the cost of each one, then choose the cost you can live with best. Also look for a third option, such as a smaller version or a trial.

## Your turn

This is the main task of the course. You will write a decision memo about the real decision you chose in chapter 7.1, and a mentor will review it.

Take your time. You do not need to finish in one sitting. If the power goes or your connection drops, write your notes on paper or in a phone notes app, then type the memo when you can.

## Step 1: Gather your notes

Before you write, collect what you already have:

- The decision you described in chapter 7.1. You can change it if a better one came to mind.
- Your learning log answers from earlier modules. Many of them are about your own decisions, risks and tasks.
- Any facts you need: dates, prices, hours, what other people said.

## Step 2: Write a fast first draft

Use the eight parts from chapter 7.2. Write quickly. Do not fix words yet. You can copy this outline into your notes:

```text
1. The decision (one question, with a date)
2. Options (two to four)
3. Criteria (three to five, with weights)
4. Trade-off (more of X, less of Y)
5. Top risks (two or three: likelihood, impact, plan)
6. The choice and why
7. What would change my mind
8. Review date (and what I will check)
```

A weighted score table is useful but optional. If you use one, keep it small. If your scores and your choice disagree, say why. That is often the most interesting part of the memo.

## Step 3: Make it clear

Now read your draft as if you were the mentor. The mentor does not know your client, your team or your university. Would they understand every line?

Then check each part against this table:

| Part | Ask yourself |
|---|---|
| The decision | Is it one clear question with a date? |
| Options | Are there at least two real options, not one real and one fake? |
| Criteria | Do my reasons in part 6 use these criteria? |
| Trade-off | Did I say what my choice costs me? |
| Top risks | Does each risk have likelihood, impact and a plan? |
| Change my mind | Is it a fact, number or date I can check? |
| Review date | Is it a real date with things to check? |

Last, do the quick checks from modules 5 and 6. Did you look for evidence against your choice? Did you leave out private details, or change names?

<Callout type="tip" title="Aim for 250 to 600 words">
Under 250 words usually means a part is missing. Over 600 usually means the important points are hidden. One page is the goal.
</Callout>

## Step 4: Submit

Paste or type your full memo in the box below. You can also use voice input and edit the text after.

If you want, you can also record a short audio or video, about three to five minutes, where you explain your decision as if to a team lead. This is good practice for remote work, where you often explain choices on a call. The recording is optional. The written memo is what the mentor reviews, so write it even if you record.

<Callout type="info" title="What your mentor looks for">
Your mentor does not grade whether your choice is right. They look at your reasoning: clear options, honest criteria, a named trade-off, real risks with plans, and a specific way to know if you were wrong. Expect questions, not a score.
</Callout>

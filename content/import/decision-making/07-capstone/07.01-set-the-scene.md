## From practice to your own life

In six modules you practiced with other people's decisions: Omar's demo, a freelancer choosing between clients, a fellow with too many tasks. Those stories were useful because they were safe. Nothing you chose there could go wrong.

Now you use the same tools on a decision that is yours. You will write a short **decision memo**: one page that explains what you are deciding, the options, how you compare them, the risks, and what you choose. Then a mentor reads it and gives you feedback.

The mentor will not judge whether your choice is "right". Remember module 1: a good decision is about good reasoning with the information you have. The mentor looks at how clearly you think.

## Your toolbox

You already have every tool you need. Here is where each one came from.

| Module | Tool you can use in your memo |
|---|---|
| 1 | Decision quality vs outcome, one-way and two-way doors |
| 2 | Naming the trade-off, comparing options with weighted criteria |
| 3 | Likelihood and impact, the pre-mortem, a review date |
| 4 | Urgent and important, impact and effort, saying no |
| 5 | Checks for confirmation bias, sunk cost, anchoring, overconfidence |
| 6 | The newspaper, mentor and rules tests |

You do not need to use every tool. A good memo uses the few that fit your decision.

## Choosing a good decision to write about

Salma works part time as a support agent for a software company in Dubai and studies computer science. Her first idea for the capstone was "Should I drink less coffee?" It is real, but there is not much to weigh. Her second idea was "Should I move to another country?" It matters a lot, but it is too big and too far away to plan in one page.

Her third idea worked: "Should I ask my manager to move to the evening shift so I can attend morning classes?" It is real, it is hers, it has clear options, and it matters in the next few weeks.

A good capstone decision has these five things:

1. **It is real.** It is happening in your work, study or a project, now or very soon. A decision you made in the last few months also works, if you can remember what you knew at the time.
2. **It is yours.** You decide, or you make the recommendation to the person who decides.
3. **It has at least two real options.** "Do it or not" counts, but a third option often appears when you think harder.
4. **It matters.** Something real changes: money, time, your study, your career, or other people.
5. **It fits on one page.** It is not your whole life plan.

| Good fits | Too small or too big |
|---|---|
| Accept a three-month contract or keep freelancing | Which font to use on your CV |
| Choose a tech stack for your graduation project | Which career to have for the next 20 years |
| Ask a client for a deadline change or work weekends | What to eat for lunch |
| Join a startup team part time or focus on exams | Whether to learn "everything about AI" |

<Callout type="warning" title="Protect other people's privacy">
Your mentor will read your memo. Do not include passwords, private client data or anything you promised to keep secret. Change names if you need to, for example "Client A". This is the newspaper test from module 6 in practice.
</Callout>

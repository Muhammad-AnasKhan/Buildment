## Why write it down

Thinking in your head feels clear until you try to write it. Writing shows the gaps: an option you never checked, a risk with no plan, a reason that is really just a feeling.

A memo is also a record. In module 1 you learned to judge a decision by what you knew at the time. When the result arrives, your memo shows exactly what you knew. And a team lead in another time zone can read it in five minutes instead of joining three calls.

## The shape of the memo

Most good decision memos have the same eight parts. Each part comes from a module you have already done.

| Part | What it answers | Module |
|---|---|---|
| 1. The decision | What exactly am I deciding, and by when? | 1 |
| 2. Options | What are the two to four real choices? | 2 |
| 3. Criteria | What matters most here, and how much? | 2 |
| 4. Trade-off | What do I give up with my choice? | 2 |
| 5. Top risks | What could go wrong, how likely, how bad, and my plan? | 3 |
| 6. The choice and why | What do I choose, and which reasons matter most? | 1 to 6 |
| 7. What would change my mind | Which new facts would make me decide differently? | 3 |
| 8. Review date | When will I check how it is going? | 3 |

Four parts need extra care:

- **The decision:** one question with a date. "Should I accept the Toronto contract, starting 1 November?" is clear. "Work stuff" is not.
- **Options:** look for a middle option, like a trial or a smaller version. That is often how a one-way door becomes a two-way door.
- **Trade-off:** say it as "more of X, less of Y". If your choice costs nothing, you have not found the trade-off yet.
- **What would change my mind:** a number, a date or an event, not "if things change".

## A complete example

Ahmad is a freelance web developer in his final year of university. A startup in Toronto has offered him a part-time contract. Here is his memo.

In module 2 you gave weights from 1 to 3. Ahmad uses 1 to 5 to show finer differences. Any scale works if you use it the same way for every option.

### Memo: the Toronto contract

**The decision:** Should I accept a three-month, 20-hour-a-week contract with a startup in Toronto, starting 1 November? I must reply by 25 October.

**Options:**

- A. Accept 20 hours and pause one of my two freelance clients.
- B. Decline and keep both freelance clients.
- C. Accept, but ask for 15 hours a week and keep both clients with smaller workloads.

**Criteria and scores** (weight 1 to 5, score 1 to 5):

| Criterion | Weight | A | B | C |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Steady income | 5 | 5 | 3 | 4 |
| Time for final semester | 4 | 2 | 4 | 3 |
| Learning and career growth | 4 | 5 | 2 | 4 |
| Keeping promises to clients | 3 | 2 | 5 | 4 |
| **Weighted total** | | **59** | **54** | **60** |

**Trade-off:** With C, I get less money than A and less free time than B. I accept some lost study time and income in exchange for steady work and keeping my word to my clients.

**Top risks:**

1. Power or internet cuts during the four hours I overlap with Toronto. Likely, high impact. Plan: keep a charged power bank and mobile data, and post written updates so a short outage does not block the team.
2. Not enough time for January exams. Medium likelihood, high impact. Plan: agree now that I work 10 hours in exam week.
3. The startup ends the contract early. Low likelihood, medium impact. Plan: keeping both clients means my income never drops to zero.

**The choice and why:** I choose C. The scores are close, so the numbers are not my main reason. The main reason is that C is easier to undo. Pausing a client may lose them for good, which is a one-way door. Fewer hours at the startup can grow later if it goes well. In a pre-mortem I imagined it is February and this went badly. The most likely cause was saying yes to everything and failing an exam, so I added the exam week plan above.

**What would change my mind:** If the startup cannot accept 15 hours, I will choose A and give my second client two weeks' notice with a proper handover. If my exams move into December, I will choose B.

**Review date:** 15 December. I will check my hours, my study grades so far and feedback from all three clients.

## Before you share a memo

Read your draft one more time with modules 5 and 6 in mind. Did you look for evidence against your choice, or only for it? Is a big number from someone else, like a first salary offer, anchoring you? Would you be comfortable if your client or mentor read every line? You do not need a section for these checks. Just fix what you find.

## Too many worries

Maha is a volunteer web developer. She is building a donation website for a small NGO. The campaign launches in three weeks. When she starts listing what could go wrong, the list gets long and she feels stressed:

1. The NGO changes the campaign text at the last minute.
2. Power cuts slow down her work.
3. The donation page fails on launch day.
4. Her only laptop breaks.
5. The designer in another time zone sends the images late.

She cannot fix everything at once. She needs to know which risks to handle first.

## Two questions for every risk

For each risk, ask two separate questions:

- **Likelihood:** how probable is it that this happens? Low, medium or high.
- **Impact:** if it does happen, how much harm does it cause? Low, medium or high.

Keep the two questions apart. A risk can be very likely and almost harmless, like a small change to the text. Another can be unlikely and very harmful, like a broken laptop with no backup.

Some teams give each risk a number score. For a small team or a freelancer, low, medium and high is enough. A number like "7 out of 10" looks exact, but it is still a guess. Simple words keep you honest.

## The risk grid

Put each risk in a simple grid. Likelihood goes down the side, impact goes across the top. The box tells you what to do.

| Likelihood | Low impact | Medium impact | High impact |
|---|---|---|---|
| **High** | Plan for it | Act now | Act now |
| **Medium** | Watch it | Plan for it | Act now |
| **Low** | Accept it | Watch it | Plan for it |

- **Act now:** change your plan this week to make the risk smaller.
- **Plan for it:** prepare a backup plan, which is what you will do if it happens.
- **Watch it:** check it from time to time.
- **Accept it:** do nothing special, and deal with it if it comes.

Here is Maha's list in the grid:

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Donation page fails | Medium | High | Act now |
| Power cuts slow work | High | Medium | Act now |
| Laptop breaks | Low | High | Plan for it |
| Late images | Medium | Low | Watch it |
| Last-minute text change | High | Low | Plan for it |

## Two ways to make a risk smaller

When you "act now", you have two tools. You can make the risk **less likely**, or make it **hurt less** if it happens. Often you can do both.

- **Donation page:** test it with a real small payment a week before launch (less likely to fail). Prepare a simple bank transfer page as a backup (hurts less).
- **Power cuts:** plan coding work for the hours when power is usually on, and keep a charged power bank for the router (less likely to lose time). Tell the NGO now that she needs two extra days of margin (hurts less).
- **Laptop:** push her code to an online repository every day. If the laptop breaks, she loses hours, not weeks.

<Callout type="tip" title="Ratings are guesses, so share them">
Your ratings are your best guess today. Ask a teammate to rate the same risks. Where you disagree, talk about it. Check your grid again each week, because likelihood and impact change.
</Callout>

## Back to Ibrahim

In the last chapter, Ibrahim knew power cuts were likely. They would delay a client's investor demo, so the impact was high. High and high means **act now**. If he had used the grid on Monday, he could have promised a later date, or told Anna on Monday that Thursday depended on the power.

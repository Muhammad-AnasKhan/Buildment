```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Ruba has a website project due in ten days. Which risk should she deal with first?"
options:
  - "The client may reply slowly to emails on Fridays (likely, short waits for small answers)"
  - "Daily power cuts will reduce her working hours (very likely, could delay delivery)"
  - "A free font she uses may be removed from the font website (possible, she can keep a copy)"
  - "Her favorite code editor may change its default theme (possible, no effect on the work)"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "Likely but low impact. Short waits for small answers will not move the deadline, so she can plan for them."
  - "Right. High likelihood and high impact on the deadline: this belongs in 'act now'."
  - "Medium likelihood and low impact belongs in 'watch it'. Keeping her own copy of the font removes most of the harm."
  - "This has no real impact on the project, so it does not need any action."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Sami keeps all his project files on one laptop. It is unlikely to break this month, but if it did, he would lose weeks of work. What is the sensible response?"
options:
  - "Do nothing for now, because the chance of the laptop breaking is low"
  - "Buy a second laptop now, so he has a spare ready in case the first one breaks"
  - "Stop taking new work until he can afford a newer, more reliable laptop"
  - "Copy his work to an online repository or USB drive after each session"
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "Low likelihood with high impact means 'plan for it'. Ignoring it risks weeks of work."
  - "A second laptop costs a lot, and his files are still only in one place unless he copies them."
  - "This turns a small chance of harm into a certain loss of time and income."
  - "Right. A regular backup is cheap and makes the impact small: if the laptop breaks, he loses hours, not weeks."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "During a pre-mortem, a junior teammate writes: 'The launch failed because only one person knew how to deploy, and she lost internet that day.' What should the team do with this reason?"
options:
  - "Add it to the list, rate its likelihood and impact, and act if it is big"
  - "Leave it off the list, because it has not happened and might make the team nervous"
  - "Ask the junior teammate to explain why they are being negative about the plan"
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. In a pre-mortem every reason goes on the list. Then the team rates it and acts on the biggest ones."
  - "Finding things that have not happened yet is the whole point of a pre-mortem."
  - "In a pre-mortem, raising a failure is the task, not negativity. Questioning the person teaches everyone to stay quiet."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Your team must choose between two free chart libraries for a dashboard prototype due next week. You read the documentation for both and built a small test with one, and it works. A teammate suggests waiting a week to read more reviews. What do you do?"
options:
  - "Wait the week, because more information always leads to a better decision"
  - "Ask the client to choose the library, since they are the ones who will use the dashboard"
  - "Choose now: the library is easy to change later, and waiting would use the whole week"
  - "Build the full dashboard with both libraries this week and compare them at the end"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "More information is only worth it if it could change the choice. Here, waiting would use the whole week before the deadline."
  - "This is a technical choice the client expects you to make. Passing it back slows things down and looks unsure."
  - "Right. This is a two-way door, and waiting would use the whole week you have. You already have enough information to decide."
  - "This doubles the work on a short deadline to answer a question that does not need that much evidence."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "An NGO moves its help line for learners from email to a messaging app. Which plan best follows this module?"
options:
  - "Switch now, check after a month, and go back if most messages wait over a day for a reply"
  - "Decide now and never revisit it, so that learners are not confused by more changes"
  - "Wait until other NGOs share results from the same change before deciding anything"
  - "Keep both email and the messaging app open forever, and decide later which one to close"
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. It decides, sets a review date, and names a specific signal that would change the decision."
  - "Never reviewing means the NGO cannot correct a mistake, even when the signs are clear."
  - "Waiting for others may take months, and their situation may be different. Learners need help now."
  - "This avoids the decision and doubles the work for a small team, with no point where they learn and choose."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "On Tuesday you realize that power cuts will probably make you miss a client deadline on Thursday. When should you tell the client?"
options:
  - "On Thursday morning, once you are completely sure you will miss the deadline"
  - "Today, with what you can deliver Thursday and a realistic date for the rest"
  - "Only if the client messages you to ask how the work is going"
  - "After you deliver, with an apology and a small discount for the delay"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "Waiting until you are sure leaves the client almost no time to adjust their own plans."
  - "Right. This is a likely, high-impact risk. Telling the client early, with a clear offer, gives them time to plan."
  - "The client cannot ask about a risk they do not know exists. Staying quiet hides it."
  - "By then the harm is done. An apology or a discount does not give the client back the time they needed to adjust."
```

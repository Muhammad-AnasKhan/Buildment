## A decision that cannot wait

Dina coordinates a coding training program at an NGO. Thirty learners start in two weeks. She must choose: sessions in a rented training hall, or live sessions online.

There is a lot she does not know. She does not know next month's power schedule. She does not know how many learners have a stable internet connection, or how many can travel to the hall. And the hall must be booked by Thursday, or someone else will take it.

Dina could keep asking questions for weeks. But if she waits, she loses the hall, and the learners do not know where to go.

## Waiting is also a choice

When information is missing, it feels safe to wait. But waiting has a cost, and it is easy to forget that cost because nothing seems to happen. The client hires someone else. The booking is gone. The team is stuck. Not deciding is a decision too.

You will rarely be certain. The goal is not to know everything. The goal is to know **enough**.

## How much information is enough?

Ask three questions before you look for more information:

1. **Would it change my choice?** If no likely answer would change what you decide, stop looking.
2. **What does waiting cost?** A lost booking, a lost client, a stuck team. Compare that cost with what you would learn.
3. **How easy is it to undo?** Remember module 1: for a two-way door, decide with less information and learn by trying. For a one-way door, it is worth collecting more.

Dina books the hall. Her survey is not perfect, but it is enough.

## Write down your assumptions

Every decision like this rests on assumptions. Dina is assuming that most learners can reach the hall, and that the hall will have power during sessions. Write your assumptions down. They are exactly the things to watch, because if one is wrong, your decision may be wrong too.

## Set a review date

Deciding under uncertainty does not mean deciding forever. Choose a **review date**: a day, set in advance, when you will look at the decision again with new information. Dina sets hers for the end of the first week of training.

A review date also helps you move forward today. You do not need a perfect decision, because you have a planned moment to correct it.

## Say what would change your mind

This is the most useful habit in this chapter. Before you start, write down the specific signal that would make you change course. Make it something you can see or count, not a feeling.

| Vague | Specific |
|---|---|
| "If the hall does not work out" | "If more than 8 learners miss the first week because they cannot reach the hall" |
| "If the client seems unhappy" | "If the client asks for more than two rounds of changes on the first screen" |
| "If the tool is too slow" | "If the page takes more than 5 seconds to load on mobile data" |

Why decide this in advance? Once you have chosen, it becomes hard to admit you were wrong. You start to explain away bad signs. A signal written down beforehand protects you from that. It also shows your team or client that you are open to changing course, which builds trust.

<Callout type="tip" title="The decision sentence">
Put it all in one sentence: "We will **[decision]**. We assume **[assumption]**. We will review on **[date]**. We will change course if **[signal]**."

Dina's version: "We will run the training in the hall. We assume most learners can reach it and it will have power. We will review at the end of week one. If more than 8 learners miss sessions because they cannot reach the hall, we move to recorded lessons with one live online call each week."
</Callout>

## Check your understanding

```quiz

question: "Adam's startup team decides to switch their bug tracking to a new free tool. Which statement best says what would change their mind?"

  - "We will switch back if the new tool still does not feel right after a while"
  - "We will not switch back, because changing tools twice in one month wastes time"

explanations:
  - "This is a feeling with no time limit. Nobody will know when it has been reached."

  - "Right. It names a time and a signal the team can actually count."
```

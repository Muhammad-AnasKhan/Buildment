## Four parts of a good concern

A concern is not an accusation. It is help with a problem the other person may not see yet.

| Part | What you say | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Facts** | What was asked, in neutral words | "You asked for 30 reviews under different names." |
| **2. Risk** | Why it is a problem for them | "The platform bans this and can remove the shop." |
| **3. Alternative** | What you can do instead | "I can set up an email asking real buyers for reviews." |
| **4. Next step** | A clear, small action | "I can have it ready by Wednesday. Shall I start?" |

Often there is a step zero: **ask a question**. "Do you mean real reviews from your first customers?" Maybe you misunderstood, and a question gives the person a polite way to change their mind.

## Example wording

Ziad's message to Thomas:

> Hi Thomas, thank you, I am glad you like the shop. About the reviews: I cannot write reviews under other names. The platform bans fake reviews and can remove a shop that uses them, which would undo our work. Instead, I can add an automatic email that asks each buyer for a review a week after delivery. Real reviews build trust that lasts. I can have it ready by Wednesday. Shall I go ahead?

It says no once, clearly. It does not call Thomas dishonest or give a speech. It moves straight to a solution.

The same shape works in small moments. When a manager thanks Maria for a feature that Iman mostly built, Maria says:

> Thank you! To be fair, Iman built most of the payment logic. I worked on the screens and testing.

## Words that help and words that hurt

| Instead of | Try |
|---|---|
| "That is illegal." | "I am worried this could break the platform rules." |
| "You are lying to the client." | "The client may read this differently from how we mean it." |
| "I refuse." | "I cannot do that part, but I can do this instead." |

## If they insist

1. **Say no again, calmly, once.** You do not need to repeat the whole argument.
2. **Put it in writing.** A short message records what you said and when.
3. **Escalate.** In a team, talk to a more senior person. As a freelancer, talk to a trusted mentor or the platform's support.
4. **Be ready to walk away.** Ending a contract is a one-way door, so think it through. But some clients cost more than they pay.

<Callout type="warning" title="Protect yourself first">
If speaking up could put your safety, income or job at serious risk, pause. Talk privately to a mentor or someone you trust and plan your next step together. You do not have to handle it alone.
</Callout>

## A message on a Sunday evening

Ziad is a freelance web developer. He has just finished an online shop for a small skincare brand. The client, Thomas, is happy with the work and sends one more request:

> "Before launch, can you create 30 five-star reviews from different names? Just to get us started. I will pay an extra 50 dollars."

The task is easy. It would take Ziad one hour. He needs the money, and Thomas is a good client who might bring more work.

But something feels wrong. Real customers would read those reviews and believe them. Most review platforms ban fake reviews, and in many countries they break consumer protection laws (laws that stop businesses from misleading buyers). If anyone finds out, Thomas's shop could be removed from the platform, and Ziad's name would be connected to it.

Ziad does not want to lose the client. He also does not want to do something he would be ashamed of. What should he do?

## Most ethical problems are grey, not black and white

Fake reviews are a fairly clear case. Many situations at work are less clear. These are **grey areas**: there is no obvious rule, and reasonable people may disagree.

Here are some you may meet in your career:

| Situation | Why it feels uncomfortable |
|---|---|
| A founder asks you to copy a competitor's website design | Is it inspiration, or is it taking someone else's work? |
| Your manager praises you for a feature a teammate built | Staying quiet is easy, but the credit is not yours |
| You want to reuse a client's data in another project | It saves time, but the data was shared for one purpose |
| Your task took 5 hours, but you estimated 8 | The client pays by the hour and expects 8 |

None of these come with a warning sign. They arrive as normal requests, often from people you like and need.

## Why this matters for your career

In remote and freelance work, your reputation travels fast. A client review, a recommendation from a team lead, or a screenshot in a group chat can follow you for years. One bad choice can become a **one-way door**, the kind of decision from module 1 that is hard to undo.

Good ethics is also good business. Clients who learn that you will not lie *for* them also learn that you will not lie *to* them. That is why they give you more responsibility.

<Callout type="info" title="This module is not about being perfect">
Everyone faces pressure: money, deadlines, a boss or a client who insists. The aim is not to judge people. The aim is to give you simple tools to pause, check, and respond in a way you can stand behind.
</Callout>

## Key words

| Word | Plain English |
|---|---|
| **Grey area** | A situation with no clear rule, where right and wrong are not obvious |
| **Integrity** | Doing what you say and being honest, even when it costs you |
| **Confidential** | Private information you must not share or reuse without permission |
| **Concern** | A worry you share with someone because something may be wrong |
| **Escalate** | Take a problem to a more senior person when you cannot solve it yourself |

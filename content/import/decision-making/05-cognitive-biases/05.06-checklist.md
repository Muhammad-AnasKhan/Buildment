## Find a bias in your own decision

Mentors read this answer. Pick a real decision from your own past, at work, in study or in a project. It does not need to be a big or painful one. Small, honest examples are the most useful.

```openquestion
prompt: "Describe one past decision of your own where a thinking trap was at work. Say: (1) the decision and what you chose, (2) which trap it was (confirmation bias, sunk cost, anchoring or overconfidence) and the signal that shows it, and (3) how the check from this module would have changed what you did."
required: true
minWords: 100
allowSpeechInput: true
modelAnswer: "Last year I took a freelance logo job. The client said in the first message that his budget was 30 dollars, and I agreed to that price. Before reading his message, I had planned to charge about 120 dollars, because the job needed around eight hours. The trap was anchoring. The signal is that my price moved to his number, but nothing about the work changed. The job took ten hours and I felt I had worked almost for free. If I had used the check, I would have written my price and my reasons before reading his budget. Then I could have said: 'For 30 dollars I can do one simple design with no revisions. For the full job with three options, my price is 120 dollars.'"
```

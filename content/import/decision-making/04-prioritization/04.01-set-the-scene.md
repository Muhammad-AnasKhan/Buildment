## Hana's week

Hana is a third-year software engineering student. She also works 20 hours a week as a part-time tester for a small company in Europe. And she is one of four students building an app for a team project.

On Sunday night she writes down her week:

| Task | When |
|---|---|
| Review for her databases exam | Exam on Thursday |
| Write a test report for her job | Due Wednesday |
| Prepare the team project demo | Demo on Saturday |
| Help a friend fix his CV | He asked "when you can" |
| An extra test her manager mentioned | "If you have time" |
| Read 140 unread messages in the team group chat | Growing every hour |

On top of this, her area has power for about eight hours a day, and the hours change.

Every task feels urgent. So Hana does what most of us do: she starts with whatever message arrives first. By Wednesday night she has answered every chat, helped her friend, and started the extra test. But the test report is late and she has not opened her exam notes.

She worked hard all week. She just worked on the wrong things first.

## Why this matters for your career

In an office, a manager can see you are overloaded and step in. In remote work, nobody sees your desk. They only see what you deliver, and when. If you say yes to everything, some promises will break, usually the important ones.

Prioritizing is not about working more hours. It is about choosing on purpose: what to do first, what to plan for later, and what to hand back or drop. It also means telling people early when a plan has to change.

<Callout type="info" title="Saying no is part of prioritizing">
Every yes uses time that another task needed. Choosing what not to do is as important as choosing what to do.
</Callout>

## Key words

| Word | Plain English |
|---|---|
| **Priority** | A task that comes before others |
| **Urgent** | Needs attention soon, because of time pressure |
| **Important** | Matters for your goals or for people who depend on you |
| **Impact** | How much difference a task makes |
| **Effort** | How much time and work a task needs |
| **Deadline** | The date or time something must be finished |
| **Renegotiate** | Go back to someone and agree on a new plan |

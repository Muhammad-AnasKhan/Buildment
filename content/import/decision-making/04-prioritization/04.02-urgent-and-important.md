## Monday morning

- The team group chat wants everyone to vote on a logo for the project.
- A friend asks a quick question about a phone app.
- Her exam is on Thursday. She has not started reviewing.

## Two questions for every task

Ask two separate questions:

1. **Is it urgent?** Will something go wrong if it waits until tomorrow?
2. **Is it important?** Does it move my goals forward, or keep a promise to someone who depends on me?

The answers give you four boxes.

| | Urgent | Not urgent |
|---|---|---|
| **Important** | **Do it now** | **Plan it:** give it a fixed time |
| **Not important** | **Keep it short:** hand it off or group it | **Drop it** or leave it for spare time |

This grid is often called the **Eisenhower matrix**. It is named after Dwight Eisenhower, a US president who spoke about the difference between urgent problems and important ones. The four-box grid was made popular later by time-management books. You do not need the history: you need the two questions.

## Hana's tasks in the grid

| Task | Box |
|---|---|
| Her manager reports that a test she ran missed a bug customers now see | Do it now |
| Review for the exam on Thursday | Plan it |
| Vote on the team logo, answer the friend's phone question | Keep it short |
| Reorganize her notes app for the third time | Drop it |

"Keep it short" does not mean ignore. It means a two-minute answer, or answering all small messages together at one time instead of one by one. If someone else can do it, hand it off.

## The box that builds careers

The **Plan it** box holds the work that changes your future: studying, learning a new skill, updating your portfolio, preparing early for a demo, resting so you do not become exhausted. None of it shouts. So it gets pushed back, again and again, until it turns into a crisis or is lost.

Protect this box on purpose:

- **Book the time first.** Put two hours of exam review in your calendar before the week fills up.
- **Use your best hours.** If you have power and internet from 8:00 to 12:00, use them for important work, not for chat.
- **Turn off notifications** during that block. Check messages at fixed times instead.

<Callout type="warning" title="Urgent for whom?">
A message marked "urgent" is urgent for the sender. It may not be important for your goals right now. Ask: "What happens if this waits until 14:00?" Often the honest answer is "nothing". A request from your manager or client usually is important, though, because your work depends on it.
</Callout>

## Common questions

**Q: What if my manager says everything is urgent?**
A: Ask them to choose the order: "Which of these should I finish first?" You will practice this in chapter 4.4.

**Q: Should I always do the important tasks before the urgent ones?**
A: No. Tasks that are both urgent and important come first. The goal is to stop the "not important" tasks from eating the time you planned for important work.

Each question is a situation from remote work, freelancing, study or an NGO. Use the tools from this module: urgent and important, impact and effort, and renegotiating early with a clear alternative. Pick the one best answer, then read every explanation.

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "You work remotely for a startup and you built its checkout page. At 10:00, a support colleague reports that checkout is failing for all customers. You had planned to spend the morning writing tests for next month's feature. What do you do first?"
options:
  - "Finish the tests first, because you planned the morning for them"
  - "Add the checkout problem to next week's task list"
  - "Look at the checkout problem now, and move the tests to later"
  - "Reply that you will look at it after lunch"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "A plan is a tool, not a promise to ignore emergencies. The tests are important but not urgent today."
  - "Customers cannot pay right now. Waiting a week turns an urgent problem into a much bigger one."
  - "Right. Checkout failing for everyone is urgent and important, and you know the code best. The tests can move."
  - "Every hour of a broken checkout loses sales. 'After lunch' treats an urgent and important task as if it can wait."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Jana is a junior designer. Her goal this year is a remote job with a company abroad, but her portfolio is a year old. Every week she says she will update it 'when things are calmer'. What is the best advice for her?"
options:
  - "Book a fixed two-hour slot each week, at a time she usually has power"
  - "Wait for a quiet month with fewer client tasks, then do it all at once"
  - "Update it only when she finds a specific job she really wants to apply for"
  - "Do not update it, because experience matters more than a portfolio"
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. This is important but not urgent, so it needs protected time. Choosing hours with power makes the plan realistic."
  - "Calm months rarely come. Important but not urgent work keeps slipping unless it has a fixed time."
  - "That turns it into an urgent rush, and good jobs often close before a rushed portfolio is ready."
  - "For remote design jobs, the portfolio is often how employers see your experience. It supports her main goal."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "You freelance for a small online shop and have two days free. Which task gives the best result for the client first?"
options:
  - "Build a customer loyalty points system the owner mentioned once (about three weeks)"
  - "Change the font in the page footer to match the new logo (one hour)"
  - "Rewrite the whole shop in a newer, faster framework (several weeks)"
  - "Fix the 'Add to cart' button, which does not work on phones (half a day)"
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "This may have real value, but it is a big project with no deadline. Plan it and split it after the quick win."
  - "Low effort, but also low impact. It is a fill-in for spare time."
  - "High effort, and customers would see little difference. This is a time sink unless there is a strong reason."
  - "Right. Every shopper on a phone is blocked from buying. High impact, low effort: a quick win."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "On Thursday evening a client writes: 'Can you also add Arabic language support by Monday?' You already promised the current features for Monday, and the translation work alone needs about a week. What is the best reply?"
options:
  - "Sure, I will add Arabic too, and work over the whole weekend to fit both in by Monday"
  - "Arabic needs about a week. Should I deliver the current features first, or start Arabic first?"
  - "Sorry, adding Arabic is not possible this week because my schedule is already full"
  - "Say nothing now, then explain on Monday how far you got with Arabic and what is left"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "This hides a conflict you already know about. The client may lose both deliveries, and trust with them."
  - "Right. It states the real limit, offers two clear options and lets the client choose the order."
  - "It is honest, but it gives the client nothing to work with. An alternative keeps the conversation going."
  - "Waiting removes the client's chance to plan. Early news gives them options."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "You promised an NGO manager a report by Sunday. On Wednesday, an internet cut has cost you two full days of work, and you will not finish everything. What should you do?"
options:
  - "Work extra hours and tell the manager only if you really miss the Sunday deadline"
  - "Send the report on Sunday with some sections missing, and hope the manager does not notice"
  - "Tell the manager today, share what is done, and offer a new date or shorter report"
  - "Ask a friend to write the missing sections without telling the manager about it"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "This waits until the last moment. If the extra hours are not enough, the manager hears the news too late to adjust."
  - "Hiding missing work damages trust much more than the delay itself."
  - "Right. Early, honest news with clear options lets the manager plan, and shows you are in control of your work."
  - "This hides a change from the person who owns the report. It can also cause quality and trust problems."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Your team lead asks you to finish both a database change and the slides for a client demo 'as top priority' by tomorrow. You can only do one of them well in time. What is the best move?"
options:
  - "Do half of each tonight, so both are partly done when the lead checks"
  - "Tell your lead you can only finish one well, and ask which comes first"
  - "Pick the one you think matters more for the client, and say nothing about it"
  - "Promise your lead both, then work through the night to finish them"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "Two half-finished tasks are often worth less than one finished task. Neither may be usable tomorrow."
  - "Right. Your lead owns the priorities and may know things you do not, such as what the client expects."
  - "You might choose well, but your lead may be planning around the other task. Make the conflict visible."
  - "This hides the risk. If anything goes wrong in the night, both tasks fail and your lead learns too late."
```

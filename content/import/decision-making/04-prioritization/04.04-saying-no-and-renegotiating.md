## A small feature by Friday

Rania is a freelance web developer. On Monday her client, Mark, writes: "Could you also add a search filter by Friday? It should be small."

Rania's week is already full. She promised Mark the payment page by Friday, and power cuts give her about five working hours a day. She has three choices:

- Say yes, and hope it works out.
- Say a flat "no".
- Say "not like this, but like this", with an alternative.

The first choice feels kind, but it often ends with both tasks late. The second protects her time, but leaves Mark with nothing. The third is usually best.

The earlier you speak, the more options the other person has. Late news removes options. That is why clients and team leads remember who warned them early.

## The shape of a good "no" or "not now"

A good message has four parts:

1. **Thank or acknowledge** the request. One sentence is enough.
2. **State the real limit** briefly. No long story, no blaming.
3. **Offer a clear alternative:** a later date, a smaller version, or a swap.
4. **Ask them to choose** or confirm.

There are a few ways to reshape a request:

| Option | What you say |
|---|---|
| Move the date | "I can do it, by next Wednesday." |
| Make it smaller | "I can do a simple version by Friday, the full one next week." |
| Swap | "If I do this first, that task moves to Monday. Which comes first?" |
| Point elsewhere | "I cannot take this, but Sawsan knows this area well." |

The word **scope** means what is included in a piece of work. "Make it smaller" is often called reducing the scope.

## Messages you can adapt

Change the names, dates and details to fit your situation.

**To a client, offering two options:**

> Hi Mark, thanks for the search filter idea. It will be useful. This week I am finishing the payment page we agreed on, so I cannot add the filter by Friday without putting both at risk. I can offer two options: (1) the payment page on Friday and the filter next Wednesday, or (2) a simple filter on Friday and the full version next week. Which works better for you?

**To a team lead, asking which comes first:**

> Hi Aisha, I am happy to take the API docs update. Right now I am on the login bug you asked me to finish by Thursday. If I start the docs today, the bug will move to Monday. Which one should come first?

**An early warning about a delay:**

> Hi Peter, a quick update on the dashboard. Power in my area has been off longer than usual this week, so I have had about four working hours a day. I can deliver the charts by Friday as planned, and the export feature by Tuesday. If you need the export sooner, tell me and I will do it before the charts.

**A clear no that leaves room for next time:**

> Thanks for inviting me to the hackathon team. I have to say no this time: I have exams until the 15th and I would not be able to do my part properly. Please ask me again next time.

<Callout type="tip" title="With a manager, ask for the order">
You rarely say a flat no to work that is part of your job. Instead, show the conflict and ask your manager to choose the order. That is not refusing. It helps them decide with real information.
</Callout>

## What to avoid

- **"I'll try my best."** It sounds like yes, so the other person plans on it.
- **Silence,** hoping the request is forgotten. It rarely is.
- **Long apologies or long excuses.** One clear sentence about the limit is more professional.
- **Saying yes now and missing the date later.** This hurts trust more than an honest "not by Friday".

In some workplaces and cultures, saying no to a client or a senior person can feel rude. In most international remote teams, a clear and early "not now, but here is what I can do" is seen as professional.

## Common questions

**Q: What if the client rejects every option?**
A: Then ask what matters most to them and agree what to drop or move. If the full request is truly impossible, say so clearly in writing now. That is far better than saying it on Friday.

**Q: Is it ever right to just say yes?**
A: Yes, when you have checked your week and you really can do it without breaking another promise. The goal is not to say no often. It is to only say yes when you mean it.

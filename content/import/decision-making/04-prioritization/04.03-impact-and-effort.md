## One developer, many requests

Karim is the only developer at a small NGO. NGO means non-governmental organization: a group that works for a social cause and is not part of the government or a business. His NGO runs a learning app for children.

Staff have sent him a long list of requests. He has a month, and he cannot do them all. Two of them:

- **Fix the sign-up form.** It shows an error on some older phones, so some families cannot create an account. He thinks it needs one afternoon.
- **Add an offline mode,** so children can keep learning during internet cuts. This is very valuable, but it will take several weeks.

## Two questions for every task

Ask:

1. **Impact:** How much difference will this make? Who benefits, how many people, and how much?
2. **Effort:** How much work is it? Count hours or days, and include waiting for other people.

In module 3, impact meant the harm a risk could cause. Here it means the good a task can do.

Rough answers are enough. Low, medium or high works well.

| | Low effort | High effort |
|---|---|---|
| **High impact** | **Quick wins:** do these first | **Big projects:** plan them and split them up |
| **Low impact** | **Fill-ins:** do them in spare time | **Time sinks:** question them or drop them |

Different teams use different names for these boxes. The idea is the same.

## Karim's list in the grid

| Task | Impact | Effort | Box |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fix the sign-up error | High | Low | Quick win |
| Add an offline mode | High | High | Big project |
| Change button colors on the settings page | Low | Low | Fill-in |
| Rebuild the admin panel in a newer framework "to look modern" | Low | High | Time sink |

The time sink is the dangerous one. It feels like real work, it can be fun, and it uses weeks. But nobody's life gets better because of it.

## Splitting big projects

Big projects often contain a quick win inside them. Karim can split the offline mode into steps:

1. Save the last lesson a child opened, so it works without internet.
2. Save a whole course.
3. Sync progress when the internet returns.

Step 1 might take a few days and help many children right away. Splitting turns one scary project into a series of smaller, useful deliveries.

<Callout type="warning" title="The quick-win trap">
If you only ever pick quick wins, the big projects never start. A good week often has a few quick wins and one protected block of time for a big project. This is the "Plan it" box from chapter 4.2 again.
</Callout>

## Limits of the grid

- **Your guesses can be wrong.** Ask the person who made the request what difference it would make for them. People also often guess too low on effort, so add some extra time.
- **Some low-impact tasks are required.** A tax form or a promise in a contract may have low impact for users, but you still have to do it.
- **Use it together with urgency.** A quick win due next month can wait for a big project due on Friday.

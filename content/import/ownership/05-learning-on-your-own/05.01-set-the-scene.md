## A new tool on Monday

Lama is a junior frontend developer. She works remotely for a small startup in London, two hours behind her time zone. On Monday her team lead writes:

> "We are moving our tests to Playwright this sprint. Can you write the tests for the login page? No rush, end of next week is fine."

A sprint is a short, fixed work period, often two weeks. Playwright is a tool for testing websites. Lama has never used it. Her team lead is busy with a release. The senior developer who knows the tool is on leave until Thursday. The internet in her area works for a few hours each evening.

She has three choices:

- Wait until Thursday and ask the senior developer to explain it.
- Message her team lead with many small questions during the release.
- Start learning on her own today, and save her questions for the parts she cannot solve.

Only the third choice moves the work forward and respects other people's time.

## Why this matters for your career

In a remote team, nobody sits next to you to show you how a tool works. People are in different time zones, busy with their own tasks, or offline. A question you send at 10:00 may get an answer at 16:00.

This means the ability to learn on your own is not a bonus. It is a core part of the job. Team leads trust people who can say "I have not used this before, but I will learn it" and then actually do it.

Learning on your own does not mean never asking for help. It means you do the work you can do first, so that when you ask, your question is small and clear.

<Callout type="info" title="This connects to ownership">
In module 1 you learned to own the outcome, not just the task. Learning the tool you need is part of owning the outcome. Nobody else can do that learning for you.
</Callout>

## Key words

| Word | Plain English |
|---|---|
| **Docs** | Short for documentation: the written guide for a tool |
| **Official docs** | Docs written by the people who make the tool |
| **Practice project** | A tiny project you build only to learn |
| **Stuck** | You cannot move forward and do not know the next step |
| **Time-box** | A fixed amount of time you give to a task |

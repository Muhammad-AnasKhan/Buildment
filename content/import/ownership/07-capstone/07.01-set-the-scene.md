## A plan that lasted four days

Asma is a junior QA tester. QA means quality assurance: testing software to find problems before users do. She works remotely for an agency and studies software engineering in the evenings.

Last month, on a Sunday night, she wrote a plan:

- Wake up at 5:00 every day.
- Study test automation for two hours every evening.
- Finish a full online course in three weeks.
- Post on LinkedIn every day.

By Thursday the plan was gone. The power was off for most of Tuesday evening. On Wednesday an urgent client bug took all her time. She missed two days, felt she had failed, and stopped.

Asma did not lack effort. Her plan had no room for real life. It had too many goals. Its habits only worked on a perfect day with power, internet and free time. It had no answer for problems she knew would come. And nobody else knew about it, so nobody asked how it was going.

## What makes a plan last

| A plan that breaks | A plan that lasts |
|---|---|
| Many goals at once | Two or three goals |
| Big daily tasks that need a perfect day | Small habits with a minimum version for bad days |
| Hopes nothing goes wrong | Names likely obstacles and a plan for each |
| No date to check progress | Review dates in the calendar |
| Kept private | One person checks in |

This module teaches the right-hand column. You will write a plan for the next six to eight weeks, a mentor will review it, and you will start it before you finish the course.

## What your mentor looks for

Your mentor does not judge how ambitious your plan is. They look at whether it is realistic for your life. A small plan you follow for eight weeks is worth far more than a big plan you drop on day four.

Expect questions like: "What happens to this habit on a day with no power?" or "How will you know this goal is done?" Good questions now stop your plan from breaking by Thursday, like Asma's did.

## Choosing what to improve

Look for ideas in three places:

- **Feedback you received** from a client, a team lead, a teacher or a mentor.
- **Your learning log answers** from modules 1 to 6. Many of them show a pattern, such as unfinished tasks or waiting too long to ask for help.
- **What your next step needs.** A job you want, a client you hope to win, or a course you start soon.

A good mix is one technical skill and one or two work habits from this course, such as finishing work or giving updates before people ask.

| Clear enough to plan | Too big or too vague |
|---|---|
| Send clients a written update twice a week | Be more professional |
| Finish and publish my portfolio site | Become a senior developer |
| Learn enough SQL to write simple reports | Learn all of data science |
| Ask for help after 45 minutes stuck, not two days | Stop being shy |

<Callout type="info" title="Share what you are comfortable with">
Your mentor will read your plan. You do not need to explain private family or health matters. A short phrase such as "family duties" or "a hard week" is enough.
</Callout>

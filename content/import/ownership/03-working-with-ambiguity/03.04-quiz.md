Each question is a short work scenario. Choose the best answer, then read the explanation for every option, including the ones you did not pick.

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Leila is a freelance developer. A new client writes: 'We need a mobile app for our restaurant.' Nothing else. What should she do first?"
options:
  - "Start designing the menu screens today, because every restaurant app needs a menu"
  - "Send a price for a full app with ordering, online payment and delivery tracking"
  - "Ask what the app should help customers or staff do, and how they will know it works"
  - "Ask which programming language and framework the client would like her to use"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "This is a silent guess. The client may want table bookings or staff scheduling, not a menu."
  - "Pricing a full app before knowing the goal may scare the client away or promise far more than they need."
  - "Right. This asks about the goal and what success looks like. Every other choice depends on the answer."
  - "Most restaurant owners have no opinion on this. It is a technical choice the client expects Leila to make."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Rami is redesigning an NGO's donation page. Halfway through, the coordinator says, 'Our newsletter sign-up also looks old.' What should he do?"
options:
  - "Write it down, ask if it belongs in this project or a later one, and keep going"
  - "Redesign the newsletter sign-up too, since it is a small extra and shows initiative"
  - "Say nothing about it, because the newsletter was not part of the original request"
  - "Pause the donation page until the coordinator decides about the newsletter sign-up"
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. This treats it as a scope question. The client decides, and the main work does not stop."
  - "Small extras add up and can push back the donation page. Changing the scope should be a shared decision."
  - "Ignoring the comment misses a real need. Asking about it is better than pretending it was not said."
  - "The donation page is clear and agreed. There is no reason to stop it while a separate question is answered."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Mona got answers to her questions from a client in Dubai. Before she starts the main work, what is the best way to make sure they agree on the goal?"
options:
  - "Thank the client and start right away, since all of her questions were answered"
  - "Forward the client's answers to her teammate, so that both of them have the details"
  - "Ask the same questions again later in the week, in case the client has changed their mind"
  - "Write the goal, what done means and what is excluded in a few lines, and ask for a yes"
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "Answers can still be misunderstood. Starting without checking can hide a gap until much later."
  - "Sharing with the team is useful, but it does not check that Mona and the client understand the goal the same way."
  - "Asking the same questions again wastes the client's time and makes Mona look like she was not listening."
  - "Right. Playing the goal back in writing shows any misunderstanding now, while it is cheap to fix."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Ahmed's client in California replies about once a day. The deadline is in two days. His question is still open: should the contact form send messages to one email address or several?"
options:
  - "Wait for the reply before writing any of the code for the contact form"
  - "Build it for one address, say so in his update, and keep the address easy to change"
  - "Build it to send to every staff email address on the team, so no message is ever missed"
  - "Message the client three more times today, so the question stays at the top"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "Waiting could use half of the time left, for a detail that is easy to change later."
  - "Right. This is a low-risk, easy-to-change assumption. Stating it keeps the work moving and lets the client correct it."
  - "This is a silent guess that could flood staff inboxes. It is also harder to explain than one clear assumption."
  - "Repeated messages put pressure on the client without moving the work forward."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Samir is moving a client's customer records to a new system. It is unclear whether records older than five years should be deleted. The client has not replied yet. What should he do?"
options:
  - "Delete them and list it as a stated assumption, so he can point to it if anyone asks"
  - "Ask a teammate what they think, and delete the old records if the teammate agrees"
  - "Move the other records now, and keep the old ones until the client gives a clear answer"
  - "Delete the old records now and ask the client afterwards if that was what they wanted"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "Deleting data cannot be undone. A stated assumption does not make a costly guess safe."
  - "The teammate does not own the client's data. Only the client, or someone they named, can decide this."
  - "Right. He keeps moving on the safe work and waits on the part that cannot be undone."
  - "Asking after a deletion is too late. If the answer is no, the records are already gone."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Priya shared her assumptions with a client, including 'English only for the first version'. Two days later the client replies: 'We also need Arabic.' What is the best response?"
options:
  - "Explain politely that the client had the assumption list and should have replied sooner"
  - "Add Arabic quietly over the weekend, so the deadline and the price stay the same"
  - "Keep the page in English only for now, since the client replied after the date she gave"
  - "Thank the client, update her list, and say what the change means for time and cost"
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "This blames the client for something normal. The list did its job: it brought out the need early."
  - "Hiding the extra work sets a pattern of unpaid work and gives the client a wrong picture of the effort."
  - "The date was there to keep things moving, not to refuse a real need. The client should still decide."
  - "Right. A wrong assumption is the system working. She adjusts openly so the client can decide with real facts."
```

## Module 3 checklist

- [ ] I can name the three clarifying questions: what success looks like, who it is for and what is out of scope
- [ ] I can turn a vague word like "better" or "soon" into a specific question
- [ ] I can write an assumption with why I think it is true and what changes if it is wrong
- [ ] I can name one kind of decision where I should wait for an answer instead of assuming
- [ ] I answered the questions in chapters 3.2 and 3.3, and finished the quiz in 3.4
- [ ] I submitted the assignment breakdown below

## Learning log

1. **Your unclear task:** Go back to the task you described in chapter 3.1. Which of the three questions (success, who it is for, out of scope) was missing at the start?
2. **Your habit:** When a task is unclear, what do you usually do: guess and start, wait, or ask? Why do you think that is?
3. **Asking:** What makes you hesitate to ask a client or a team lead a question? What would make it easier?
4. **Next time:** Write one sentence you will use the next time you send someone your assumptions.

## Your assignment breakdown

Here is a real kind of message that freelancers and volunteers receive. Read it as if it came to you.

> Hi! We are a small NGO that runs free coding workshops for young people. Our website is old and nobody visits it. Can you make our website better? We have a small budget and would like to see something soon. Thanks, Salma (programs coordinator)

Turn it into a clear starting point. If you have a real unclear task of your own right now, you may use that instead. A simple list is fine. A mentor will review your answer.

```openquestion
prompt: "Break down the assignment above (or a real unclear task of your own). Include: (1) the goal in one or two sentences as you understand it, (2) what success could look like, (3) who the work is for, (4) three to five clarifying questions, each with your best guess, (5) at least three assumptions, each with what changes if it is wrong, and (6) what you would start today and what you would wait on."
required: true
minWords: 150
allowSpeechInput: true
modelAnswer: "Goal: help the NGO get more young people to find the website and sign up for workshops. Success could be: more workshop sign-ups through the site each month, and staff can update workshop dates without a developer. Who it is for: young people aged 15 to 25, mostly on phones, and Salma's team, who update the site. Questions: 1. What should a visitor do on the site? My guess: sign up for a workshop. 2. Who updates the site now? My guess: Salma, a few times a month. 3. What does 'soon' mean? My guess: a first version in three weeks. 4. What is the budget range? My guess: enough for a small site, not a full platform. 5. Is anything out of scope? My guess: no online payments and no new logo. Assumptions: the new site keeps both Arabic and English, like the old one (if only Arabic is needed, it saves time); the current hosting stays (if wrong, I need a day to move it); sign-ups go to a simple form, not a full system (if wrong, much more time and cost, so this is the riskiest). Start today: look at the current site on a phone, list what is broken or slow, and sketch a simple home page with a clear sign-up button. Wait on: the sign-up system and anything that changes hosting or costs money."
```

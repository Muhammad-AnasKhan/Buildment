## Learning log

1. **Your first example:** Go back to what you wrote in chapter 2.1. Looking at it now, did you report the problem or propose a fix? What would you do differently?
2. **Your blind spot:** Which kind of gap do you usually stop noticing: repeated questions, manual work, outdated documents, or tasks nobody owns? Why do you think that is?
3. **Act or ask:** Think of your current work, study or volunteering. Name one thing you could fix on your own and then tell people, and one thing where you must ask first.
4. **What holds you back:** What usually stops you from speaking up about a gap: not being sure, not wanting to look rude, being new, or something else? What would make it easier?

## Your initiative challenge

Find one real gap in a team, project or community you belong to. It can be your workplace, a freelance client, a study group, a volunteer group, an open source project or a local community. Choose something small enough that one step could help.

Then write a short proposal, as if you were sending it to the person who can say yes. A mentor will review it. You do not have to send it before you submit here, but you should know who you would send it to.

```openquestion
prompt: "Write your proposal in five parts. Problem: what you saw, with facts or numbers if you can. Impact: who it affects and what it costs. Proposed step: one small first step. Effort: how much time or help it needs, and who does it. Who to ask: the person who can say yes, and the exact question you will ask them. Do not use real names of other people."
required: true
minWords: 100
allowSpeechInput: true
modelAnswer: "Problem: In our volunteer tech group, new members ask in the chat how to get access to the shared drive and the task board. I counted 9 of these questions in the last month, and each one waited about a day for an answer. Impact: New volunteers lose their first days waiting, and two stopped replying before they got access. The two coordinators answer the same question again and again. Proposed step: I write a one-page welcome note with the access steps and pin it in the chat. Effort: About two hours of my time to write it, and ten minutes of a coordinator's time to check it is correct. Who to ask: Our volunteer coordinator. I will ask: 'Can I write a pinned welcome note with the access steps? I can send you a draft by Thursday to check.'"
```

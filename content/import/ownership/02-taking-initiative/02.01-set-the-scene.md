## The same question, every week

Nour and Karim are volunteer mentors for a free coding program run by a small NGO. The program has about sixty students, a shared chat group and a setup guide that explains how to install the tools.

The setup guide is two years old. Some of the steps no longer work. Every week, new students post the same question in the chat: "The install fails at step 4. What do I do?" A mentor answers, the student continues, and the next week a new student asks again.

Karim answers the question every time. He is kind and quick. But he thinks: "The guide is not my job. The coordinator wrote it. If it needs fixing, she will fix it."

Nour answers too. After the third time, she stops and counts. In one month, eleven students got stuck on step 4. Each one lost an evening, and some gave up and did not come back. She sends the coordinator a short message: what she noticed, how many students it affected, and an offer to rewrite step 4 herself in one evening if the coordinator agrees.

The coordinator says yes the same day.

## Why this matters for your career

Karim did nothing wrong. He did his job. Nour did her job **and** noticed a gap that nobody owned. She did not complain, and she did not change the guide without asking. She brought a small, clear proposal to the right person.

That is initiative. In module 1 you practiced owning your own tasks. This module goes one step further: noticing what is missing around your tasks, and offering a fix in a way people can say yes to.

When you work with a remote team or a client, this is one of the clearest signals people see. A team lead remembers the person who said "I noticed this, here is a fix" much longer than the person who only did what was on the list.

<Callout type="info" title="Initiative is not doing everything yourself">
Taking initiative does not mean fixing every problem alone or doing other people's work without asking. It means noticing, checking, and proposing. Often the right move is a short message, not a big project.
</Callout>

## Key words

| Word | Plain English |
|---|---|
| **Initiative** | Starting or suggesting something useful before you are asked |
| **Gap** | The difference between how things are and how they could be |
| **Proactive** | Acting early, before a problem grows |
| **Reactive** | Acting only after a problem appears or someone asks |
| **Proposal** | A short suggestion of what to do, with enough detail to say yes or no |
| **Within your role** | Inside the work and decisions your team expects you to own |

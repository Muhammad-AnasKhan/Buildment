Six short work scenarios about ownership and accountability. Each one has one best answer. Read the explanation after every question, even when you get it right: the reasons matter more than the score.

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Heba builds a sales report page for a client. All her tests pass. The client's sales team works mostly from phones while visiting shops. Which check best shows that the page will work for the people who need it?"
options:
  - "Ask a teammate to review her code for naming, style and structure before it is merged"
  - "Run the full test suite again on her laptop, to be sure that nothing changed"
  - "Write clear notes about the code, so that the next developer understands it"
  - "Open the report on a phone and load it with a real month of the client's sales"
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "A code review is useful, but it checks the code, not whether the page works for the sales team on their phones."
  - "Her tests already pass on her laptop. Running them again tells her nothing new about phones or real data."
  - "Good notes help the next developer. They do not tell Heba whether the sales team can use the page."
  - "Right. This checks the outcome where it matters: on the device the sales team uses, with realistic data."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "During a server update for an NGO client, Hamza missed a step. The donation page was down for 20 minutes on Sunday afternoon before he fixed it. The client has not noticed. What should Hamza do?"
options:
  - "Tell the client today what happened, for how long, the fix, and what he will change"
  - "Say nothing, because the page is working again and the client did not notice it"
  - "Mention it at next month's check-in call, if the client asks about the website"
  - "Send a long apology that explains each update step in detail and how bad he feels about it"
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. The client may have lost donations and deserves to know. The four parts give them the facts and show he has a plan."
  - "The client may have lost donations in those 20 minutes. If they find out later, they will wonder what else he has not told them."
  - "A month is too late, and 'if the client asks' means they may never hear it from him."
  - "A long apology puts his feelings at the center. The client needs short facts: impact, fix and what changes."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Which sentence from a mistake report shows the most accountability?"
options:
  - "The deployment script failed last night, and the old version of the site went live"
  - "Mistakes were made during the release, and the team is now looking into them"
  - "I ran the old deployment script, so the old version of the site went live"
  - "Testing did not catch the problem, so the old version of the site went live"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "This makes it sound like the script failed by itself. It hides who did what."
  - "'Mistakes were made' names no one and nothing. The reader cannot tell what happened."
  - "Right. It says 'I' and states exactly what happened. That is clear, and it is easy to act on."
  - "This points at testing instead of reporting the writer's own part first."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Majd promised a client a new feature by Thursday. By Tuesday, long power cuts have left him only three hours of work in two days. He now knows Thursday is not possible. What should he do?"
options:
  - "Work through every night when the power is back, and say nothing unless he misses the Thursday date"
  - "Tell the client today, offer what he can deliver by Thursday, and give a realistic new date"
  - "Deliver the feature on Thursday without testing it, and fix any bugs the client finds later"
  - "Wait until Thursday morning, then explain about the power cuts and ask the client for time"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "This hides a real risk. If the power fails again, the client finds out too late to change their plans."
  - "Right. Telling the client early, with what is possible and when, lets them plan. That is owning the result."
  - "This meets the date but not the outcome. The client gets broken work and more problems later."
  - "Waiting removes the client's chance to adjust while there is still time to do something."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Diana asks Basel to add a button that exports the orders page to a spreadsheet file. What is the most useful question for Basel to ask before he starts?"
options:
  - "Which color and size should the new export button be, to match the rest of the page?"
  - "Should the export code go into a new file, or into the existing orders page code?"
  - "Can I use a free library to create the spreadsheet file, or should I write all of my own code?"
  - "Who will use the exported file, and what will they do with it after they download it?"
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "This is a fair detail to settle later, but it does not tell Basel what problem the export solves."
  - "Basel can usually decide this himself. It is about the code, not about what the user needs."
  - "This is a reasonable technical question, but the answer depends on what the file is for, which he does not know yet."
  - "Right. Knowing who uses the file and why tells Basel which columns, format and size really matter."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Two days ago, Ola delivered a change so that an NGO's volunteers get a weekly summary email. The client accepted it. The first emails go out this morning. What best shows ownership of the outcome?"
options:
  - "Check the sending logs this morning, then ask the coordinator if volunteers got the email"
  - "Start her next task, because the client already tested and accepted the change two days ago"
  - "Ask the coordinator to send her a written report about the emails every week"
  - "Redesign the email template while she waits, so that it looks more modern"
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. This closes the loop. If something went wrong on the first real send, she finds out on day one, not week three."
  - "Acceptance is not the outcome. The first real send is where hidden problems usually show up."
  - "This gives the coordinator extra work every week. One short check now is enough."
  - "Nobody asked for a new design, and it does not check whether the emails arrived at all."
```

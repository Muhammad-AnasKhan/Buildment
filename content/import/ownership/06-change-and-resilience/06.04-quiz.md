```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Dalia is building a mobile app for a small shop. The owner writes: 'Can we add a loyalty points system? Should be easy.' What should Dalia do first?"
options:
  - "Agree, because a happy client matters more than the original plan"
  - "Ask what the owner wants the points to achieve for the shop"
  - "Say no, because the contract does not include a points system"
  - "Build a quick version tonight, then show it to the owner"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "Agreeing before knowing the goal or the cost can break the deadline and the budget, and that will not keep the client happy."
  - "Right. Knowing the goal, such as more repeat customers, helps Dalia find the right solution and size the change."
  - "The contract matters, but a flat no closes the conversation. The change may be worth doing for an agreed extra price or time."
  - "This spends time before Dalia knows the real need. The quick version may solve the wrong problem."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Adam's client asks for a change that adds about a week of work. The client has a fixed launch date. What should Adam send the client?"
options:
  - "A short note that he will work weekends, so the launch date can stay the same"
  - "A message that the change is not possible, because the launch date is fixed"
  - "A full new project plan with every task listed, before he discusses the change"
  - "Two options: keep the date and add the change after launch, or move the date"
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "This hides the real cost. If anything else goes wrong, the launch is at risk and the client did not get to choose."
  - "The change may still be possible after launch. Saying it is impossible removes options the client may want."
  - "A full plan takes time and may be wasted if the client chooses differently. Agree the direction first."
  - "Right. Options with their costs let the client make the trade-off, and the fixed date stays protected."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "On a call, a client agrees to drop one feature so that a new one can be added. The call ends. What should Farah do next?"
options:
  - "Send a short written summary of the change and the new plan to the client"
  - "Start the new feature right away, since everyone agreed on the call"
  - "Wait for the client to send a written request before doing anything"
  - "Tell only her own team, because the client was on the call and already knows"
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. A written summary makes sure everyone remembers the same decision, and gives a record if questions come later."
  - "Starting is fine, but without a written record, people often remember a call differently a week later."
  - "Farah can confirm the decision herself. Waiting for the client to write it slows the project for no good reason."
  - "People leave calls with different memories of what was agreed. The client needs the summary too."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Sami works on an NGO website. The team lead changes the main color and layout for the third time this month. Sami feels frustrated. What is the most useful thing he can do?"
options:
  - "Make the change quietly, and keep his frustration to himself"
  - "Reply in the team channel that the constant changes are wasting everyone's time"
  - "Make the change, and ask the lead in private what is driving the repeated changes"
  - "Refuse the change until the lead promises that the design is final"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "Doing the work is good, but the pattern stays hidden. If something is causing repeated changes, nobody fixes it."
  - "A public complaint makes the lead feel attacked and turns a project problem into a personal one."
  - "Right. Sami does the work and raises the pattern calmly. There may be a reason he does not know, such as unclear feedback from a donor."
  - "Nobody can promise a final design. Refusing the work damages trust and does not stop the changes."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Ines has spent ten days on a report feature. The client says the feature is no longer needed. Ines feels the work was wasted. Which response helps her most?"
options:
  - "Ask the client to pay a penalty for the cancelled feature, as soon as possible"
  - "Note what can be reused and what she learned, then plan the next priority"
  - "Keep the feature in the product anyway, because it is already built"
  - "Decide to ask for more detail before every task from now on, to avoid this"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "If the contract covers cancelled work, Ines can raise it calmly. But a penalty does not help her plan, and asking right away harms the relationship."
  - "Right. Parts of the code may be reused, and the next priority keeps the project moving. Her frustration is fair, and this still helps."
  - "Unneeded features add code to test and maintain. The client decides what the product needs."
  - "Clarifying helps, but some changes happen however well a task was defined. More questions would not have stopped this one."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Mahmoud is a junior developer. A client messages him directly with a change request. His team lead normally agrees all changes with clients. What should Mahmoud do?"
options:
  - "Make the change, because the client is the one who pays for the project"
  - "Ignore the message, because the client should have contacted the lead"
  - "Reply that he cannot help, and ask the client to contact the lead instead"
  - "Thank the client, say he will check with his lead, and forward it to the lead"
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "The client pays, but the lead plans the work and the cost. A change made without the lead can break the plan or the budget."
  - "Ignoring a client looks careless, and the change request may be important."
  - "This is polite but leaves the work to the client. Mahmoud can pass it on himself in one minute."
  - "Right. The client gets a quick, polite answer, and the person who agrees changes can check the impact."
```

## A message on Wednesday morning

Rania is a frontend developer. She works remotely for an NGO that runs a health information website. For two weeks she has built a new sign-up form. It is almost finished, and the launch is planned for next Monday.

On Wednesday morning, her project manager, Tom, sends a message:

> "Sorry for the late change. Our donor now wants the form to collect a phone number and a preferred language, and to work without the newsletter step. Can we still launch Monday?"

Rania reads it twice. Some of her work will need to change. Part of the newsletter step she built will not be used. On top of that, the power in her area has been off most evenings this week, so she has fewer working hours than usual.

Her first feeling is frustration. That is a normal reaction. Then she has to decide what to do next.

## Change is normal in projects

Most projects change while they are being built. A client sees the first version and understands what they really need. A donor changes the rules. A competitor launches something new. A budget is cut. None of this means anyone failed. It is how real work goes.

Setbacks are normal too. A **setback** is something that stops your progress or pushes it back: a lost client, a failed exam, a broken laptop, or days without power or internet. Some setbacks come from our own mistakes. Many come from things outside anyone's control.

<Callout type="info" title="What resilience means in this module">
Resilience here is not about being tough or always feeling fine. Difficult events are difficult, and some are outside anyone's control. This module is about practical things you can do: understand the change, adjust the plan, take one small next step, and ask other people for support.
</Callout>

## What is still your choice

You cannot always choose what changes. You can usually choose your next step. Rania could reply in anger, quietly work every night to finish by Monday, or ask a few questions and agree a realistic plan with Tom. Each choice leads to a different week, and to a different level of trust.

In modules 1 to 5 you practiced owning outcomes, taking initiative, working with unclear tasks, finishing your work and learning on your own. This module adds the moments when the plan itself changes.

## Key words

| Word | Plain English |
|---|---|
| **Requirement** | Something the client or team says the product must do |
| **Scope** | The full list of work that was agreed |
| **Impact** | The effect a change has on time, cost, quality or other work |
| **Setback** | Something that stops your progress or pushes it back |
| **Support** | Help from other people: advice, time, a new deadline or someone to listen |

## A hard week

Mariam works part time as a junior QA tester (a person who tests software to find problems) for a remote startup. She is also studying for a computer science degree.

Last week, power and internet in her area were cut for six days. She could not join the team calls. She missed the deadline for her test report, and she could not take an online quiz for her course. When the connection comes back, she has 40 unread messages and three late tasks. She feels she has fallen behind everyone, and she is tired.

None of this was her fault. It is still her situation, and she wants to find a way forward.

## Your situation is not your worth

After a setback, many people blame themselves: "I should have done better", "Everyone else manages". Be careful with this voice. Power cuts, illness, family needs and events in your area are not personal failures. Some setbacks are outside anyone's control, and no amount of planning would have stopped them.

It helps to sort what happened into two groups:

| Outside my control | Within my control, next time |
|---|---|
| The power and internet cut | Keeping work files saved for offline use |
| How long it lasted | Agreeing with my team what to do if I lose access |
| The quiz date | Asking early what happens if I miss a quiz |

The right column is not a list of mistakes. It is a list of small things that may help next time. Some setbacks have an empty right column, and that is fine too.

## Five recovery steps

**1. Take care of the basics first.** Safety, rest, food and family come before work. Recovery is slower when you are exhausted, so a night of sleep is part of the plan, not a delay.

**2. Tell the people affected.** Send short, factual updates. You do not need to share private details or apologize for things you could not control.

> Hi Leo, I was without power and internet from Monday to Saturday, so my test report is late. I am back online now. I can send the report by Wednesday. Is that OK, or is another task more urgent?

**3. Choose one small next step.** Not the whole list. One task you can finish today, such as "send the update" or "test the login page". Finishing one thing gives you a starting point. You practiced this in module 4.

**4. Ask for support.** Support is not a favor you have to earn. Teams and courses expect it. Be clear about what would help:

- A new deadline: "Could I have until Wednesday?"
- Less work for a while: "Which of these three tasks can wait?"
- Help with a task: "Could someone work with me for 30 minutes on the test setup?"
- A rule you may not know: "Can I take the quiz I missed on another day?"
- Someone to talk to: a friend, a family member, a mentor or a fellow learner.

**5. Plan around what keeps happening.** Some setbacks repeat, like daily power cuts. You cannot fix the cause, but you can adjust: work offline when possible, charge a power bank, and agree with your team which hours you are usually online.

<Callout type="warning" title="When it is more than a hard week">
If you feel sad or hopeless for a long time, cannot sleep, or cannot focus on anything, talk to someone you trust. If your fellowship, university or employer offers wellbeing support or a counsellor, ask your mentor how to reach it. Asking for this kind of help is a sensible step, not a weakness.
</Callout>

## Common questions

**Q: What if I really did cause the setback?**
A: Own it the way you practiced in module 1: what happened, the impact, the fix and what changes. Then move on to the next step. Owning a mistake does not mean punishing yourself for it.

**Q: What if I do not want my team to know what is happening at home?**
A: You decide how much to share. "I had a family emergency" or "I lost power and internet" is enough. Focus on the impact on work and your new plan.

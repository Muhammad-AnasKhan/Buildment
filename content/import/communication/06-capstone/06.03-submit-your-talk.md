## Your turn

This is the main task of the course. You will give the talk you prepared in chapter 6.2, and a mentor will review it.

Take your time. You do not need to finish in one sitting. If the power goes or your connection drops, your script is still in your notes. Come back and continue when you can.

## Step 1: Choose your delivery

There are three ways to submit. All three are accepted in the same way, and the mentor uses the same checks for each.

| Delivery | Good when | What you submit |
|---|---|---|
| Video recording | Your connection can upload a few minutes of video | A link to the video |
| Audio-only recording | Video is too heavy for your connection or phone | A link to the audio file |
| Written script | You cannot record, or uploading is not possible now | The full script, typed in the box |

An audio-only file is usually much smaller than a video of the same length, so it uploads faster on a weak connection. For a demo, you can describe what is on screen as you go, for example "Now I tap Book, and the confirmation appears."

## Step 2: If you record

- **Record offline.** Use your phone's voice recorder or camera app. You do not need internet to record, only to upload.
- **Find a quiet moment.** Short background noise is fine. Do not wait for a perfect room.
- **One take is enough.** If you lose your place, pause, breathe and continue. Real talks have small mistakes too.
- **Upload when the connection is better.** Use a service such as Google Drive and set sharing to "Anyone with the link can view".
- **Test the link.** Open it in a private browser window where you are not signed in. If it asks you to sign in or request access, your mentor will see the same thing.

## Step 3: If you write the script

Write the full words you would say, not only notes or headings. Your mentor should be able to read it out loud as your talk.

- Write the opening, the three parts and the ending, in order.
- For a demo, add short notes in square brackets for what is on screen, for example: [show the booking page].
- Keep it to about 600 to 750 words, the length of a five-minute talk.

## Step 4: A last check

Before you submit, go back to the short list in chapter 6.2 one more time. Then ask yourself one question: if my listener heard only the first 30 seconds, would they know my main point?

<Callout type="info" title="What your mentor looks for">
Your mentor does not grade your accent, your English level or your video quality. They look at clarity: a main point in one sentence, an easy structure, one clear example, words that fit the listener, and an ending that says what happens next. Expect feedback and questions, not a score.
</Callout>

## Step 5: Submit

Paste your recording link in the link field, or type your full script in the box. You can also use voice input and edit the text after. If you recorded, you can still add a few lines in the box, for example your main point, but it is not required.

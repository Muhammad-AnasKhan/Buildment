## From topic to script

Saeed is a frontend developer who freelances for a small online shop in Amman. He chose his capstone topic quickly: a demo of the faster product page he built. Then he practiced it once, out loud, with a timer. It took eleven minutes. He never said his main point, and he ended with "So, yeah, that is it."

Nothing was wrong with his topic. He just went from topic to talking without a plan. This chapter gives you the plan, in six steps. Each step uses a tool you already know.

## Step 1: The point and the listener

Start with what you wrote in chapter 6.1. Who is listening, and what is your main point? Write the point as one sentence that your listener would understand (modules 1 and 2).

Then ask: what does this listener already know, and what do they need from me? A client needs to know what changes for their business. A team lead needs to know what to do next. An interviewer needs to know what you did and what you learned.

## Step 2: Three parts and an ending

Use the plan from module 4. For a five-minute talk, the time splits roughly like this:

| Section | Time | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Opening | about 30 seconds | Says the point and what the three parts are |
| Part 1 | about 1 minute | First supporting group of ideas |
| Part 2 | about 1.5 minutes | Second group, often the main example |
| Part 3 | about 1 minute | Third group |
| Ending | about 30 seconds | Repeats the point and says what happens next |

For a demo, a good set of three parts is: the problem, the result working, and one key decision you made. Leave a little time for the unexpected.

## Step 3: Add one example or story

Listeners forget lists of facts. They remember one clear example or one short story (modules 2 and 5). Pick one, put it in the part where your point needs the most help, and keep it under a minute: situation, problem, what you did, result, lesson.

## Step 4: Write it for the ear

A script is for listening, not reading, so write it the way you speak:

- **Short sentences.** One idea in each.
- **Signposts.** These are short phrases that tell the listener where they are, like "First", "The second part is" or "To finish".
- **Say the point twice.** Once at the start and once at the end.

As you saw in chapter 4.2, many people speak about 120 to 150 words a minute. So a five-minute script is about 600 to 750 words. Here is Rawan's opening and ending from chapter 6.1:

```text
Opening:
In our last release, automatic tests caught three bugs before
any customer saw them. My point today: ten minutes of test setup
per feature saves us hours of fixing later. I will cover three
things: how we worked before, what I set up, and what it saved.

Ending:
So, ten minutes of setup per feature saved us hours. My request:
for the next two weeks, let us add tests to every new feature.
I can work with anyone who wants help setting them up.
```

## Step 5: Practice out loud

Reading your script silently is not practice. **Rehearse** it: say it out loud, the way you will give it, with a timer. Then record yourself on your phone and listen once. You will hear long sentences and filler words that you cannot see on paper.

If you can, practice once for a friend, a classmate or a family member. Afterwards, ask them to say your main point in one sentence. This is the summarize-back check from module 3, used in reverse. If they cannot say it, your listener will not either.

## Step 6: Prepare for questions and problems

Write down three questions your listener is likely to ask, with a short answer for each (module 4). If you do not know an answer, plan to say "I will find out and send it to you by tomorrow."

Then plan for things going wrong. Power or internet can cut out in the middle of a live demo. Keep a short recording of the demo working and a few screenshots ready. Use test data, never real customer data.

## Check it against a short list

| Check | Ask yourself |
|---|---|
| Point | Can I say my main point in one sentence? |
| Listener | Did I choose words this listener knows? |
| Structure | Do I have an opening, three parts and an ending? |
| Example | Is there one clear example or short story? |
| Time | Did a timed practice run fit in about five minutes? |
| Ending | Do I repeat the point and say what happens next? |
| Safety | Is there no private data or secret in it? |

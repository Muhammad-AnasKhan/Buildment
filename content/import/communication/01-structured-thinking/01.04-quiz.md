```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Salma, a freelance designer, writes to her client after a call. Her notes: the call was useful; the client approved the new design; she needs the text for the FAQ page by Monday, or the launch moves a week; she will share a test link tomorrow. Which first sentence is best?"
options:
  - "Thank you for the call today, it was very useful to go through the design together."
  - "Here is a short summary of everything that we discussed in our call today."
  - "Tomorrow I will share a test link, so you can see the new design working."
  - "Could you send the FAQ text by Monday? Without it, the launch moves a week."
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "A kind opening, but it gives the client nothing to act on. The request is pushed further down."
  - "This promises a summary in call order, which is story order. The client still has to search for what matters."
  - "This is good news, but the client does not need to act on it. The one thing that can delay the launch is still hidden."
  - "Right. It gives the request, the deadline and why it matters, all in the first line."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "A company that hires remote developers asks Ruba: 'Can you start on 1 June?' She can start on 1 June, but only part time until her final university exams end on 14 June. Which reply opening is best?"
options:
  - "Thank you for the offer! I am finishing my degree, and my exams end on 14 June."
  - "Yes, I can start on 1 June: part time until my exams end on 14 June, then full time."
  - "I would love to join the team, and I am sure we can find a start date that works."
  - "My final exams are in early June, so my start date depends on the exam schedule."
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "This gives a reason before the answer. The company must guess whether she can start on 1 June or not."
  - "Right. It answers the question with 'yes' in the first word, then gives the condition clearly."
  - "Friendly, but it avoids the question. The company still does not know if 1 June works."
  - "This sounds like a 'no' or a 'maybe', even though she can start on 1 June. The real answer is lost."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Ziad must tell his team lead that a release will be late. His message has four parts: the new release date, the reason, what he needs from the lead, and the story of how he found the bug. Which order is best?"
options:
  - "The new date, the reason, what he needs from the lead, then the story only if useful"
  - "The story of how he found the bug, the reason, the new date, then what he needs"
  - "What he needs from the lead, then the story of the bug, the reason and the new date"
  - "The reason, the new date, the story of the bug, and what he needs at the very end"
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. The most important news comes first, then why, then the action. The story is optional detail at the end."
  - "This is story order. The lead reads about the bug before learning the release is late."
  - "The request comes first, but the lead cannot judge it yet: the new date is at the very end."
  - "The date is near the start, but the request is last, where a busy reader may never reach it."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Yara groups the points in her weekly project update under four labels: Done, In progress, Problems, Blocked. What is the main problem with these labels?"
options:
  - "Four groups is too many for a weekly update to a manager, so she should use two"
  - "The labels should be full sentences, so the manager understands each group better"
  - "Problems and Blocked overlap, so some points could fit in either group"
  - "The Done group should always come last, because finished work matters least"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "Two to four groups is a good range. Four clear groups are fine for a weekly update."
  - "Short labels are easier to skim. The labels are short enough; the problem is what they mean."
  - "Right. A blocked task is also a problem, so the reader cannot tell where to look. Each point should fit in one group only."
  - "Order matters, but the question is about the labels themselves. Also, finished work is often useful for the reader to see."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Tariq's update to a client already has the main point first. Now he has three groups: Done this week, Next week, and Need your decision by Thursday. In which order should the groups go?"
options:
  - "Done this week, Next week, Need your decision by Thursday"
  - "Next week, Done this week, Need your decision by Thursday"
  - "Done this week, Need your decision by Thursday, Next week"
  - "Need your decision by Thursday, Done this week, Next week"
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "This is time order, which works for steps. But here the client must act, and that part is last."
  - "This order has no clear logic, and the part the client must act on is still last."
  - "The decision is in the middle, so a reader who skims after the first group may miss it."
  - "Right. Action first: the client sees what to decide and by when. Then the other groups follow in time order."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Lina's team lead asks in chat: 'Why is the app so slow today?' Lina does not know yet. She has checked the database and the network, and will check the server logs next. Which first sentence is best?"
options:
  - "I checked the database and the network this morning, and both of them look normal."
  - "I don't know yet. I will check the server logs next and update you by 2 pm."
  - "It might be the server, but I am not sure, so I am still looking into it."
  - "There could be many reasons, such as the database, the network or the server."
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "Useful detail, but it does not answer the question. The lead must guess whether Lina knows the cause."
  - "Right. When you do not have the answer, that is your point. Say so first, then say what happens next and when."
  - "This sounds like an answer, but it is a guess. The lead may act on it as if it were true."
  - "This lists possibilities without saying what Lina knows, what she will do, or when the lead will hear more."
```

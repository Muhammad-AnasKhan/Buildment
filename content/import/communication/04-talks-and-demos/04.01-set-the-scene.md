## Five minutes on a call

Anas is a freelance web developer. For six weeks he has built a booking app for a small dental clinic in Germany. Today the clinic owner, Marta, has a 30-minute video call with him. She tells him: "Please show us what is finished in the app. You have about five minutes, then we have questions."

Anas knows the app very well. He wrote every line. But in the first minute he starts with the database, then jumps to a bug he fixed, then opens the wrong page. Marta's manager asks, "Sorry, can patients book an appointment yet?" Anas answers for three minutes. The call ends and nobody is sure what is finished.

The app was good. The five minutes were not. Marta now has to send an email to ask the questions the demo should have answered.

## Why this matters for your career

In remote work, a lot of trust is built in short moments on a screen. You will meet many of them:

- A **client check-in**, where you show progress on a freelance project.
- A **team demo**, where each person shows what they finished this week or this sprint (a sprint is a short work period, often one or two weeks).
- A **job interview**, where you are asked: "Walk us through a project you are proud of."
- A **hackathon or NGO pitch**, where you have three to five minutes to explain an idea to judges or funders.

People in these meetings are busy, and they often join from different time zones. They remember the main point, what they saw working, and how you handled their questions. A short, clear talk makes your work look as good as it really is.

The good news: this is a skill, not a talent. You do not need perfect English or a loud voice. You need a simple plan, a little practice and a backup for when things go wrong.

## Key words

| Word | Plain English |
|---|---|
| **Talk** | A short, planned piece of speaking to a group, with or without slides |
| **Demo** | Showing your work running, not only describing it |
| **Outline** | A short plan of what you will say, in order |
| **Q&A** | "Questions and answers": the time after a talk when people ask you questions |
| **Screen share** | Showing your screen to others on a video call |
| **Backup** | A second option ready for when the first one fails |

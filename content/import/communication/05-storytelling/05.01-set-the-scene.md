## Two updates, one project

Rasha and Karim build a data collection app for an NGO. Field workers use it to record the needs of families in shelters. Every month, the two developers give a short update to the NGO's program manager, who then reports to the funders.

This month, Rasha goes first. She says:

> We fixed 14 bugs, made the forms load faster, added offline saving, and improved the upload screen.

Every word is true. The manager nods and writes "14 bugs, offline saving" in her notes.

Then Karim says:

> Last month, Mariam, one of our field workers, lost a whole day of forms when the connection dropped in the middle of an upload. She had to visit 30 families again. So we added offline saving. Last week the network was down all day in her area. Mariam filled in 42 forms anyway, and they all uploaded that night when the connection came back.

A week later, the manager meets the funders. She does not remember "14 bugs". She tells them about Mariam and the 42 forms. That story is the reason the funders understand why the app matters.

## Why stories stick

It is a common observation, at work and in daily life: people remember stories better than lists. Think of the last talk you heard. You probably remember an example or a moment better than the numbers on the slides.

A story is easier to remember for a few simple reasons:

- **It has a person.** We care about Mariam more than about "users".
- **It has a problem.** A problem makes the listener want to know what happens next.
- **It has cause and effect.** "The connection dropped, so we added offline saving" is easier to hold in your head than a separate fact.
- **It is easy to repeat.** The manager could tell Karim's story to the funders in her own words.

Notice that Karim did not replace the facts. His story carried one fact (offline saving) and one result (42 forms with no internet). A good work story is not decoration. It is a way to deliver a fact so that people remember it.

<Callout type="info" title="Stories are not only for big talks">
You can use a two or three sentence story in a weekly update, a client message, a job interview or a project demo. Most work stories are short.
</Callout>

## Key words

| Word | Plain English |
|---|---|
| **Work story** | A short, true account of one moment at work and how it turned out |
| **Situation** | The background: where you were and what was going on |
| **Problem** | The thing that went wrong or the challenge you faced |
| **Result** | What changed because of what you did, ideally with a number |
| **Lesson** | What you learned, or the point the story proves |
| **Pitch** | A short talk that asks someone to support, buy or fund an idea |

## Module 5 checklist

- [ ] I can explain why a story should reach the problem in its first one or two sentences

## Learning log

1. **Your moment:** Go back to the moment you described in chapter 5.1. Which of the five parts was missing or weak in your first version?
2. **Your part:** In your story, which actions did you take yourself, and which did others take? How will you make that clear?
3. **Your story bank:** List three or four true stories you could use in an interview. For each one, write which question it could answer, such as a mistake, a deadline or learning something fast.
4. **Out loud:** Say your story out loud and time it. How long did it take, and what did you cut to keep it under a minute?

## Your work story

Use the moment from chapter 5.1, or another true moment from work, study or a project. Keep it to about 80 to 120 words, so it takes under a minute to say. You may mark the five parts, or write it as one paragraph. A mentor will review your story.

```openquestion
prompt: "Write one short, true work story using the five parts: situation, problem, what you did, result, lesson. Use 'I' for your own actions and give a clear result, with a number if you can. Then add one sentence: where would you use this story (an update, a pitch, an interview or your capstone talk), and what point does it prove?"
required: true
minWords: 90
allowSpeechInput: true
modelAnswer: "Situation: Last year I volunteered as the only developer for a small NGO that sends food parcels. Problem: Volunteers tracked deliveries in three different spreadsheets, so some families got two parcels and others got none. What I did: I asked three volunteers how they worked, then built one shared form that checks if a family already received a parcel this week. I trained the team in a 20 minute call and wrote a one page guide in Arabic. Result: In the first month, double deliveries fell from about 30 to 2. Lesson: I learned to talk to the people who use a tool before I build it. I would use this story in an interview when someone asks about solving a real problem for users. It proves that I start from the user's problem, not from the code."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Bilal is shaping a story about how he made a slow page faster on an NGO's website. Which sentence works best as the result of his story?"
options:
  - "In the end, everything went really well and the client was happy"
  - "I worked very hard on it for many days and nights, even in power cuts"
  - "The page now loads in two seconds instead of nine on a slow phone"
  - "I learned that speed is one of the most important things in any app"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "This is too vague. 'Went well' does not tell the listener what changed or how much."
  - "This describes effort, not the result. The listener still does not know what changed for the NGO."
  - "Right. It names a clear change with numbers, so the listener can see and repeat the result."
  - "This is a lesson, not a result. It belongs at the end, after the listener knows what changed."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "In an interview, Tamer tells a story with 'we' in every sentence: 'We found the bug, we rewrote the module, we shipped it.' The interviewer asks: 'And what did you do?' What should Tamer change next time?"
options:
  - "Say 'we' for the team's work and 'I' for the parts he did himself"
  - "Say 'I' in every sentence, so the whole project sounds like his work"
  - "Keep 'we' everywhere, because talking about himself sounds proud"
  - "Leave the team out, and pick a story where he worked fully alone"
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. It is honest about the team and still shows the interviewer exactly what Tamer did."
  - "This takes credit for other people's work. A follow-up question can easily show it is not true."
  - "The interviewer is hiring Tamer, not the team. Without 'I', they cannot judge his work."
  - "Most real work happens in teams. Teamwork stories are fine as long as his own part is clear."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Huda has five minutes to demo her clinic queue app to an NGO that runs small health clinics. How should she open?"
options:
  - "With how she learned Flutter, from her first tutorial up to this app"
  - "With a list of all twelve features, so the NGO sees everything at once"
  - "With a tour of the code structure, to show the app is well built"
  - "With one patient who waited four hours, and how the app cuts that wait"
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "This story is about Huda, not about the NGO's problem. It is not relevant to what they need."
  - "A list of features is easy to forget. The NGO does not yet know why any feature matters."
  - "Code structure matters to developers. The NGO wants to know if the app helps their patients."
  - "Right. One person's real problem shows the NGO why the app matters, before they see a screen."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "In the team's short daily call, each person has about one minute. Yesterday, after a long search, Omar found that a login bug came from an expired key. He fixed it and added an alert. What should he say?"
options:
  - "The whole story of his search, step by step, so the team can learn from it"
  - "That the bug is fixed, the cause was an expired key, and he added an alert"
  - "Nothing about the bug, because daily calls are for plans, not for past work"
  - "The story of a harder bug from last month, because it is more interesting"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "The full story will not fit in one minute. He can offer to share details later with anyone who needs them."
  - "Right. Point first, then a very short story: the cause and what he did. It fits the minute and helps the team."
  - "The team needs to know the bug is fixed. A finished task is useful news in a daily call."
  - "This story is not relevant. The team needs to hear about yesterday's work, not a different bug."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Leen built an app for her university club. About 60 people used it. Before an interview, a friend tells her to say 'thousands of users' because it sounds better. What should she do?"
options:
  - "Say about 60 people used it, and stress what she learned from them"
  - "Say 'thousands', because interviewers expect numbers to be rounded up"
  - "Leave out the number of users, so nobody can ask her about it later"
  - "Choose another story, because a club app with 60 users is too small"
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. A true, small number is believable, and what she learned from real users is the strong part of the story."
  - "This is not rounding, it is a false claim. One follow-up question can expose it and damage trust."
  - "Interviewers often ask about numbers anyway. A real number makes the story more believable, not less."
  - "Size is not what matters. A small project with real users and a clear lesson is a good interview story."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Ahmad's story: 'A tutoring site I run went down in exam week, because the free hosting plan had a traffic limit. I moved it to a paid plan in one hour and added a traffic alert.' Which sentence is the best lesson to end it?"
options:
  - "Hosting is always a very complicated part of building any website"
  - "I am a fast worker who can solve almost any problem under pressure"
  - "Now I check hosting limits before every busy period, not after"
  - "Free hosting is bad, and nobody should use it for a real project"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "This is a general opinion, not something the story shows. It does not say what Ahmad will do differently."
  - "This is a claim about himself. The story already shows he works fast, and the lesson should be about what he learned."
  - "Right. It follows directly from the story and shows what he now does differently."
  - "This is too broad. Free hosting can be fine. The real lesson is to check the limits before busy times."
```

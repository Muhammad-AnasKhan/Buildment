## Group discussion guide

Use this guide in a cohort or mentor session. One person, usually the mentor, acts as the facilitator. It works on a video call, an audio-only call or in a room.

**Topic:** "A junior developer on a remote team is stuck on a problem. Should they ask the team right away, or try alone first? If they try alone first, for how long?"

There is no single right answer. The topic is a tool: the real practice is listening.

### Roles

| Role | Who | What they do |
|---|---|---|
| Facilitator | One person, often the mentor | Reads the rules, keeps time, gives each speaker a turn |
| Speakers | Three to five people | Share views, summarize others, ask questions |
| Observer | One person (optional) | Does not speak, takes notes on the observer questions |

In a small group, the facilitator can also be the observer.

### Rules

1. **Summarize before you reply.** Before you give your view, summarize the previous speaker in one or two sentences, then ask: "Did I get that right?" The previous speaker says yes or corrects you. Only then do you speak.
2. **One person speaks at a time.** Use the "raise hand" button or a sign in the chat to ask for a turn.
3. **No interrupting.** If you think of a reply while someone is speaking, write one word and keep listening.
4. **If someone's connection drops,** the facilitator keeps their turn and goes back to them when they return.

### Time plan (30 minutes)

| Part | Time | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Start | 3 min | The facilitator reads the topic and the rules |
| Round 1: views | 8 min | Each speaker gives their view in about one minute |
| Round 2: summarize and reply | 10 min | Each speaker summarizes another speaker, checks, then replies |
| Round 3: questions | 5 min | Each speaker asks one open, specific question to another speaker |
| Debrief | 4 min | The observer shares notes, then everyone answers one debrief question |

### Observer questions

- How many summaries were correct the first time, and how many were corrected?
- Did anyone start replying before the speaker had finished?
- Which question got the most useful answer? Was it open or closed?

**Debrief question for everyone:** "When was it hardest for you to keep listening, and what pulled your attention away?"

### If you cannot meet as a group

Do the same discussion with one partner, such as a mentor, a friend or a classmate, for 15 minutes. Take turns giving views, and use the summarize-before-you-reply rule every time. If a call is not possible because of power or internet, have the discussion as a written chat thread with the same rule: summarize the last message before you reply to it.

## Learning log

1. **Your example:** Go back to the misunderstanding you described in chapter 3.1. Would a summary or a clarifying question have caught it? Write the exact sentence you could have used.
2. **Your hardest moment:** When is listening hardest for you: calls in English, weak audio, long chat messages, or something else? What is one habit from this module you will use there?
3. **Your question habit:** What closed question do you often ask at work or in study? Rewrite it as an open, specific question.
4. **This week:** Name one real conversation in the next week where you will summarize back what you heard. Who is it with?

## Your reflection on listening

After the discussion, write about how you listened. Be honest: the goal is to notice your habits, not to look perfect. A mentor will review your reflection.

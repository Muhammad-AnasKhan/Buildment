```quiz
type: mcq
question: "In a team call, Mira says: 'The payment API changes on the 15th. The old endpoints keep working until the end of the month, but new features only go into the new version.' Which summary is correct?"
options:
  - "So we must move everything to the new API before the 15th. Is that right?"
  - "So the old endpoints stop on the 15th, and new features use the new version. Right?"
  - "So the old API works until the month ends, and new features must use the new one. Right?"
  - "Okay, the API is changing soon. I will look into the details when I have some free time."
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "This mixes up the dates. The 15th is when the new version arrives, not the last day for the old one."
  - "The old endpoints keep working until the end of the month, not only until the 15th. This summary would make the team rush."
  - "Right. It keeps both dates and the rule for new features, in Mira's meaning, and asks her to confirm."
  - "This is not a summary. It shows nothing of what was heard, so Mira cannot correct a mistake."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Walid, a startup founder, asks you: 'Can you build a dashboard for our sales team?' Which first question is most useful?"
options:
  - "Which decisions will the sales team make with the dashboard each week?"
  - "Do you want the dashboard built in React, like the rest of your web app?"
  - "Would you like charts on the dashboard, or is a simple table enough?"
  - "Do you have an example of a dashboard you like that I could follow?"
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. The purpose decides everything else: which numbers to show, how often to update them, and whether charts or tables fit best."
  - "This is a closed, technical question. You can usually decide it yourself, and it says nothing about what the team needs."
  - "Charts or tables is a real choice, but it depends on what the team will use the dashboard for. Ask about the purpose first."
  - "An example helps with the look, but it does not tell you which numbers matter to this sales team."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Lina is on a call with a client in Norway. The audio cuts in the middle of a sentence about the budget, and she misses the number. What should she do?"
options:
  - "Keep listening, and ask a teammate after the call what the budget was"
  - "Guess the most likely number from the rest of the talk and write it down"
  - "Wait for the end of the call, then ask the client to repeat everything"
  - "Say right away that the audio cut, and ask for the budget figure again"
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "The teammate may have missed it too, or heard it wrongly. The client is the only sure source, and she is on the call now."
  - "A guessed budget is a dangerous assumption. It can shape the whole project."
  - "Waiting means the rest of the call is built on a missing number. Asking the client to repeat everything also wastes her time."
  - "Right. Asking at once, about only the part she missed, is quick and polite, and gets the exact figure from the client."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "In a team review, Joseph is explaining why the last release was late. Halfway through, Samir realizes Joseph is about to blame Samir's slow code review. What is the best thing for Samir to do?"
options:
  - "Interrupt now and explain his side before the others form an opinion"
  - "Write a keyword, let Joseph finish, then summarize his point and reply"
  - "Stay quiet in the meeting and message Joseph privately to complain"
  - "Start typing his reply in the meeting chat while Joseph is still talking"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "Interrupting stops Samir from hearing the full point. It also makes the talk feel like a fight, not a review."
  - "Right. Samir hears everything, shows he understood, and then gives his side calmly. A summary first also lowers the tension."
  - "Samir's view is useful for the team review. Complaining privately later does not help the team understand what happened."
  - "Typing while Joseph talks means Samir is not listening, and the team sees it."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Nisreen works for a company in New York, about seven hours behind Gaza. At 9:00 her time she gets a task: 'Update the onboarding emails.' She is not sure which emails or whether the design should change. What should she do?"
options:
  - "Send one message with numbered questions and say what she will do until she hears back"
  - "Wait until her manager comes online, then ask her questions on a quick call"
  - "Send each question as a separate message, as it comes to her during the morning"
  - "Make her best guess on every part, and list her open questions in Friday's weekly report"
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. One clear message gets answered in one round, and her default plan keeps the work moving while New York sleeps."
  - "Waiting seven hours loses most of her working day, and the questions could be asked in writing now."
  - "Many separate messages are hard to follow and easy to miss. Her manager may answer some and not others."
  - "Guessing on everything risks days of wrong work. Friday is far too late to learn that a guess was wrong."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Tamer is interviewing a user to improve an online course app. Which question will most likely get honest, useful information?"
options:
  - "Do you like the new search page that we added last month, more than the old one?"
  - "Was the search page easy to use the last time you tried to find a course?"
  - "What did you do the last time you searched for a course in the app?"
  - "Wouldn't the search page be easier to use if we added more filters to it?"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "This is a closed question. Many users say yes to be polite, so you learn little."
  - "This is closer, because it asks about a real moment. But it can still be answered with yes or no."
  - "Right. It is open and asks about real behavior, so the user describes what actually happened, including problems."
  - "This is a leading question: it suggests the answer Tamer wants. Users often agree with it, even when filters are not the real problem."
```

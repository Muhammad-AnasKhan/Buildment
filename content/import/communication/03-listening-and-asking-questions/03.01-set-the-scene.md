## A week of the wrong work

Majd is a freelance web developer. His client, Marieke, manages an NGO in the Netherlands that runs after-school study programs. On Monday they have a short voice call. His internet is weak that day, and the audio cuts a few times.

Marieke says: "We need an export for the donations page, so the finance team can see everything each month."

Majd hears "export" and "see everything". He spends five days building a beautiful PDF report with charts and monthly totals. On Friday he shows it to Marieke.

She is polite, but disappointed. "This looks great. But our accountant needs a spreadsheet file she can open and filter. And 'everything' means every single donation, not totals."

The spreadsheet export takes Majd one afternoon. The PDF report took a week, and nobody needs it.

## What went wrong

Majd is a good developer. The problem was not his code. He heard the words, but he did not check what Marieke needed. One sentence at the end of the call could have saved the week:

> "So you need a file of all donations each month that the finance team can open. What will they do with it?"

That sentence does two things. It **summarizes** what he heard, so Marieke can correct it. And it **asks a question** about the purpose, which is where the real need was hiding.

## Why listening is a skill

Many people think listening is easy: you stay quiet while the other person talks. But while we stay quiet, many of us are busy planning what to say next. We hear the first part, guess the rest, and start preparing our answer.

Remote work makes this harder:

- Audio cuts out, and cameras are often off.
- People speak with different accents, and English may be a second language for both of you.
- Notifications and other tabs pull your attention away.
- Across time zones, a misunderstanding can take a whole day to discover.

In module 2 you practiced explaining your ideas so others understand them. This module is the other half of the conversation: understanding what others mean. Like explaining, listening and asking questions use simple habits that you can practice.

<Callout type="info" title="Asking is not a weakness">
Many new team members stay quiet because they do not want to look slow. In good remote teams, it is the opposite. A clear question at the start shows that you are thinking about the work.
</Callout>

## Key words

| Word | Plain English |
|---|---|
| **Active listening** | Listening with full attention, then showing what you understood |
| **Summarize** | Say the main point again, shortly, in your own words |
| **Clarifying question** | A question that makes a request clearer |
| **Open question** | A question that needs more than yes or no to answer |
| **Closed question** | A question you can answer with yes, no or one word |
| **Assumption** | Something you believe is true but have not checked |

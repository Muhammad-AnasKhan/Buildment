```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Majd is a freelance mobile developer. His laptop is five years old, and his client's app is due on Friday. He says: 'I do not keep a backup of my code, because Allah protects my work.' Which teaching fits best?"
options:
  - "He is right, because keeping backups would show he doubts Allah"
  - "He should copy his code to three places every hour, day and night"
  - "Tie it and trust: keep one backup, and rest his heart on Allah"
  - "He should stop the project, since an old laptop might fail him"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "The man in the hadith asked whether to tie his camel or leave it loose and trust. The answer was to tie it. A backup is not doubt."
  - "One sensible backup is the means. Copying every hour, day and night, turns the plan back into worry."
  - "Right. 'Tie it and trust' (Jami' at-Tirmidhi 2517, graded hasan): one backup is tying the camel, and his heart can then rest on Allah."
  - "An old laptop is a risk to plan for, not a reason to give up the work. A backup covers the risk."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Tamer wants to go to a job fair in another town on Monday. He calls himself 'one who trusts Allah' and has not checked the opening times, saved the bus fare or printed his CV. Which answer names his mistake and the fix?"
options:
  - "Trust without means: take a CV, the fare and the opening times"
  - "Means without trust: he should worry more about the fair itself"
  - "No mistake: real trust means leaving all the details to Allah"
  - "Too much planning: he should not go to the job fair at all"
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. Like the people of Yemen who came for Hajj without provisions (Sahih al-Bukhari 1523), he skips the means and calls it trust. Allah told them: take provisions (Quran 2:197)."
  - "Tamer is not over-planning or over-worrying. He has not planned at all, so the means are what is missing."
  - "The pilgrims from Yemen said the same, and they ended up asking people for food. The Quran told them to take provisions."
  - "He has not planned anything yet, so too much planning cannot be his problem. Going is good; going prepared is better."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Lubna must upload a design project by 18:00 on Thursday. The upload takes about 40 minutes, and her router needs grid power. The grid usually comes from 14:00 to 20:00 this week, but the schedule changed twice last week. Which plan ties the camel best?"
options:
  - "Start the upload at 17:00, since the grid usually comes at 14:00"
  - "Make dua and upload whenever the power happens to come on"
  - "Ask her teacher now to accept late work if the power fails"
  - "Finish by Wednesday, upload early, and know where else to upload"
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "17:00 plus 40 minutes leaves only 20 minutes, and it depends on a schedule that changed twice last week. That is a camel left loose."
  - "Dua is good, but this plan has no means and no backup for the most likely problem: no power at the right time."
  - "Asking ahead is not wrong, but it plans for failure instead of a way to succeed. Uploading early is the stronger means."
  - "Right. An early finish gives time for the 40-minute upload whenever the grid comes, and a second place to upload is the backup. Then she can trust Allah with the rest."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Sawsan's father has lost his job. She says she trusts Allah to provide, and this week she is sending applications every day and helping in her aunt's shop. A friend says: 'If you really trusted Allah, you would not be running around so much.' Which reply fits the story of Hajar, peace be upon her (Sahih al-Bukhari 3364)?"
options:
  - "Her friend is right: real trust would let her sit and wait calmly"
  - "Hajar trusted fully and still searched seven times: both belong"
  - "Hajar's story is about water, so it says nothing about finding work"
  - "Running around shows weak trust, so she should do half as much"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "Hajar said 'then He will not abandon us', and then she ran between Safa and Marwa. Sitting and waiting was not what her trust looked like."
  - "Right. Complete trust and full effort were in the same person on the same day. Sawsan's work is not a sign of weak trust."
  - "The story is about water, but the teaching is about trust and effort together, which applies to any need."
  - "How hard she works does not measure her trust. Hajar's seven runs were part of her trust, not a lack of it."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "A youth group wants to collect winter clothes for families in their neighbourhood next month. Which plan follows the pattern of the Hijra best?"
options:
  - "Announce it today and trust that people will bring what is needed"
  - "Plan it alone in secret, so that nobody else can make a mistake"
  - "Prepare early, give each person a job, get news, then trust Allah"
  - "Wait until they are sure it will succeed before they start at all"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "This is trust without the planning. The Hijra had months of preparation before they set out."
  - "The Hijra used many people, each with a job: family, helpers and a skilled guide. Doing it alone leaves out that help."
  - "Right. Early preparation, a job for each person, a source of news and then trust is the pattern of Sahih al-Bukhari 3905 and the cave."
  - "Nobody can be sure of the result. In the cave, the plan had done its part and the trust carried them. Waiting for certainty means never starting."
```

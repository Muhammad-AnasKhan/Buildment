```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Majd is a freelance mobile developer. His laptop is five years old, and his client's app is due on Friday. He says: 'I do not keep a backup of my code, because Allah protects my work.' What would tie the camel here?"
options:
  - "Rely on Allah's care, since a backup would show doubt in His protection"
  - "Keep copies in three places and check them several times a day"
  - "Finish the app first, and make a backup once it is handed over"
  - "Keep one backup, then leave the rest of his worry with Allah"
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "The man in the hadith asked whether to tie his camel or leave it loose and trust. The answer was to tie it. A backup is not doubt."
  - "Copies in three places, checked several times a day, turn a sensible means into the worry loop. One backup is enough for this risk."
  - "The old laptop could fail before Friday, which is exactly when the work would be lost. A backup made afterwards protects nothing."
  - "Right. One backup is tying the camel (Jami' at-Tirmidhi 2517, graded hasan). After that, his heart can rest on Allah."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Ihab wants to go to a job fair in another town on Monday. He calls himself 'one who trusts Allah'. He has not checked the opening times, saved the bus fare or printed his CV. What should he do before Monday?"
options:
  - "Go anyway, and trust that the details will sort themselves out there"
  - "Check the times, save the fare and print his CV, then go and trust"
  - "Go, and trust that someone at the fair will print a CV for him"
  - "Make dua for the fair to go well, since that is the part that counts"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "This is the mistake of the people of Yemen who came for Hajj without provisions (Sahih al-Bukhari 1523). They called it trust and ended up asking others."
  - "Right. Allah told the pilgrims to take provisions (Quran 2:197). For Ihab, the provisions are the times, the fare and the CV, and then trust."
  - "Hoping someone else will cover his means is what the pilgrims did when they asked people for food. The Quran told them to bring their own."
  - "Dua is good and he should make it. But the pilgrims show that dua and trust do not replace the provisions."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Lubna must upload a design project by 18:00 on Thursday. The upload takes about 40 minutes, and her router needs grid power. The grid usually comes from 14:00 to 20:00 this week, but the schedule changed twice last week. Which plan ties the camel best?"
options:
  - "Finish by Wednesday, upload early, and know a second place to upload"
  - "Start the upload at 17:00, since the grid usually comes by 14:00"
  - "Make dua, and upload whenever the power comes on during Thursday"
  - "Upload at 17:00, and ask her teacher for more time if it fails"
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. An early finish gives time for the 40-minute upload whenever the grid comes, and a second place to upload is the backup. Then she can trust Allah with the rest."
  - "17:00 plus 40 minutes leaves only 20 minutes, and it depends on a schedule that changed twice last week. That is a camel left loose."
  - "Dua is good, but this plan has no means and no backup for the most likely problem: no power at the right time."
  - "Asking for more time after the upload has failed, at the last moment, is a weak backup. The teacher may say no, and the problem was easy to avoid."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "After seven months of applications and practice, Nizar gets a remote job. He tells his friends: 'Nobody gave me this. I did it myself, with my own work.' Which describes his view best?"
options:
  - "A fair view, since he did the work and the credit is his to take"
  - "Trust without means, since he forgot to thank his family for their help"
  - "His heart rested on his effort, but the job came from Allah"
  - "A risky view, so he should hide his effort to keep his trust pure"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "His work was real and it mattered. But the hadith from chapter 1.2 says people can only benefit him with what Allah has written. The work was the means, not the source."
  - "He did not skip the means; he took them for seven months. Thanking his family is good, but that is not the mistake here."
  - "Right. This is means without trust, seen after a success. His effort was the means through which the job came, and the job came from Allah."
  - "Hiding effort is not what tawakkul asks. He can speak of his work and still know that the result came from Allah."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "It is 23:30 the night before Rasha's online exam. She has studied all week, charged her devices and arranged a backup room with power. She plans to read her notes until 02:00 and make dua. What is best now?"
options:
  - "Read until 02:00 and make dua, so the means and the trust are both there"
  - "Make dua and sleep now, as her means are taken and rest is one of them"
  - "Read until she feels sure, since feeling sure is a sign of real trust"
  - "Plan a second backup room, then read a little more, then go to sleep"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "This sounds balanced, but the means are already taken. Reading until 02:00 is not a means now. It is worry, and it will leave her tired for the exam."
  - "Right. Her part is done. Sleep is the means that is left, and making dua and resting her heart is the trust."
  - "Feeling sure may never come before an exam, and it is not the measure of trust. Tawakkul is taking the means and leaving the rest with Allah."
  - "She already has a backup for the thing most likely to fail. A second one at midnight is planning with no end."
```

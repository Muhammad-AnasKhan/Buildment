```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Ola's family has had to close their small sewing workshop after twenty years. At home that night her father weeps, then says 'Inna lillahi wa inna ilayhi raji'un', and says nothing against Allah. Ola's uncle tells her the tears show weak faith. Which view is closest to the Sunnah?"
options:
  - "The uncle is right, since a strong believer does not shed tears"
  - "The father's tears fit faith, and he guarded what he said"
  - "The tears are fine only if nobody in the family sees them fall"
  - "The tears are fine, but saying the istirja aloud was not needed"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "The Prophet, peace be upon him, wept when his son was dying and called his tears mercy (Sahih al-Bukhari 1303). Tears are not weak faith."
  - "Right. The eye sheds tears and the heart grieves, and the believer says only what pleases Allah (Sahih al-Bukhari 1303). The father did exactly that."
  - "The Prophet's tears were seen by his companions, and he did not hide them. Grief does not need to be secret."
  - "The istirja is what the Quran praises the patient for saying when a calamity strikes (Quran 2:156). It was the right thing to say."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Sawsan learned this morning that her thesis supervisor is leaving the university, so her final defence is delayed by a term. That night, in her dua, she tells Allah how tired and sad she is. Her friend says that complaining in dua spoils sabr. Which teaching answers her friend?"
options:
  - "Yaqub told Allah of his grief and kept beautiful patience"
  - "Sabr jamil means saying nothing about sadness, even in your duas"
  - "Complaint in dua is fine only once the hardship has fully ended"
  - "A patient person keeps dua short and leaves out their feelings"
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. Yaqub, peace be upon him, said he complained of his grief and sorrow only to Allah (Quran 12:86), and his words 'beautiful patience' are in the Quran (Quran 12:18). Complaint to Allah fits sabr; complaint against Allah does not."
  - "Beautiful patience is not silence. The prophet who said it also wept and poured out his grief to Allah."
  - "Yaqub complained to Allah in the middle of his hardship, not after it ended. Dua is for the hard time itself."
  - "Nothing in the sources limits dua to short words without feelings. Telling Allah what is in your heart is part of turning to Him."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Malak lies awake the night before her exam results are published, with a tight feeling of distress in her chest. Her sister suggests she wait until the results come, and say the istirja if they are bad. What does module 3 teach about that night?"
options:
  - "Words are only for after a loss, so she should wait for the result"
  - "She should hide the distress, since feeling it shows she lacks trust"
  - "The istirja is the one dua for every hard moment, so it is enough"
  - "The Prophet had words for distress itself; she can say them now"
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "The Sunnah has words for distress and worry before any result, not only for a loss after it."
  - "The Prophet asked Allah for refuge from worry and grief (Sahih al-Bukhari 6369). Distress is something to bring to Allah, not to hide."
  - "The istirja fits the moment a loss strikes. The Sunnah gives other words for other moments, such as a night of distress."
  - "Right. The Prophet used to say the dua of distress at times of distress (Sahih al-Bukhari 6346). A sleepless, worried night is exactly that moment."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Areej has waited nine months for a decision on her place in a study exchange. She tells a friend: 'It has been so long, Allah must have forgotten my case.' Which reply is most faithful to the sources in this module?"
options:
  - "Nine months is short, as Yusuf waited exactly seven years"
  - "Long waits show Allah's anger, so she should ask what she did wrong"
  - "Yusuf waited several years, and still named Allah's kindness"
  - "She should stop asking about it, since asking shows weak patience"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "The Quran says Yusuf stayed in prison 'several years' (Quran 12:42) and gives no number. Do not state a number as fact, and do not shrink her wait by comparing it."
  - "Hardship is not a sign of Allah's anger; the prophets were tested most of all (Jami' at-Tirmidhi 2398, graded hasan sahih). Never read a delay as a punishment."
  - "Right. Yusuf, peace be upon him, waited several years (Quran 12:42), and at the end said his Lord is subtle in what He wills (Quran 12:100). A long wait is not being forgotten."
  - "Asking about her case is using the means, and asking Allah again and again is part of waiting well."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Waleed, a junior accountant, lost his job when his firm lost its biggest client. For two weeks he has told his wife he is 'being patient'. He has not updated his CV, and he has not replied to his old manager, who offered to pass his name to other firms. What would turn this into the sabr that module 3 teaches?"
options:
  - "Wait quietly a few more weeks, until the sadness has fully gone"
  - "Accept that the loss was written, so more effort is now pointless"
  - "Update his CV and take up his old manager's offer of help"
  - "Keep his worry from his wife, so that she does not feel it too"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "Sabr does not wait for feelings to disappear before acting. Whoever tries to be patient, Allah makes him patient (Sahih al-Bukhari 1469)."
  - "Qadr is a reason to act, not to stop. The companions asked 'then why act?' and were told to act (Sahih al-Bukhari 4949)."
  - "Right. Updating his CV keeps him moving, and accepting help is seeking help, which Allah tells the believers to do with patience (Quran 2:153)."
  - "Hiding need is not sabr. Telling his wife is part of letting the people around him help."
```

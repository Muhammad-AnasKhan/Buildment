```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Jad learns on a call that his remote team is being cut. In the first minute he says the istirja and nothing against Allah. Then he sits quietly for an hour. His cousin says real sabr was to keep working. Which view fits Sahih al-Bukhari 1283?"
options:
  - "His first words were sabr, and a quiet hour after them does not undo it"
  - "His cousin is right that sabr means carrying on with the work that day"
  - "His sabr starts later, once he has had time to think about the news"
  - "His words count, but the hour of sitting still weakened his sabr"
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. The Prophet, peace be upon him, taught that patience is at the first shock (Sahih al-Bukhari 1283). Jad held his tongue in that moment. Sitting quietly afterwards is human, not a failure."
  - "Sabr does not ask a person to act as if nothing happened. The Prophet's own eyes filled with tears at a loss (Sahih al-Bukhari 1303)."
  - "The hadith puts sabr at the first shock, not later. What Jad said in the first minute already counted."
  - "A quiet hour after a shock does not cancel the steadiness he showed. The hadith is about the first moment, not a ban on sadness after it."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "For a month Rima has kept her worry about her father's unpaid medical bills to herself, and now she cannot sleep. Her older brother has twice offered to help. What fits the sabr this module teaches, tonight?"
options:
  - "Carry it alone a little longer, and ask him if it gets any worse"
  - "Make dua about it, and also keep it quiet to spare her brother"
  - "Tell her brother tonight, and accept the help he offered"
  - "Ask for help, but first try the other options she can find"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "Waiting until it gets worse keeps the need hidden. Allah tells believers to seek help through patience and prayer (Quran 2:153)."
  - "Dua is right, but pairing it with silence is still hiding need. Here the missing step is simply to ask."
  - "Right. Her brother has offered twice. Accepting that help is seeking help, which is part of sabr, not a break from it."
  - "Trying her own options is fine, but a month of carrying it alone is already enough. Sabr does not make asking the last resort."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Malak lies awake the night before her exam results are published, with a tight feeling of distress in her chest. Her sister suggests she wait for the results, and say the istirja then if they are bad. What does module 3 teach about this night?"
options:
  - "Wait for the result, and say the istirja then if the news is bad"
  - "Keep the distress to herself, so her family can sleep in peace"
  - "Say the istirja tonight, since it suits any hard moment as well"
  - "Say the dua of distress tonight, the words for times like this"
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "The istirja fits the moment a loss strikes. The Sunnah also gives words for distress before any result comes."
  - "Letting her family sleep is kind, but the distress itself is something to bring to Allah, not to hide."
  - "The istirja is for a loss. For a night of distress, the Sunnah gives other words."
  - "Right. The Prophet used to say the dua of distress at times of distress (Sahih al-Bukhari 6346). A sleepless, worried night is that moment."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Sameer has waited ten months for his old university abroad to send his transcript, which a new employer needs. He emails the office politely every two weeks. A friend tells him: 'Stop emailing. If Allah wants it, it will come.' What does module 3 suggest?"
options:
  - "Stop emailing for now, and let the wait itself be his sabr"
  - "Keep emailing politely, and keep trusting Allah with the result"
  - "Email daily instead, since more pressure shows more effort"
  - "Stop emailing, and read the long delay as Allah closing this door"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "Waiting well is not waiting idly. Yaqub kept sending his sons to search (Quran 12:87)."
  - "Right. His emails are the means, and his trust is with Allah. Waiting well keeps both going."
  - "Daily emails are not more patient, and they may harm the request. Polite, steady follow-up is the means."
  - "A long delay cannot be read as a sign that Allah has closed the door. Allah does not let the reward of those who do good be lost (Quran 12:90)."
```

question: "Waleed lost his accounting job. He tells his wife he is 'being patient', but he has not updated his CV or answered his old manager, who offered to pass his name to other firms. What turns this into the sabr module 3 teaches?"
options:
  - "Give himself a few more quiet weeks before he does anything"
  - "Accept the loss as written, and wait for the next door to open"
  - "Update his CV, and take up his old manager's offer of help"
  - "Keep the worry from his wife for now, to protect her from it"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "Sabr does not wait for the feeling to pass before acting. Whoever tries to be patient, Allah makes him patient (Sahih al-Bukhari 1469)."
  - "Qadr is a reason to act, not to wait. The companions asked 'then why act?' and were told to act (Sahih al-Bukhari 4949)."
  - "Right. Updating his CV keeps him moving, and accepting help is seeking help, which Allah tells believers to do (Quran 2:153)."
  - "Protecting his wife is kind, but hiding need is not sabr. Telling her lets the people around him help."

## Learning log

1. **The first shock:** Think of the first moment of a hard piece of news. What helped you hold steady, or what would have helped? If you prefer, answer about the provided case below.
2. **Sabr or silence:** When you say "I am being patient", do you sometimes mean "I am telling nobody"? What would sabr that keeps moving look like instead? If you prefer, answer about the provided case.

4. **Waiting well:** Which of the four parts of waiting well is hardest for you, and what small step would make it easier? If you prefer, answer about the provided case.

## Your plan for waiting well

This task brings the module together on one wait. Everyone waits for something: a result, a reply, a document, a decision made by someone else. The wait itself is out of your hands. What you do during it is not. Your plan shows that you can tell those two apart, and that you can fill a wait with good work, means, words and people, instead of emptiness or worry alone.

You may write about a wait in your own life, or about the provided case below. Both count exactly the same. You never need to share anything personal in this course, and your mentor will not ask you to.

Here is what a strong answer looks like, told about a fictional person. Ghadeer is a junior designer. A small agency has told her they will decide within six weeks whether to give her a twelve-month contract. The decision is theirs, and its timing is theirs. Ghadeer's plan starts by saying exactly that. Then it lists what she keeps doing: she finishes the two small jobs she already has, and she keeps her weekly hour helping her younger brother with maths. Her means are concrete: she sends the agency one polite follow-up after three weeks, and she keeps her portfolio updated. Her backup is two applications to other studios in week four. Her words are named with their moments: the refuge from worry and grief when her mind runs ahead at night, and "hasbuna Allah wa ni'ma al-wakil" before she sends the follow-up. Finally, she names her mother and her mentor as the people she will tell if it gets heavy. She writes one sentence for the hardest moment: "Yusuf waited several years and still named Allah's kindness; six weeks is not being forgotten."

Look at the shape of Ghadeer's answer. It separates what is hers from what is not. Every part has a concrete action, a time or a name. The words are tied to moments, not listed. And the hardest belief, "I am forgotten", has a prepared answer from the sources.

A weak answer to the same wait would say: "I will be patient and make dua, and inshallah it will work out." Every word of that is sincere. But it names no action, no means, no moment and no person. It would feel right, and it would leave Ghadeer alone with six empty weeks.

**The provided case.** If you prefer not to use your own situation, use this one. Kinan is 23 and finished his accounting degree last term, with good grades. A small firm abroad has offered him a remote bookkeeping job. The offer depends on his graduation certificate, and the firm will hold the place for three months. The university's records office says certificates for his year are delayed and will be issued "in two to four months". Kinan has already emailed the records office twice and received a letter confirming that he completed all his courses. The firm says it needs the certificate itself. Meanwhile, Kinan helps with the accounts at his father's small shop two days a week, and he tutors two cousins in maths. His parents and his older sister, a teacher, live with him. He has a mentor in the fellowship. Since the news, he has stopped tutoring, and he lies awake thinking that four years of study may have been for nothing. Write Kinan's plan for waiting well.

## How your mentor will read it

Your mentor reads your plan with the table below. The rubric grades your reasoning and your plan only, never your faith, your feelings or your personal circumstances.

| Part | Met | Partly | Not yet |
|---|---|---|---|
| The wait | Says what is out of your hands and what is yours | Describes the wait but does not separate the two | No clear wait described |
| Good you keep doing | At least one named good action with a time | A good action, but vague ("be productive") | Nothing kept going |
| Means and backup | At least one means with a date, and a backup | A means but no backup, or no date | No means at all |
| Words for the moments | At least one sourced word or dua, tied to a moment | Words named, but not tied to a moment | No words, or a dua presented as a guarantee |
| People and the hard thought | A named person to tell, and a prepared answer to "I am forgotten" | One of the two | Neither |

**Pass rule:** your plan passes when no part is "Not yet" and at least three parts are "Met".

The mentor then replies with one or two short questions, for example: "What will you do if the backup also takes months?" or "When exactly will you say the dua you chose?" You answer them in writing or in a voice note. That reply is part of the gate, so look out for it after you submit.

If you feel overwhelmed for a long time, or you have thoughts of harming yourself, talk to your mentor or the fellowship coordinator today, and to a doctor or counsellor if you can. This course does not replace professional care.

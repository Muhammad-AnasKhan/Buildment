```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Asil's aunt gave her a used laptop so she can study programming. Asil thanks Allah for it every day. But she spends her evenings on it scrolling, and she has not opened her course in three weeks. Which part of shukr is she missing most?"
options:
  - "Gratitude in the heart, since she has not seen it as a blessing yet"
  - "Gratitude on the tongue, since a daily thanks is still too little"
  - "Gratitude in action, using the laptop for what it was given for"
  - "Gratitude by comparison, looking at people who have no laptop"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "Asil does see the laptop as a blessing; she thanks Allah for it every day. Her heart has noticed it."
  - "She already thanks Allah daily. More words would not change what is missing."
  - "Right. Gratitude also lives in action. The Prophet, peace be upon him, showed it when he asked 'should I not be a grateful servant?' (Sahih al-Bukhari 4836). Using the laptop for her course is shukr in action."
  - "Looking at those below you is a tool for noticing, and Asil has already noticed. What is missing is using the blessing well."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Adnan reads the meaning of Quran 14:7 about gratitude. He decides that if he thanks Allah every day this month, a new client will pay his rent by the end of the month. What does module 4 say about his plan?"
options:
  - "Allah's promise is true, but its shape and timing are Allah's"
  - "His plan is right, since a month of thanks will bring the client"
  - "The promise is about the next life, so he should expect little now"
  - "He should wait to thank Allah until the client comes, to be sincere"
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. Allah's promise is true, and what the 'more' is, and when, is His. Adnan can thank Allah every day and keep looking for clients, without tying his thanks to one result."
  - "No verse or dua comes with a promised worldly result on a date. Treating thanks as a payment for a client misreads the promise."
  - "The verse does not limit the 'more' to the next life. What it will be, and when, is Allah's to choose."
  - "Gratitude does not wait for the next good thing. Adnan already has blessings to thank Allah for today."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Mais is a freelance writer. She sometimes copies paragraphs from other writers' articles into her client work without saying so. When a friend raises it, Mais says: 'Allah is Most Merciful; He will forgive me.' Which describes her state best?"
options:
  - "Healthy hope, since trust in Allah's mercy is praised in the Quran"
  - "Carelessness, since her hope has no fear of her own wrong"
  - "Balance, since she named both Allah's mercy and her own act"
  - "Despair, since she has given up on writing her own work"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "Hope in Allah's mercy is good, but here it is used as a reason to keep doing wrong. Allah's mercy is near to those who do good (Quran 7:56)."
  - "Right. This is the hope wing without the fear wing. Fear, in this course, is care about your own wrong choices, such as presenting other people's words as your own."
  - "Naming her act is not the same as caring about it. Balance would lead her to stop copying and put it right."
  - "Despair is losing hope in Allah's mercy. Mais has plenty of hope; what is missing is fear."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Mahdi tells his cousin: 'Allah does not need my thanks, so thanking Him every day changes nothing.' Which answer fits what module 4 teaches?"
options:
  - "He is right, so his thanks are better given to people who help"
  - "He is wrong, since Allah's help waits on each person's thanks"
  - "He is right, so thanks matter most at the big moments of life"
  - "Allah does not need the thanks, and the thanks still do Mahdi good"
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "Thanking people is part of gratitude, but it does not replace thanking Allah."
  - "Allah is not waiting on anyone's thanks. The benefit of gratitude goes to the grateful person."
  - "Gratitude is for ordinary days as well as big ones. Noticing small blessings is where it starts."
  - "Right. Whoever is grateful is grateful for his own good (Quran 31:12). Mahdi's daily thanks change Mahdi."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Last month Jawad missed a deadline without telling his client. Since then he has apologised, delivered the work and agreed a discount, and the client has accepted. Still, every night he lies awake, sure that Allah will not forgive him. What does his heart need now?"
options:
  - "More hope, since he has put it right and mercy is near"
  - "Some fear and some hope, so he stays careful but not too sad"
  - "More fear, so the lesson of the missed deadline stays with him"
  - "Less thought about Allah, and more focus on his next client"
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. Jawad's fear has already done its work: he put the wrong right with the client. What is missing is hope. Allah tells those who have wronged themselves not to despair of His mercy (Quran 39:53)."
  - "A balance sounds wise, but Jawad is not short of fear. Adding 'some fear' to a heart that is already despairing keeps it on one wing."
  - "His fear is already strong enough to keep him awake. More of it would push him further toward despair."
  - "Turning away from Allah does not mend the missing wing. Thinking well of Allah does (Sahih al-Bukhari 7405)."
```

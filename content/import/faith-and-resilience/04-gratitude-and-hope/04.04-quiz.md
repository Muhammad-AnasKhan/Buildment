```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Asil's aunt gave her a used laptop so she can study programming. Asil thanks Allah for it every day, but she spends her evenings on it scrolling and has not opened her course in three weeks. Which part of shukr is she missing most?"
options:
  - "Gratitude in the heart, since she does not see it as a blessing"
  - "Gratitude on the tongue, since she should thank Allah more often"
  - "Gratitude in action, using the laptop for her study"
  - "Nothing, since daily thanks to Allah is the whole of gratitude"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "Asil does see the laptop as a blessing; she thanks Allah for it every day. Her heart and tongue are already grateful."
  - "She already thanks Allah daily. More words would not change what is missing."
  - "Right. Gratitude also lives in action, as the Prophet, peace be upon him, showed when he said 'should I not be a grateful servant?' (Sahih al-Bukhari 4836). Using the laptop for her course is shukr in action."
  - "Gratitude has three places: the heart, the tongue and action. Daily thanks covers two of them, not the third."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Adnan reads the meaning of Quran 14:7: if you are grateful, Allah will give you more. He decides that if he thanks Allah every day this month, a new client will surely pay his rent by the end of the month. What does module 4 say about his plan?"
options:
  - "The verse is a promise, but what the 'more' is and when is Allah's"
  - "He is right, as a month of thanks guarantees a client by month end"
  - "The verse is only about the next life, so he should expect nothing now"
  - "He should thank Allah only after a client comes, to be sincere"
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. Allah's promise is true, and its shape and timing are His. Adnan can thank Allah every day and keep looking for clients, without tying his gratitude to one result."
  - "No verse or dua comes with a promised worldly result on a date. Treating thanks as a payment for a client misreads the promise."
  - "The verse does not limit the 'more' to the next life. What it will be, and when, is Allah's to choose."
  - "Gratitude does not wait for the next good thing. Adnan already has blessings to thank Allah for today."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Mais is a freelance writer. She sometimes copies paragraphs from other writers' articles into her client work without saying so. When a friend raises it, Mais says: 'Allah is Most Merciful; He will forgive me.' Which describes her state best?"
options:
  - "Healthy hope, since trusting Allah's mercy is always praiseworthy"
  - "Despair, because she has stopped expecting anything good"
  - "Balanced, as she mentioned both Allah's mercy and her own act"
  - "Hope with no fear: carelessness about her own wrong choice"
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "Hope in Allah's mercy is good, but here it is used as a reason to keep doing wrong. Allah's mercy is near to those who do good (Quran 7:56)."
  - "Despair is losing hope in Allah's mercy. Mais has plenty of hope; what is missing is fear."
  - "Mentioning her act is not the same as caring about it. Balance would lead her to stop copying and put it right."
  - "Right. This is the hope wing without the fear wing. Fear, in this course, is the awareness that keeps you careful about your own wrong choices, such as presenting other people's words as your own."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Ayman drifted far from his prayers during a hard year, and now he feels ashamed. He tells his cousin: 'I have gone too far. Allah will not accept me back, so there is no point starting again.' Which response follows this module?"
options:
  - "Agree that some mistakes are too big, and give him time alone"
  - "Remind him of Quran 39:53, and help him take one step back"
  - "Tell him the hard year was a punishment for what he neglected"
  - "Tell him to fear Allah more, since his heart is not afraid enough"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "No mistake is bigger than Allah's mercy for someone who turns back. Allah says not to despair of His mercy (Quran 39:53)."
  - "Right. Ayman has fear and has lost hope. Allah tells those who have wronged themselves not to despair of His mercy (Quran 39:53), and He comes near to those who come near to Him (Sahih al-Bukhari 7405)."
  - "Hardship is not a sign of Allah's anger, and fear in this course is never used to read events. This would push Ayman further into despair."
  - "Ayman already has fear; that is why he is ashamed. What his heart is missing is hope, so more fear breaks the balance further."
```

type: mcq
question: "This month Hadeel started her first paid remote internship. In the same month her father fell ill and had to stop working at his shop. She feels guilty for being happy about the internship. Which teaching fits her month?"
options:
  - "She should hide her happiness until her father is fully well again"
  - "She should focus only on gratitude, and stop feeling worried"
  - "She should focus only on patience, since the hard news is bigger"
  - "Gratitude for the good and patience with the hard, together"
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "Hiding her happiness is not needed. Gratitude for a good thing is not disloyal to someone who is ill."
  - "Gratitude does not cancel worry about her father. Both can be true, and her worry is something to bring to Allah."
  - "Patience with her father's illness does not cancel gratitude for the internship. The believer can do both."
  - "Right. The Prophet said the believer is grateful when good comes and patient when harm comes, and both are good for him (Sahih Muslim 2999). One month can hold both."

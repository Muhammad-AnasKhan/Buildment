```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Hazem, a junior accountant who lives with his parents, has two allowed job offers and must answer both by Thursday. Both employers have answered every question he asked. On Wednesday night he is still reading about the two companies. He wants another week, because he still cannot tell which job will turn out better. What fits this module?"
options:
  - "Ask both employers for another month, so that less is left unknown"
  - "Decide by Thursday with his facts; how it ends is with Allah"
  - "Keep reading, since a careful decision needs more certainty first"
  - "Let the deadline pass, so that the choice is no longer his to carry"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "He already has the facts he needed. More time will not show him how each job turns out; only Allah knows what tomorrow brings (Quran 31:34)."
  - "Right. He has done his part: the facts are known. Being keen on what benefits you includes not giving up and not freezing (Sahih Muslim 2664). He decides by the deadline and trusts Allah with the outcome."
  - "A good decision is made with the means you have, not with certainty about the outcome. Reading more will not give him that certainty."
  - "Letting the deadline pass is still a decision, made by the deadline instead of by him. Consult, decide, then trust."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Arwa teaches children at a learning centre in her town. She is deciding whether to apply for a one-year study programme abroad. She sends her mentor one line: 'What should I do?' Her mentor knows the programme well, but knows little about Arwa's situation. What would make this consultation more useful?"
options:
  - "Ask a few more people the same question, and follow the majority"
  - "Leave it, since a mentor's answer will be general for anyone"
  - "Ask the mentor to decide for her, since he knows the programme"
  - "Share her facts, and ask what the programme is like day to day"
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "More people asked the same vague question gives more opinions, not more knowledge. A vote does not replace a clear question."
  - "A mentor's answer is general only when the question is. With her facts and a clear question, his knowledge of the programme becomes useful to her."
  - "He knows the programme, but the decision belongs to the one who will live it. Consult, decide, then trust (Quran 3:159)."
  - "Right. A clear question names the part the adviser knows, and sharing her facts lets him answer her real situation. Then she weighs his answer and decides."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Taghreed works as a designer at a small print shop. She must choose between two allowed short courses. She thinks istikhara is kept for very big decisions, such as moving to another country, so she does not pray it for this choice. What fits the Sunnah?"
options:
  - "She is right, since istikhara is kept for life-changing choices"
  - "She should ask a scholar first whether the courses are allowed"
  - "She can pray it here too; it was taught for all kinds of matters"
  - "She should wait for a bigger decision and pray istikhara then"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "The Prophet, peace be upon him, taught istikhara for all their matters, the way he taught a surah of the Quran (Sahih al-Bukhari 1166). It is not kept for big choices only."
  - "Checking is right when she is unsure. Two ordinary short courses are plainly allowed, so that step is already done."
  - "Right. The hadith says the Prophet taught istikhara for all their matters (Sahih al-Bukhari 1166). A choice between two courses is one of them."
  - "There is no need to wait. Istikhara is for the choice in front of her, whatever its size."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Hanan chose a remote contract after gathering facts, asking people and praying istikhara. After four months the company closed and the contract ended. Her cousin tells her, 'You chose wrong. Your istikhara must have been turned down.' What fits the teaching?"
options:
  - "She chose with care; a hard result does not prove she chose wrong"
  - "Her cousin is right, so next time she should choose the safer job"
  - "She should repeat istikhara now to learn which offer was right"
  - "She should stop praying istikhara, since it did not keep her job"
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. She did her part well. The dua asked Allah for the good wherever it is, and for contentment with it. Hardship cannot be read as proof that she chose wrong or that Allah is angry with her. Now she uses her backup and takes the next step."
  - "Nobody could know the company would close; only Allah knows what tomorrow brings (Quran 31:34). A careful decision is judged by how it was made, not by an outcome no one could see."
  - "Istikhara is for a choice that is in front of you. This choice is already made and lived; her next step is what to do now."
  - "Istikhara asks Allah to choose the good and to make you content with it. It is not a promise that no hard days will come."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Maysa decided on Sunday to accept a job at a small design studio near her home. She had gathered the facts and consulted her parents and a mentor. Since then she has asked five more people. No new facts have come, but each new opinion makes her want to change her mind. What fits the Quran's order for decisions?"
options:
  - "Keep asking people until most of those she knows agree with her choice"
  - "Change her decision each time a new person disagrees with it"
  - "Ask two more people and pray istikhara again, and then act on it"
  - "Act on her decision and trust Allah, unless a real new fact appears"
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "Asking until most people agree has no clear end, and it looks for comfort rather than knowledge. Consult, decide, then trust."
  - "Opinions without new facts are not a reason to reverse a careful decision. She would never be able to act."
  - "This sounds balanced, but she has already consulted and prayed. More rounds of the same steps only delay her. Now the right step is simply to act."
  - "Right. The Quran's order is to consult, decide, and then put your trust in Allah (Quran 3:159). A real new fact could change things; a new opinion alone does not."
```

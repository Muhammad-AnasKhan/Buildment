```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Hazem, a junior accountant who lives with his parents, has two allowed job offers and must answer both by Thursday. Both employers have already answered every question he asked. On Wednesday night he is still reading about the two companies and wants another week, because he still cannot tell which job will turn out better. What fits this module?"
options:
  - "Ask both employers for another month, so nothing is left unknown"
  - "Keep reading, since a good decision needs certainty about the end"
  - "Decide by Thursday with his facts; nobody can know how it ends"
  - "Let the deadline pass, so that the choice is not on his shoulders"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "He already has the facts he needed. No amount of time will show him how each job turns out; only Allah knows what tomorrow brings (Quran 31:34)."
  - "A good decision is made with the means you have, not with certainty about the outcome. Reading more will not give him that certainty."
  - "Right. He has done his part: the facts are known. Being keen on what benefits you includes not giving up and not freezing (Sahih Muslim 2664). He can decide by the deadline and trust Allah with the outcome."
  - "Letting the deadline pass is still a decision, made by the deadline passing instead of by him. Consult, decide, then trust."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Arwa teaches children at a learning centre in her town. She is deciding whether to apply for a one-year study programme abroad. Her biggest unknown is what the programme is really like, day to day. Who is the best person to ask about that?"
options:
  - "A former student of the programme, about its classes and daily life"
  - "Her whole family group chat, asking them to vote on whether to go"
  - "Nobody, since asking people would show she does not trust Allah"
  - "Her youngest cousin, who loves her and will back any choice she makes"
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. The question is about the programme, so the best person to ask is someone who has been inside it. She can ask her family about the family's needs, each person about what they know."
  - "A vote counts opinions, and most of her family have not seen the programme. It adds voices, not knowledge."
  - "The Quran praises believers who run their affairs by consultation (Quran 42:38). Asking people who know is part of taking the means."
  - "Care matters, but her cousin does not know the programme. A good adviser has knowledge of the question, as well as care and honesty."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Zuhair, a freelance developer who rents a desk at a shared office, must choose between two allowed contracts. He says, 'I will pray istikhara tonight, so I do not need to read the contracts or ask anyone.' What fits the teaching?"
options:
  - "He is right, because istikhara replaces reading and asking advice"
  - "He should wait for a dream before he reads either of the contracts"
  - "He should read and ask only if no clear sign comes after istikhara"
  - "He reads the contracts and asks people, and prays istikhara too"
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "Every source this course uses agrees that a person studies the matter and consults people as well as praying istikhara. It does not replace them."
  - "No dream or sign is required for istikhara. Reading the contracts is his part, whatever he dreams."
  - "Reading and asking are not a backup for when no sign comes. They are part of deciding well from the start, and no sign is required."
  - "Right. Istikhara is for allowed choices, and it goes with taking the means of knowing and with shura, not instead of them."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Taghreed chose a remote contract after gathering facts, asking people and praying istikhara. After four months the company closed and the contract ended. At her desk at home, she keeps saying, 'If only I had taken the other offer.' What fits the teaching?"
options:
  - "Her istikhara failed, so she should not rely on it next time"
  - "Say 'Allah decreed, and He did what He willed', then take her next step"
  - "Keep going over the other offer in her mind, so she learns from her mistake"
  - "Accept that careful decisions are pointless, since nobody sees the end"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "A hard outcome does not mean the istikhara failed. The dua asks Allah to decree the good wherever it is and to make her content with it. Hardship is not a sign of Allah's anger."
  - "Right. The Prophet, peace be upon him, taught that when something befalls you, you do not say 'if only', but 'Allah decreed, and He did what He willed' (Sahih Muslim 2664). Then she uses her backup and takes the next step."
  - "Learning is good, but going over the other offer again and again is the 'if only' the hadith warns about. Her decision was made with care."
  - "A careful decision is still her part, even when the end is hard. What she controls is how she decides, not how it turns out."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Maysa decided on Sunday to accept a job at a small design studio near her home, after gathering the facts and consulting her parents and a mentor. Since then she has asked five more people. No new facts have come, but each new opinion makes her want to change her mind. What fits the Quran's order for decisions?"
options:
  - "Keep asking people until everyone she knows agrees with her choice"
  - "Change her decision each time a new person disagrees with it"
  - "Act on her decision and trust Allah, unless real new facts appear"
  - "Pray istikhara again each time she hears a new opinion from someone"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "Asking until everyone agrees never ends, and it looks for comfort rather than knowledge. Consult, decide, then trust."
  - "Opinions without new facts are not a reason to reverse a careful decision. She would never be able to act."
  - "Right. The Quran's order is to consult, decide, and then put your trust in Allah (Quran 3:159). A real new fact could change things; a new opinion alone does not."
  - "She may repeat istikhara if she wishes, but using it for every new opinion keeps her from ever acting. Her decision is made; now she trusts Allah."
```

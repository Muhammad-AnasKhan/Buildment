## Module 5 checklist

- [ ] I check that each option is allowed before I weigh it, and I ask a scholar I trust when I am unsure
- [ ] I separate facts from fears, find the facts I am missing, and can say what matters most to me in one sentence
- [ ] I consult people about what each of them knows best, listen to learn, and still make the decision myself
- [ ] I can say what istikhara is and what it is not: for allowed choices, with research and advice, and with no dream needed
- [ ] I answered the questions in chapters 5.2 to 5.4, and finished the quiz in 5.5
- [ ] I submitted my decision record below

## Learning log

1. **Facts and fears:** In a decision you made before, which important fact did you find out only after you had decided? If you prefer, answer about the provided case: which fact does Kholoud most need before she pays?
2. **Your advisers:** Who do you usually ask first when you must decide? Do they know the matter, care about you, or both? If you prefer, answer about the provided case: who could Kholoud ask, and about what?
3. **Istikhara:** What had you heard about istikhara before this module that you now see differently? If you prefer, answer about the provided case: what might Kholoud's friends tell her about istikhara, and what would you say?
4. **After deciding:** When a careful decision turns out hard, what do you want to say to yourself? If you prefer, answer about the provided case: what should Kholoud say if her course turns out less useful than she hoped?

## Your decision record

This task brings the whole module together on one choice. A decision record is a short, honest note of how you decided. It names the options and whether each is allowed, the facts, and what matters most. It says who you consulted and what each person added, where istikhara fits, the decision and the first step. It ends with what you leave with Allah. It is not a test of whether you picked the "right" option. Nobody can know that in advance. It shows whether you did your part well.

Choose a real choice in front of you, or one you made recently, between two or three allowed options. It could be a job, a client, a course, how to spend some savings, or where to study. If you prefer not to use your own life, use the provided case below. Both count the same.

The help in this gate is lighter than in earlier gates, on purpose. You have now built several plans with long worked examples beside you. This time there is one short example, and the rest of the thinking is yours.

Here is the example. Hussam, a junior designer, had two clients wanting him for the same month. He wrote the facts, found one missing fact with a single message, and named what mattered most in one sentence. He asked a designer friend about the work and his father about the family's needs. Then he prayed istikhara and chose. He wrote a first step with a date, and a backup in case the client paid late.

If you prefer not to use your own situation, use this provided case. Kholoud is 24 and works as a freelance web developer from her family's home. Most of her clients are small shops that want simple websites. She has saved 600 shekels to spend on one short course that could bring her better clients. Option A is an eight-week online course in mobile app development. It costs 550 shekels, has live classes on Tuesday and Thursday evenings at 19:00, and gives a certificate at the end. Option B is a twelve-week course in back-end development that she can study at her own pace. It costs 400 shekels, has a mentor who reviews a project every two weeks, and gives no certificate. Two of her shop clients asked her this year whether she can build apps. Power at home comes for about eight hours a day, at different times, so she cannot be sure of power at 19:00. She does not know whether course A records its live classes. She also does not know whether course B's reviews are done by real people. Her friend Duha took course A last year and liked it. Her father, who paid for half of her laptop, wants her to choose "something with a certificate". Course A starts in ten days; course B can start any time.

There is also an optional, harder part for anyone who wants more stretch. In real decisions, two people you trust sometimes give advice that cannot both be followed. In Kholoud's case, imagine her mentor says the back-end course fits the clients she has, while her father still wants the certificate. If you take this part, add a short paragraph. How would you weigh the two pieces of advice? What would you say to the person whose advice you do not follow, and why? This part is not graded on its own, but your mentor may ask about it in the follow-up.

## How your mentor will read it

Your mentor will read your decision record with the table below. Two careful people can make different decisions from the same facts, and both can pass. The rubric grades your reasoning and your plan only, never your faith, your feelings or your personal circumstances.

| Part | Met | Partly | Not yet |
|---|---|---|---|
| Options and permission | Every option named and checked as allowed, with a scholar asked if unsure | Options named, but not checked | No clear options |
| Facts and priorities | Key facts, one missing fact found or planned, and what matters most in one sentence | Facts listed, but no priority named | Only feelings or guesses |
| Consultation | Two or more people, each asked about what they know, with what each added | People named, but no question or answer | Nobody asked, or only a vote |
| Istikhara and decision | Istikhara alongside the facts and advice, no dream expected, a decision with a reason and a first step | A decision, but no reason or no first step | No decision |
| Trust and backup | What you leave with Allah, a backup, and no "if only" if it goes badly | Trust named, but no backup | Neither |

**Pass rule:** your record passes when no part is "Not yet" and at least three parts are "Met".

The mentor then replies with one or two short questions. For example: "What would change your decision?" or "Who else knows this part of the choice?" You answer them in writing or in a voice note. That reply is part of the gate, so look out for it after you submit.

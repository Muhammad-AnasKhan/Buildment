## Your decision record

This task brings the whole module together on one choice. A decision record is a short, honest note of how you decided: the options, whether each is allowed, the facts, what matters most, who you consulted and what each person added, where istikhara fits, the decision, the first step, and what you leave with Allah. It is not a test of whether you picked the "right" option. Nobody can know that in advance. It shows whether you did your part well.

Choose a real choice in front of you, or one you made recently, between two or three allowed options. It could be a job, a client, a course, how to spend some savings, or where to study. If you prefer not to use your own life, use the provided case below. Both count the same.

Here is what a strong answer looks like, told about a fictional person. Hussam is a junior designer choosing between two clients who want him for the same month. Both projects are ordinary, allowed work. He writes the facts: client A pays $600 and needs the work in three weeks; client B pays $450, but the work is in his favourite area, packaging, and the client has said more work may follow. He marks one missing fact, whether "more work may follow" is real, and asks client B directly. They say they have two more products planned this year. He writes his priority in one sentence: "Building a portfolio in packaging matters most this year, and I must earn at least $400 this month." He asks a designer friend who works in packaging what such clients are like, and his father whether the family can manage with $450 this month. He prays istikhara after that. He decides on client B, writes to accept the same day, and politely tells client A. His backup is to take a small logo job in the second half of the month if client B's payment is late. He ends with the outcome he leaves with Allah: whether client B really sends more work.

Notice the shape of Hussam's record. Every option is checked. Facts and fears are separate. The missing fact is found with one message. Each person is asked about what they know. Istikhara comes after the means. The decision has a reason, a first step and a backup, and there is no "if only" waiting at the end.

Compare it with a weak record for the same choice: "I prayed istikhara and felt better about B, so I took it." It may even be the same decision. But it has no facts, no priorities, no people, no first step and no backup. If client B's payment is late, Hussam would have nothing to fall back on and nothing to learn from.

If you prefer not to use your own situation, use this provided case. Kholoud is 24 and works as a freelance web developer from her family's home. Most of her clients are small shops that want simple websites. She has saved 600 shekels to spend on one short course that could bring her better clients. Option A is an eight-week online course in mobile app development. It costs 550 shekels, has live classes on Tuesday and Thursday evenings at 19:00, and gives a certificate at the end. Option B is a twelve-week course in back-end development that she can study at her own pace. It costs 400 shekels, has a mentor who reviews a project every two weeks, and gives no certificate. Two of her shop clients asked her this year whether she can build apps. Power at home comes for about eight hours a day, at different times, so she cannot be sure of power at 19:00. She does not know whether course A records its live classes, or whether course B's reviews are done by real people. Her friend Duha took course A last year and liked it. Her father, who paid for half of her laptop, wants her to choose "something with a certificate". Course A starts in ten days; course B can start any time.

## How your mentor will read it

Your mentor will read your decision record with the table below. Two careful people can make different decisions from the same facts, and both can pass. The rubric grades your reasoning and your plan only, never your faith, your feelings or your personal circumstances.

| Part | Met | Partly | Not yet |
|---|---|---|---|
| Options and permission | Every option named and checked as allowed, with a scholar asked if unsure | Options named, but not checked | No clear options |
| Facts and priorities | Key facts, one missing fact found or planned, and what matters most in one sentence | Facts listed, but no priority named | Only feelings or guesses |
| Consultation | Two or more people, each asked about what they know, with what each added | People named, but no question or answer | Nobody asked, or only a vote |
| Istikhara and decision | Istikhara alongside the facts and advice, no dream expected, a decision with a reason and a first step | A decision, but no reason or no first step | No decision |
| Trust and backup | What you leave with Allah, a backup, and no "if only" if it goes badly | Trust named, but no backup | Neither |

**Pass rule:** your record passes when no part is "Not yet" and at least three parts are "Met".

The mentor then replies with one or two short questions. For example: "What would change your decision?" or "Who else knows this part of the choice?" You answer them in writing or in a voice note. That reply is part of the gate, so look out for it after you submit.

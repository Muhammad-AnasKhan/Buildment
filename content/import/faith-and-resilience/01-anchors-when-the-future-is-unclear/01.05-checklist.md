## Sort one situation

This task brings the whole module together in one situation. You will name what only Allah knows, find the part that is yours, choose today's step, and write a short dua. The point is not to make the uncertainty go away. Nobody can do that. The point is to show that you can stand inside an uncertain situation and still know what to do next.

You can use a situation from your own life, as long as it is one you are comfortable writing about. You never have to share anything personal. If you prefer, use the provided case of Ziad below. Both choices are equal, and your mentor reads them the same way.

Here is what a strong answer looks like, told about a fictional person. Hiba's family runs a small sweets shop. The lease ends next month, and the owner of the building will decide whether to renew it. Hiba helps her father with the accounts. She has been lying awake, imagining the shop closed. In her answer, she first names what only Allah knows: whether the owner will renew, and whether a new place would be found if he does not. Then, next to each one, she names her part. She can help her father prepare a short, polite letter asking to renew, with the record of rent paid on time for four years. She can make a list of two other places nearby, just in case. She can keep the accounts in order this month, so that the shop is ready either way.

Hiba's step for today is small and has a time. She writes: "This evening at 20:00, sit with my father for thirty minutes. Write the first draft of the letter." Her source is Sahih Muslim 2664: be keen on what benefits you, seek Allah's help, and do not give up. She explains that the letter and the list are the "be keen" part, and that she will leave the owner's decision with Allah. Her dua is short and in her own words. Notice the shape of her answer. Every unseen question has a part next to it. The step is one thing, not five. And the source is linked to a real part of the plan, not added at the end as decoration.

Compare it with a weaker answer to the same situation. "Everything is in Allah's hands, so I will just wait and make dua." That answer names the unseen correctly and stops there. It has no part of her own, no step and no source linked to a plan. It sounds faithful, but it leaves out half of what the module taught.

If you prefer not to use your own situation, use this provided case. Ziad is 25. He studied computer engineering and graduated two years ago. He works four days a week at a phone repair shop and takes small freelance tasks in the evenings. For a year he has wanted to apply for a master's degree in data science at a university that offers an online option. Six weeks ago, the university paused applications for this year. Its page says only that a new opening date will be announced in the coming months. Ziad has his transcript ready. He still needs two reference letters and an English language certificate. The test costs money and needs an appointment at a test centre, and he has saved $150 towards it. Every morning he checks the university page before work. His father thinks he should forget the degree and focus on the shop. His friend says he should simply wait. Lately Ziad has started to think: maybe this door is closed to me, and there is no point preparing anything.

## How your mentor will read it

A mentor reads your answer with the table below. The rubric grades only your reasoning and your plan, never your faith, your feelings or your circumstances. Your dua is for you; your mentor will not grade it.

| Part | Met | Partly | Not yet |
|---|---|---|---|
| What only Allah knows | Two or more unseen things, named clearly | One unseen thing, or unseen and own parts mixed | Nothing named, or everything called unseen |
| What is yours to do | Three or more parts, each next to an unseen thing | One or two parts, or vague ones such as "try harder" | No part of your own |
| Today's step | One small step with a time, for today or tomorrow | A step that is too big or has no time | No step |
| Source | A module 1 source, linked to one part of the plan | A source named but not linked to the plan | No source |

**Pass rule:** your answer passes when no part is "Not yet" and at least three parts are "Met".

The mentor then replies with one or two short questions. For example, your mentor may ask which part of your plan sits next to the second unseen thing. Or your mentor may ask what you will do if today's step does not happen. You answer them in writing or in a voice note. That reply is part of the gate, so look out for it after you submit.

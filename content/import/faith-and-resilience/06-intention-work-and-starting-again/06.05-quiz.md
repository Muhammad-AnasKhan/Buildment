```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Ashraf drives deliveries for a pharmacy in his town and gives most of his wages to his mother for rent and food. He says this money is 'just bills', and that only what he gives to charity counts as a good deed. What fits this module?"
options:
  - "He is right, because only charity to other people counts as a deed"
  - "It counts only if he keeps nothing at all of his wages for himself"
  - "Spending on family, seeking Allah's pleasure, is itself rewarded"
  - "It counts only when he also earns enough to give to charity as well"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "The Prophet, peace be upon him, taught that whatever you spend seeking Allah's pleasure is rewarded, even the food you put in your wife's mouth (Sahih al-Bukhari 56). Spending on family counts."
  - "Nothing in the hadith asks him to keep nothing for himself. Providing for his family with a good intention is the good deed."
  - "Right. With a good intention, what he spends on his mother and the household is a good deed (Sahih al-Bukhari 56), and actions are by intentions (Sahih al-Bukhari 1)."
  - "His spending on his family does not wait for charity to become a good deed. It is one already, with a good intention."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Mona, a second-year student who studies in a shared reading room, signs up for five online courses at once over the summer, each needing an hour a day, on top of a part-time job. After one week she is exhausted and behind in all five. What fits the Sunnah's teaching?"
options:
  - "Keep one or two she can manage, and do them well, a little each day"
  - "Keep all five and sleep less each night until she catches up again"
  - "Drop all five, since she clearly cannot learn anything new right now"
  - "Wait until next summer, and then try all five courses together again"
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. Take on only the deeds you can manage (Sahih Muslim 782), and the most beloved deeds are the consistent ones, even if small (Sahih al-Bukhari 6464). One or two courses, kept up, will teach her more."
  - "No one overburdens himself without being overcome (Sahih al-Bukhari 39). Less sleep would make the overload worse, not better."
  - "One week of overload is a setback, not proof that she cannot learn. The lesson is to take on less, not nothing."
  - "Trying all five again next summer repeats the same cause. A believer is not stung from the same hole twice (Sahih al-Bukhari 6133)."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Osaid, a freelance logo designer who works from a desk in his uncle's shop, promised a client a logo by Tuesday. On Monday he saw he would be two days late, but he said nothing. On Wednesday the client wrote to ask where it was. What should he do now?"
options:
  - "Say nothing more, since the delay was decreed for him in any case"
  - "Blame the power cuts, so that the client does not feel let down"
  - "Give up freelancing, since he clearly cannot keep his promises"
  - "Tell the client honestly, apologise, give a date, and learn"
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "Qadr does not cancel his part. His silence was a wrong to the client, and a wrong is something to put right with the person."
  - "An excuse turns an apology into a defence. He should name his own part plainly: he knew and did not say."
  - "One late logo is a setback, not proof that he is unfit for the work. He can learn and start again."
  - "Right. The delay is a setback, but the silence was a wrong. He puts it right with the client, asks Allah's forgiveness for the silence, and changes how he works so it does not happen twice (Sahih al-Bukhari 6133)."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Sufyan, who studies with a group in his university library, copied part of a classmate's assignment and handed it in as his own. Now he regrets it deeply, and he feels his mistake is too big for Allah to forgive. What fits the teaching?"
options:
  - "His mistake is too big to be forgiven, so he should simply accept it"
  - "Ask forgiveness, not repeat it, and put right what he can"
  - "Since Allah forgives everything, he does not need to do anything else"
  - "Hide it and never think about it again, so that the feeling goes away"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "Allah tells His servants who have wronged themselves not to despair of His mercy (Quran 39:53). No mistake is too big to turn back from."
  - "Right. Those who do wrong remember Allah, ask forgiveness and do not knowingly persist (Quran 3:135). Because the wrong touched other people, he also puts right what he can, for example by doing that work again honestly. If he is unsure what putting it right requires, he can ask a scholar he trusts."
  - "Forgiveness is open to those who turn back, but turning back includes not persisting (Quran 3:135), and a wrong to people is put right with them too."
  - "Hiding it leaves the wrong in place and the regret with nowhere to go. Turning back to Allah and putting right what he can is the way forward."
```

question: "Duaa, a junior developer who studies at a community centre near her home, is learning to build mobile apps. She is thinking of stopping, because she does not know whether any app work will come to her in the next year. What fits the teaching?"
options:
  - "Stop for now, and start again once she is sure work will come"
  - "Learn only on days when she has a whole day free, so it counts"
  - "Keep learning a little each day, and leave the outcome with Allah"
  - "Study through the night for a month, so she is ready if work comes"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "Certainty about the future may never come. If the Hour comes with a seedling in your hand, plant it (Al-Adab al-Mufrad 479, graded sahih): effort does not wait for certainty."
  - "Whole free days are rare, and one bad day takes them. The deeds most beloved to Allah are the consistent ones, even if small (Sahih al-Bukhari 6464)."
  - "Right. She keeps planting, a little and often, and leaves the harvest with Allah. That is the seedling hadith (Al-Adab al-Mufrad 479, graded sahih) and consistency (Sahih al-Bukhari 6464) together."
  - "No one overburdens himself without being overcome (Sahih al-Bukhari 39). A month of nights would likely end in exhaustion, not readiness."

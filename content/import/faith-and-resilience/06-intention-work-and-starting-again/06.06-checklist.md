## Your fresh start plan

This task brings the module together on one setback. A fresh start plan is a short, honest note that turns a failure into a next step. It says what happened in plain words, keeps any wrong apart from the setback, puts right what can be put right, names what you will change so it does not happen twice, sets your intention and a small daily habit, and says what you will do when "if only" comes back. It is not a confession, and nobody will judge the failure itself. It shows how you will start again.

Choose a setback from your work or study in the past year: a missed deadline, a failed exam, a lost client, a project that did not work. It does not have to be large. If you prefer not to use your own life, use the provided case below. Both count the same.

Here is what a strong answer looks like, told about a fictional person. Hamdi is a second-year student who failed his statistics module. He writes it plainly: he studied only in the last week, missed two practice sessions because of power cuts, and did not ask for help when he was lost. He sees that there was no wrong to anyone in it: it is a setback, not a sin, and so there is nothing to repent for and no one to apologise to. He finds two causes, studying in bursts and not asking for help, and writes two changes: forty minutes of statistics every morning except Friday, and a question to his tutor every week. His intention line is "I study to understand, to serve people with what I learn, and to please Allah." When "if only I had started earlier" comes back, he will say "Allah decreed, and He did what He willed", read his plan, and do that day's forty minutes. His first step is to email his tutor on Sunday about the retake.

Look at the shape of Hamdi's plan. It is honest without being cruel to himself. It does not invent a sin where there is none. Each change removes a cause he found. The habit is small, has a time, and can survive a bad day. And the plan has a first step with a day.

Compare it with a weak plan for the same failure: "I failed because I am bad at maths. If only I had studied more. I will try harder next time." It blames who he is instead of what he did, it stays in "if only", and "try harder" changes nothing he can see on Monday morning.

If you prefer not to use your own situation, use this provided case. Shatha is 23, a final-year graphic design student who also takes small freelance jobs. Abu Saleh, a family friend, runs a bakery near her home. He asked her to design a new menu board and a printed menu for 250 shekels, paid 100 in advance, and needed them by the 15th of the month. That same week Shatha had two exams. She worked in long bursts late at night, fell behind, and did not tell him. She delivered on the 29th, two weeks late. In the rush, two prices were wrong: the large loaf showed 3 shekels instead of 5, and the sesame cake 8 instead of 6. The bakery printed 200 menus before anyone noticed, and reprinting them cost 180 shekels. Abu Saleh was polite, but he said he would ask someone else next time, and he has not yet paid the other 150 shekels. Shatha feels ashamed. She avoids walking past the bakery, and she is thinking of stopping all freelance work until she graduates. She has one small job left, a flyer for a tutoring centre due in three weeks, and her final project is due in two months.

If a setback is weighing on you for a long time, you do not have to carry it alone. If you feel overwhelmed for a long time, or you have thoughts of harming yourself, talk to your mentor or the fellowship coordinator today, and to a doctor or counsellor if you can. This course does not replace professional care.

## How your mentor will read it

Your mentor will read your plan with the table below. Different people will make different plans for the same setback, and that is fine. The rubric grades your reasoning and your plan only, never your faith, your feelings or your personal circumstances, and never the size of the failure.

| Part | Met | Partly | Not yet |
|---|---|---|---|
| Honest account | What happened in plain words, with any wrong named apart from the setback | Events told, but with excuses, or setback and wrong mixed up | No account, or only blame on others |
| Putting right | A concrete step with the person affected, or a clear reason none is needed | A vague apology "if needed" | Nothing, where someone was wronged |
| Lesson and change | One to three specific changes, each removing a cause | A general lesson such as "be more careful" | No lesson |
| Intention and daily habit | A one-line intention, and a small habit with a time and a size | Only one of the two | Neither |
| Leaving "if only" | What you will say and do when regret returns, and a first step with a date | Regret named, but no plan for it | Stuck in "if only", or giving up |

**Pass rule:** your plan passes when no part is "Not yet" and at least three parts are "Met".

The mentor then replies with one or two short questions. For example: "Which of your changes removes the biggest cause?" or "What will you do on the first day your habit is missed?" You answer them in writing or in a voice note. That reply is part of the gate, so look out for it after you submit.

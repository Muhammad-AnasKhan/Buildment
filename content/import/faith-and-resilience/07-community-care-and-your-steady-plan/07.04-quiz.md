```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Nawal, a copywriter, lost her biggest client, half her income. Since then she spends evenings looking at friends' posts about new jobs and has stopped sending proposals. She says: 'Allah has given good things to them and given up on me.' What fits first?"
options:
  - "Count her blessings each evening until the sad feeling has passed"
  - "Go over what went wrong with the client until she feels sure of it"
  - "Hold on to hope in Allah's mercy, and send a new proposal tomorrow"
  - "Accept the loss as decreed, and wait for a new client to find her"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "Gratitude is a strong practice, but her words are about despair of Allah's mercy, and she has stopped acting. Counting blessings does not answer either of those first."
  - "Learning from a mistake is good (Sahih al-Bukhari 6133), but going over it again and again does not bring a new client. Her bigger problem is that she has given up hope and stopped sending proposals."
  - "Right. Allah tells His servants not to despair of His mercy (Quran 39:53), and Yaqub, peace be upon him, told his sons not to despair of relief from Allah (Quran 12:87). The Prophet, peace be upon him, also told the believer not to give up (Sahih Muslim 2664). Hope comes with a step: one proposal tomorrow."
  - "The loss was decreed, but belief in qadr is a reason to act, not to wait. When the companions asked whether to rely on what was written, the Prophet said: act (Sahih al-Bukhari 4949)."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Haitham, a computer science student, has felt low and tired for six weeks, since his father lost his job. His final exams start next month. He has told nobody, and he studies late at night when the house is quiet. What fits first?"
options:
  - "Tell his mentor or the coordinator this week how he has been"
  - "Pray more at night, and read about stress online for a few weeks"
  - "Wait until his exams are over, and then see if he still feels low"
  - "Keep it to himself for now, since his family has enough worry"
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. Six weeks of feeling low is a sign to talk to a real person now. When the Prophet, peace be upon him, was afraid, he told Khadijah, may Allah be pleased with her (Sahih al-Bukhari 3). His mentor or the fellowship coordinator can also help him find a doctor or counsellor."
  - "Prayer is good and should continue, and reading can help. But this plan still keeps him alone for weeks more. After six weeks, the step the course asks for is a real person, this week."
  - "Waiting is understandable with exams coming, but six weeks is already a long time. Feeling low can also make studying harder, so telling someone now helps the exams too."
  - "His family's worry is real, but his is real too. The Prophet told the person closest to him when he was afraid (Sahih al-Bukhari 3). Telling someone is not adding a burden; it is taking the means."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Rawya awaits the result of a remote internship interview, due Thursday. For three evenings she has refreshed her email and read forum posts about offers, and has not touched her portfolio. Tonight the power is on from 18:00 to 22:00. What fits first?"
options:
  - "Make dua for the result, and leave the portfolio until after Thursday"
  - "Keep checking the forum, so that she hears the news as early as others"
  - "Leave the result with Allah, and use tonight's power on her portfolio"
  - "Message the company tonight to ask if they can tell her the result early"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "Dua is a good part of waiting, but leaving her own work for days is not. Trust is in the heart; her next step is still hers to take."
  - "Checking the forum does not change the result, and it uses the hours that are hers. No soul knows what it will earn tomorrow (Quran 31:34)."
  - "Right. Only Allah knows what tomorrow brings (Quran 31:34). Her part is to be keen on what benefits her and not give up (Sahih Muslim 2664). Tonight that means four hours of power on her portfolio."
  - "Asking a company is sometimes fine, but they have already given a date. Messaging them tonight tries to reach what is not hers, while her own step waits."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Sajed accepted the first of two allowed job offers after checking the pay and hours, asking his mentor and his father, and praying istikhara. The night before he signs, no new fact has come, but he keeps doubting. What fits first?"
options:
  - "Pray istikhara again each night until the doubt about it has gone"
  - "Sign as planned, and put his trust in Allah now that he has decided"
  - "Ask more people for advice until most of them agree with his choice"
  - "Put off signing for a month to see how the other company does first"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "You may repeat istikhara when you are still unsure before you decide. But Sajed has already done each step and decided, and no new fact has come. The Quran's order after deciding is trust, not more asking."
  - "Right. The Quran teaches the order: consult, decide, then put your trust in Allah (Quran 3:159). He has gathered the facts, consulted and prayed istikhara. Now the step is to act and trust."
  - "Consulting is part of deciding (Quran 42:38), and he has already done it. Asking until people agree turns advice into a vote, and it puts off the trust that comes after deciding."
  - "Waiting a month will not show him the unseen. Only Allah knows what tomorrow holds (Quran 31:34), and putting off the decision he has made only adds to the worry."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Ibtisam, a junior web developer, has spent her weekends building a website, without pay, for the Quran school in her neighbourhood. After two months nobody at the school has thanked her, and she feels the work was wasted. What fits first?"
options:
  - "Stop the work for now, since the people she helps do not seem to value it"
  - "Ask the school to thank her in front of the parents, so the work is seen"
  - "Keep going, but work on the parts that people notice and praise"
  - "Renew her intention for Allah, and set a weekly pace she can keep up"
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "Feeling unthanked is human, but her work is not wasted. Actions are by intentions, and each person will have what they intended (Sahih al-Bukhari 1)."
  - "Thanking people matters, and it is part of thanking Allah (Sunan Abi Dawud 4811, graded sahih). But that is the school's part. Her part is the intention behind her own work."
  - "Aiming at praise moves the intention away from Allah. Each person will have what they intended (Sahih al-Bukhari 1), and the purpose of our lives is to worship Allah (Quran 51:56)."
  - "Right. Actions are by intentions (Sahih al-Bukhari 1), and work done for Allah is part of the purpose of our lives (Quran 51:56). A pace she can keep matters too: the deeds most loved by Allah are the most consistent, even if small (Sahih al-Bukhari 6464)."
```

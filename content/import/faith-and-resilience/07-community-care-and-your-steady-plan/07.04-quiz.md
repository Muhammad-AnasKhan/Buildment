```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Kinan, a junior developer, has a remote coding test on Sunday at 9:00 from his family's flat. Power in his area comes about 8 hours a day, on a schedule that changes. On Saturday night he says: 'I have put my trust in Allah, so I will not charge my power bank. Whatever is written will happen.' Which teaching fits first, and why?"
options:
  - "Tie it and trust: charge the power bank, plan a backup, then trust Allah"
  - "Qadr: the result is already written, so preparing changes nothing at all"
  - "Istikhara: pray first to find out whether he should sit the test at all"
  - "Sabr: stay calm on Sunday and accept whatever happens with the power"
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. The Prophet, peace be upon him, told the man with the camel to tie it and trust (Jami' at-Tirmidhi 2517, graded hasan), and the pilgrims who skipped provisions and called it trust were told to take provisions (Sahih al-Bukhari 1523 with Quran 2:197). Trust is in the heart; charging the power bank and planning a backup are the means."
  - "Belief in qadr is part of faith, but it is never a reason to stop. When the companions asked whether they should rely on what was written, the Prophet said: act (Sahih al-Bukhari 4949)."
  - "Istikhara is for choosing between permissible options. Kinan has already chosen to sit the test. What he is missing is the means, not a choice."
  - "Patience will help on Sunday, but sabr is not waiting without doing anything. Tonight there is a clear step that is his to take: charge the power bank."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Sana, a final-year nursing student, did not pass a certification exam she had prepared for over three months. That evening at her aunt's house she cried, and told her sister how disappointed she was. A relative said: 'If you had real sabr, you would not cry.' Which teaching fits first?"
options:
  - "The relative is right: real sabr means showing no sadness to anyone"
  - "She should hide her feelings from her sister, so nobody else worries"
  - "Tears are not a lack of sabr; the Prophet wept, and sabr keeps going"
  - "She should stop preparing, since failing shows it was not written for her"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "This is a common belief, but the sources say otherwise. The Prophet wept at his son's death and said the eye sheds tears and the heart grieves, and we say only what pleases our Lord (Sahih al-Bukhari 1303)."
  - "Sabr is not hiding need. Yaqub, peace be upon him, wept and complained of his grief to Allah, and still had beautiful patience (Quran 12:84 to 87). Telling her sister is not a failure of sabr."
  - "Right. Grief is human: the Prophet wept, and said we say only what pleases our Lord (Sahih al-Bukhari 1303). Sabr is in what she says and does next, such as booking the exam again, not in having no tears."
  - "One result does not tell her what Allah has written for her future. In module 1 you saw that the believer is told to be keen on what benefits and not to give up (Sahih Muslim 2664)."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Kareem, a designer who works from a small shared office, scrolls past posts from friends abroad about new jobs and degrees. One evening he tells his brother: 'I have wasted two years. Allah's mercy is not for someone like me.' Which teaching fits first?"
options:
  - "Gratitude only: list his blessings until the sad feeling goes away"
  - "Hope: do not despair of Allah's mercy, and take one step this week"
  - "Mercy alone: it is wide, so there is no need to change his habits"
  - "Comparison: keep checking their posts to push himself to work harder"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "Gratitude is a strong practice, but it is not pretending the sadness away, and it does not answer his words about mercy. What he said is despair, which hope answers."
  - "Right. Allah tells His servants not to despair of His mercy (Quran 39:53), and Ibrahim, peace be upon him, asked who despairs of his Lord's mercy except those who are astray (Quran 15:56). Despair is one of the two ways the balance of hope and fear breaks. Hope comes with action: one step this week."
  - "This is the other way the balance breaks: carelessness, hope without care. Hope in Allah's mercy comes with good work, not instead of it."
  - "Comparison is what led to his despair. Looking at those above you is the habit the Sunnah turns around: look at those below you, as a tool you choose for yourself (Sahih Muslim 2963)."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Yaman works in data entry at his uncle's office and has two permissible job offers. Last night he prayed istikhara before he had checked the pay or the hours, or asked anyone about either job. This morning he says he will wait for a dream to show him which offer to take. Which teaching fits first?"
options:
  - "Keep waiting for a dream, since that is how istikhara is answered"
  - "Skip istikhara, since nobody can know which offer will work out"
  - "Take the higher pay now, since asking people shows a lack of trust"
  - "Gather the facts and consult people; istikhara needs no dream"
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "Many people believe this, but the hadith of istikhara has no dream in it (Sahih al-Bukhari 1166), and no sound text requires one. Istikhara asks Allah to choose and to make the good easy."
  - "Not knowing the outcome is exactly why istikhara exists: in the dua, the believer says that Allah knows and he does not (Sahih al-Bukhari 1166)."
  - "Consulting is not weak trust. The Quran praises believers whose affairs are run by consultation among themselves (Quran 42:38)."
  - "Right. The course order is: learn the facts, ask people who know (Quran 42:38), and pray istikhara (Sahih al-Bukhari 1166). No dream is needed. He may pray it again once he has the facts, then go ahead with what seems best."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Rasha, a freelance illustrator who works from home, missed a client's deadline by four days and did not warn the client before it passed. The client is upset. Since then she keeps saying: 'If only I had refused that second project.' Which response fits first?"
options:
  - "Keep going over what she should have done until she feels certain"
  - "Tell the client honestly, put it right, and plan her work better"
  - "Treat it as written by Allah, so an apology or a change is not needed"
  - "Stop freelancing, since one missed deadline shows she is not suited"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "Going over it again and again is the 'if only' that the Prophet warned about: say instead 'Allah decreed, and He did what He willed', and take the next step (Sahih Muslim 2664)."
  - "Right. Missing a promised deadline without telling the client is a wrong to put right with the client. Then she learns from it, so she is not stung from the same hole twice (Sahih al-Bukhari 6133), and leaves 'if only' behind (Sahih Muslim 2664)."
  - "Qadr is not a reason to skip putting things right. The companions were told to act (Sahih al-Bukhari 4949), and the believer learns from a mistake (Sahih al-Bukhari 6133)."
  - "One failure is not a verdict on her. The believer is told not to give up (Sahih Muslim 2664), and the course taught starting again after a setback."
```

## Module 4 checklist

- [ ] I can write a claim as "reason, so claim" and find the hidden assumption between them
- [ ] I can use the flip test to check that I found the right assumption
- [ ] I ask three questions before I accept a cause: a third factor, the reverse cause and chance
- [ ] I check where a chart's axis starts and what time period it shows
- [ ] I answered the questions in chapters 4.2 to 4.4, and finished the quiz in 4.5
- [ ] I submitted the hidden assumption and its test below

## Learning log

1. **Your first claim:** Go back to the claim you wrote in chapter 4.1. Now that you know the flip test, what is the bridge between its reason and its claim?
2. **Cause:** Think of a time you believed that one thing caused another. Looking back, could a third factor or a reverse cause explain it? A made-up example is fine.
3. **Charts and stories:** Which do you find harder to question: a chart with numbers, or a story from a friend? Why?
4. **AI answers:** If an AI tool told you "X causes Y" tomorrow, which question would you ask first, and why that one?

## Your hidden assumption

Choose one claim you heard or read: from a friend, a seller, a message or an AI tool. It can be the same claim from chapter 4.1. If you prefer not to use a claim from your own life, use one of these instead:

- "This shop has the biggest sign on the street, so it must have the lowest prices."
- "Nour got a scholarship after joining the study group, so the study group will get me a scholarship too."

Write the claim as "reason, so claim". Then find the hidden assumption and use the flip test to show it matters. Finish with one small, cheap way to test it. A simple list is fine. A mentor will read your answer and may ask how you would carry out your test.

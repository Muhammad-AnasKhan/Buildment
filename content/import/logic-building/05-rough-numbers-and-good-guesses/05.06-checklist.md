## Module 5 checklist

- [ ] I can break a big number question into small guesses and multiply them
- [ ] I write each guess down as an assumption, with round numbers
- [ ] I give a low and a high answer instead of one exact-looking number
- [ ] Before I trust a number, I check its size, its units, and what it means per person or per day
- [ ] I can find an average and the middle value, and say when the average misleads
- [ ] I answered the questions in chapters 5.2 to 5.4, and finished the quiz in 5.5

## Learning log

1. **Your first reaction:** Think back to the bakery question in chapter 5.1. Before you read Rania's steps, what did your mind do: guess one number, give up, or start to break it down? What would you do now?
2. **The hardest guess:** Which kind of small guess feels hardest for you: how many people, how often something happens, or how much something costs? Why do you think that is?
3. **A number to check:** Describe a number you saw recently, in a message, a shop, an advert or an AI answer, and one quick check you could run on it. If nothing comes to mind, use the post "Our 40 students drank 8,000 cups of tea."
4. **Average or middle:** When would you ask for the middle value instead of the average? Give one example from study, work or shopping.

## Your estimate

Choose one number question that is useful to you, from study, work, shopping or your street. If you prefer not to use your own life, use the question in the box below about a small cafe. A simple list is fine. You do not need a table, and the numbers only need to be rough. What matters is that every guess is written down where someone else can see it. A mentor will read your answer and may suggest a guess to test. Do not worry if your final number turns out far from the real one. A clear estimate with a wrong guess inside it is easy to fix. An estimate with hidden guesses is not.

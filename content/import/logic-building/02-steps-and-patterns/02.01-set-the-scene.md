## A big lunch on Friday

Maryam's family is hosting twelve relatives for lunch on Friday at 14:00. Everyone wants to help, and everyone starts at once.

Her brother Omar buys the bread on Thursday evening, so it is dry by Friday. Her mother wants to cook the rice first thing in the morning, but nobody soaked it. Her cousin Nour sets the table at 9:00, then has to clear it again, because the table is needed for chopping vegetables. At 13:30 the chicken is still cooking, because nobody put it in the oven early enough.

Every person did a useful job. The problem was the order. Some jobs must come before others: you soak the rice before you cook it. Some jobs can happen at the same time: one person chops the salad while the rice soaks. And some jobs should happen late: buying fresh bread and setting the table.

## Maryam writes it down

After lunch, Maryam says: "Next time, let us write the steps down." She makes a short list for the next family meal.

1. Thursday: buy rice, chicken and vegetables. Do not buy bread yet.
2. Friday 11:00: wash the rice and leave it to soak.
3. Friday 11:30: put the chicken in the oven.
4. While the chicken cooks: chop the salad.
5. Friday 12:30: buy fresh bread.
6. Friday 13:00: cook the rice.
7. Friday 13:30: set the table.

Her list is not fancy. It is clear about what, when and in which order. Anyone in the family could pick it up and help without asking her.

That is the heart of this module. A list of clear steps in order is called an algorithm. Computers are given algorithms to follow, but so are cooks, shop assistants and new freelancers. You do not need any code to think this way.

## A warm-up puzzle

Here is a small puzzle about order. Some steps need your hands, and some only need time.

```predict
prompt: "Maryam needs rice and a salad. Washing the rice takes 5 minutes of her time. Then it must soak for 30 minutes. Then it cooks for 20 minutes. Chopping the salad takes 15 minutes of her time. She starts at 12:00 and works alone. What is the earliest time both can be ready?"
options:
  - id: one-by-one
    label: "13:10"
  - id: side-by-side
    label: "12:55"
correctOptionId: side-by-side
explanation: "She can chop the salad while the rice soaks, because soaking does not need her hands. So the rice decides the time: 5 plus 30 plus 20 is 55 minutes, which makes 12:55. Doing every job one after another would take 70 minutes."
```

The idea in this puzzle comes back again and again. Look for steps that must wait for each other, and steps that can happen side by side.

## Key words

| Word | Plain English |
|---|---|
| **Algorithm** | A list of clear steps, in order, that gets a task done |
| **Step** | One clear action, such as "soak the rice for 30 minutes" |
| **Order** | Which step comes first, second, third and so on |
| **Pattern** | Something that repeats or changes in a regular way |
| **Rule** | The instruction that makes each item in a pattern |
| **Flowchart** | A drawing of a decision, with yes and no arrows |

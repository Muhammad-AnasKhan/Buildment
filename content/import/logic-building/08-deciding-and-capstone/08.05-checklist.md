## Your thinking toolkit

These are the habits from the whole course, one line each. You do not need to remember them all at once. Come back to this list when a choice or a claim feels hard.

- **Read slowly:** check what the question really asks before you answer it.
- **Fast or slow:** answer fast when it is cheap and easy to undo, and stop to check when money, deadlines or other people are involved.
- **Be stuck well:** draw it, try a small case, work backward, or ask a clear question.
- **Write steps:** write them so someone else could follow them without asking you.
- **Check the pattern:** test a rule on every item, not only the first two.
- **Draw the decision:** a flowchart with yes and no paths makes a choice easy to follow.
- **Read rules carefully:** "if it rains, the street is wet" does not mean a wet street proves rain.
- **Cross out:** in a puzzle, write what you know and cross out what cannot be true.
- **Find the assumption:** ask what must be true, but was not said, for the claim to work.
- **Cause or coincidence:** when two things rise together, look for a third factor.
- **Read charts honestly:** check the axis, the time period and the size of the sample.
- **Guess well:** break a big number into small guesses, and say each guess out loud.
- **Sanity check:** ask if a number fits its size, its units and what you already know.
- **Watch the average:** one very large number can pull an average, so look at the middle value too.
- **Split the argument:** find the claim, the reasons and the evidence, and name any trap.
- **Spot the scam:** pay first, pressure to act today and too much money for little work all mean stop and check.
- **Disagree well:** say the other view fairly first, then reply politely with a reason.
- **Ask well:** goal, context, steps, format and how to check.
- **Check always:** rough numbers, the source and another way. A name of a source is not a source.
- **Know when not to:** private data, health and legal decisions, your own learning and honest work.
- **Decide fairly:** judge a choice by what you knew, and ask which option you would regret more.
- **Test small:** cheap, quick, real and able to fail, with the go or stop line written first.

Look at the list once more. Most of these habits are questions you ask before you trust, decide or act. That is what clear thinking looks like: not knowing every answer, but knowing what to ask.

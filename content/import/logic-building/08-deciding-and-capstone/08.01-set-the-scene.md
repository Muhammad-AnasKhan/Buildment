## A stall at the weekend market

Nour sews bags at home. Friends like them, and a few have paid her for one. Now she has a bigger idea: renting a stall at the weekend market. Before you meet her thinking, try the first sum she had to do.

```predict
prompt: "Nour sells each bag for 25 shekels. The cloth and thread for one bag cost 15 shekels. The stall costs 50 shekels for the day. How many bags must she sell so she does not lose money?"
options:
  - id: two
    label: "2 bags, since 2 x 25 shekels is 50"
  - id: five
    label: "5 bags, since each earns her 10"
correctOptionId: five
explanation: "Each bag earns her 25 - 15 = 10 shekels after materials. To cover 50 shekels, she needs 50 / 10 = 5 bags. The fast answer, 2 bags, forgets that each bag also costs her 15 shekels to make."
```

If you said 2 bags, good: now you know the trick. Reading slowly, from module 1, catches it.

## Every habit in one choice

Nour does not know how the day will go. She cannot know. But she can think it through, and she uses almost every habit from this course without noticing.

- **Steps (module 2).** She lists what must happen: book the stall, sew enough bags, carry them there, set prices, collect money, count at the end.
- **Rules (module 3).** The market rule says: "If you cancel less than two days before, you lose the fee." She reads it carefully. It does not say what happens if the market itself closes.
- **Assumptions (module 4).** She assumes people at this market buy bags. She has never checked that.
- **Rough numbers (module 5).** She needs 5 bags just to cover the stall. She has 12 ready.
- **Weak arguments (module 6).** A neighbour says: "Everyone who sells there does well." That is "everyone does it", with no evidence.
- **AI check (module 7).** She asks an AI chat tool how many people visit the market. It says "about 2,000 a day" and gives no source. She marks it as unchecked.

All those habits still leave one question: what should she **do**? That is this module. You will learn to judge a decision by what you knew at the time, not by luck. You will learn to try a small, cheap test before a big bet. Nour, for example, could share a stall with a friend for one day before she pays for a whole month.

## The capstone

Then comes the capstone. You will think through one choice in five parts: the steps, two hidden assumptions, a rough number, a check of an AI answer and a decision. You can use a real choice from your study, work or daily life. Or you can use one of three provided scenarios. Both are fully welcome. Nobody has to share anything personal.

At the end, you walk a mentor through your five parts in about five minutes.

## Key words

| Word | Plain English |
|---|---|
| **Uncertainty** | Not knowing for sure how things will turn out |
| **Outcome** | What actually happened after you decided |

| **Regret** | The feeling of wishing you had chosen differently |
| **Small test** | A cheap, quick try that tells you something before you commit |

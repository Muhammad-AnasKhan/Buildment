## Learning log

There are no right or wrong answers here. Write two to four sentences for each question, in simple English. Short answers are fine, and nobody grades your grammar. If you prefer not to use your own life, use one of the puzzles or stories from this module instead.

1. **Too fast:** Think of a time you answered too fast and then had to fix it. It can be small: a sum, a message or a promise about time. Or use a puzzle from this module that caught you. What made the fast answer feel right?
2. **Read slowly:** Which trick from chapter 1.2 catches you most often: a question that asks something you did not expect, extra details that do not matter, or small words such as "all but"? What will you do differently next time?
3. **Fast or slow:** Name one choice in a normal week where a fast answer is fine, and one where you will now stop and check. Which of the three questions decides it?
4. **When stuck:** Which of the four moves will you try first the next time you are stuck: draw it, try a small case, work backward or ask a clear question? Why that one?

Here is an example of a short answer to the first question, from Khaled, a made-up learner: "In chapter 1.1 I said the pen costs 1 shekel. It felt right because 11 minus 10 is 1. I did not put my answer back into the question to test it. Next time I will check my answer against every fact before I say it." Your answers can be this short. You will look back at this log at the end of the course, so write what is true for you today.

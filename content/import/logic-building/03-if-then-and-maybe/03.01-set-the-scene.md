## One rule, three friends

Huda finds an advert for the Next Step Scholarship (a made-up scholarship). It pays for a one-year course in design. The advert has one rule in big letters:

**"If you are under 25 and have finished secondary school, you can apply."**

Huda sends the advert to three friends.

- **Sami** is 23. He finished secondary school four years ago.
- **Nour** is 27. She finished secondary school and then university.
- **Yousef** is 19. He is in his last year of secondary school. He finishes in two months.

Before you read on, decide for yourself who can apply today.

## Why the answer is one

The rule has two parts joined by the word **and**. A person must fit **both** parts.

Sami is under 25, and he has finished secondary school. Both parts are true, so he can apply.

Nour has more education than anyone in the group. She feels sure she can apply. But the rule does not ask for more education. It asks for an age under 25, and Nour is 27. One part is false, so the rule does not let her apply.

Yousef is young enough. But "have finished" means finished already, not finishing soon. Today, one part is false. In two months, he can apply.

This is the heart of the module. A rule says exactly what it says. It does not say what feels fair, or what we hope it says.

## And the "maybe"

Look at the end of the rule again: "you **can apply**". It does not say "you will get the scholarship". Sami can apply, but he may not win. The rule tells us who is allowed in, not who wins.

Many mistakes come from reading more into a rule than it says. In this module you learn to read rules slowly, to tell what really follows from them, and to solve puzzles with them. A computer uses these same skills when it checks a form. You need them too when you check what an AI tool tells you.

## Key words

| Word | Plain English |
|---|---|
| **Rule** | A sentence that says what happens when something is true |
| **Condition** | Something that must be true for the rule to apply |
| **And** | Both parts must be true |
| **Or** | At least one part must be true; both is fine too |
| **Not** | Turns true into false, and false into true |
| **State** | The one situation something is in right now |

## Module 3 checklist

- [ ] I can split a rule into its parts and check each part on its own
- [ ] I know that "or" in a rule means one or both, and that "and" means every part
- [ ] I can say which facts let me conclude something from a rule, and which do not
- [ ] I can solve a small logic grid without guessing
- [ ] I answered the questions in chapters 3.2 to 3.5, and finished the quiz in 3.6
- [ ] I solved and explained the puzzle below

## Learning log

1. **Slow reading:** Think of a rule you read this week, on a form, a sign or a message. Did you read every part of it, or did you stop at the first part that fit?
2. **Backwards:** When did you last hear someone reason from a result back to a cause, like "the street is wet, so it rained"? You can use an example from a film, a story or the chapters if you prefer.
3. **The grid:** What helped you more in the logic grid puzzles: the clues, or the hidden facts such as "each person has exactly one"? Why?
4. **Next time:** Which habit from this module will you use first the next time a rule or a puzzle confuses you?

## Your puzzle

Everyone solves the same puzzle here, so you do not need to share anything about your own life. Read it slowly. Draw a small grid on paper if it helps.

A community centre has three study groups: **English**, **maths** and **drawing**. Three volunteers, **Amal**, **Khaled** and **Nour**, each lead exactly one group. No two lead the same group. Here are the clues:

1. If Amal leads the maths group, then Nour leads the English group.
2. Nour does not lead the maths group.
3. Nour does not lead the English group.

Work out who leads each group. Then explain how you got there, one step at a time, in your own words. Say which clue or which hidden fact you used for each step. At the end, check your answer against all three clues. Clue 1 is an if-then rule, so think back to chapter 3.3 about what it lets you conclude.

A simple numbered list of steps is fine. You do not need to draw the grid in your answer. A mentor will read your explanation, and a clear explanation matters more than speed.

## Learning log

1. **Your trap:** Which of the six traps do you think is easiest for you to fall for, as a reader? Why do you think it works on you?
2. **Your first move:** When someone says something you disagree with, what do you usually do first? What would you like to do first from now on?
3. **An offer to check:** Think of an offer you have seen: a job, a scholarship, a prize or a sale. Which warning signs did it have, and which did it not have? If nothing comes to mind, use Basel's message from chapter 6.4.
4. **What would change your mind:** Pick a study habit you have now. What evidence would make you change it? Be specific: what would you need to see?

## Your polite reply

Write a polite reply to a weak claim. You can use a claim you have really met, in a message, an advert or a conversation. If you prefer not to use your own life, use the forwarded message below. A simple list is fine, and your reply can be short. A mentor will read it and may suggest a way to make it clearer or kinder. Remember the goal: the other person should feel heard, and should also see clearly what the claim is missing.

> Everyone at university is switching to studying only from short videos. Books are a waste of time. A popular video maker said so. If you keep reading books, you will fall behind and fail your degree.
